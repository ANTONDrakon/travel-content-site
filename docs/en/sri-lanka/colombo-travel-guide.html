Welcome to Colombo — Sri Lanka’s bustling capital where the old world meets the new in a swirl of colors, spices, and sounds. As a first-time visitor, you’ll be struck by the city’s raw energy: tuk-tuks weaving through traffic, the scent of cinnamon and cardamom drifting from street stalls, and the serene Indian Ocean lapping at the shore. Colombo isn’t a polished, postcard-perfect city; it’s a living, breathing organism that invites you to dive into its chaotic charm. Whether you’re exploring colonial-era buildings in the Fort area or bargaining at Pettah Market, every corner tells a story of trade, resilience, and warmth.

What makes this a great destination in 2026? For one, it’s the perfect gateway to Sri Lanka’s iconic beaches and hill country, but the city itself is a treasure trove of experiences. You’ll find world-class museums, vibrant street art, and a food scene that blends Sinhalese, Tamil, Muslim, and Dutch influences. Plus, with new direct flights and improved infrastructure, getting here has never been easier. Colombo is affordable, friendly, and endlessly surprising — ideal for travelers seeking an authentic South Asian adventure without the crowds of Bangkok or Mumbai.

Spend your mornings sipping king coconut water by the Galle Face Green, your afternoons wandering through temples and markets, and your evenings enjoying fresh seafood at a beachside restaurant. The city’s rhythm is laid-back yet lively, and its people are genuinely welcoming. Whether you’re a budget backpacker or a luxury seeker, Colombo offers a slice of Sri Lankan life that will linger in your memory long after you’ve left.

How to Get There

Colombo’s Bandaranaike International Airport (CMB) is your main entry point, located about 35 kilometers north of the city center. From Moscow, you can fly direct with Aeroflot or connect via Dubai or Doha with Emirates, Qatar Airways, or flydubai. A round-trip economy ticket from Moscow typically costs between ₽45 000 and ₽85 000 (≈ $500–$950), depending on the season. Where to Stay

Colombo’s neighborhoods offer distinct vibes, from the business district of Fort to the trendy cafes of Colombo 7 (Cinnamon Gardens). For luxury, book the Galle Face Hotel (from ₽25 000/night, ≈ $275) — a colonial-era icon steps from the ocean. Mid-range travelers love the Cinnamon Red hotel in Colombo 1, with rooftop pool and city views (₽8 000–₽12 000/night, ≈ $88–$130). Budget options include hostels like Colombo City Hostel in Colombo 3 (₽1 500–₽3 000/night, ≈ $16–$33) or guesthouses in the Mount Lavinia area near the beach. Top 15 Attractions

  • 1. Gangaramaya Temple — A stunning Buddhist temple blending Sri Lankan, Thai, Indian, and Chinese architecture. Don’t miss the museum with eclectic artifacts. Free entry, donations welcome. Open daily 6:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  • 2. Galle Face Green — A 500-meter oceanfront promenade perfect for sunset strolls, kite flying, and street food. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  • 3. Pettah Market — A chaotic, colorful bazaar where you can buy spices, textiles, electronics, and fresh produce. Haggling is expected. Free entry. Open daily 8:00 AM–7:00 PM.
  • 4. National Museum of Colombo — Sri Lanka’s largest museum, with artifacts dating back 2,500 years, including the throne of the Kandyan kings. Entry: ₽1 500 (≈ $16). Open 9:00 AM–5:00 PM (closed Mondays).
  • 5. Colombo Lotus Tower — A 350-meter telecommunications tower with an observation deck offering 360-degree city views. Entry: ₽2 500 (≈ $27). Open 9:00 AM–10:00 PM.
  • 6. Viharamahadevi Park — A lush public park with a giant Buddha statue, playgrounds, and a small lake. Free entry. Open 6:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  • 7. Dutch Hospital Shopping Precinct — A restored 17th-century hospital turned into a dining and shopping complex. Great for colonial architecture and craft beer. Free entry. Open 10:00 AM–11:00 PM.
  • 8. Independence Memorial Hall — A grand monument commemorating Sri Lanka’s independence from Britain in 1948. Surrounded by gardens. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  • 9. Seema Malaka Temple — A serene Buddhist temple built on stilts over Beira Lake, designed by Geoffrey Bawa. Free entry, donations appreciated. Open 6:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  • 10. Beira Lake — A scenic lake in the heart of the city, ideal for boat rides and birdwatching. Boat rides: ₽500 (≈ $5.50). Open 8:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  • 11. Jami Ul-Alfar Mosque — A striking red-and-white striped mosque in Pettah, built in 1909. Non-Muslims can enter the courtyard. Free entry. Open 9:00 AM–12:00 PM and 2:00 PM–5:00 PM (closed during prayer times).
  • 12. Colombo National Museum (Natural History) — A separate wing of the National Museum focusing on Sri Lanka’s flora, fauna, and geology. Entry: ₽1 000 (≈ $11). Open 9:00 AM–5:00 PM (closed Mondays).
  • 13. Mount Lavinia Beach — A popular sandy beach just 12 km south of Colombo, perfect for swimming and sunset drinks. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  • 14. Nelum Pokuna Mahinda Rajapaksa Theatre — A modern performing arts venue shaped like a lotus flower. Check for dance or music performances. Tickets from ₽2 000 (≈ $22). Open for events only.
  • 15. Barefoot Gallery & Shop — A charming cafe and boutique showcasing local handicrafts, batik, and art. Great for souvenirs. Free entry. Open 10:00 AM–7:00 PM (closed Sundays).

Getting Around

Colombo’s public transport is cheap but chaotic. The city has a light rail system (Colombo Metro) under construction, but for 2026, buses and tuk-tuks remain king. Local buses cost ₽30–₽100 (≈ $0.30–$1.10) per ride, but expect crowds. For comfort, use ride-hailing apps like Uber or PickMe (tuk-tuks from ₽200–₽500, ≈ $2.20–$5.50). Taxis are pricier at ₽1 000–₽3 000 (≈ $11–$33) for short trips. Rental cars with drivers cost about ₽5 000 (≈ $55) per day. For airport transfers, consider Kiwitaxi — a pre-booked service with fixed prices starting at ₽3 500 (≈ $38). Walking is pleasant in areas like Fort and Cinnamon Gardens, but avoid long walks during peak heat (12:00–3:00 PM).

Where to Eat

Colombo’s food scene is a melting pot of flavors. For street food, head to Galle Face Green in the evening for isso vade (shrimp fritters) and kottu roti (chopped flatbread with vegetables and meat) — prices start at ₽300 (≈ $3.30). Mid-range restaurants like Ministry of Crab (Crab Curry, ₽4 500, ≈ $50) or The Lagoon (seafood platters, ₽3 000, ≈ $33) are excellent. Budget eateries like Hotel de Pilawoos (rice and curry, ₽800, ≈ $8.80) or Upali’s (buffet, ₽1 500, ≈ $16) serve authentic local dishes. Don’t miss hoppers (bowl-shaped pancakes) for breakfast — try them at Nuga Gama (₽600, ≈ $6.60). Fine dining at The Gallery Cafe (₽6 000, ≈ $66) offers a unique art-filled setting.

Best Time to Visit

Colombo has a tropical climate with two monsoon seasons. The best time is December to March (dry season), when skies are clear and humidity is lower. Temperatures hover around 28–32°C (82–90°F). Prices for flights and hotels peak during this period — expect to pay 20–30% more. The inter-monsoonal months (April and November) see brief afternoon showers but fewer crowds. May to October is the southwest monsoon, with heavy rain and rough seas — but flights can be 40% cheaper. If you’re on a budget, consider visiting in June or September; just pack an umbrella. Visa & Documents

For Russian citizens, Sri Lanka requires an Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) — a simple online application processed within 24 hours. Apply at eta.gov.lk. The cost is ₽3 500 (≈ $38) for a 30-day tourist visa. You’ll need a valid passport (6+ months), a return ticket, and proof of accommodation. Upon arrival, you’ll get a 30-day visa stamp. Extensions are possible at the Department of Immigration in Colombo (₽5 000, ≈ $55). 10 Travel Tips

Don't forget travel insurance!

get travel insurance
  1. Get a local SIM: Buy a Dialog or Mobitel SIM at the airport (₽1 000 for 15 GB, ≈ $11). Use Airalo for an eSIM if you prefer to book online before arrival.
  2. Dress modestly: Cover shoulders and knees when entering temples. Remove shoes and hats before entering.
  3. Bargain wisely: Haggling is expected in Pettah Market, but be respectful. Start at 50% of the asking price.
  4. Stay hydrated: Drink bottled water (₽50, ≈ $0.55) or coconut water (₽100, ≈ $1.10). Avoid tap water.
  5. Use tuk-tuk apps: Download PickMe or Uber to avoid overcharging. Metered tuk-tuks are rare.
  6. Watch out for scams: Be wary of “free” tours or gemstone deals. Stick to licensed guides.
  7. Try local food: Don’t miss kottu roti, hoppers, and curry. Street food is safe if freshly cooked.
  8. Plan for rain: Carry an umbrella or raincoat, especially between May and October.
  9. Learn a few words: “Ayubowan” (hello) and “Istuti” (thank you) go a long way.
  10. Book tours in advance: For popular attractions like the Lotus Tower or a guided city tour, check GetYourGuide for skip-the-line options and reviews.
