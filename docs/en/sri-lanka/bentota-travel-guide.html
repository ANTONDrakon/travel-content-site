Imagine stepping off a plane and, within two hours, finding yourself on a golden beach where the Indian Ocean laps gently at the shore, palm trees sway overhead, and the only sound is the rustle of leaves and the distant call of a peacock. That’s Bentota — a coastal town on Sri Lanka’s southwestern coast that feels like a secret handshake between luxury and raw nature. Bentota isn’t a bustling city; it’s a resort town, a place built for slowing down. The Bentota River weaves through the landscape, creating a lush tapestry of mangroves, coconut groves, and quiet lagoons. The atmosphere is one of serene indulgence: think sundowner cocktails on the beach, turtle hatcheries, and the occasional monitor lizard crossing your path. Why visit? Because Bentota offers a rare blend of relaxation and adventure — you can kayak through a mangrove tunnel in the morning and be sipping fresh king coconut by noon. It’s also the gateway to the Galle Fort (just 45 minutes away) and the Sinharaja Rainforest (a couple of hours drive), making it a perfect base for exploring Sri Lanka’s southern highlights.

For me, Bentota is the kind of place where you forget what day it is. The pace is dictated by the tides, not your watch. Families love it for the calm waters and safe beaches; couples adore it for the sunset cruises; solo travelers find peace in yoga retreats. The town itself is small — a main road lined with shops selling sarongs and spices, a few local eateries, and that endless stretch of sand. What makes Bentota special is its accessibility: it’s close enough to Colombo (about 85 km) to feel connected, yet far enough to feel like a world away. Whether you’re here for the Bentota Beach Hotel’s famous seafood buffet or to watch baby turtles scuttle into the sea, Bentota wraps you in a warm, lazy embrace. And in 2026, with improved infrastructure and new eco-lodges opening, it’s only getting better.

The best part? Bentota is affordable. You can live like royalty on a mid-range budget, or backpack your way through with a few thousand rubles a day. The locals are incredibly friendly, the food is a riot of spices and coconut, and the sunsets are free. So, if you’re looking for a destination that combines beach bliss with cultural richness, without the crowds of Bali or Phuket, Bentota is calling your name.

How to Get There

The main gateway is Bandaranaike International Airport (CMB) in Colombo, about a 2-hour drive from Bentota. Direct flights from Moscow, St. Petersburg, and other Russian cities are available seasonally, with average prices ranging from ₽55 000 to ₽90 000 (≈ $600 to $1000) for a round trip in economy class. From Europe, you can often find deals for around ₽40 000 to ₽70 000 (≈ $515 (€450) to $915 (€800)). Most flights have a layover in Dubai, Doha, or Istanbul. Once you land, you can take a pre-booked taxi (₽4 500 to ₽7 000, or ≈ $50 to $80) or hop on a local bus (₽300, or ≈ $3.50) to Aluthgama station, then a short tuk-tuk to Bentota. For the best flight deals, I always check options on Aviasales — you can compare prices across hundreds of airlines and find the cheapest dates. Just remember, prices fluctuate, so book early for peak season (December to March).

Where to Stay

Bentota’s accommodation scene is wonderfully diverse. The most popular area is the beachfront strip, where you’ll find everything from five-star resorts to charming guesthouses. For luxury, the Bentota Beach Hotel (from ₽12 000 per night, ≈ $130) offers direct beach access, multiple pools, and a famed seafood buffet. Mid-range travelers love Villa Bentota (₽5 000 to ₽8 000, ≈ $55 to $90) — a boutique property with a pool and garden. Budget backpackers flock to Bentota Rest House (₽2 000 to ₽3 500, ≈ $22 to $40) for basic rooms and a great location. If you prefer quieter stays, head to the river side (e.g., River House), where you can wake up to the sound of birds. For families, self-catering apartments near Aluthgama market are a steal. I always recommend checking prices on Hotellook to compare hotels across platforms — you can often save 20-30% by booking in advance. For a wider selection, Booking is my go-to for verified reviews and free cancellation. If you’re visiting during peak season (December to March), book at least 2-3 months ahead, as prices can double.

Top 15 Attractions

  • 1. Bentota Beach — The main beach, with golden sand and calm waters perfect for swimming. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  • 2. Bentota River Safari — A 2-hour boat ride through mangroves, spotting crocodiles, monkeys, and birds. ₽1 500 per person (≈ $17). Daily 8 AM – 5 PM.
  • 3. Turtle Hatchery — A conservation project where you can see baby turtles and release them into the sea. ₽500 entry (≈ $6). Open 8 AM – 6 PM.
  • 4. Brief Garden (Bevis Bawa Garden) — A stunning 5-acre garden with sculptures and tropical plants, 15 minutes from Bentota. ₽1 000 (≈ $11). Open 8 AM – 5 PM.
  • 5. Kande Vihara Temple — A hilltop Buddhist temple with giant Buddha statues and panoramic views. Free (donations welcome). Open 6 AM – 6 PM.
  • 6. Galapata Raja Maha Vihara — An ancient cave temple with 18th-century murals, 10 km inland. Free. Open 7 AM – 6 PM.
  • 7. Lunuganga Estate — Geoffrey Bawa’s country house, with beautiful gardens and architecture. ₽1 500 (≈ $17). Open 9 AM – 5 PM.
  • 8. Induruwa Beach — A quieter stretch of sand just south of Bentota, great for sunbathing. Free. Open 24/7.
  • 9. Bentota Lagoon — Perfect for kayaking or stand-up paddleboarding. Rentals from ₽800 per hour (≈ $9). Open daylight hours.
  • 10. Sea Turtle Conservation Project — Another hatchery near the river mouth. ₽500 (≈ $6). Open 8 AM – 6 PM.
  • 11. Aluthgama Market — A bustling local market for spices, tea, and souvenirs. Free entry. Open 7 AM – 8 PM (closed Sundays).
  • 12. Madu Ganga River Safari — A larger river system with islands and cinnamon plantations, 20 minutes away. ₽2 000 (≈ $22). 2-hour tours available.
  • 13. Barberyn Island — A tiny island accessible by boat, with a small temple. Boat ride about ₽1 000 (≈ $11).
  • 14. Bentota Diving Center — Offers PADI courses and reef dives. ₽4 000 per dive (≈ $45). Open 8 AM – 5 PM.
  • 15. Cinnamon Island — A visit to a working cinnamon plantation on the river. ₽1 200 (≈ $13). Tours run daily.

Getting Around

Bentota is small enough to walk along the beach, but for exploring further, you have options. Tuk-tuks are everywhere: short rides within town cost ₽200 to ₽400 (≈ $2 to $4.50). For longer trips (e.g., to Galle), negotiate around ₽1 500 (≈ $17). Local buses are dirt cheap (₽50 to ₽100 per ride, ≈ $0.50 to $1.10) but crowded — they run along the main road from Aluthgama to Galle. Renting a scooter is popular: ₽1 500 to ₽2 500 per day (≈ $17 to $28), plus fuel. You’ll need an international driver’s permit. For airport transfers, I recommend Kiwitaxi — they offer fixed prices, English-speaking drivers, and you can book online. A one-way trip from CMB to Bentota costs around ₽5 500 (≈ $60). If you need a local SIM for navigation, grab an eSIM from Airalo before you travel — it’s cheap and hassle-free.

Where to Eat

Bentota’s food scene is a love letter to Sri Lankan cuisine — think rice and curry, hoppers, and fresh seafood. For a splurge, Bentota Beach Hotel’s buffet (₽3 500 per person, ≈ $40) offers everything from grilled prawns to sambol. Mid-range gems include Luna’s Food Corner (₽800 to ₽1 500 per dish, ≈ $9 to $17) for authentic kottu roti and devilled chicken. For street food, hit the Aluthgama night market — try isso vade (shrimp fritters) for ₽100 (≈ $1.10). Budget eaters love Wijaya Beach Restaurant (₽400 to ₽800, ≈ $4.50 to $9) for simple rice and curry. Don’t miss fresh king coconut from roadside stalls (₽100, ≈ $1.10). A meal at a local eatery costs ₽500 to ₽1 000 (≈ $6 to $11), while a fancy dinner might run ₽3 000 (≈ $34). Pro tip: eat where the locals eat — the food is spicier but unforgettable.

Best Time to Visit

Bentota has two monsoon seasons, but the best time is December to March (peak season). During these months, the weather is dry, sunny, and perfect for beach days — temperatures hover around 28–32°C. Prices are highest: hotel rates can double. April to September is the southwest monsoon season, with heavy rain and rough seas. But it’s also the cheapest time to visit, with hotel prices dropping by 40-50%. I’ve visited in June and still had sunny mornings — just expect afternoon downpours. October and November are transition months, with occasional rain but fewer crowds. For budget travelers, aim for May or September; for perfect weather, book December. The water temperature is always warm (27–30°C), so swimming is possible year-round.

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens need an Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) to enter Sri Lanka. Apply online at the official ETA website (eta.gov.lk) at least 24 hours before departure. The process is simple: fill in your passport details, travel dates, and pay the fee — ₽3 500 (≈ $38) for a standard tourist visa (30 days, double entry). Approval usually comes within 24 hours; print the confirmation. Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months from arrival. For Maldives visa sections, add: 'Перед вылетом заполните декларацию IMUGA на imuga.immigration.gov.mv и получите QR-код — без него не посадят на рейс.' (Note: This applies if you’re combining Sri Lanka with the Maldives.) Keep a copy of your hotel booking and return ticket handy — immigration may ask. The ETA is valid for 6 months from issue, so you can apply in advance.

10 Travel Tips

  1. Learn a few Sinhala phrases — “Ayubowan” (hello) and “Istuti” (thank you) go a long way with locals.
  2. Drink bottled water — Tap water isn’t safe. A 1.5L bottle costs ₽100 (≈ $1.10).
  3. Bargain respectfully — In markets and tuk-tuks, negotiate but don’t be aggressive. Start at 50% of the asking price.
  4. Cover up at temples — Shoulders and knees must be covered; remove shoes and hats before entering.
  5. Use sunscreen and mosquito repellent — The sun is strong, and dengue mosquitoes are active at dawn/dusk.
  6. Try local hoppers — These bowl-shaped pancakes are a breakfast staple, often with an egg in the center.
  7. Book a river safari — It’s the best way to see wildlife; go early morning for the most active animals.
  8. Carry small bills — Many shops won’t have change for large notes (₽5 000+). Break money at a bank.
  9. Check visa requirements early — Apply for your ETA at least a week before to avoid last-minute stress.
  10. Stay connected — Buy a local SIM (Dialog or Mobitel) at the airport for about ₽1 000 (≈ $11) with 10GB data. Or use Airalo for an eSIM.
