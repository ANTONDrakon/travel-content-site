Unawatuna is a crescent-shaped beach town on Sri Lanka’s southern coast, about 5 kilometers east of Galle. It’s the kind of place where life slows to the rhythm of the waves—think turquoise water, golden sand, and a thick canopy of palm trees that shade rustic cafes and guesthouses. The vibe is laid-back but lively: backpackers swap stories over fresh king coconut, families splash in the gentle surf, and local fishermen haul in their catch at sunset. Unlike the party-heavy beaches of Hikkaduwa, Unawatuna feels like a genuine tropical escape, where you can spend the morning snorkeling over coral reefs and the afternoon dozing in a hammock.

Why is 2026 the year to visit? The town has matured without losing its soul. New boutique hotels have opened alongside family-run guesthouses, and the restaurant scene now rivals Colombo’s for fresh seafood and Sri Lankan curries. Yet the main draw remains the same: the bay’s calm, reef-protected waters are perfect for swimming year-round, and the nearby UNESCO-listed Galle Fort offers a dose of colonial history just a tuk-tuk ride away. Prices are still reasonable compared to the Maldives or Thailand, making Unawatuna a top pick for travelers who want beach bliss without the premium markup.

Whether you’re a solo traveler looking for yoga retreats, a couple seeking romantic sunsets, or a family craving safe swimming, Unawatuna delivers. The local community is welcoming, the street food is addictive (try the kottu roti from a roadside stall), and every evening ends with the same orange-pink sky melting into the Indian Ocean. It’s not just a destination—it’s a state of mind.

How to Get There

The nearest international airport is Bandaranaike International Airport (CMB) in Colombo, about 150 kilometers north of Unawatuna. Direct flights from Moscow (SVO) to CMB take roughly 8–9 hours, with average prices ranging from ₽45 000–₽80 000 (≈ $490–$870) for a round-trip economy ticket in 2026. From Europe, flights from London or Frankfurt cost around ₽55 000–₽100 000 (≈ $600–$1090). Once you land, the easiest transfer is a pre-booked taxi or a Kiwitaxi private car (about ₽4 500–₽7 000 / $49–$76 for the 2.5-hour drive). Where to Stay

Unawatuna’s accommodation is clustered along the beach road and the quieter side streets. For budget travelers, guesthouses like Thambili Cabanas offer basic rooms with fans from ₽1 500–₽3 000 (≈ $16–$33) per night. Mid-range options include The Palms, a boutique property with a pool and garden, at ₽5 000–₽8 000 (≈ $54–$87) per night. If you want luxury, Jetwing Lighthouse (a 10-minute drive) has sea-view suites from ₽12 000–₽18 000 (≈ $130–$196) per night. Top 15 Attractions

  1. Unawatuna Beach – The main beach with calm waters, perfect for swimming and sunbathing. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  2. Jungle Beach – A secluded cove 20 minutes’ walk through the forest, ideal for snorkeling. Free entry. Open daily, 6:00–18:00.
  3. Galle Fort – A 17th-century Dutch fort with ramparts, cafes, and museums. Entry: free (museum ₽500 / $5). Open 24/7; museum 9:00–17:00.
  4. Japanese Peace Pagoda – A white stupa on a hilltop with panoramic ocean views. Free entry. Open 6:00–18:00.
  5. Rumassala Hill – A jungle-covered hill with hiking trails and a mythical connection to the Ramayana. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  6. Snorkeling at Coral Reef – Rent gear (₽1 000 / $11) and spot turtles, clownfish, and rays. Best at high tide.
  7. Sea Turtle Hatchery – A conservation center where you can see baby turtles. Entry: ₽1 500 (≈ $16). Open 8:00–17:00.
  8. Boat Safari in Madu Ganga – A 2-hour ride through mangroves, with stops at cinnamon island and a Buddhist temple. ₽3 000 (≈ $33) per person. Open 7:00–17:00.
  9. Dalawella Beach – A quieter stretch with a famous swing for photos. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  10. Mirissa Whale Watching – A day trip (1 hour drive) to see blue whales and dolphins. ₽5 000–₽7 000 (≈ $54–$76) per person. Season: November–April.
  11. Galle National Museum – Exhibits on Sri Lankan history and colonial artifacts. Entry: ₽500 (≈ $5). Open 9:00–17:00 (closed Monday).
  12. Yatagala Raja Maha Viharaya – A 2,000-year-old Buddhist temple with cave paintings. Entry: free (donations welcome). Open 6:00–18:00.
  13. Koggala Lake – A serene lake with a cinnamon island and a turtle sanctuary. Boat tour: ₽2 500 (≈ $27) per person. Open 7:00–17:00.
  14. Weligama Bay – A surf spot 20 minutes east, great for beginners. Board rental: ₽1 500 (≈ $16) per hour.
  15. Sunset at Unawatuna Pier – A local spot to watch the sun dip into the sea. Free entry. Best at 17:30–18:30.

Getting Around

Unawatuna is walkable for most things, but for longer trips you’ll need transport. Tuk-tuks are the most common option: short rides within town cost ₽200–₽400 (≈ $2–$4), while trips to Galle Fort run ₽500–₽800 (≈ $5–$9). Always negotiate the fare before getting in. Local buses (route 350 or 2) connect Unawatuna to Galle and Matara for ₽50–₽100 (≈ $0.50–$1) per ride—they’re cheap but crowded. Scooter rentals are popular: expect to pay ₽1 500–₽2 500 (≈ $16–$27) per day, plus fuel (₽500 for a full tank). You’ll need an international driver’s permit. For airport transfers, pre-book a Kiwitaxi private car for convenience. There’s no metro or train in Unawatuna, but the coastal railway from Galle to Colombo is scenic and costs ₽300–₽600 (≈ $3–$7) for a second-class seat.

Where to Eat

Unawatuna’s food scene is a mix of Sri Lankan staples and international fare. For authentic local dishes, head to Kingfisher Restaurant on Beach Road—try the deviled prawns or a rice-and-curry plate (₽1 200–₽2 000 / $13–$22). Wijaya Beach Restaurant serves fresh grilled fish (₽1 500–₽2 500 / $16–$27) right on the sand. Street food is a highlight: look for kottu roti (chopped roti with veggies and meat) from a cart for ₽400–₽600 (≈ $4–$7), or fresh king coconut for ₽100 (≈ $1). For a splurge, The Love Restaurant offers a fusion menu with ocean views (mains ₽2 500–₽4 000 / $27–$44). Breakfast spots like Barefoot Café serve pancakes and smoothie bowls for ₽800–₽1 200 (≈ $9–$13). Overall, a meal costs between ₽500 (street food) and ₽4 000 (fine dining) per person.

Best Time to Visit

The best weather is from December to April, during the dry season. Days are sunny and hot (28–32°C), with calm seas perfect for swimming and snorkeling. This is also peak tourist season, so prices for accommodation can double—book early. May to August brings the southwest monsoon: expect rain and rougher seas, but also fewer crowds and lower rates (hotels drop to ₽2 000–₽4 000 / $22–$44 per night). September to November is a shoulder season with occasional showers and good surf. For the best balance of weather and value, aim for January or February—clear skies, warm water, and manageable crowds. Average temperatures stay around 30°C year-round, so pack light cotton clothes and a rain jacket.

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens need an Electronic Travel Authorization (ETA) to enter Sri Lanka. Apply online at the official ETA website (eta.gov.lk) at least 24 hours before departure. The process takes about 10 minutes: fill in your passport details, travel dates, and pay the fee of $35 (≈ ₽3 200) for a standard tourist visa (valid for 30 days, double entry). You’ll receive a PDF via email—print it out and carry it with your passport. On arrival at CMB, present the ETA and your passport (valid for at least 6 months). No visa is issued on arrival for Russians; the ETA is mandatory. 10 Travel Tips

Don't forget travel insurance!

get travel insurance
  1. Cash is king – Most small shops and street stalls only accept cash; use the ATMs in Galle (few in Unawatuna). Carry small bills (₽100–₽1 000 notes).
  2. Bargain respectfully – Negotiate prices for tuk-tuks, souvenirs, and tours, but do it with a smile. A 10–20% discount is reasonable.
  3. Protect against sun and bugs – The sun is intense year-round; use reef-safe sunscreen. Mosquitoes are active at dusk—bring DEET repellent or use a mosquito net.
  4. Respect local customs – Dress modestly (shoulders and knees covered) when visiting temples. Remove shoes and hats before entering.
  5. Drink bottled water – Tap water is not safe; buy large bottles (₽100–₽150 / $1–$2) from shops. Avoid ice in street drinks.
  6. Learn a few Sinhala words – “Ayubowan” (hello) and “istuti” (thank you) go a long way. Most people speak English, but locals appreciate the effort.
  7. Book tours through reputable operators – For whale watching or safaris, use GetYourGuide or your hotel’s recommended guides to avoid scams.
  8. Stay connected – Buy a local SIM card (Dialog or Mobitel) at CMB airport for ₽1 000–₽1 500 (≈ $11–$16) with 10 GB data. Alternatively, use an Airalo eSIM for convenience.
  9. Check the tide times – Snorkeling is best at high tide; low tide exposes the reef and makes swimming difficult. Ask your hotel for a tide chart.
  10. Travel insurance is a must – Medical facilities are basic in Unawatuna; serious cases go to Colombo. Get insurance that covers evacuation and water activities.
