15 Best Things to Do in Bentota 2026

Updated: July 2026 · Expert guide · ⏱ 5 min read

If you’re dreaming of a Sri Lankan beach escape that mixes golden sands, river adventures, and a touch of history, Bentota is your spot. Tucked along the southwest coast, this little paradise is famous for its calm lagoon, turtle hatcheries, and lush gardens. I’ve spent hours wandering its streets and floating in its waters, and I can tell you—every corner feels like a postcard. Whether you’re a solo traveler, a couple, or a family, Bentota’s attractions are easy to love. Let me walk you through the best things to do here in 2026, with all the details you need to plan a perfect trip.

15 Best Attractions

1. Bentota Beach

Bentota Beach is the heart of the town—a long stretch of golden sand kissed by gentle waves. It’s perfect for sunbathing, swimming, or just sipping a king coconut while watching fishermen. The beach is less crowded than Hikkaduwa, so you’ll find your own quiet patch easily. In the evening, the sunset paints the sky in pinks and oranges.

Ticket price: Free

Hours: Open 24/7

How to get there: Walk from most hotels along Galle Road (A2 highway), or take a tuk-tuk from the Bentota Railway Station for about ₽500 (≈ $5.50).

Tip: Visit at sunrise (around 6 AM) for a peaceful start—bring a towel and camera.

2. Brief Garden, Bevis Bawa

This enchanting garden, designed by landscape architect Bevis Bawa (brother of Geoffrey Bawa), is a hidden gem of tropical art and flora. Winding paths lead you past sculptures, ponds, and vibrant blooms, all set on a hillside with lagoon views. It feels like stepping into a living painting—each turn reveals a new surprise. The garden’s quirky charm makes it a favorite for photographers.

Ticket price: ₽1 200 (≈ $13) per person

Hours: 8:30 AM – 5:00 PM daily

How to get there: Take a tuk-tuk from Bentota town (15 minutes, ~₽1 000 / $11) or hire a taxi from your hotel.

Tip: Wear mosquito repellent and comfortable shoes—the paths are uneven but worth every step.

3. Bentota River Safari

A boat ride along the Bentota River is a must—it’s the best way to see mangroves, monitor lizards, and colorful birds. The safari typically lasts 1–2 hours and includes stops at cinnamon islands and local villages. You’ll feel the cool breeze as the boat glides through narrow canals. It’s a serene escape from the beach bustle.

Ticket price: ₽3 000–₽4 500 (≈ $33–$50) per person for a shared boat

Hours: 7:00 AM – 5:00 PM; best at 8 AM or 4 PM

How to get there: Book through your hotel or walk to the river jetty near the Bentota Bridge (5-minute walk from the beach).

Tip: Bring binoculars for birdwatching—early mornings are prime for spotting kingfishers and eagles. Tours can be booked on GetYourGuide.

4. Kande Viharaya Temple

This Buddhist temple in nearby Aluthgama is a spiritual highlight, featuring a giant seated Buddha statue that stands 30 meters tall. The temple complex is peaceful, with colorful murals and a relic chamber. Locals come here to pray, and you’ll feel a sense of calm as you climb the steps. The views from the top are lovely.

Ticket price: Free (donations welcome)

Hours: 6:00 AM – 8:00 PM daily

How to get there: Take a tuk-tuk from Bentota (10 minutes, ~₽600 / $6.50) or walk 20 minutes north along Galle Road.

Tip: Dress modestly (cover shoulders and knees) and remove shoes before entering.

5. Turtle Hatchery (Sea Turtle Conservation Project)

Bentota’s turtle hatchery is a conservation center that protects sea turtle eggs and releases hatchlings into the ocean. You’ll see tanks with baby turtles, injured adults, and even albino turtles. It’s educational and heartwarming—especially for kids. The staff explain how you can help protect these gentle creatures.

Ticket price: ₽1 000 (≈ $11) per person

Hours: 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM daily

How to get there: Walk south along the beach from Bentota town (15 minutes) or take a tuk-tuk for ₽400 (≈ $4.50).

Tip: Visit between January and April for the best chance to see hatchling releases.

6. Galapatha Raja Maha Viharaya

This ancient Buddhist temple, dating back to the 12th century, is a quiet spot with a giant reclining Buddha and old stone carvings. The temple sits on a small hill, surrounded by lush trees. It’s less touristy than other sites, so you can enjoy the serene atmosphere. The murals inside tell stories from the Jataka tales.

Ticket price: Free (donations appreciated)

Hours: 6:00 AM – 6:00 PM daily

How to get there: Take a tuk-tuk from Bentota (15 minutes, ~₽800 / $9) or drive via the A2 highway.

Tip: Bring a small offering (like flowers) if you wish to participate in local customs.

7. Bentota Lagoon

The lagoon is a tranquil waterbody where the Bentota River meets the sea, surrounded by mangroves and palm trees. You can kayak, paddleboard, or simply float in the calm waters. It’s a great spot for birdwatching—look for herons, cormorants, and kingfishers. The sunset reflections here are magical.

Ticket price: Free to access; kayak rental ~₽1 500 (≈ $16) per hour

Hours: Open 24/7; best at sunrise or sunset

How to get there: Walk to the lagoon from the beach (5 minutes) or take a tuk-tuk from town.

Tip: Bring a waterproof camera—the mangroves are photogenic.

8. Lunuganga Estate

Geoffrey Bawa’s country estate is a masterpiece of landscape architecture, with gardens, lakes, and sculptures. You can wander through themed sections like the “Cinnamon Garden” and “Japanese Garden.” The estate feels like a dream—quiet, elegant, and full of inspiration. It’s a short drive from Bentota.

Ticket price: ₽1 500 (≈ $16) per person

Hours: 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM daily (closed on Poya days)

How to get there: Take a taxi from Bentota (20 minutes, ~₽1 500 / $16) or hire a tuk-tuk for ₽1 000 (≈ $11).

Tip: Book a guided tour in advance through GetYourGuide to learn about Bawa’s vision.

9. Beruwala Fish Market

This bustling market, just south of Bentota, is where locals buy fresh seafood daily. You’ll see piles of tuna, snapper, and prawns, plus the lively banter of fishermen. It’s a sensory experience—smells, sounds, and colors collide. Great for foodies who want to see Sri Lanka’s coastal life.

Ticket price: Free to enter

Hours: 5:00 AM – 10:00 AM daily (best before 8 AM)

How to get there: Take a tuk-tuk from Bentota (10 minutes, ~₽500 / $5.50) or walk 25 minutes south along the beach.

Tip: Go early for the best catch—buy some fish to cook at your hotel if you have facilities.

10. Paradise Island Club

This water sports hub on the Bentota River offers jet skiing, banana boats, and windsurfing. It’s a fun spot for adrenaline seekers—the staff are professional and safety-conscious. The setting is beautiful, with river views and palm trees. You can also rent kayaks or stand-up paddleboards.

Ticket price: ₽2 500–₽5 000 (≈ $27–$55) per activity

Hours: 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM daily

How to get there: Walk from the Bentota Bridge (5 minutes) or take a tuk-tuk from town.

Tip: Book a combo package for discounts—check GetYourGuide for deals.

11. Cinnamon Island

A small island in the Bentota River where cinnamon is grown and processed. You’ll see how cinnamon bark is peeled, dried, and rolled—it’s fascinating. The island is lush and peaceful, with a short guided tour. You can buy fresh cinnamon sticks as souvenirs.

Ticket price: Included in most river safaris (~₽500 / $5.50 extra if booked separately)

Hours: 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM daily

How to get there: Take a river safari from the Bentota jetty—most stop here.

Tip: Combine with a river safari for the best value—bring cash for cinnamon purchases.

12. Richland Boutique

A charming store in Bentota town selling handcrafted souvenirs, batik fabrics, and local spices. It’s a great place to pick up gifts like wooden masks or tea sets. The owner is friendly and shares stories about the crafts. Prices are fair, with no hard selling.

Ticket price: Free to browse

Hours: 9:00 AM – 8:00 PM daily

How to get there: Walk from Bentota Railway Station (5 minutes) or take a tuk-tuk.

Tip: Bargain politely—10–15% off is standard for multiple items.

13. Barberyn Island

A tiny island off Bentota’s coast, accessible by boat, with a small Buddhist temple and a lighthouse. It’s a quiet escape with panoramic ocean views. The island is uninhabited except for monks, so it feels like a secret. Perfect for a picnic or meditation.

Ticket price: Free (boat rental ~₽2 000 / $22 round trip)

Hours: Best visited at low tide (check local tide charts)

How to get there: Rent a boat from the Bentota beach (negotiate with fishermen) or join a guided tour.

Tip: Bring water and snacks—there are no shops on the island.

14. Kosgoda Sea Turtle Conservation Project

A larger hatchery than Bentota’s, located 10 km north, with more species and educational programs. You can volunteer to release hatchlings or help clean the tanks. It’s run by a dedicated team focused on conservation. The experience is hands-on and memorable.

Ticket price: ₽1 000 (≈ $11) per person

Hours: 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM daily

How to get there: Take a tuk-tuk from Bentota (20 minutes, ~₽1 200 / $13) or a bus on the A2 highway.

Tip: Call ahead to check if hatchlings are available for release—usually November to April.

15. Bentota Railway Station

This charming colonial-era station is a great photo spot, with its red-tiled roof and vintage charm. Trains pass through regularly, connecting Bentota to Colombo and Galle. You can take a short ride just for the experience—the coastal views are stunning. It’s also a practical hub for travel.

Ticket price: Free to enter; train tickets from ₽50 (≈ $0.55) to nearby towns

Hours: 5:00 AM – 10:00 PM daily

How to get there: Walk from Bentota town center (10 minutes) or take a tuk-tuk.

Tip: Check the schedule at Booking.com for train tickets—book in advance during peak season.

1-Day, 2-Day, 3-Day Itineraries

1-Day Itinerary: Beach & River Bliss

Start at 7 AM with a Bentota River Safari (₽3 500 / $39) to see mangroves and wildlife. By 9:30 AM, head to Bentota Beach for a swim and breakfast at a beachside café. At 11 AM, visit the Turtle Hatchery (₽1 000 / $11) for a quick tour. Lunch at a local restaurant (₽1 500 / $16). At 2 PM, explore Brief Garden (₽1 200 / $13) for an hour. End the day at 5 PM with sunset at the lagoon—free and magical. Total cost: ~₽7 200 (≈ $79).

2-Day Itinerary: Culture & Nature

Day 1: Start with Kande Viharaya Temple (free) at 8 AM. Then, take a river safari (₽3 500 / $39) at 10 AM. Lunch at noon. Visit Lunuganga Estate (₽1 500 / $16) at 2 PM. Evening: dinner and beach walk.

Day 2: At 7 AM, visit Beruwala Fish Market (free). Then, head to Kosgoda Turtle Hatchery (₽1 000 / $11) at 9 AM. Lunch. Afternoon: Galapatha Raja Maha Viharaya (free) at 2 PM. Evening: kayak on the lagoon (₽1 500 / $16). Total cost: ~₽7 500 (≈ $82) plus meals.

3-Day Itinerary: Full Exploration

Day 1: Same as 2-day Day 1, plus a sunset boat to Barberyn Island (₽2 000 / $22).

Day 2: Same as 2-day Day 2, plus a visit to Cinnamon Island (included in river safari).

Day 3: At 8 AM, visit Brief Garden (₽1 200 / $13). Then, water sports at Paradise Island Club (₽3 500 / $39) from 10 AM. Lunch. Afternoon: shopping at Richland Boutique (free). Evening: train ride to Aluthgama (₽50 / $0.55) and back. Total cost: ~₽10 250 (≈ $113) plus meals.

Tours & Excursions

Bentota has several excellent tours to enhance your trip. The Bentota River Safari (₽3 500–₽4 500 / $39–$50 per person) is the most popular—book it on GetYourGuide for a 2-hour guided boat ride with cinnamon island stops. A day trip to Galle Fort (₽5 000 / $55 per person) includes transport and a guide, exploring Dutch colonial architecture. For nature lovers, a Kosgoda Turtle Hatchery tour (₽2 000 / $22 per person) includes release experiences. Tours can be booked on GetYourGuide or through local hotels. Private tours cost more (₽8 000–₽12 000 / $88–$132), but group tours are budget-friendly.
