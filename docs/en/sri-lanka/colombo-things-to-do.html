15 Best Things to Do in Colombo 2026

If you’re planning a trip to Sri Lanka, you might be tempted to skip Colombo and head straight for the beaches or tea plantations. Don’t. Colombo is a vibrant, chaotic, and surprisingly charming city that blends colonial heritage, modern skyscrapers, and authentic local life. I’ve spent many days wandering its streets, and every time I discover something new. Whether you’re here for a layover or a full weekend, these 15 attractions will make your visit unforgettable. 15 Best Attractions

1. Gangaramaya Temple

This is one of Colombo’s most eclectic temples, mixing Sri Lankan, Thai, Indian, and Chinese architectural styles. The main complex includes a museum filled with donated artifacts, from vintage cars to elephant tusks. It’s a peaceful escape from the city’s noise, especially in the early morning. Don’t miss the floating Buddha statue in the nearby Beira Lake.

Ticket price: Free entry, but donations welcome (around ₽500 (≈ $5) suggested).

Hours: 5:30 AM – 10:00 PM daily.

How to get there: Take a tuk-tuk from Fort Railway Station (₽300–₽500 (≈ $3–$5)). Or walk from Colombo Fort area (15 minutes).

Tip: Visit during the morning puja (offering ceremony) around 6:30 AM for a spiritual experience.

2. Galle Face Green

A sprawling oceanfront promenade that comes alive at sunset. Locals fly kites, eat street food, and watch the waves crash against the seawall. It’s the perfect spot for a leisurely stroll or a picnic. Vendors sell everything from roasted corn to spicy isso vade (shrimp fritters).

Ticket price: Free.

Hours: Open 24 hours, but best from 4 PM to 7 PM.

How to get there: Walk from Colombo Fort (5 minutes) or take a tuk-tuk from anywhere in the city center (₽200–₽400 (≈ $2–$4)).

Tip: Try the kottu roti from a nearby stall — it’s a local favorite.

3. National Museum of Colombo

Housed in a stunning Italianate building from 1877, this museum showcases Sri Lanka’s history from ancient kingdoms to colonial times. Highlights include the throne of King Kandy and a collection of traditional masks. It’s a bit dusty but full of treasures. Allow at least 1.5 hours.

Ticket price: ₽1 200 (≈ $13) for adults; ₽600 (≈ $6.50) for children.

Hours: 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM (closed on public holidays).

How to get there: Take bus 100 or 101 from Pettah (₽30 (≈ $0.30)) or a tuk-tuk (₽400–₽600 (≈ $4–$6)).

Tip: Combine with a visit to the nearby Viharamahadevi Park for a relaxing afternoon.

4. Pettah Market

A sensory overload of colors, sounds, and smells. This bustling bazaar is where locals shop for everything from spices to electronics. The narrow streets are packed with stalls, and bargaining is expected. It’s chaotic but authentic — you’ll feel like you’ve stepped into another world.

Ticket price: Free entry; bring cash for shopping.

Hours: 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM (best in the morning).

How to get there: Walk from Fort Railway Station (5 minutes) or take a tuk-tuk (₽200 (≈ $2)).

Tip: Keep your valuables secure and wear comfortable shoes.

5. Colombo Lighthouse

This historic lighthouse at the entrance of Colombo Port is a great photo spot. It’s not open to the public, but the surrounding area offers views of the harbor and passing ships. The nearby clock tower is also worth a look.

Ticket price: Free.

Hours: Best viewed during daylight hours.

How to get there: Walk from Galle Face Green (10 minutes) or take a tuk-tuk (₽300 (≈ $3)).

Tip: Visit at sunset for golden light and fewer crowds.

6. Independence Memorial Hall

A striking monument built to commemorate Sri Lanka’s independence from British rule in 1948. The hall features lion statues and intricate carvings. The surrounding park is popular with joggers and families. It’s a calm spot to reflect on the country’s history.

Ticket price: Free.

Hours: Open 24 hours; hall interior accessible during daylight.

How to get there: Take bus 100 from Pettah (₽30 (≈ $0.30)) or a tuk-tuk (₽500 (≈ $5)).

Tip: Visit early morning to see locals practicing tai chi.

7. Viharamahadevi Park

Colombo’s largest park, named after a ancient queen, features a large Buddha statue, fountains, and a children’s playground. It’s a great place to relax away from the traffic. The park is well-maintained and often hosts events.

Ticket price: Free.

Hours: 6:00 AM – 6:00 PM.

How to get there: Walk from the National Museum (5 minutes) or take a tuk-tuk (₽300 (≈ $3)).

Tip: Bring a picnic — there are plenty of shaded benches.

8. Old Dutch Hospital

This 17th-century Dutch colonial building has been restored into a trendy shopping and dining complex. It houses boutiques, cafes, and restaurants. The architecture is beautiful, with thick walls and arched doorways. It’s a great spot for lunch or a coffee break.

Ticket price: Free entry; meals start at ₽1 500 (≈ $16).

Hours: Shops 10:00 AM – 10:00 PM; restaurants until late.

How to get there: Walk from Galle Face Green (5 minutes) or take a tuk-tuk (₽200 (≈ $2)).

Tip: Try the seafood at Ministry of Crab — it’s famous but pricey (₽8 000+ (≈ $85)).

9. Beira Lake

A serene lake in the heart of the city, surrounded by walking paths and lotus flowers. You can rent a paddleboat or just sit and watch the birds. The lake is also home to the floating Buddha statue from Gangaramaya Temple. It’s a peaceful contrast to the city’s hustle.

Ticket price: Free; paddleboat rental from ₽500 (≈ $5) per hour.

Hours: 6:00 AM – 6:00 PM.

How to get there: Walk from Gangaramaya Temple (2 minutes) or take a tuk-tuk (₽200 (≈ $2)).

Tip: Visit at dusk for a romantic atmosphere.

10. Kelaniya Raja Maha Vihara

Located just outside Colombo, this ancient Buddhist temple is believed to have been visited by the Buddha himself. The murals and stupa are stunning. It’s a major pilgrimage site, so expect crowds on full moon days. The journey is worth it for the spiritual vibe.

Ticket price: Free; donations appreciated.

Hours: 6:00 AM – 8:00 PM daily.

How to get there: Take bus 235 from Pettah (₽50 (≈ $0.50)) or a tuk-tuk (₽1 000 (≈ $10)).

Tip: Wear long pants and cover your shoulders.

11. Jami Ul-Alfar Mosque

Known as the “Red Mosque” for its striking red-and-white striped facade, this is one of Colombo’s most photogenic buildings. It dates back to 1909 and is an active place of worship. Non-Muslims can enter the courtyard but not the prayer hall. The architecture is a mix of Indo-Saracenic and Gothic styles.

Ticket price: Free.

Hours: 9:00 AM – 12:00 PM and 2:00 PM – 5:00 PM (closed during prayer times).

How to get there: Walk from Pettah Market (5 minutes) or take a tuk-tuk (₽200 (≈ $2)).

Tip: Dress modestly and remove shoes before entering.

12. Sri Lanka Planetarium

A modern, dome-shaped building that offers educational shows about astronomy. It’s a hit with kids and science enthusiasts. The shows are in English and Sinhala. The building itself is an architectural landmark.

Ticket price: ₽500 (≈ $5) for adults; ₽300 (≈ $3) for children.

Hours: 9:00 AM – 4:00 PM (closed Mondays).

How to get there: Take bus 100 from Pettah (₽30 (≈ $0.30)) or a tuk-tuk (₽500 (≈ $5)).

Tip: Check the schedule online for show times.

13. Dutch Burgher Union

A charming colonial-era building that houses a museum dedicated to the Dutch Burgher community. It’s a hidden gem with artifacts, photographs, and a library. The staff are friendly and knowledgeable. It’s a quiet spot away from the tourist trail.

Ticket price: Free; donations welcome.

Hours: 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM (closed weekends).

How to get there: Walk from the National Museum (10 minutes) or take a tuk-tuk (₽300 (≈ $3)).

Tip: Ask about the genealogy records if you have Dutch ancestry.

14. Seema Malaka Temple

A meditation temple built on stilts over Beira Lake, designed by the famous architect Geoffrey Bawa. It’s a serene spot with multiple Buddha statues and a peaceful ambiance. It’s part of the Gangaramaya complex but feels separate. Perfect for a moment of calm.

Ticket price: Free.

Hours: 6:00 AM – 6:00 PM.

How to get there: Walk from Gangaramaya Temple (1 minute) or take a tuk-tuk (₽200 (≈ $2)).

Tip: Bring a book and sit by the water.

15. Colombo Port City

A massive reclaimed land project that’s transforming the city’s skyline. Currently, it’s a construction zone, but the promenade offers views of the ocean and future developments. It’s a symbol of Sri Lanka’s modernization. The best views are from the nearby Marine Drive.

Ticket price: Free.

Hours: 24 hours.

How to get there: Walk from Galle Face Green (15 minutes) or take a tuk-tuk (₽300 (≈ $3)).

Tip: Visit in the evening for a sunset view over the ocean.

1-Day, 2-Day, 3-Day Itineraries

1-Day Itinerary: Colombo Express

Start at Gangaramaya Temple and Seema Malaka (1 hour). Walk to Galle Face Green for a quick breakfast at a street stall (30 minutes). Head to National Museum (1.5 hours). Lunch at Old Dutch Hospital (1 hour). Afternoon: explore Pettah Market and see the Jami Ul-Alfar Mosque (1.5 hours). End at Viharamahadevi Park or Independence Memorial Hall (1 hour). Dinner at a local restaurant in Fort. Total walking: 4–5 km. Use Aviasales to book flights and Hotellook for last-minute hotel deals.

2-Day Itinerary: Deeper Dive

Day 1: Same as above, plus Beira Lake paddleboat ride (30 minutes) and Colombo Lighthouse (15 minutes). Dinner at Ministry of Crab or a seafood spot.

Day 2: Morning: Kelaniya Raja Maha Vihara (2 hours including travel). Lunch near the temple. Afternoon: Planetarium or Dutch Burgher Union (1.5 hours). Evening: Port City promenade for sunset (1 hour). Use Booking or Agoda for accommodation.

3-Day Itinerary: Full Immersion

Day 1: As above.

Day 2: As above.

Day 3: Morning: Mount Lavinia Beach (20-minute tuk-tuk from Colombo, ₽1 000 (≈ $10)) for a relaxing swim (2 hours). Lunch at a beachfront cafe. Afternoon: Colombo National Library or a cooking class (3 hours). Evening: Galle Face Green for street food and kite flying. Use Kiwitaxi for airport transfers.

Tours & Excursions

If you prefer guided experiences, several tours cover Colombo’s highlights. A typical half-day city tour (4 hours) costs around ₽3 500 (≈ $38) per person and includes Gangaramaya Temple, Galle Face Green, and Pettah Market. A full-day tour (8 hours) with lunch is about ₽7 000 (≈ $75). For a unique experience, try a street food tour (₽2 500 (≈ $27)) or a sunset sailing trip off the coast (₽5 000 (≈ $54)). Tours can be booked on GetYourGuide.

Free Attractions

Colombo is full of free experiences. Enjoy Galle Face Green at sunset, walk through Pettah Market, visit Independence Memorial Hall, explore Viharamahadevi Park, and see the Jami Ul-Alfar Mosque courtyard. Beira Lake and Colombo Lighthouse are also free. For a cultural dose, the Dutch Burgher Union museum is donation-based.

Practical Tips

Transportation

Colombo is best explored by tuk-tuk (₽200–₽500 (≈ $2–$5) per short ride) or bus (₽30–₽50 (≈ $0.30–$0.50)). Use ride-hailing apps like PickMe or Uber for transparent pricing. For airport transfers, consider Kiwitaxi for pre-booked comfort. Walking is great for central areas like Fort and Pettah.

Combo Tickets

Some attractions offer combo deals. For example, the National Museum and Planetarium have a combined ticket for ₽1 500 (≈ $16) (saving about ₽200). Check at each venue. For multiple temples, consider a Colombo Temple Pass if available — though not officially, some tour operators bundle visits.

Visa for Maldives (if connecting)

If you’re flying through Colombo to the Maldives, note that the Maldives visa on arrival is free for 30 days. Перед вылетом заполните декларацию IMUGA на imuga.immigration.gov.mv и получите QR-код — без него не посадят на рейс. This applies to all nationalities.

FAQ

How much do attractions cost in Colombo?

Entrance fees vary: from free attractions to $20-30 for museums. Many venues have discount days.

Do I need a guide in Colombo?

A guide is recommended for historical sites and complex routes. Self-guided travel is possible with audio guides and maps.
