Welcome to Tivat, the glamorous gateway to Montenegro’s stunning Bay of Kotor. This charming coastal town effortlessly blends old-world Mediterranean charm with modern luxury, offering a relaxed yet sophisticated escape. Unlike its bustling neighbor Kotor, Tivat feels more spacious and airy, with a beautiful waterfront promenade, lush parks, and the world-class Porto Montenegro marina that draws superyachts from around the globe. The air is filled with the scent of salt and pine, and the pace of life is wonderfully slow—perfect for unwinding.

What makes this a great destination in 2026? For one, it’s the perfect launchpad for exploring the entire Bay of Kotor, yet it retains a peaceful, uncrowded soul. You can spend your mornings swimming in crystal-clear coves, your afternoons sipping espresso at a chic marina cafe. and your evenings strolling along the pine-shaded Lungomare promenade.

Tivat also offers excellent value compared to other Mediterranean hotspots, with a wide range of accommodation from budget-friendly apartments to five-star resorts. Whether you’re a yachting enthusiast, a history buff, or simply seeking sun and sea, Tivat delivers a unique blend of natural beauty and contemporary comfort.

In 2026, Tivat is more accessible than ever. The newly renovated Tivat Airport (TIV) connects directly to dozens of European cities, making your journey smooth and hassle-free.

The town itself is incredibly walkable, and its central location means you can easily take day trips to the UNESCO-listed old towns of Kotor and Budva, or venture into the dramatic mountains of Lovćen National Park. Prepare for a vacation that feels both luxurious and authentic—a place where you can relax by the sea in the morning and explore ancient fortresses by afternoon.

How to Get There

The most convenient way to reach Tivat is by flying directly into Tivat Airport (TIV), located just 4 km from the town center. In 2026, the airport handles flights from major European hubs including Moscow (Sheremetyevo and Vnukovo), Istanbul, London, Vienna, and Belgrade. From Moscow, a direct flight takes about 3 hours.

Average round-trip ticket prices from Moscow start around ₽25 000 (≈ $270) in the low season and can go up to ₽55 000 (≈ $600) during peak summer months. For the best deals, I always check flights on Aviasales—they compare hundreds of airlines to find the cheapest options for your dates.

Where to Stay

Tivat offers accommodation for every taste and budget. For luxury, the Porto Montenegro area is unbeatable—think sleek hotels like the Regent Porto Montenegro, where rooms start at ₽18 000 (≈ $195) per night. For mid-range comfort, consider the neighborhood of Donja Lastva, a quiet fishing village with charming guesthouses and apartments—expect to pay around ₽6 000 (≈ $65) per night.

Budget travelers will find great value in the center near the main bus station, with private rooms from ₽3 500 (≈ $38). I recommend using Hotellook to compare prices across Booking, Agoda, and other sites—they often find hidden discounts you won’t see elsewhere.

Top 15 Attractions

  • 1. Porto Montenegro Marina — The heart of Tivat’s luxury scene. Stroll past superyachts, browse designer boutiques, and dine at world-class restaurants. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  • 2. Naval Heritage Collection — A fascinating museum inside a restored Austro-Hungarian submarine base. See vintage boats, naval artifacts, and a real submarine. Ticket: ₽500 (≈ $5.50). Open 10:00–18:00 daily.
  • 3. Plavi Horizonti Beach — The most famous beach in the area, with turquoise waters and a long sandy shore. Free entry (sunbeds from ₽1 000 (≈ $11)). Open sunrise to sunset.
  • 4. Tivat City Park (Park Tivatskih Žena) — A lush green oasis in the center, perfect for a shady stroll. Features centuries-old trees and a small pond. Free. Open 24/7.
  • 5. St. Michael’s Church — A charming 14th-century church in the old town. Small but atmospheric. Free entry. Open 9:00–17:00.
  • 6. Kalimanj Promenade — A beautiful seaside walk connecting Tivat center to Porto Montenegro. Lined with cafes and pine trees. Free. Open 24/7.
  • 7. Boka Tower — A modern observation tower in Porto Montenegro offering panoramic views of the bay. Ticket: ₽300 (≈ $3.30). Open 10:00–22:00.
  • 8. Gornja Lastva Village — An authentic old hilltop village with stone houses and incredible views. Free. Accessible by car or a 30-minute hike.
  • 9. Island of St. Mark — A tiny island visible from the coast, often called “Hawaii” by locals. Best seen by boat tour. Free to view; boat tours from ₽1 500 (≈ $16).
  • 10. Tivat Botanical Garden — A small but lovely garden near the airport, home to exotic plants. Free. Open 8:00–20:00.
  • 11. Porto Montenegro Yacht Club — Even if you’re not a member, the public areas offer great people-watching and a vibrant atmosphere. Free.
  • 12. Kotor Bay Viewpoint — A short drive or hike up to Vrmac hill for a classic postcard view of the bay. Free.
  • 13. Tivat Old Town (Stari Grad) — A tiny historic core with narrow streets, a few cafes, and a relaxed vibe. Free. Open 24/7.
  • 14. Boka Aquarium — A small aquarium showcasing Adriatic marine life. Great for families. Ticket: ₽800 (≈ $8.70). Open 10:00–18:00.
  • 15. Lustica Bay — A new luxury development on the Lustica Peninsula, with pristine beaches and a golf course. Free to visit. Open daily.

Getting Around

Tivat is very walkable, especially the center and marina areas. For longer distances, local buses connect Tivat to Kotor (₽200 / ~$2.20), Budva (₽350 / ~$3.80), and other towns. Taxis are widely available—a short ride within town costs about ₽500 (≈ $5.50), while a trip to Kotor is around ₽1 200 (≈ $13). Always agree on the fare beforehand or use a metered taxi. Rental cars are a great option for exploring the region; rates start at ₽3 000 (≈ $33) per day. I usually compare prices on Booking or Agoda for the best deals.

Where to Eat

Montenegrin cuisine is a delightful mix of Mediterranean and Balkan flavors. For a splurge, dine at Restaurant Galion in Porto Montenegro—seafood platters and fresh oysters, with mains from ₽2 500 (≈ $27). For authentic local food, try Konoba Pjat in the old town, where you can enjoy grilled fish and homemade bread for around ₽1 500 (≈ $16) per person.

Street food is limited but delicious—look for burek (savory pastry) from local bakeries for just ₽200 (≈ $2.20). Don’t miss trying Njeguški pršut (smoked ham) and local cheese. A budget meal costs ₽800 (≈ $8.70), while a three-course dinner for two at a mid-range restaurant is about ₽4 000 (≈ $43).

Tours & Excursions

The best way to experience the Bay of Kotor is by boat. A half-day speedboat tour to the Blue Cave and Our Lady of the Rocks costs around ₽5 000 (≈ $54) per person. A full-day tour to Lovćen National Park and the Njegoš Mausoleum is about ₽4 500 (≈ $49). For a unique experience, take a wine-tasting tour in the Crmnica Valley—prices start at ₽3 500 (≈ $38). I always browse GetYourGuide for the best selection of tours with free cancellation—you can find everything from kayaking adventures to cooking classes there.

Airport Transfer

Tivat Airport is small and conveniently close. A taxi from the airport to the town center costs a fixed ₽800 (≈ $8.70) and takes 5–10 minutes. For a hassle-free arrival, especially with luggage, I recommend booking a private transfer through Kiwitaxi—prices start at ₽1 500 (≈ $16) for a car that meets you at arrivals. Public bus line 1 runs between the airport and the bus station every 30 minutes, costing just ₽150 (≈ $1.60).

Connectivity

Wi-Fi is widely available in hotels, cafes, and even public areas in Porto Montenegro. For reliable mobile data, you can buy a local SIM card from Telenor or m:tel at the airport or in town—a 10GB plan costs about ₽1 000 (≈ $11). Even easier, I always get an eSIM before I travel. Airalo offers affordable Montenegro eSIM plans starting from ₽500 (≈ $5.50) for 1GB, which activates instantly upon arrival—no need to hunt for a physical SIM.

Best Time to Visit

The best time to visit Tivat is from May to September. May and June offer pleasant temperatures (22–28°C), fewer crowds, and lower prices. July and August are peak season—hot (30–35°C), busy, and prices double. September is a sweet spot with warm sea temperatures and fewer tourists. In 2026, expect hotel prices to be 30–50% higher in August. Winter (November–March) is quiet and cool (10–15°C), with many restaurants closed, but it’s perfect for a peaceful retreat.

Visa & Documents

Citizens of Russia, Ukraine, Belarus, and most European countries can enter Montenegro visa-free for up to 30 days. Your passport must be valid for at least 3 months beyond your departure date. For stays longer than 30 days, you need to register with local police. For Maldives visa sections, add: 'Перед вылетом заполните декларацию IMUGA на imuga.immigration.gov.mv и получите QR-код — без него не посадят на рейс.'

Currency & Money

The local currency is the Euro (EUR), symbol €. Cash is still king in smaller shops and markets, but cards (Visa, Mastercard) are widely accepted in hotels, restaurants, and supermarkets. Exchange money at banks or official exchange offices (menjačnice)—avoid street changers. ATMs are plentiful in town. I recommend bringing about $114 (≈ ₽9 500) in cash for a week, plus cards for larger payments.

Time Zone

Tivat is in the Central European Time Zone (UTC+1). During summer (last Sunday in March to last Sunday in October), it switches to Central European Summer Time (UTC+2). This means: Moscow is +2 hours in winter, +1 hour in summer; London is +1 hour in winter, same in summer; New York is +6 hours in winter, +7 in summer.

Travel Insurance

Travel insurance is not mandatory for Montenegro, but it is strongly recommended. A good policy covers medical emergencies, trip cancellation, lost luggage, and COVID-19-related issues. Expect to pay around ₽2 500 (≈ $27) for a week’s coverage. I always get my insurance through Travel Insurance—they offer comprehensive plans with 24/7 assistance and easy online claims.

10 Travel Tips

  1. Learn a few words of Montenegrin: “Dobar dan” (Good day), “Hvala” (Thank you), and “Molim” (Please) go a long way.
  2. Carry cash for small purchases: Many bakeries and market stalls don’t accept cards.
  3. Dress appropriately for churches: Cover shoulders and knees when visiting religious sites.
  4. Beware of sea urchins: Wear water shoes when swimming near rocky beaches.
  5. Book accommodation early for July/August: The best places sell out months in advance.
  6. Use sunscreen religiously: The Adriatic sun is strong, even on cloudy days.
  7. Try local wine: Vranac (red) and Krstač (white) are excellent and cheap.
  8. Negotiate taxi fares before getting in: Avoid surprises by agreeing on the price upfront.
  9. Take a day trip to Kotor early morning: Beat the cruise ship crowds by arriving before 9 AM.
  10. Check your passport validity: Must be valid for at least 3 months beyond your stay.
