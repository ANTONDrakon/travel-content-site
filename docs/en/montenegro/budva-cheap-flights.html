How to Find Cheap Flights to Budva 2026

Planning a trip to the beautiful Montenegrin coast in 2026? Budva, with its stunning beaches, historic Old Town, and vibrant nightlife, is a dream destination. But getting there without breaking the bank? That’s the real challenge. I’ve been there—scrolling through endless flight options, feeling overwhelmed by price fluctuations.

Don’t worry, I’ve got your back. In this guide, I’ll share everything you need to know about finding cheap flights to Budva in 2026, from the best airports and airlines to insider hacks that’ll save you hundreds of euros. Let’s dive in!

Which Airports Serve Budva?

Budva doesn’t have its own airport, but two nearby hubs make it super accessible. The closer one is Tivat Airport (TIV), just 20 km (about 20 minutes by car) from Budva. The other is Podgorica Airport (TGD), 65 km away (roughly an hour’s drive). Both are well-connected, but Tivat is more convenient for beach lovers. Flights to Tivat tend to be pricier, while Podgorica often offers cheaper options. I usually check both on Aviasales—it’s a lifesaver for comparing routes.

Key airlines serving these airports include:

  • Air Serbia – via Belgrade
  • Turkish Airlines – via Istanbul
  • Ryanair – budget flights from European cities
  • Wizz Air – another budget favorite
  • Montenegro Airlines – regional connections

Direct Flights to Budva (Tivat Airport)

Direct flights to Tivat are a godsend if you want to skip layovers. In 2026, airlines like Ryanair and Wizz Air operate seasonal routes from cities like London, Moscow, Istanbul, and Belgrade. Prices vary wildly depending on demand, but here’s a rough idea:

  • London (STN) to TIV: Ryanair offers one-way tickets from about ₽8 500 (≈ $90) in low season, rising to ₽18 500 (≈ $200) in summer.
  • Moscow (SVO) to TIV: Direct flights via Aeroflot or S7 start at ₽15 000 (≈ $160) one-way, but can hit ₽35 000 (≈ $380) in July.
  • Istanbul (IST) to TIV: Turkish Airlines has direct flights from about ₽12 000 (≈ $130) one-way.

Pro tip: Check Aviasales for the best deals—they aggregate all airlines and show you the cheapest dates. I’ve snagged a London-Tivat round trip for ₽14 000 (≈ $150) by booking in March for a June trip.

Connecting Flights: The Cheapest Route to Budva

If you’re flexible with time, connecting flights are your wallet’s best friend. The cheapest options often go through major hubs like Istanbul, Belgrade, or Vienna. For example:

  • London to TGD via Belgrade: Air Serbia offers connecting flights from ₽10 000 (≈ $110) one-way, with a 2-3 hour layover. Total travel time: 5-7 hours.
  • New York to TIV via Istanbul: Turkish Airlines has deals from ₽45 000 (≈ $490) round-trip, with a stop in Istanbul. It’s longer (12+ hours), but way cheaper than direct.
  • Dubai to TGD via Vienna: Austrian Airlines connects via Vienna for about ₽20 000 (≈ $220) one-way.

Flights can be compared across dates on Aviasales—their calendar view shows you the cheapest days to fly. I always use this feature to avoid peak prices. For instance, flying on a Tuesday instead of a Friday saved me ₽5 000 (≈ $55) on a recent trip.

Duration-wise, connecting flights take 4-10 hours depending on the layover. If you’re on a budget, consider a longer layover in a city like Istanbul—you can explore for a few hours and still save money.

10 Hacks for Cheap Flights to Budva

Ready to save big? Here are my top 10 hacks, tested and proven:

  1. Use Aviasales’ Price Calendar: It shows the cheapest days to fly. I once saved ₽8 000 (≈ $87) by shifting my trip by two days.
  2. Book Connecting Flights via Istanbul: Turkish Airlines often has budget-friendly layovers. Use Aviasales to compare.
  3. Fly to Podgorica Instead of Tivat: Podgorica is 65 km away, but flights are ₽5 000-10 000 (≈ $55-110) cheaper. Take a bus or rent a car.
  4. Set Price Alerts: On Aviasales, you can set alerts for specific routes. I got a notification for a price drop and booked instantly.
  5. Be Flexible with Dates: Mid-week flights (Tuesday-Thursday) are cheaper. Avoid Fridays and Sundays.
  6. Check Budget Airlines: Ryanair and Wizz Air fly to Tivat from many European cities. Baggage fees add up, so pack light.
  7. Use Incognito Mode: Airlines track your searches. Clear cookies or use incognito to avoid price hikes.
  8. Book in Advance for Summer: For July-August, book 3-4 months ahead. Last-minute deals are rare in peak season.
  9. Consider Nearby Airports: Dubrovnik (Croatia) is 90 km away, with more flight options. Then take a bus to Budva (2 hours).
  10. Combine with Hotel Deals: Use Hotellook to find accommodation near the airport. Some hotels offer shuttle discounts.

When to Book Your Budva Flight

Timing is everything. Based on 2025 data, the sweet spot for booking to Budva is:

  • For summer travel (June-August): Book 3-4 months in advance (March-April). Prices rise sharply after May.
  • For shoulder season (April-May, September-October): Book 2-3 months ahead. You’ll find good deals.
  • For off-season (November-March): Book 1-2 months ahead. Prices are low, but availability is high.

I recommend checking Aviasales for historical price trends—they show you when prices are lowest. In my experience, booking on a Tuesday at 3 AM (UTC) often yields the best deals.

Flight Insurance: What to Do If Your Flight is Delayed or Cancelled

Let’s be real—flight disruptions happen. I once had a 6-hour delay in Istanbul, and it was a nightmare. Here’s what to do:

  • Check your rights: Under EU Regulation 261/2004, if your flight is delayed over 3 hours or cancelled, you may be entitled to compensation of $286 (€250)-$686 (€600) (approx. ₽25 000-60 000). This applies to flights departing from the EU or arriving on EU airlines.
  • Document everything: Save boarding passes, emails, and screenshots. Ask the airline for a written reason.
  • Claim compensation: You can file a claim directly with the airline or use a service like AirHelp. I’ve used them before—they take a cut, but it’s hassle-free.
  • Travel insurance: Consider a policy that covers flight delays. Services like Booking.com offer add-on insurance when you book hotels.

If your flight is cancelled, the airline must offer a refund or rebooking. For compensation, check with AirHelp or similar services—they handle the paperwork for a fee.
