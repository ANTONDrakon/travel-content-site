Welcome to Budva, the pearl of the Montenegrin Adriatic and a destination that effortlessly blends ancient charm with vibrant modern energy. This is a place where cobblestone streets whisper stories of Venetian rule, while the scent of pine and sea salt fills the air.

Budva isn't just a resort; it's a living postcard of terracotta rooftops, medieval fortresses, and some of the most stunning beaches on the Adriatic coast. The atmosphere here is intoxicating — a perfect mix of laid-back Mediterranean living and lively nightlife that pulses until dawn.

What makes this a great destination in 2026? Because it offers incredible value for money compared to other European hotspots, yet delivers world-class experiences. You can swim in crystal-clear waters in the morning, explore a 2,500-year-old Old Town at sunset. and dance under the stars at a beach club by night.

The city is a gateway to the entire Montenegrin coast, from the glamorous Porto Montenegro in Tivat to the breathtaking Bay of Kotor. Whether you're a history buff, a beach lover, or a foodie, Budva welcomes you with open arms and a glass of local Vranac wine.

The city's magic lies in its duality. By day, families with children splash in the shallow coves, and couples stroll along the marina. By night, the energy shifts — bars spill onto the streets, live music fills the squares, and the ancient walls glow under warm lights. Budva is safe, accessible, and surprisingly affordable for a Mediterranean gem. It's the kind of place where you come for a week and end up staying for a month. How to Get There

Budva is served by two main airports: Tivat Airport (TIV), just 20 km away, and Podgorica Airport (TGD), about 65 km away. From major European cities like Moscow, Istanbul, London, and Berlin, you can find direct flights during the summer season (May–October).

Average round-trip prices from Moscow range from ₽25 000 (≈ $270) to ₽45 000 (≈ $490), while from Western Europe, expect ₽15 000 (≈ $160) to ₽30 000 (≈ $330). Where to Stay

Budva's accommodation ranges from budget-friendly hostels to five-star resorts. The Old Town is the heart of the action — expect narrow streets, historic buildings, and higher prices. A double room in a mid-range hotel here costs ₽8 000 (≈ $87) to ₽15 000 (≈ $163) per night. For a quieter vibe, try Slovenska Plaza, a modern area with beachfront apartments starting at ₽5 000 (≈ $54) per night.

Luxury seekers should head to Becici or Rafailovici, where five-star hotels like the Splendid Conference Center & Spa charge ₽25 000 (≈ $272) to ₽50 000 (≈ $544) per night. Budget travelers can find hostels from ₽1 500 (≈ $16) per bed. For the best rates, check hotel prices on

Top 15 Attractions

  • 1. Budva Old Town (Stari Grad) — A maze of narrow streets, medieval churches, and charming squares. Entry is free, but some churches charge ₽200 (≈ $2). Open 24/7.
  • 2. Citadela Fortress — A 15th-century fortress with panoramic views of the coast. Tickets: ₽500 (≈ $5). Open daily 9:00–22:00 (summer), 9:00–17:00 (winter).
  • 3. Mogren Beach — Two sandy coves connected by a tunnel. Free entry. Open 24/7. Sunbeds: ₽1 000 (≈ $11) per day.
  • 4. Sveti Stefan — A iconic island-resort, now a luxury Aman hotel. You can view it from the mainland for free. No public access without a reservation.
  • 5. Becici Beach — A long, sandy beach with Blue Flag status. Free entry. Sunbeds: ₽800 (≈ $9) per day.
  • 6. Dukley Marina — A modern marina with yachts, restaurants, and bars. Free to walk around. Open 24/7.
  • 7. Church of St. John (Sv. Ivan) — A 7th-century church in the Old Town. Free entry, but donations welcome. Open daily 9:00–18:00.
  • 8. Podmaine Monastery — A serene Orthodox monastery just outside town. Free entry. Open daily 8:00–20:00.
  • 9. Budva Riviera Beaches — 15+ beaches along the coast, including Jaz, Ploce, and Kamenovo. Most are free. Sunbeds: ₽500–1 500 (≈ $5–16) per day.
  • 10. Nikola's Cave (Spila) — A small cave near the Old Town with historical artifacts. Free entry. Open daily 10:00–18:00.
  • 11. Aquapark Budva — A water park with slides and pools. Tickets: ₽2 500 (≈ $27) for adults, ₽1 500 (≈ $16) for children. Open 10:00–19:00 (June–September).
  • 12. Mogren Fortress — A smaller fortress near Mogren Beach. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  • 13. Old Town Walls — Walk along the medieval walls for stunning views. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  • 14. Museum of Budva — A small museum in the Old Town with archaeological finds. Tickets: ₽300 (≈ $3). Open 9:00–17:00 (closed Mondays).
  • 15. Dance Square (Trg Pjesme) — A lively square with a fountain and cafes. Free. Open 24/7.

Getting Around

Budva is compact and walkable, especially the Old Town. For longer distances, buses are cheap and frequent. A single ride within Budva costs ₽100 (≈ $1). Taxis are widely available: a typical ride within town costs ₽300–500 (≈ $3–5), but always agree on the price beforehand. For exploring the coast, renting a car is a good option — prices start at ₽2 500 (≈ $27) per day. Scooters and bikes can be rented for ₽1 500 (≈ $16) per day. There is no metro in Budva.

Where to Eat

Budva's cuisine is a delicious mix of Mediterranean and Balkan flavors. Must-try dishes include crni rižot (black risotto with squid ink), njeguški pršut (smoked ham), and fresh grilled fish. For a budget meal, head to a bakery for burek (savory pastry) for ₽150 (≈ $2).

A sit-down lunch at a local konoba (tavern) costs ₽1 000–2 000 (≈ $11–22) per person. For fine dining, restaurants on the marina charge ₽3 000–5 000 (≈ $33–54) per person. Street food is popular — try ćevapi (grilled minced meat) from a stand for ₽300 (≈ $3). Local wine is excellent: a glass of Vranac costs ₽200–400 (≈ $2–4).

Tours & Excursions

Budva is a perfect base for day trips. The most popular excursion is a boat tour of the Bay of Kotor, visiting Perast and Our Lady of the Rocks — prices start at ₽3 000 (≈ $33) per person. Another must-do is a wine tasting tour in the Crmnica Valley, costing ₽4 000 (≈ $44) per person.

For nature lovers, a hike to Lovćen National Park (including Njegoš Mausoleum) costs ₽2 500 (≈ $27) for a guided tour. Adventure seekers can try rafting on the Tara River — the deepest canyon in Europe — for ₽5 000 (≈ $54) per person. For a full list of options, check tours and excursions on

Best Time to Visit

The best time to visit Budva is May–June and September–October. During these months, temperatures range from 25–30°C, the sea is warm, and crowds are thinner. July–August is peak season — expect temperatures around 35°C, crowded beaches, and higher prices. Accommodation costs double in August. Winter (November–March) is quiet and cool (10–15°C), with many beach bars closed, but it's perfect for sightseeing and hiking. Prices for flights and hotels drop by 50% or more.

Visa & Documents

For Russian citizens, Montenegro offers visa-free entry for up to 30 days with a valid foreign passport. You must have a passport valid for at least 3 months beyond your departure date. No visa is required for stays under 30 days. For longer stays, contact the Montenegrin embassy. Always carry a printed copy of your hotel reservation and return ticket. Currency & Money

Montenegro uses the Euro (EUR), symbol €. Credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) are widely accepted in hotels, restaurants, and larger shops. However, smaller konobas and market stalls prefer cash. Exchange money at banks or exchange offices — avoid street changers. ATMs are plentiful. How much cash to bring? For a week, $229 (€200)–300 (≈ ₽20 000–30 000) in cash should cover meals, tips, and small purchases. Larger expenses like hotels and tours can be paid by card.

Time Zone

Budva is in the Central European Time (CET) zone, UTC+1. In summer (last Sunday in March to last Sunday in October), it switches to CEST (UTC+2). Time difference: Moscow is +1 hour (summer) or +2 hours (winter); London is -1 hour; New York is -6 hours; Bangkok is +5 hours.

Travel Insurance

Travel insurance is not mandatory for entry to Montenegro, but it is highly recommended. A good policy covers medical emergencies, trip cancellation, lost luggage, and adventure activities like rafting or hiking. Prices start at ₽1 500 (≈ $16) for a week of basic coverage.
