How to Find Cheap Flights to Tivat 2026

Updated: July 2026 · Expert guide · ⏱ 5 min read

If you’re dreaming of Montenegro’s stunning Adriatic coast, turquoise bays, and the glamorous Porto Montenegro marina, Tivat is your gateway. But let’s be real — flights to Tivat Airport (TIV) can sometimes feel like a splurge. I’ve been there, refreshing flight search pages at 2 a.m., hoping prices drop. The good news? With a little strategy, you can snag a bargain for 2026. Here’s my personal guide to finding cheap flights to Tivat, based on years of travel-hacking and local tips.

First, a quick note on airlines serving Tivat. The airport is small but well-connected. Key carriers include Air Serbia (via Belgrade), Turkish Airlines (via Istanbul), and low-cost favorites like Ryanair and Wizz Air (seasonal from European hubs). Also, look out for Montenegro Airlines (though limited) and charter flights from Russia, the UK, and Germany. Prices vary wildly, so let’s break it down.

Direct Flights

Direct flights to Tivat are a luxury — they save time but often cost more. In 2026, expect these routes:

  • From Moscow (SVO/DME): S7 Airlines and Aeroflot occasionally operate direct charters, especially in summer. Prices: ₽25 000 – ₽45 000 (≈ $270 – $490) round-trip.
  • From Istanbul (IST): Turkish Airlines flies daily. Prices: ₽18 000 – ₽30 000 (≈ $195 – $325) round-trip.
  • From Belgrade (BEG): Air Serbia offers multiple weekly flights. Prices: ₽12 000 – ₽20 000 (≈ $130 – $215) round-trip.
  • From London (LGW/STN): Ryanair and Wizz Air run seasonal direct flights (May-September). Prices: ₽8 000 – ₽15 000 (≈ $85 – $160) one-way.
  • From Warsaw (WAW): LOT Polish Airlines and Wizz Air. Prices: ₽10 000 – ₽18 000 (≈ $110 – $195) round-trip.

Direct flights are rare from other regions, so connecting options are your best bet for savings.

Connecting Flights

For most travelers, connecting flights are the cheapest way to reach Tivat. The key hubs are Belgrade, Istanbul, Vienna, and Munich. Here’s the scoop:

  • Via Belgrade (BEG): Air Serbia often has great deals. From New York (JFK) to Tivat via Belgrade: ₽35 000 – ₽55 000 (≈ $380 – $600) round-trip. Total travel time: 12-16 hours.
  • Via Istanbul (IST): Turkish Airlines and Pegasus. From Bangkok (BKK) to Tivat: ₽28 000 – ₽45 000 (≈ $305 – $490) round-trip. Duration: 15-20 hours.
  • Via Vienna (VIE): Austrian Airlines. From Dubai (DXB) to Tivat: ₽25 000 – ₽40 000 (≈ $270 – $435) round-trip. Duration: 10-14 hours.
  • Via Munich (MUC): Lufthansa. From Toronto (YYZ) to Tivat: ₽40 000 – ₽60 000 (≈ $435 – $650) round-trip. Duration: 14-18 hours.

Pro tip: Flights can be flexible — meaning you can adjust dates to save big. For example, flying mid-week (Tuesday or Wednesday) is often 20-30% cheaper than weekends. Compare prices across dates using tools like Aviasales or Booking to see the full range. I once saved ₽10 000 (≈ $110) by shifting my departure by two days!

10 Hacks for Cheap Flights

Ready to save? Here are my tried-and-tested tips for 2026:

  1. Use price comparison sites. Start with Aviasales — it aggregates hundreds of airlines and shows the cheapest dates in a calendar view. I always set a price alert there.
  2. Book via aggregators but check airline sites. Sometimes Booking or Agoda have exclusive deals, but compare directly with the airline’s website for baggage fees.
  3. Fly into nearby airports. Consider Podgorica (TGD) — it’s 90 minutes by bus from Tivat. Flights there are often ₽5 000 – ₽10 000 (≈ $55 – $110) cheaper. Then take a bus or rent a car.
  4. Be flexible with dates. Use the “whole month” search feature on Aviasales. A Tuesday departure in May might be ₽8 000 (≈ $85) less than a Friday flight.
  5. Book connecting flights separately. Sometimes booking a cheap flight to Belgrade (BEG) and then a separate ticket to Tivat saves money. Just allow 3-4 hours for layovers.
  6. Use incognito mode. Airlines track your searches. Clear cookies or use a private browser to avoid price hikes.
  7. Check low-cost carriers. Ryanair and Wizz Air have seasonal routes from London, Berlin, and Warsaw. Prices start at ₽5 000 (≈ $55) one-way — but watch for add-ons like luggage.
  8. Book early for summer, late for winter. For July-August, book 3-4 months ahead. For November-February, you can snag deals 2-3 weeks before departure.
  9. Use a travel credit card. Many cards offer miles or cashback. For example, a card with 2% back on flights could save you ₽1 000 (≈ $11) on a ₽50 000 ticket.
  10. Consider charter flights. Tour operators like TUI or Pegas Touristik sometimes sell empty seats at discounts. Check local travel agencies.

One more hack: if you’re flying from Asia or the US, consider a stopover in Istanbul. Turkish Airlines often offers free hotel stays for long layovers — a bonus if you’re on a budget!

When to Book

Timing is everything. Based on 2025 trends (and projecting into 2026):

  • For summer travel (June-August): Book 4-6 months in advance. Prices start climbing in March. I booked my July 2025 flight to Tivat in February and saved ₽15 000 (≈ $165).
  • For shoulder season (April-May, September-October): Book 2-3 months ahead. Deals pop up frequently.
  • For winter (November-March): Book 1-2 months ahead. Flights are cheap, but availability is limited — some routes shut down in December.
  • Best day to book: Tuesday or Wednesday. Airlines often release sales on Monday evenings, and by Tuesday, prices are lower.
  • Best time to fly: Early morning (6-8 a.m.) or late night (10 p.m.-midnight) flights are cheapest. I once flew from Moscow to Tivat at 6 a.m. For ₽16 000 (≈ $175) — a steal!

Use Aviasales price alerts — they’ll email you when prices drop for your route. Also, check Hotellook for package deals (flight + hotel), which can save another 10-15%.

Flight Insurance

Even with cheap flights, disruptions happen. Delays, cancellations, or lost luggage can ruin a trip. Here’s what to do:

  • Check your rights: Under EU Regulation 261/2004 (and Montenegro’s similar laws), you may be entitled to compensation of ₽12 000 – ₽40 000 (≈ $130 – $435) for delays over 3 hours or cancellations. Keep your boarding pass and any communication from the airline.
  • File a claim: Contact the airline first. If they refuse, use a service like Flight Insurance — they handle the paperwork for a fee (usually 25% of compensation). For example, if your flight from Istanbul to Tivat is delayed 5 hours, you could get ₽25 000 (≈ $270).
  • Travel insurance: Buy a policy from Booking or a specialist like World Nomads. For ₽1 500 – ₽3 000 (≈ $16 – $33), you’re covered for medical emergencies, trip interruptions, and baggage loss.
  • Real example: A friend’s flight from London to Tivat was cancelled last minute. They filed a claim via Flight Insurance and received ₽30 000 (≈ $325) within 6 weeks. The service took a 20% cut, but it was worth it.

Pro tip: If you’re booking a connecting flight, ensure you have at least 2 hours between connections. For separate tickets, consider insurance that covers missed connections — it’s a lifesaver.

I hope this guide helps you find affordable flights to Tivat in 2026. Remember, the key is flexibility — in dates, airports, and airlines. Use tools like Aviasales to compare, and don’t forget to check Kiwitaxi for airport transfers once you land (prices start at ₽2 000 / ≈ $22 for a private car to Tivat center). And for staying connected, grab an eSIM from Airalo — ₽1 000 (≈ $11) for 5 GB of data. Happy travels!
