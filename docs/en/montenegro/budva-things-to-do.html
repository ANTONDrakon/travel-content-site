15 Best Things to Do in Budva 2026

Budva is the crown jewel of Montenegro’s coastline—a place where ancient stone walls meet turquoise Adriatic waters, and where every cobblestone alley whispers stories of empires past. I’ve wandered its streets countless times, and I can tell you: this city isn’t just a stopover; it’s a destination that pulls you in with its charm.

From the moment you step into the Old Town, with its Venetian-era churches and bustling squares, you’ll understand why Budva is a must-visit. Whether you’re a history buff, a beach lover, or a foodie, here are the 15 best things to do in Budva in 2026—each one a gem waiting to be discovered.

15 Best Attractions

1. Budva Old Town (Stari Grad)

The heart of Budva, this fortified medieval town is a labyrinth of narrow streets, red-roofed houses, and lively piazzas. You’ll find ancient churches like the Church of St. John and the Church of St. Mary, plus countless cafes and souvenir shops. It’s a living museum where every corner reveals a new vista of the sea.

Ticket price: Free to enter; some churches may charge a small donation. Hours: Open 24/7; shops and cafes typically 9 AM–11 PM. How to get there: The Old Town is centrally located, a short walk from most hotels in Budva; follow signs from the main promenade. Tip: Visit early morning (7–9 AM) to avoid crowds and capture stunning photos without tourists.

2. Mogren Beach

Just a 10-minute walk from the Old Town, Mogren Beach is a stunning stretch of golden sand flanked by dramatic cliffs. The water is crystal-clear, perfect for swimming, and the beach is divided into two sections connected by a tunnel. It’s a favorite among locals for its laid-back vibe and natural beauty.

Ticket price: Free; sunbeds and umbrellas from ₽1 500 (≈ $16) per day. Hours: Always open; lifeguards on duty 9 AM–7 PM in summer. How to get there: From the Old Town, walk along the coastal path past the Hotel Avala; it’s a scenic 10-minute stroll. Tip: Bring water shoes—the pebbles near the water can be sharp.

3. Citadela Fortress

Perched on a rocky outcrop overlooking the Old Town, this 19th-century fortress offers panoramic views of the Adriatic and the city. Inside, you’ll find a library, a wine bar, and a small museum detailing Budva’s maritime history. It’s also a venue for summer concerts and events.

Ticket price: ₽500 (≈ $5.50) for adults; free for children under 7. Hours: 9 AM–8 PM daily (summer); 10 AM–5 PM (winter). How to get there: Enter through the Old Town’s main gate and follow signs up the hill; it’s a 5-minute walk. Tip: Go at sunset for breathtaking views and fewer crowds; the wine bar here is worth a splurge.

4. Church of St. John (Crkva Svetog Ivana)

This 7th-century church is the oldest in Budva, with a striking bell tower that dominates the Old Town skyline. Inside, you’ll find a mix of Romanesque and Gothic architecture, plus a small treasury with icons and relics. It’s a serene spot away from the bustle. Ticket price: Free; donations appreciated. Hours: 9 AM–6 PM daily. How to get there: Located in the heart of the Old Town, near the main square; easy to find. Tip: Climb the bell tower (₽200 ≈ $2.20) for a unique vantage point over the rooftops.

5. Budva Marina

A modern marina filled with luxury yachts and fishing boats, Budva Marina is a lively spot for a stroll, a coffee, or a meal at one of the waterfront restaurants. It’s also the departure point for boat tours to nearby islands and beaches. The atmosphere is glamorous yet relaxed.

Ticket price: Free to walk around; boat tours from ₽3 500 (≈ $38). Hours: Always open; restaurants 8 AM–midnight. How to get there: Adjacent to the Old Town, just past the main promenade. Tip: Book a sunset boat tour (via GetYourGuide) for a magical experience—many include a glass of wine.

6. Slovenska Beach

Budva’s longest beach, Slovenska Beach stretches for nearly 2 kilometers and is ideal for families and sun-seekers. It’s well-equipped with sunbeds, umbrellas, and water sports rentals, and the water is calm and shallow. The beach promenade is lined with cafes and shops. Ticket price: Free; sunbeds from ₽1 200 (≈ $13) per day. Hours: Always open; lifeguards 9 AM–7 PM. How to get there: A 5-minute walk from the Old Town; follow the coastal road east. Tip: Head to the eastern end for quieter spots; rent a paddleboard (₽1 000 ≈ $11) for fun.

7. St. Nicholas Island (Sveti Nikola)

Also known as “Hawaii,” this small island is a 15-minute boat ride from Budva Marina. It features a pristine beach, a pine forest, and a restaurant serving fresh seafood. You can hike to the top for panoramic views or simply relax on the sand. Ticket price: Boat transfer ₽600 (≈ $6.50) round-trip; island entry free. Hours: Boats run 9 AM–7 PM (summer). How to get there: Take a water taxi from Budva Marina or Slovenska Beach. Tip: Bring snorkeling gear—the water around the island is teeming with fish.

8. Podmaine Monastery

A serene Orthodox monastery set in a lush garden just outside Budva, Podmaine dates back to the 15th century. It’s known for its beautiful frescoes and peaceful atmosphere, offering a glimpse into Montenegro’s spiritual heritage. The surrounding hills are perfect for a short hike. Ticket price: Free; donations welcome. Hours: 8 AM–6 PM daily. How to get there: A 20-minute walk from the Old Town; take the road toward Podmaine village. Tip: Combine with a visit to the nearby Budva Olive Tree (see below).

9. Budva Olive Tree

Estimated to be over 2,000 years old, this ancient olive tree is a living monument near the Podmaine Monastery. It’s a symbol of Budva’s enduring history and a popular photo spot. The tree is surrounded by a small park with benches for contemplation. Ticket price: Free. Hours: Always open. How to get there: A 5-minute walk from Podmaine Monastery; follow signs. Tip: Visit in late afternoon for golden light; bring a picnic to enjoy under the branches.

10. TQ Plaza

Budva’s largest shopping mall, TQ Plaza offers a mix of international brands, a supermarket, a cinema, and a food court. It’s a great escape from the heat or rain, and kids will love the indoor playground. The rooftop terrace has a bar with views of the mountains. Ticket price: Free entry; cinema tickets from ₽500 (≈ $5.50). Hours: 9 AM–10 PM daily. How to get there: A 15-minute walk from the Old Town; take bus line 1 or 2. Tip: Check for sales on local brands like “Montenegro” clothing.

11. Budva City Museum

Housed in a 19th-century building, this museum showcases Budva’s history from Illyrian times to the present. Exhibits include archaeological finds, traditional costumes, and maritime artifacts. It’s a small but well-curated collection that adds depth to your visit. Ticket price: ₽400 (≈ $4.40). Hours: 9 AM–5 PM (closed Mondays). How to get there: Inside the Old Town, near the Church of St. John. Tip: Combine with a visit to the Citadela for a historical day out.

12. Dukley Beach Club

A chic beach club on the outskirts of Budva, Dukley offers a glamorous day out with sunbeds, a pool, and a restaurant serving Mediterranean cuisine. It’s popular with a young crowd and hosts DJ sets in summer. The atmosphere is lively but not overwhelming. Ticket price: Free entry; sunbeds from ₽2 000 (≈ $22). Hours: 10 AM–8 PM (summer). How to get there: A 20-minute walk from the Old Town along the coastal path; or take a taxi for ₽500 (≈ $5.50). Tip: Book a sunbed in advance via Hotellook for better rates.

13. Budva Old Town Walls

The medieval walls surrounding the Old Town are a UNESCO-listed structure dating back to the Venetian era. You can walk along sections of the walls for stunning views of the Adriatic and the city. The walls are especially magical at night when lit up. Ticket price: Free to walk along; some sections require a ₽200 (≈ $2.20) donation. Hours: Always open. How to get there: Access from various points in the Old Town; follow signs for “City Walls.” Tip: Walk the full circuit (about 30 minutes) for the best photo ops.

14. Beach of Jaz

Located 3 kilometers west of Budva, Jaz Beach is a long pebble-and-sand beach known for its clear water and vibrant nightlife. It’s also a venue for music festivals in summer. The beach has several bars and restaurants, plus water sports rentals. Ticket price: Free; sunbeds from ₽1 500 (≈ $16). Hours: Always open; lifeguards 9 AM–7 PM. How to get there: Take bus line 5 from Budva bus station (₽150 ≈ $1.60); or a 10-minute taxi ride. Tip: Visit on a weekday to avoid festival crowds; the sunset here is spectacular.

15. Budva Promenade (Riva)

The main promenade along the coast connects the Old Town to Slovenska Beach, lined with palm trees, benches, and cafes. It’s perfect for a leisurely stroll, a bike ride, or people-watching. The promenade comes alive in the evening with street performers and vendors. Ticket price: Free. Hours: Always open. How to get there: Starts at Budva Marina and extends east along the coast. Tip: Rent a bike from a nearby shop (₽300 ≈ $3.30 per hour) for a fun ride to Mogren Beach.

1-Day, 2-Day, 3-Day Itineraries

1-Day Itinerary

Start your day at 9 AM with a coffee at a cafe on the Budva Promenade, then explore the Old Town (Stari Grad) for 2 hours, visiting the Church of St. John and Citadela Fortress. At noon, head to Mogren Beach for a swim and lunch at a beachside restaurant. Spend the afternoon at Slovenska Beach or take a boat to St. Nicholas Island. End the day with a sunset walk along the Old Town Walls and dinner at a restaurant in the Old Town. Cost: Free attractions plus ₽1 500 (≈ $16) for lunch and transport.

2-Day Itinerary

Day 1: Follow the 1-day itinerary. Day 2: Start at Podmaine Monastery and the Budva Olive Tree (9–11 AM). Then, visit the Budva City Museum (11 AM–12:30 PM). After lunch, take a taxi to Jaz Beach for the afternoon (1–5 PM). In the evening, enjoy a sunset boat tour from Budva Marina (book via GetYourGuide). Cost: ₽3 500 (≈ $38) including boat tour and transport.

3-Day Itinerary

Day 1 and 2 as above. Day 3: Take a day trip to Sveti Stefan (a 15-minute bus ride, ₽200 ≈ $2.20) to see the iconic islet resort—you can’t enter without a hotel booking, but the view from the beach is free. Return for lunch at TQ Plaza, then spend the afternoon at Dukley Beach Club. In the evening, explore the Budva Marina for a farewell dinner. Cost: ₽4 000 (≈ $44) including transport and meals.

Tours & Excursions

For a deeper dive into Budva’s surroundings, consider a guided tour. Popular options include a half-day boat tour to the Blue Cave and Our Lady of the Rocks (₽4 500 ≈ $49) or a full-day trip to Durmitor National Park (₽8 000 ≈ $87). Walking tours of the Old Town start at ₽1 000 (≈ $11) and are led by local historians. You can easily book these excursions through GetYourGuide—they offer free cancellation and verified reviews. Free Attractions

Budva is surprisingly budget-friendly. Free attractions include the Old Town (Stari Grad), Mogren Beach, Budva Promenade, the Old Town Walls, Podmaine Monastery, the Budva Olive Tree, and the Budva Marina. You can also explore the coastal path to Jaz Beach for free. For a no-cost activity, watch the sunset from the Citadela’s outer walls—it’s a magical experience without a ticket.

Practical Tips

Transportation

Budva is walkable, especially the Old Town and beaches. For longer distances, use local buses (lines 1, 2, 5; ₽150 ≈ $1.60 per ride). Taxis cost ₽500–₽1 000 (≈ $5.50–$11) for trips within the city. For airport transfers, consider Kiwitaxi for fixed rates from Tivat or Podgorica airports (₽2 500 ≈ $27). To stay connected, grab an Airalo eSIM for data—plans start at ₽500 (≈ $5.50) for 1 GB.

Combo Tickets

Look for the “Budva Pass” (₽1 500 ≈ $16) available at the tourist office, which includes entry to Citadela Fortress, the City Museum, and a boat tour discount. It’s valid for 3 days and saves you about 30% compared to individual tickets. Also, many hotels offer free shuttle services to beaches like Jaz—ask at your accommodation.

When to Visit

June to September is peak season with warm weather (25–35°C) and lively events. For fewer crowds, visit in May or October—temperatures are mild (20–25°C) and prices lower. Book accommodations via Booking or Agoda for the best deals; I recommend staying in the Old Town for easy access to attractions.
