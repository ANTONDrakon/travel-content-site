15 Best Things to Do in Muscat 2026

Updated: July 2026 · Expert guide · ⏱ 5 min read

Table of Contents

  • 15 Best Attractions
  • 1-Day, 2-Day, 3-Day Itineraries

When I first arrived in Muscat, the capital of Oman, I was struck by how different it felt from other Gulf cities. There are no skyscrapers competing for attention—instead, the city hugs the coastline with whitewashed buildings, dramatic mountains, and a sense of calm that's rare in this part of the world.

Muscat's attractions blend ancient history with modern elegance, from the glittering Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque to the bustling Mutrah Souq. Whether you're a history buff, a beach lover, or a foodie, this city offers something special. In this guide, I'll walk you through the 15 best things to do in Muscat in 2026, with practical tips on tickets, hours, and how to get around. Let's dive in!

15 Best Attractions

1. Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque

This is Muscat's crown jewel—a stunning mosque that can accommodate up to 20,000 worshippers. The main prayer hall features one of the world's largest chandeliers (14 meters tall) and a hand-laid Persian carpet weighing 21 tons. The architecture blends Ottoman, Persian, and Omani styles, with white marble and intricate floral motifs. It's a place of peace and awe, and non-Muslims are welcome outside prayer times.

  • Ticket price: Free entry
  • Hours: Saturday–Thursday 8:00 AM–11:00 AM (closed to tourists during Friday prayers)
  • How to get there: Take a taxi (₽1 500–₽2 000 / ≈ $16–$22 from city center) or bus #1 from Ruwi. You can also book a transfer via Kiwitaxi for door-to-door convenience.
  • Tip: Dress modestly—women must cover hair, arms, and legs. Scarves are available at the entrance if you forget.

2. Mutrah Corniche

This 3-kilometer waterfront promenade is perfect for a sunset stroll. You'll see traditional Omani dhows bobbing in the harbor, the majestic Al Alam Palace in the distance, and locals fishing off the rocks. The Corniche connects the Mutrah Souq to the Riyam Park area, making it a great starting point for exploring. The sea breeze and mountain backdrop create a postcard-perfect scene.

  • Ticket price: Free
  • Hours: Open 24/7, but best at sunset (around 5:30 PM–6:30 PM)
  • How to get there: Walk from Mutrah Souq (10 minutes) or take a taxi from anywhere in Muscat (₽1 000–₽1 500 / ≈ $11–$16).
  • Tip: Bring a camera—the golden hour light on the harbor is magical. Also, grab a karak chai (spiced tea) from a street vendor.

3. Mutrah Souq

One of the oldest markets in the Arabian Peninsula, Mutrah Souq is a labyrinth of narrow alleys filled with frankincense, silver jewelry, pashminas, and spices. The scent of oud and saffron hangs in the air, and shopkeepers are friendly but not pushy. It's a great place to buy Omani souvenirs like khanjars (traditional daggers) or frankincense resin. Haggling is expected—start at half the asking price.

  • Ticket price: Free to enter; budget ₽5 000–₽10 000 (≈ $54–$108) for souvenirs
  • Hours: Daily 9:00 AM–1:00 PM and 4:00 PM–9:00 PM (closed Friday mornings)
  • How to get there: Located at the end of Mutrah Corniche; walk from any nearby hotel or take a taxi to "Mutrah Souq."
  • Tip: Visit in the evening when the souq is lit with lanterns and less crowded. Carry cash—many stalls don't accept cards.

4. Al Alam Palace

The official residence of the Sultan of Oman, Al Alam Palace is a visual feast with its blue-and-gold facade and mirrored columns. You can't enter the palace itself, but the view from the gate is stunning. The palace sits between the Mirani and Jalali forts, making it a focal point of Old Muscat. The surrounding gardens are immaculate, and the area feels regal yet serene.

  • Ticket price: Free to view from outside
  • Hours: Always visible, but best at sunrise or sunset
  • How to get there: Take a taxi to "Al Alam Palace" (₽1 500–₽2 000 / ≈ $16–$22) or walk from Mutrah Corniche (30 minutes along the waterfront).
  • Tip: Combine with a visit to the nearby forts (Mirani and Jalali) for a full historical experience. Photography is allowed from the gate.

5. Royal Opera House Muscat

This architectural masterpiece is the cultural heart of Muscat, hosting opera, ballet, and classical concerts. The building blends Omani design with Italian marble and crystal chandeliers—even if you don't attend a show, the guided tour is worth it. The acoustics are world-class, and the lobby often features art exhibitions. It's a symbol of Oman's commitment to the arts.

  • Ticket price: Guided tours: ₽3 500 (≈ $38) for adults; show tickets start at ₽7 000 (≈ $76)
  • Hours: Tours daily at 10:30 AM, 11:30 AM, and 12:30 PM (check schedule for shows)
  • How to get there: Taxi from city center (₽1 500 / ≈ $16) or bus #4 from Ruwi. You can also book tickets via GetYourGuide for skip-the-line access.
  • Tip: Book show tickets in advance—popular performances sell out. Dress smart casual for evening shows.

6. Bait Al Zubair Museum

This private museum offers a deep dive into Omani culture, with exhibits on traditional weapons, costumes, jewelry, and household items. The building itself is a restored Omani house with a courtyard, giving it an authentic feel. Highlights include a collection of khanjars and a full-scale model of a traditional Omani wedding. It's a must for understanding the country's heritage.

  • Ticket price: ₽2 500 (≈ $27) for adults; free for children under 12
  • Hours: Saturday–Thursday 9:00 AM–6:00 PM; Friday 2:00 PM–6:00 PM
  • How to get there: Located in Old Muscat, a 15-minute walk from Al Alam Palace. Taxi from city center (₽1 500 / ≈ $16).
  • Tip: Visit early to avoid crowds. The museum shop sells high-quality Omani crafts.

7. Qantab Beach

Just 20 minutes from the city center, Qantab Beach is a hidden gem with turquoise waters and golden sand. It's less crowded than other beaches, making it perfect for a quiet swim or snorkeling. The surrounding cliffs offer shade, and there are a few small cafes nearby. The water is calm and clear—ideal for families.

  • Ticket price: Free
  • Hours: Open 24/7; best in the morning (8:00 AM–11:00 AM)
  • How to get there: Drive or take a taxi (₽2 000–₽3 000 / ≈ $22–$33 from city center). Public buses don't go directly; consider renting a car via Aviasales for flexibility.
  • Tip: Bring your own snorkel gear—there's a small reef just offshore. Avoid midday heat (12:00 PM–3:00 PM).

8. Sultan's Armed Forces Museum

Housed in a former British army barracks, this museum traces Oman's military history from ancient times to the present. Exhibits include weapons, uniforms, and vehicles, plus a section on the Dhofar War. The grounds are well-maintained, with tanks and aircraft on display outside. It's fascinating for history enthusiasts.

  • Ticket price: ₽1 500 (≈ $16) for adults; free for children under 10
  • Hours: Saturday–Thursday 8:00 AM–1:00 PM (closed Friday)
  • How to get there: Taxi from city center (₽1 500 / ≈ $16) or bus #2 from Ruwi.
  • Tip: Allow 1–2 hours. The museum is near the Royal Opera House, so combine both visits.

9. Riyam Park

This hillside park offers panoramic views of Muscat and the harbor. The centerpiece is a giant incense burner sculpture (symbolizing Oman's frankincense trade) that lights up at night. There are walking paths, children's play areas, and cafes. It's a popular spot for families and photographers, especially at sunset.

  • Ticket price: Free
  • Hours: Daily 8:00 AM–10:00 PM
  • How to get there: Walk from Mutrah Souq (15 minutes uphill) or take a taxi (₽1 000 / ≈ $11).
  • Tip: Bring water and wear comfortable shoes—the climb is steep but short. Visit at night for the illuminated incense burner.

10. Qurum Natural Park

Muscat's largest public park is a green oasis with lakes, fountains, and gardens. It's perfect for a picnic, jog, or lazy afternoon. The park has a small zoo, a rose garden, and a children's train. During winter (November–February), the weather is ideal for outdoor activities.

  • Ticket price: Free; small fee for train ride (₽500 / ≈ $5.50)
  • Hours: Daily 8:00 AM–10:00 PM
  • How to get there: Taxi from city center (₽1 500 / ≈ $16) or bus #4 from Ruwi.
  • Tip: Visit on a Friday afternoon for a family-friendly atmosphere. Bring a blanket for a picnic.

11. Wadi Shab

A short drive from Muscat, Wadi Shab is a stunning canyon with emerald pools and waterfalls. The hike takes about 45 minutes through rocky terrain, leading to a swim through a narrow gorge to a hidden waterfall. It's an adventure you won't forget—the water is cool and refreshing. Wear water shoes and bring a waterproof bag.

  • Ticket price: Free (parking ₽500 / ≈ $5.50)
  • Hours: Best visited early morning (7:00 AM–10:00 AM) to avoid heat
  • How to get there: Drive (1 hour from Muscat) or book a tour via GetYourGuide (₽7 000–₽10 000 / ≈ $76–$108 per person, including transport).
  • Tip: Go during weekdays to avoid crowds. Bring snacks and plenty of water—there are no facilities inside.

12. Amouage Factory Tour

Amouage is one of the world's most luxurious perfume brands, and their factory in Muscat offers a fascinating behind-the-scenes tour. You'll learn about the distillation process, see rare ingredients like frankincense and rose, and even create your own scent. The showroom is a sensory delight—expect to leave with a bottle (or two).

  • Ticket price: Free tour; perfume bottles start at ₽15 000 (≈ $163)
  • Hours: Saturday–Thursday 9:00 AM–5:00 PM (tours by appointment)
  • How to get there: Taxi from city center (₽2 000 / ≈ $22) or book a ride via Kiwitaxi.
  • Tip: Call ahead to reserve a tour. The factory shop has exclusive scents not available elsewhere.

13. Muscat Gate Museum

This small museum in Old Muscat tells the story of the city's six historic gates. Each gate has a unique design and history, from defense purposes to trade routes. The museum is housed in a restored gate, and the exhibits include maps, photos, and artifacts. It's a quick stop but rich in local lore.

  • Ticket price: ₽1 000 (≈ $11)
  • Hours: Saturday–Thursday 9:00 AM–1:00 PM and 4:00 PM–7:00 PM
  • How to get there: Located near Al Alam Palace; walk from there (5 minutes).
  • Tip: Combine with a visit to the palace and forts for a half-day historical tour.

14. Shatti Al Qurum Beach

This urban beach in the Qurum district is popular with locals and expats. It's lined with cafes, restaurants, and a promenade, making it a great spot for a sunset walk or a swim. The water is clean, and there are lifeguards on duty. It's also close to the Opera Galleria Mall for shopping.

  • Ticket price: Free
  • Hours: Open 24/7; best at sunset
  • How to get there: Taxi from city center (₽1 500 / ≈ $16) or bus #4.
  • Tip: Rent a kayak or paddleboard from nearby vendors (₽3 000 / ≈ $33 per hour).

15. Daymaniyat Islands

This nature reserve is a paradise for snorkelers and divers, with crystal-clear waters, coral reefs, and sea turtles. The islands are protected, so only guided tours are allowed. You'll see colorful fish, rays, and maybe even dolphins. It's a half-day trip from Muscat that's worth every minute.

  • Ticket price: Tour packages start at ₽8 000 (≈ $87) per person (includes boat, gear, and lunch)
  • Hours: Tours depart at 8:00 AM, return by 1:00 PM (best October–April)
  • How to get there: Book a tour via GetYourGuide or local operators. Transport from Muscat included.
  • Tip: Bring reef-safe sunscreen and a waterproof camera. Snorkeling gear is provided, but you can bring your own mask for comfort.

1-Day, 2-Day, 3-Day Itineraries

1-Day Itinerary: Muscat Highlights

Morning (8:00 AM–12:00 PM): Start at Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque (1 hour). Then head to Mutrah Corniche for a walk (30 minutes) and explore Mutrah Souq (1 hour).
Lunch (12:00 PM–1:30 PM): Eat at Bait Al Luban (traditional Omani) near the Corniche (₽4 000 / ≈ $43 per person).
Afternoon (1:30 PM–4:30 PM): Visit Al Alam Palace (30 minutes) and Bait Al Zubair Museum (1 hour).
Evening (5:00 PM–7:00 PM): Watch sunset at Riyam Park, then dinner at a Corniche café (₽3 000 / ≈ $33).

2-Day Itinerary: Culture & Nature

Day 1: Follow the 1-day itinerary above.
Day 2: Morning trip to Wadi Shab (7:00 AM–12:00 PM). Lunch at a local restaurant near the wadi. Afternoon at Qantab Beach (1:00 PM–4:00 PM). Evening at Royal Opera House Muscat for a show or tour (book in advance).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do attractions cost in Muscat?

Entrance fees vary: from free attractions to $20-30 for museums. Many venues have discount days.

Do I need a guide in Muscat?

A guide is recommended for historical sites and complex routes. Self-guided travel is possible with audio guides and maps.
