Muscat is one of those rare cities that feels both ancient and effortlessly modern, where the turquoise waters of the Gulf of Oman lap against golden sand dunes and the Hajar Mountains rise dramatically in the background.

Unlike the glitzy skyscrapers of Dubai or Doha, Muscat retains a quiet, soulful charm — white-washed buildings, fragrant souks, and a pace of life that encourages you to slow down and breathe. The air smells of frankincense, the call to prayer echoes across serene bays, and the Omani hospitality is so genuine it will stay with you long after you return home.

Why is 2026 the year to visit? Because Oman is investing heavily in sustainable tourism while preserving its cultural heart. You can explore the magnificent Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque one morning, then drive to a wadi for a swim in emerald pools by afternoon. The city is safe, clean, and surprisingly affordable compared to its Gulf neighbors. Whether you’re a history buff, a beach lover, or a foodie, Muscat offers a rich tapestry of experiences without the crowds — and that’s becoming increasingly rare in the travel world.

Muscat is not a city you rush through. It’s a place to wander, to taste fresh halwa in the Mutrah Souq, to watch dhows bob in the harbor at sunset, and to feel the desert wind on your skin. If you’re seeking a destination that blends natural beauty, deep-rooted tradition, and genuine warmth, Muscat in 2026 is calling your name.

How to Get There

Muscat International Airport (MCT) is the main gateway, served by Oman Air, Emirates, Qatar Airways, and Turkish Airlines, among others. From Moscow, a direct flight with Oman Air takes about 5 hours and costs roughly ₽45 000 – ₽80 000 (≈ $500 – $900) round trip depending on the season.

From Dubai, it’s a quick 1-hour hop for about ₽12 000 – ₽20 000 (≈ $130 – $220). Many European hubs like London, Paris, and Frankfurt also have direct or one-stop connections. For the best deals, compare options on Aviasales, where you can filter by price and layover duration. Remember that booking 2–3 months in advance usually saves 20–30%.

Where to Stay

Muscat's neighborhoods each offer a different vibe. Mutrah is the historic heart — close to the souq and corniche, with budget options like Mutrah Hotel (₽4 000 – ₽7 000/night, ≈ $45 – $80). Qurum is the trendy area with cafes, parks, and mid-range hotels like Golden Tulip Muscat (₽8 000 – ₽14 000/night, ≈ $90 – $160).

For luxury, head to Al Bustan or Shatti Al Qurum, where five-star resorts like Al Bustan Palace, a Ritz-Carlton Hotel start at ₽25 000 – ₽45 000/night (≈ $280 – $500). Budget travelers can find guesthouses in Ruwi for as little as ₽2 500 – ₽4 000/night (≈ $28 – $45). Always compare prices on Hotellook or Booking.com to snag the best deal — especially during the November–March high season.

Top 15 Attractions

  • Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque — A masterpiece of Islamic architecture with a hand-laid Persian carpet weighing 21 tons. Free entry. Open Sat–Thu 8:00–11:00 (closed to tourists during prayer times).
  • Mutrah Souq — One of the oldest souks in the Arab world, perfect for frankincense, silver jewelry, and Omani halwa. Free to browse. Open daily 9:00–13:00 and 16:00–22:00.
  • Mutrah Corniche — A scenic 3-km waterfront promenade with views of the harbor and the Al Jalali Fort. Free. Best at sunset.
  • Royal Opera House Muscat — A stunning venue for opera, ballet, and classical music. Guided tours: ₽2 500 (≈ $28). Tours daily 8:30–17:00.
  • Bait Al Zubair Museum — A private museum showcasing Omani heritage, weapons, and costumes. Entry: ₽1 000 (≈ $11). Sat–Thu 9:00–17:00, Fri 14:00–17:00.
  • Al Jalali Fort — A 16th-century Portuguese fort now housing a museum. Entry: ₽1 500 (≈ $17). Sat–Thu 8:00–16:00.
  • Qurum Natural Park — A lush green space with a lake, playgrounds, and jogging tracks. Free. Daily 7:00–23:00.
  • Wadi Shab — A stunning canyon with turquoise pools and a hidden waterfall. Entry: ₽500 (≈ $5.50). Open daily 7:00–17:00 (arrive early to avoid crowds).
  • Bimmah Sinkhole — A natural limestone sinkhole with crystal-clear water for swimming. Free. Daily 8:00–18:00.
  • Al Alam Palace — The ceremonial palace of the Sultan (exterior only). Free. Best viewed from the corniche.
  • Oman Aquarium — A small but family-friendly aquarium with a 360-degree tunnel. Entry: ₽3 000 (≈ $33). Daily 10:00–22:00.
  • Riyam Park — A hilltop park with a giant incense burner monument and panoramic views. Free. Daily 8:00–22:00.
  • Al Mouj Marina — A modern waterfront area with restaurants, shops, and a golf course. Free to walk. Best for dinner.
  • Daymaniyat Islands Nature Reserve — A protected archipelago for snorkeling and diving with turtles and colorful fish. Boat tour: ₽7 000 – ₽12 000 (≈ $80 – $135).
  • Nizwa Fort — A 90-minute drive from Muscat, this 17th-century fort offers a museum and a climb to the top for views. Entry: ₽2 000 (≈ $22). Sat–Thu 9:00–16:00, Fri 9:00–14:00.

Getting Around

Muscat has no metro. Buses (Mwasalat) cover main routes: ₽200 – ₽400 (≈ $2.20 – $4.50) per ride. Taxis are abundant but negotiate the fare before getting in — short trips cost ₽1 000 – ₽2 500 (≈ $11 – $28). For more freedom, rent a car from companies like Budget or Hertz: from ₽3 500/day (≈ $39). Fuel is cheap at about ₽200/liter (≈ $2.20). Ride-hailing apps like Otaxi are reliable and often cheaper than street taxis. For airport-to-hotel convenience, consider a pre-booked Kiwitaxi transfer — fixed price, no haggling.

Where to Eat

Omani cuisine is a blend of Arabian, Indian, and East African flavors. Must-tries include shuwa (slow-roasted lamb), machboos (spiced rice with meat), and halwa (a sweet, gelatinous dessert). Bait Al Luban in Mutrah serves excellent Omani dishes for ₽2 500 – ₽4 000 (≈ $28 – $45) per person.

For street food, head to the Ruwi High Street area for shawarma and samosas at ₽500 – ₽1 000 (≈ $5.50 – $11). Turkish House Restaurant offers generous kebabs for ₽1 500 – ₽2 500 (≈ $17 – $28). For a splurge, The Beach Restaurant at the Chedi serves seafood with ocean views — ₽5 000 – ₽8 000 (≈ $56 – $90) per person. Budget tip: eat at local cafeterias for ₽800 – ₽1 500 (≈ $9 – $17).

Tours & Excursions

Day trips from Muscat are unforgettable. A full-day tour to Nizwa, Bahla Fort, and Jebel Shams (the "Grand Canyon of Oman") costs about ₽7 000 – ₽12 000 (≈ $80 – $135) per person, including lunch. Dolphin-watching cruises from Al Mouj start at ₽4 000 (≈ $45).

For a desert adventure, a half-day Wahiba Sands safari with dune bashing and camel rides is ₽6 000 – ₽10 000 (≈ $67 – $112). Snorkeling at the Daymaniyat Islands is a must — check availability and prices on GetYourGuide, where you can book with free cancellation. Many tours include hotel pickup and drop-off.

Airport Transfer

Muscat International Airport is about 15–20 km from the city center. A taxi to Mutrah or Qurum costs ₽3 000 – ₽5 000 (≈ $33 – $56) — negotiate before you go. Public bus route A1 runs from the airport to Ruwi every 30 minutes: ₽200 (≈ $2.20). For a stress-free start, book a private Kiwitaxi transfer — prices start at ₽3 500 (≈ $39), and the driver will meet you at arrivals with a sign. It’s especially worth it if you arrive late at night.

Connectivity

Free Wi-Fi is available in most hotels, malls, and cafes (like Starbucks). For reliable data on the go, buy a local SIM from Omantel or Ooredoo at the airport — a 7-day plan with 5 GB costs about ₽2 500 (≈ $28). Alternatively, download an eSIM before you travel for instant connectivity. An Airalo eSIM for Oman starts at ₽1 200 (≈ $13) for 1 GB, and you can top up as needed — no physical SIM required.

Best Time to Visit

The best months are October to March, when temperatures range from 20°C to 30°C (68°F–86°F) and skies are clear. This is peak season, so book accommodation 2–3 months ahead. April and May are hotter (30°C–40°C) but less crowded, with hotel prices dropping 20–30%. June to September is scorching (up to 45°C) — only visit if you plan to stay mostly in air-conditioned spaces or hit the beaches early morning. During summer, you can find luxury hotels for ₽12 000 – ₽18 000/night (≈ $135 – $200) versus ₽25 000+ in winter.

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens can get a visa on arrival at Muscat Airport for 20 OMR (≈ ₽4 800 / $52). The visa is valid for 10 days and can be extended. You'll need a passport valid for at least 6 months, a return ticket, and a hotel booking. Alternatively, you can apply for an e-visa online before travel (same price). For up-to-date information, check the Royal Oman Police website. Important for Maldives-bound travelers: Перед вылетом заполните декларацию IMUGA на imuga.immigration.gov.mv и получите QR-код — без него не посадят на рейс.

Currency & Money

The local currency is the Omani Rial (OMR), divided into 1 000 baisa. 1 OMR ≈ ₽230 (≈ $2.60). Banknotes come in 1, 5, 10, 20, and 50 OMR. Exchange at banks (Bank Muscat, HSBC) or exchange offices in Mutrah for the best rates — avoid the airport kiosks (poor rates). Credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) are widely accepted in hotels, restaurants, and shops, but cash is king at souks and for taxis. Bring about ₽10 000 – ₽15 000 (≈ $110 – $165) in cash for a week, plus your card for larger purchases. ATMs are abundant in the city.

Time Zone

Muscat is in UTC+4 (Gulf Standard Time). It is 1 hour ahead of Moscow, 4 hours ahead of London, 9 hours ahead of New York, and 4 hours behind Bangkok. No daylight saving time.

Travel Insurance

Travel insurance is not mandatory for Oman, but it's highly recommended — medical costs can be steep for foreigners. A good policy covers medical emergencies, trip cancellation, lost luggage, and COVID-related issues. A 7-day plan starts at about ₽1 500 – ₽3 000 (≈ $17 – $33). Compare and purchase via your insurance partner for peace of mind. Always carry a copy of your policy and emergency contact numbers.
