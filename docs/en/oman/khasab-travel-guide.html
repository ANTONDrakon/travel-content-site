Welcome to Khasab, the hidden gem of Oman’s Musandam Peninsula—a place where jagged mountains plunge into turquoise fjords and life moves at the pace of a gentle dhow sail. This small port city, often called the “Norway of Arabia,” feels worlds away from the glitzy skyscrapers of Dubai, just a two-hour drive north.

The air smells of salt and frankincense, and the streets are quiet, save for the occasional fisherman mending nets. Khasab isn’t about flash; it’s about raw, untouched beauty—think dramatic limestone cliffs, dolphins leaping in crystal-clear waters, and ancient forts that whisper stories of Portuguese traders.

Why is 2026 the year to visit? Because this is the year to experience Oman’s wild north before it becomes a mainstream hotspot. The Omani government has been quietly investing in sustainable tourism here, but Khasab still feels like a secret. You’ll find no crowded beaches or neon-lit malls—just genuine hospitality, world-class snorkeling, and landscapes that’ll make your Instagram followers weep with envy.

Whether you’re kayaking through the Khor Ash Sham fjord, sipping cardamom coffee at a local café, or bargaining for a pashmina in the souk, Khasab offers a rare blend of adventure and tranquility. Plus, with direct flights from Dubai and Muscat becoming more frequent, it’s never been easier to escape the ordinary.

Khasab is also a gateway to the broader Musandam region, where remote mountain villages like Kumzar and Tawi offer glimpses into a traditional way of life. The people here are incredibly warm—don’t be surprised if a shopkeeper invites you for tea. And the wildlife? From spinner dolphins to sea turtles and even the occasional whale shark, the waters around Khasab are a marine paradise. So pack your sunscreen, a sense of adventure, and an empty stomach for the seafood. Khasab 2026 is calling.

How to Get There

The main gateway is Khasab Airport (KHS), a small but efficient hub with flights from Muscat (Oman Air, ₽12 500–₽18 000, ≈ $135–$195), Dubai (Air Arabia, ₽8 000–₽12 000, ≈ $87–$130), and seasonal charters from Doha. From Muscat, the flight is just 1 hour 20 minutes; from Dubai, a breezy 45 minutes. If you’re coming from further afield, connect via Dubai International Airport (DXB) or Muscat International Airport (MCT).

For budget travelers, a bus from Muscat to Khasab (₽1 500–₽2 500, ≈ $16–$27) takes about 6 hours but offers stunning coastal views. Driving from Dubai (2.5 hours via the Hatta border crossing) is also popular—just remember to get your Oman visa sorted in advance. Check flight options on

Where to Stay

Khasab’s accommodation scene is small but charming, with options for every wallet. For luxury with a view, the Atana Khasab Hotel (₽12 000–₽18 000 per night, ≈ $130–$195) offers spacious rooms overlooking the harbor, a pool, and an excellent seafood restaurant. Mid-range travelers love the Khasab Hotel Apartments (₽5 500–₽8 000 per night, ≈ $60–$87) for their clean, self-catering units near the souk.

Budget backpackers should check out Salama’s Guesthouse (₽2 000–₽3 500 per night, ≈ $22–$38), a family-run spot with shared bathrooms and authentic Omani breakfasts. Top 15 Attractions

  1. Khasab Fort – A 17th-century Portuguese fort turned museum. Climb the towers for panoramic harbor views. ₽1 200 (≈ $13). Sat–Thu 8:00–14:00, Fri 8:00–11:00.
  2. Khor Ash Sham (Musandam Fjord) – The crown jewel of the region. Take a dhow cruise through dramatic cliffs, spot dolphins, and swim in secluded coves. Free to enter; dhow tours from ₽9 000 (≈ $98) per person. Daily sunrise to sunset.
  3. Dolphin Watching – Spinner and bottlenose dolphins are almost guaranteed. Tours depart at 9:00 AM. ₽7 000–₽10 000 (≈ $76–$109) per person, 3–4 hours.
  4. Telegraph Island – A historic island in the fjord where a British telegraph cable was laid in 1864. Accessible by kayak or dhow. ₽1 500 (≈ $16) entry. Daylight hours.
  5. Khasab Souk – A traditional market selling frankincense, spices, silver jewelry, and pashminas. Free entry. Sat–Thu 9:00–13:00 & 16:00–21:00, Fri 16:00–21:00.
  6. Jabal Harim – The highest peak in Musandam (2,087 m). Drive up for breathtaking views of the fjords and Oman–UAE border. Free. 4WD required; best at sunrise or sunset.
  7. Kumzar Village – A remote fishing village at the tip of the peninsula, accessible only by boat. See traditional stone houses and the Kumzari language. ₽3 000 (≈ $33) boat trip. Departures at 8:00 AM.
  8. Bassa Beach – A white-sand beach perfect for swimming and picnics. Free. Accessible by 4WD (30 min from Khasab). Always open.
  9. Seebi Island – A tiny, uninhabited island with pristine snorkeling. ₽2 500 (≈ $27) boat transfer. Best visited November–April.
  10. Wadi al-Abadeen – A dry riverbed with dramatic rock formations and a natural pool after rains. Free. 4WD recommended. Always open.
  11. Khasab Museum – Housed in the old fort, this small museum showcases Omani maritime history and traditional weapons. ₽1 000 (≈ $11). Sat–Thu 8:00–14:00.
  12. Ras al-Khaimah Border Crossing – Just 20 km north, this crossing offers a peek into the UAE’s northern emirate. Free to visit. Open 24/7 (visa required for UAE entry).
  13. Dhow Building Yard – Watch traditional wooden boats being built by hand at the old port. Free. Weekdays 8:00–12:00.
  14. Al-Bilad Village – An abandoned village with crumbling mud-brick houses and a sundial. Free. Accessible by 4WD. Always open.
  15. Night Dhow Cruise – A magical evening sail with dinner under the stars, often accompanied by Omani music. ₽12 000–₽15 000 (≈ $130–$163) per person. Departs at 18:00.

Getting Around

Khasab has no public metro or city buses, but getting around is easy. Taxis are the main mode—flag one down; short trips within town cost ₽1 500–₽2 500 (≈ $16–$27), while a full-day hire runs ₽8 000–₽12 000 (≈ $87–$130).

For independence, rent a 4WD from companies like Hertz or local agencies for ₽5 000–₽7 000 per day (≈ $54–$76); mandatory for off-road adventures. Scooters are available for ₽2 000 per day (≈ $22), but stick to paved roads. Walking is pleasant in the compact town center. For airport transfers, book a Kiwitaxi private car via

Where to Eat

Khasab’s food scene is simple but delicious. For local Omani cuisine, head to Al-Mutanabi Restaurant (₽3 000–₽5 500 per meal, ≈ $33–$60) for shuwa (slow-cooked lamb) and maqbous (spiced rice). Seafood Paradise (₽4 000–₽6 000, ≈ $43–$65) serves fresh grilled hammour and king prawns right off the boat.

For street food, try shawarma wraps and samosa chaat at stalls near the souk for ₽1 000–₽2 000 (≈ $11–$22). Budget travelers love Al-Farooj Cafeteria for cheap biryani and karak chai (₽800–₽1 200, ≈ $9–$13). For a splurge, the Atana Khasab Hotel’s seafood buffet (₽8 000, ≈ $87) on Fridays is legendary. Tip: always try the local dates and Omani halwa for dessert.

Tours & Excursions

The best way to experience Khasab is through guided tours. A full-day dhow cruise (8:00 AM–5:00 PM) includes dolphin watching, snorkeling, lunch, and visits to Telegraph Island and Seebi Island—prices range from ₽9 000–₽15 000 per person (≈ $98–$163). Mountain safaris to Jabal Harim and Kumzar cost ₽7 000–₽10 000 (≈ $76–$109) and include a 4WD driver and guide.

For a unique experience, book a kayaking tour through the fjords (₽5 000–₽7 000, ≈ $54–$76) with equipment and snacks included. Most tours can be arranged at your hotel or through local operators like Musandam Sea Tours. Compare and book excursions on

Airport Transfer

Khasab Airport (KHS) is just 3 km from the city center, so getting to your hotel is quick. Taxis wait outside arrivals; a ride to town costs ₽1 500–₽2 000 (≈ $16–$22). Private transfers from companies like Kiwitaxi cost ₽3 000–₽4 000 (≈ $33–$43) and can be pre-booked for peace of mind. Visa & Documents

Citizens of most countries, including Russia, can get a visa on arrival at Khasab Airport for 20 OMR (≈ $52). You’ll need a passport valid for at least 6 months, a return ticket, and proof of accommodation.

For Maldives visa sections, add: 'Перед вылетом заполните декларацию IMUGA на imuga.immigration.gov.mv и получите QR-код — без него не посадят на рейс.' Alternatively, you can apply for an e-visa online via the Royal Oman Police website (processing time 2–3 days, cost 5 OMR ≈ $13). Overstaying incurs a fine of 10 OMR per day (≈ $26). Always carry a copy of your visa and passport.

Currency & Money

The local currency is the Omani rial (OMR), subdivided into 1,000 baisa. 1 OMR ≈ $2.60 (or ₽230). Notes: 1, 5, 10, 20, 50 OMR; coins: 5, 10, 25, 50 baisa. Exchange currency at the airport, banks (Bank Muscat, HSBC), or exchange offices in the souk—rates are similar. Credit cards (Visa/Mastercard) are accepted at hotels, larger restaurants, and tour operators, but cash is king at the souk, taxis, and small eateries. ATMs are plentiful in town. Bring about 100–150 OMR (≈ ₽23 000–₽34 500, ≈ $260–$390) in cash for a week to cover incidentals.

Time Zone

Khasab operates on Gulf Standard Time (GST), UTC+4. No daylight saving. Time difference from major hubs: Moscow +1 hour, London +3 hours, New York +8 hours, Dubai same time, Singapore –4 hours. Plan your calls and tours accordingly—most activities start early (7–8 AM) to avoid the midday heat.
