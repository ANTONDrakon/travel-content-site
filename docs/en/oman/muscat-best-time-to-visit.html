Best Time to Visit Muscat 2026: Seasons & Weather

When I first started planning my trip to Muscat, I was honestly a bit overwhelmed by the climate. Oman’s capital is a city of contrasts—where rugged mountains meet the Arabian Sea, and ancient souks sit alongside modern mosques. The weather here isn’t just a footnote; it’s a key character in your travel story. Muscat has a subtropical, arid climate, which means hot summers and mild winters.

But there’s a nuance: humidity from the sea can make the heat feel more intense, while the winter months offer near-perfect conditions. In this guide, I’ll walk you through what to expect month by month, so you can pick the season that matches your dream vacation—whether that’s lounging on pristine beaches, exploring historic forts, or diving into local festivals.

Understanding the seasons is crucial because Muscat’s charm shifts dramatically. From November to March, the city is a haven of pleasant breezes and clear skies. April to October brings scorching temperatures, but also lower prices and fewer crowds. Let’s break it all down so you can plan your 2026 adventure with confidence.

The real variable is temperature, which dictates everything from your packing list to your daily itinerary.

High Season

Muscat’s high season runs from November to March. This is when the weather is at its most comfortable: daytime temperatures hover around 24–30°C, nights are cool and pleasant, and the sea is warm enough for swimming without being oppressive. The city comes alive with tourists from Europe, the Gulf, and beyond, all seeking respite from colder climates or simply the perfect winter escape.

Why is this the best time? You can explore the Sultan Qaboos Grand Mosque without breaking a sweat, wander through the Muttrah Souq in the afternoon, or take a day trip to the stunning Wadi Shab. The evenings are perfect for dining al fresco at beachfront restaurants.

However, this popularity comes with a price. Hotel rates can double or triple compared to summer. A standard double room in a mid-range hotel might cost ₽15 000 – ₽25 000 (≈ $160 – $270) per night, while luxury resorts easily hit ₽40 000 – ₽70 000 (≈ $430 – $750). Flights from major hubs also see a spike.

During high season, it’s wise to book accommodations well in advance. I recommend checking options on Hotellook or Booking to compare prices and secure your spot. For those looking for a broader selection, Agoda also has great deals, especially for beach resorts. Don’t wait until the last minute—rooms fill up fast, especially around December holidays and the Muscat Festival in January-February.

Low Season

The low season spans April to October, with the peak of summer (June to August) being the most challenging. Daytime temperatures regularly exceed 40°C, and humidity can make it feel even hotter. The sun is intense, and outdoor activities between noon and 4 PM are best avoided. But don’t write off this period entirely—there are some significant pros.

First, prices are dramatically lower. You can find hotel rooms for ₽5 000 – ₽10 000 (≈ $55 – $110) per night in good hotels. and flights are often half the price of high season. Second, the city is much quieter. You’ll have beaches like Qurum and Azaiba almost to yourself, and popular attractions won’t have queues. The sea is incredibly warm (31–32°C), perfect for swimming if you don’t mind the heat.

The main con is the heat. Sightseeing requires careful planning—start early (around 6 AM), take breaks in air-conditioned malls or cafes, and return to your hotel for a siesta during the hottest hours. Many locals do the same. Also, some smaller tour operators may reduce services, but major excursions are still available. If you’re on a budget or hate crowds, low season can be a smart choice, especially in April, May, September, and October when temperatures are more bearable (30–37°C).

Best Time for Beach Vacation

If your main goal is to soak up the sun and swim in the sea, the optimal months are October to May. During this period, water temperatures range from 23°C to 29°C, which is comfortable for long swims, snorkeling, and water sports. The air temperature is also pleasant, especially from November to March, when you can lounge on the sand without overheating.

For the absolute best beach experience, aim for November and December. The weather is warm but not scorching, the sea is calm, and the humidity is low. Beaches like Shatti Al Qurum and Al Bustan are ideal. In summer (June to September), the water is bath-like (31–32°C), which some people love, but the heat and humidity can be draining. If you’re a sun-worshipper who doesn’t mind sweating, you’ll still have a great time—just bring plenty of sunscreen and a wide-brimmed hat.

Many beachfront resorts offer day passes or private access. For a luxurious beach day, consider booking through GetYourGuide for guided snorkeling trips or dolphin-watching tours that depart from the coast. Prices for such excursions start around ₽4 500 (≈ $50) per person.

Best Time for Sightseeing

For exploring Muscat’s cultural and historical treasures, the most comfortable months are November through March. This is when you can walk through the Royal Opera House, explore the Al Jalali and Al Mirani forts, or hike the rugged mountains of the Hajar range without feeling like you’re melting. Daytime temperatures are perfect for outdoor activities—around 24–30°C.

I particularly love February and March, when the weather is crisp and clear. The light is beautiful for photography, especially at sunset near the Corniche. If you’re interested in guided tours, you can book excursions through GetYourGuide, which offers everything from city tours to desert safaris in Wahiba Sands. Prices for half-day tours range from ₽3 500 – ₽7 000 (≈ $38 – $75).

During summer, sightseeing is still possible but requires strategy. Plan visits to indoor attractions like the Bait Al Zubair Museum or the Oman Aquarium in the afternoon. Early morning or late evening is best for outdoor sites. If you’re determined to see everything, consider hiring a private driver through Kiwitaxi for air-conditioned comfort—rates start around ₽2 500 (≈ $27) per hour.

Rainy Season

Muscat doesn’t have a true rainy season like tropical destinations. The term “rainy season” here refers to the winter months—December to March—when occasional light showers occur. Total precipitation is minimal: even in the wettest month (January), you’ll only see about 10 mm of rain, often spread over just 2-3 days. These are usually brief, refreshing showers that clear up quickly, leaving the air fresh and the landscape greener.

Is it worth visiting during this time? Absolutely. The rain is so light that it rarely disrupts plans. In fact, many travelers prefer this period because the rain brings cooler temperatures and a pleasant atmosphere. The only downside is that some outdoor activities, like desert camping or wadi hiking, might be slightly muddy, but it’s rarely a deal-breaker.

If you’re worried about connectivity, I recommend getting a local eSIM from Airalo before your trip. It’s affordable (starting at ₽1 000 (≈ $11) for 1 GB) and keeps you online for booking changes or weather updates.

Events & Festivals

Muscat’s cultural calendar is vibrant, and timing your visit around a festival can add a special layer to your experience. Here are the major events by month:

  • January-February: Muscat Festival — The biggest event of the year, held at the Al Amerat Park. It features cultural shows, traditional crafts, food stalls, and a funfair. Expect large crowds and higher hotel prices. It’s a fantastic way to dive into Omani heritage.
  • February: Oman International Marathon — A growing event that attracts runners from around the world. The route often passes along the scenic Corniche.
  • March: Muscat Fashion Week — Showcasing local and international designers. A glamorous affair with runway shows and exhibitions.
  • April: Khareef Festival (preparations) — While the main Khareef Festival is in Salalah (June-September), Muscat sees cultural events leading up to it, including music and dance performances.
  • June-September: Salalah Khareef Festival — Though not in Muscat, many travelers use Muscat as a base to fly to Salalah for the monsoon season. It’s worth noting if you’re in Oman during this time.
  • July: Eid al-Adha — A major Islamic holiday. Expect some closures on the first day, but also festive markets and family gatherings.
  • November: National Day (18 November) — Celebrated with fireworks, parades, and street decorations. It’s a patriotic and joyful time to be in the city.
  • December: New Year’s Eve — Beach parties and fireworks at the Al Bustan Palace and other resorts. Book early if you plan to attend.

For a more intimate experience, check local listings for small cultural events at the Royal Opera House, which hosts performances year-round. Tickets can be booked online starting from ₽2 000 (≈ $22).

Conclusion

So, when is the best time to visit Muscat in 2026? If you want perfect weather for both beach and sightseeing, aim for November to March. This high season offers comfortable temperatures, clear skies, and vibrant festivals. You’ll pay more, but the experience is unmatched. For budget travelers or those who prefer solitude, April, May, September, and October are sweet spots—warm but manageable, with lower prices and fewer tourists. Summer (June-August) is only for the heat-tolerant, but it’s the cheapest time.

No matter when you go, Muscat will welcome you with its warm hospitality and stunning landscapes. Just remember to book flights early for peak months—you can check options on Aviasales for competitive rates. For example, a round-trip from Moscow in January might cost ₽35 000 – ₽55 000 (≈ $380 – $590), while in July it could drop to ₽22 000 – ₽35 000 (≈ $240 – $380).

Also, for visa requirements, note that many nationalities get a visa on arrival, but it’s always smart to check current rules.
