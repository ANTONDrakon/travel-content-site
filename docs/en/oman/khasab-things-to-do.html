15 Best Things to Do in Khasab 2026

If you’re looking for a destination that feels like a secret waiting to be discovered, Khasab is your answer. Nestled in the northern tip of Oman’s Musandam Peninsula, this charming port town is often called the “Norway of Arabia” for its dramatic fjords and towering limestone cliffs. I’ve spent time exploring every corner of Khasab, from its bustling souk to the serene waters of the Strait of Hormuz, and I can tell you—it’s a place that surprises you at every turn.

Whether you’re a nature lover, a history buff, or just someone craving a peaceful escape, Khasab offers a unique blend of adventure and tranquility. In this guide, I’ll walk you through the 15 best things to do in Khasab in 2026, complete with practical tips to make your trip unforgettable.

15 Best Attractions in Khasab

From ancient forts to dolphin-filled waters, here are the must-see attractions that define Khasab’s charm. Each one offers a glimpse into the region’s rich culture and stunning landscapes.

1. Khasab Fort

This 17th-century Portuguese fort stands proudly in the heart of town, its thick walls telling stories of Omani resilience. Inside, you’ll find a museum with artifacts, traditional weapons, and exhibits on local history. The rooftop offers panoramic views of the harbor and mountains—perfect for photos. It’s a quick visit but a meaningful one, especially if you love history.

Ticket price: ₽500 (≈ 5 OMR / $13) for adults, free for children under 10.
Hours: Saturday–Thursday 8:00 AM–2:00 PM, Friday 8:00 AM–11:00 AM.
How to get there: It’s a 5-minute walk from the main souk. Just head toward the harbor and you’ll see it on the hill.
Tip: Go early in the morning to avoid the heat and enjoy the quiet.

2. Dolphin Watching in the Fjords

The Musandam fjords are home to playful dolphins that often swim alongside boats. A typical tour takes you through the dramatic inlets, where you can spot bottlenose and spinner dolphins leaping in the waves. The crystal-clear waters and jagged cliffs make this a magical experience. Most tours include stops for swimming and snorkeling.

Ticket price: ₽3 000–₽4 500 (≈ 30–45 OMR / $78–$117) per person for a half-day tour.
Hours: Tours usually depart at 9:00 AM and return by 2:00 PM.
How to get there: Book through local operators at the Khasab Marina, or check options on GetYourGuide.
Tip: Bring a hat and sunscreen—the sun reflects off the water intensely.

3. Telegraph Island (Jazirat Maqlab)

This tiny island in the fjords was once a British telegraph station in the 1860s. Today, it’s a popular stop for boat tours, where you can snorkel in the clear waters and explore the ruins. The isolation of the island gives it a hauntingly beautiful vibe. It’s a must for history enthusiasts and snorkelers alike.

Ticket price: Included in most fjord tours (₽3 000–₽4 500 / 30–45 OMR).
Hours: Accessible only during daytime tours.
How to get there: By dhow boat from Khasab Marina—tours are easily arranged.
Tip: Bring snorkeling gear; the coral reefs around the island are vibrant.

4. Musandam Fjords Cruise

A cruise through the Musandam fjords is the highlight of any trip to Khasab. The towering limestone cliffs rise straight from the turquoise water, creating a landscape that feels otherworldly. You’ll pass remote fishing villages, hidden coves, and maybe even spot sea turtles. Many cruises include lunch and unlimited soft drinks.

Ticket price: ₽4 000–₽6 000 (≈ 40–60 OMR / $104–$156) per person for a full-day cruise.
Hours: 9:00 AM–4:00 PM.
How to get there: Book at the marina or online via GetYourGuide.
Tip: Choose a dhow (traditional wooden boat) for an authentic experience.

5. Khasab Souk

This traditional market is a sensory delight, with stalls selling spices, frankincense, Omani halwa, and handwoven textiles. The souk is small but lively, and the vendors are friendly and open to haggling. It’s the perfect place to pick up souvenirs like silver jewelry or pottery. Don’t miss the fresh dates—they’re incredible.

Ticket price: Free entry; items range from ₽100–₽2 000 (≈ 1–20 OMR / $2.60–$52).
Hours: Daily 8:00 AM–1:00 PM and 4:00 PM–9:00 PM.
How to get there: Located on the main road near the fort, a 2-minute walk from the harbor.
Tip: Visit in the evening when the atmosphere is cooler and more vibrant.

6. Jebel Harim (Mountain of Women)

This 2,087-meter peak is the highest in the Musandam Peninsula, offering breathtaking views of the fjords and the Strait of Hormuz. The drive up is an adventure itself, with winding roads and dramatic switchbacks. At the top, you’ll find a military radar station and a few picnic spots. It’s a great half-day trip for hikers and drivers.

Ticket price: Free to visit; 4WD rental costs ₽8 000–₽12 000 (≈ 80–120 OMR / $208–$312) per day.
Hours: Open 24/7, but best visited in daylight.
How to get there: Drive 30 km from Khasab via a gravel road—4WD required. Check Kiwitaxi for transfers.
Tip: Bring a jacket; it can be 10°C cooler at the summit.

7. Khor Ash Sham (The Fjords)

This is the main fjord system in Musandam, a network of deep inlets surrounded by rugged cliffs. It’s the best place for kayaking, with calm waters and hidden beaches to explore. You can also camp on the shore if you’re feeling adventurous. The silence here is profound, broken only by the call of seabirds.

Ticket price: Free; kayak rental costs ₽1 500–₽2 500 (≈ 15–25 OMR / $39–$65) per day.
Hours: Always accessible.
How to get there: Drive 15 km north of Khasab to the entry point.
Tip: Go at sunrise for the best light and fewer crowds.

8. Al Hallab (The Hot Springs)

Hidden in the mountains near the village of Rawdah, these natural hot springs are a soothing retreat. The water is warm and rich in minerals, and the surrounding date palms create a lush oasis. It’s a bit of a hike to reach, but the reward is a relaxing soak in a serene setting. Locals believe the waters have healing properties.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Open 24/7.
How to get there: Drive 20 km from Khasab toward Rawdah, then hike 1 km.
Tip: Bring a towel and change of clothes; there are no facilities.

9. Khasab Marina

The marina is the hub for all water activities, from dhow cruises to fishing trips. It’s a picturesque spot with yachts bobbing in the water and mountains in the background. You can also grab a coffee at the nearby café and watch the boats come and go. It’s a great starting point for your fjord adventures.

Ticket price: Free to walk around; boat rentals start at ₽2 000 (≈ 20 OMR / $52).
Hours: 24/7.
How to get there: A 5-minute walk from the souk, near the fort.
Tip: Book your dhow tour here directly for better prices.

10. Qasr Al Hallab (The Old Fort)

This lesser-known fort in the village of Hallab offers a glimpse into rural Omani life. It’s a small structure made of stone and mud, with a watchtower overlooking the valley. The fort is partially ruined but has a rustic charm. It’s a quiet spot for history lovers away from the crowds.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Always open, but best visited in daylight.
How to get there: Drive 25 km from Khasab toward the village of Hallab.
Tip: Combine this with a visit to the hot springs nearby.

11. Zighy Bay

This exclusive beach resort area features a stunning stretch of white sand and crystal-clear water. It’s a bit pricey, but you can access the beach for a day pass. The views of the mountains meeting the sea are postcard-perfect. It’s ideal for a luxurious day of relaxation.

Ticket price: ₽1 000–₽2 000 (≈ 10–20 OMR / $26–$52) for a day pass at the Six Senses Resort.
Hours: 8:00 AM–6:00 PM.
How to get there: Drive 30 km south of Khasab on the coastal road.
Tip: Book a lunch at the resort for access to the beach.

12. Sunken Graveyard (Wadi Shah)

This unique site features ancient graves carved into the rocks of a dry wadi. The graves are thousands of years old, with distinct stone markings. It’s a hauntingly beautiful place that speaks to the region’s prehistoric past. The wadi itself is a nice spot for a short hike.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Open 24/7.
How to get there: Drive 10 km from Khasab toward the village of Shah.
Tip: Wear sturdy shoes; the terrain is rocky.

13. Snorkeling at Dibba

Just a short drive from Khasab, the waters around Dibba are teeming with marine life. You’ll see colorful coral reefs, parrotfish, and even the occasional octopus. The visibility is excellent, especially in the morning. It’s a top spot for beginner and experienced snorkelers alike.

Ticket price: Free if you bring your own gear; guided tours cost ₽1 500–₽2 500 (≈ 15–25 OMR / $39–$65).
Hours: Best in the morning 8:00 AM–12:00 PM.
How to get there: Drive 20 km south of Khasab to Dibba.
Tip: Book a tour on GetYourGuide for equipment and transport.

14. Khasab Castle Viewpoint

This spot near the fort offers a panoramic view of the entire town, harbor, and surrounding mountains. It’s a popular photo spot, especially at sunset when the golden light hits the cliffs. There’s a small bench where you can sit and soak it all in. It’s free and easily accessible.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: 24/7.
How to get there: Walk up the hill behind the fort (5 minutes).
Tip: Bring a camera—this is the best spot for wide-angle shots.

15. Musandam Pearl Farm

This small farm cultivates natural pearls in the fjords, reviving an ancient Omani tradition. You can take a tour to see how pearls are grown and harvested. The farm also sells pearl jewelry at reasonable prices. It’s a unique cultural experience that supports local artisans.

Ticket price: ₽500 (≈ 5 OMR / $13) for a guided tour.
Hours: Saturday–Thursday 9:00 AM–1:00 PM.
How to get there: Located near the marina; ask for directions at the tourist office.
Tip: Buy pearls directly here—they’re cheaper than in the souk.

1-Day, 2-Day, and 3-Day Itineraries

To help you make the most of your time, here are optimized routes that cover the best attractions without rushing.

1-Day Itinerary

Morning: Start at Khasab Fort (8:00 AM–9:30 AM), then head to the souk for a quick browse (9:30 AM–10:30 AM). Late morning: Take a half-day dolphin-watching cruise (10:30 AM–2:00 PM). Afternoon: Have lunch at a local café near the marina (2:00 PM–3:00 PM), then visit the Khasab Castle Viewpoint (3:00 PM–3:30 PM). Evening: Drive to Zighy Bay for sunset (4:00 PM–6:00 PM).

2-Day Itinerary

Day 1: Follow the 1-day itinerary above. Day 2: Morning: Hike to Jebel Harim (7:00 AM–12:00 PM). Afternoon: Explore Khor Ash Sham by kayak (1:00 PM–4:00 PM). Evening: Relax at the hot springs (4:30 PM–6:00 PM).

3-Day Itinerary

Day 1: Same as day 1 above. Day 2: Full-day Musandam fjords cruise, including Telegraph Island and snorkeling (9:00 AM–4:00 PM). Day 3: Morning: Visit the sunken graveyard and Qasr Al Hallab (8:00 AM–12:00 PM). Afternoon: Snorkel at Dibba (1:00 PM–4:00 PM). Evening: Walk the souk for last-minute souvenirs.

Tours & Excursions

Tours are the easiest way to experience Khasab’s highlights, especially the fjords. Popular options include:

  • Dolphin-watching half-day tour: ₽3 000–₽4 500 (≈ 30–45 OMR / $78–$117) per person—includes snorkeling and refreshments.
  • Full-day fjord cruise: ₽4 000–₽6 000 (≈ 40–60 OMR / $104–$156) per person—includes lunch, drinks, and multiple stops.
  • Private dhow charter: ₽10 000–₽15 000 (≈ 100–150 OMR / $260–$390) for up to 6 people—customizable itinerary.
  • Jebel Harim 4WD tour: ₽5 000–₽7 000 (≈ 50–70 OMR / $130–$182) per vehicle—includes driver and guide.

These tours can be booked in advance on GetYourGuide for convenience.
