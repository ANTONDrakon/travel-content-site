Istanbul is a city that straddles two continents, where the call to prayer echoes across the Bosphorus and the scent of freshly baked simit mingles with the salty sea breeze. Walking through its bustling streets feels like stepping into a living museum — Byzantine mosaics sit alongside Ottoman mosques, and ancient spice markets buzz with the energy of modern cafes.

This is a place where East meets West not just geographically, but in every aspect of life: from the architecture to the cuisine, from the rhythm of daily life to the warmth of its people. Whether you're sipping çay in a hidden alley or watching the sunset over the Galata Tower, Istanbul captivates with its layers of history and vibrant present. Why is 2026 the year to visit? The city is constantly evolving, with new museums, renovated public spaces, and a growing food scene that blends tradition with innovation. You'll find world-class art at the Istanbul Modern, contemporary design in the Karaköy district, and centuries-old hammams that have been lovingly restored. Prices remain reasonable compared to other global capitals, making it an accessible destination for travelers on various budgets.

Plus, the Turkish people are famously hospitable — you'll often be offered tea while shopping in the Grand Bazaar or invited into a local's home for a meal. It's a city that rewards curiosity and invites you to slow down, explore, and get lost in its maze of stories. Beyond the iconic landmarks, Istanbul offers a sensory overload that's hard to replicate. The taste of a perfectly grilled fish sandwich by the Galata Bridge, the sight of whirling dervishes in a centuries-old monastery, the sound of street vendors calling out their wares — these are the moments that stay with you. Whether you're a history buff, a foodie, or a shopaholic, Istanbul has something to offer.

And with its strategic location as a hub between Europe and Asia, it's also a perfect starting point for exploring the rest of Turkey. So pack your walking shoes, bring an appetite, and get ready to fall in love with one of the world's most enchanting cities.

How to Get There

Istanbul is served by two main airports: Istanbul Airport (IST) on the European side and Sabiha Gökçen Airport (SAW) on the Asian side. IST is the primary hub for international flights, while SAW handles many low-cost carriers and domestic routes. From Moscow, a round-trip flight typically costs between ₽25 000 and ₽50 000 (≈ $270–$540), depending on the season and airline.

From London, expect to pay around ₽20 000–₽40 000 (≈ £170–£340), and from New York, prices range from ₽60 000 to ₽100 000 (≈ $650–$1080). Where to Stay

Istanbul's neighborhoods each have their own personality, so choose based on your travel style. Sultanahmet is the historic heart — perfect for first-time visitors who want to be steps from the Hagia Sophia and Blue Mosque. Expect boutique hotels here, with rooms from ₽6 000 to ₽15 000 per night (≈ $65–$160). Karaköy and Galata are trendy and artsy, with rooftop bars and design hotels; mid-range options cost ₽5 000–₽12 000 per night (≈ $55–$130).

For nightlife and shopping, head to Taksim and Beyoğlu, where hostels start at ₽1 500 (≈ $16) and luxury hotels exceed ₽20 000 (≈ $215). On the Asian side, Kadıköy offers a more local vibe with affordable guesthouses from ₽3 000 (≈ $32) per night. Top 15 Attractions

  • Hagia Sophia — A masterpiece of Byzantine architecture, now a mosque and museum. Marvel at the massive dome and stunning mosaics. Ticket: ₽1 200 (≈ $13). Open daily 9:00–17:00 (closed during prayer times).
  • Blue Mosque (Sultan Ahmed Mosque) — Famous for its six minarets and blue Iznik tiles. Free entry, but dress modestly. Open daily 8:30–17:00 (closed during prayers).
  • Topkapi Palace — The opulent residence of Ottoman sultans, with sprawling courtyards and the Harem. Ticket: ₽1 500 (≈ $16). Open Wed–Mon 9:00–17:00 (closed Tuesdays).
  • Basilica Cistern — An underground water reservoir with eerie columns and Medusa heads. Ticket: ₽1 000 (≈ $11). Open daily 9:00–18:00.
  • Grand Bazaar — One of the largest covered markets in the world, with over 4,000 shops. Free entry. Open Mon–Sat 8:30–19:00 (closed Sundays).
  • Spice Bazaar (Egyptian Bazaar) — A fragrant market selling spices, teas, and Turkish delights. Free entry. Open daily 8:00–19:00.
  • Galata Tower — Climb for panoramic views of the city and Bosphorus. Ticket: ₽800 (≈ $9). Open daily 8:30–22:00.
  • Süleymaniye Mosque — A grand Ottoman mosque with a peaceful courtyard and stunning views. Free entry. Open daily 9:00–17:00 (closed during prayers).
  • Dolmabahçe Palace — A lavish 19th-century palace blending Ottoman and European styles. Ticket: ₽1 200 (≈ $13). Open Tue–Sun 9:00–16:00 (closed Mondays).
  • Istanbul Archaeological Museums — Three museums housing artifacts from Greek, Roman, and Ottoman eras. Ticket: ₽800 (≈ $9). Open Tue–Sun 9:00–17:00 (closed Mondays).
  • Chora Church (Kariye Museum) — Famous for its stunning Byzantine mosaics and frescoes. Ticket: ₽1 000 (≈ $11). Open daily 9:00–17:00 (closed Wednesdays).
  • Bosphorus Cruise — A boat ride between Europe and Asia, with views of palaces and fortresses. Ticket: ₽500–₽1 500 (≈ $5–$16) depending on duration. Multiple departures daily.
  • Maiden's Tower — A small tower on an islet in the Bosphorus, with a cafe and museum. Ticket: ₽600 (≈ $7). Open daily 9:00–19:00.
  • Miniatürk — A park with miniature models of Turkey's landmarks. Fun for kids. Ticket: ₽400 (≈ $4). Open daily 9:00–18:00.
  • Rahmi M. Koç Museum — An industrial museum with vintage cars, trains, and ships. Ticket: ₽600 (≈ $7). Open Tue–Sun 10:00–17:00 (closed Mondays).

Getting Around

Istanbul has an extensive public transport network. The metro (lines M1, M2, M3, M4, M6, M7, M8, M9) is fast and efficient — a single ride costs ₽15 (≈ $0.16) with an Istanbulkart (reloadable card, ₽50 for the card itself). Trams (T1 line) are great for tourists, connecting Sultanahmet to Kabataş; same price as metro. Buses and ferries also accept Istanbulkart. Taxis start at ₽20 (≈ $0.22) per km, but beware of scams — always insist on the meter.

For a more comfortable option, use ride-hailing apps like BiTaksi. Car rental is possible but traffic is chaotic; expect ₽1 500–₽3 000 per day (≈ $16–$32) for a compact car. A weekly transport pass (Istanbulkart with 50 rides) costs about ₽750 (≈ $8).

Where to Eat

Turkish cuisine is a highlight of any visit. Start with street food: simit (sesame bread ring) from a cart for ₽15 (≈ $0.16), or a balık ekmek (fish sandwich) by the Galata Bridge for ₽100 (≈ $1). For a sit-down meal, try Çiya Sofrası in Kadıköy for authentic Anatolian dishes (₽500–₽1 000 per person, ≈ $5–$11). Mikla in Beyoğlu offers modern Turkish cuisine with a view — tasting menu from ₽3 000 (≈ $32). Karaköy Güllüoğlu is legendary for baklava (₽150 for a portion, ≈ $1.60).

For a budget meal, a döner wrap from a local shop costs ₽100–₽200 (≈ $1–$2). Don't miss trying çay (Turkish tea, ₽10–₽20, ≈ $0.11–$0.22) and Turkish coffee (₽40–₽80, ≈ $0.43–$0.86). A week's food budget: ₽7 000–₽15 000 (≈ $75–$160) for mid-range dining.

Tours & Excursions

To make the most of your time, consider guided tours. A full-day historical walking tour of Sultanahmet costs around ₽3 000–₽5 000 (≈ $32–$54) and covers Hagia Sophia, Blue Mosque, Topkapi Palace, and Basilica Cistern. A Bosphorus dinner cruise with entertainment is ₽4 000–₽8 000 (≈ $43–$86). For a unique experience, take a day trip to the Princes' Islands (₽1 500–₽3 000, ≈ $16–$32) or a hot air balloon ride in Cappadocia (from ₽15 000, ≈ $160) — book in advance.

For cultural immersion, join a cooking class (₽4 000, ≈ $43) or a whirling dervish ceremony (₽1 500, ≈ $16). You can browse and book a wide range of tours on GetYourGuide, which offers user reviews and flexible cancellation. Book excursions in advance:excursions in Istanbul

Airport Transfer

From Istanbul Airport (IST), the most affordable option is the Havaist bus to Taksim or Sultanahmet for ₽150 (≈ $1.60) — journey takes about 1–1.5 hours. The metro (M11 line) connects IST to the city center for ₽30 (≈ $0.32), but it's a longer ride. A taxi from IST to Sultanahmet costs ₽800–₽1 200 (≈ $9–$13) depending on traffic. From Sabiha Gökçen (SAW), the Havabus to Taksim costs ₽120 (≈ $1.30), and a taxi is around ₽700–₽1 000 (≈ $8–$11).

For a hassle-free door-to-door service, you can pre-book a private transfer on Kiwitaxi — prices start at ₽2 500 (≈ $27) and include meet-and-greet at the airport. Book an airport transfer:airport transfer to Istanbul

Connectivity

Free Wi-Fi is available in most hotels, cafes, and some public areas (like metro stations), but speeds can be slow. For reliable internet, buy a local SIM card from Turkcell, Vodafone, or Türk Telekom — a tourist SIM with 20 GB data costs about ₽800 (≈ $9) and is valid for 30 days. You'll need your passport to register. An even more convenient option is an eSIM, which you can activate before you travel.

Best Time to Visit

Spring (April–May) and autumn (September–October) are ideal — temperatures are mild (15–25°C), crowds are thinner, and hotel prices are moderate. Summer (June–August) is hot (up to 35°C) and crowded, with peak prices; a hotel room might cost 30–50% more. Winter (December–February) is chilly and rainy (5–10°C), but you'll find fewer tourists and lower prices — plus, the city looks magical in the mist. For a balance of good weather and affordability, aim for late April or early October.

Note that Ramadan (dates vary) can affect restaurant hours, but it's a fascinating time to experience iftar (breaking the fast) in public squares.

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens can visit Turkey visa-free for up to 60 days. You need a passport valid for at least 6 months from your entry date. No visa application is required — just show your passport at immigration. For other nationalities, check the Turkish e-Visa website (www.evisa.gov.tr) — most Western passport holders can get a visa online for about $50–$60. Always carry a printed copy of your return flight and hotel booking, as border officers may ask.

Currency & Money

The local currency is the Turkish lira (TRY), symbol ₺. As of 2026, 1 USD ≈ 30 TRY (rates fluctuate). Exchange money at exchange offices (döviz) in the city — rates are better than at airports or hotels. Avoid street changers. Credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) are widely accepted in hotels, restaurants, and shops, but small vendors and taxis prefer cash. Withdraw lira from ATMs (they're everywhere) — your bank may charge a fee.

Bring about ₽5 000–₽10 000 (≈ $54–$108) in cash for a week, depending on your spending. Tipping is appreciated: 10% in restaurants, small change for porters and guides.

Time Zone

Istanbul operates on Turkey Time (TRT), which is UTC+3 year-round. There is no daylight saving time. Compared to Moscow: same time (UTC+3). Compared to London: 2 hours ahead. Compared to New York: 8 hours ahead. Compared to Dubai: 1 hour behind. Keep this in mind when booking flights or scheduling calls.

Travel Insurance

Travel insurance is not mandatory for entering Turkey, but it's highly recommended. It covers medical emergencies (including COVID-19 treatment), trip cancellations, lost luggage, and theft. A basic policy for a week costs around ₽1 500–₽3 000 (≈ $16–$32), while comprehensive coverage is ₽4 000–₽8 000 (≈ $43–$86).
