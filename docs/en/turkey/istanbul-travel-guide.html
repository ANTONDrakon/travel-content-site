Istanbul is a city that straddles two continents, a sprawling metropolis where the echoes of Byzantine emperors and Ottoman sultans blend with the hum of modern life. Walking through its streets, you'll smell the sea breeze from the Bosphorus, hear the call to prayer from a thousand minarets, and taste the sweet, flaky layers of baklava.

It’s a place of magnificent contradictions: ancient bazaars next to sleek shopping malls, quiet tea gardens beside roaring traffic. The atmosphere is electric, chaotic, and deeply welcoming—a sensory overload that somehow feels like home.

Why is 2026 the year to visit? Because Istanbul is more accessible than ever, with new flight routes and a thriving cultural scene. You can explore the grandeur of the Hagia Sophia, lose yourself in the Grand Bazaar. and cruise the Bosphorus Strait—all in a single day.

The city offers incredible value for travelers, with world-class history, food, and hospitality at a fraction of the cost of other European capitals. Whether you’re a history buff, a foodie, or a shopaholic, Istanbul delivers an unforgettable experience that will leave you planning your return before you’ve even left.

How to Get There

Istanbul is served by two major airports: Istanbul Airport (IST) on the European side and Sabiha Gökçen Airport (SAW) on the Asian side. IST is the primary hub for international flights, while SAW handles many budget carriers and domestic routes. From Moscow, a direct round-trip flight with Turkish Airlines or Aeroflot typically costs between ₽35 000 and ₽65 000 (≈ $380–$700).

From London, flights range from ₽12 000 to ₽25 000 (≈ $130–$270), and from Dubai, you can expect prices around ₽15 000 to ₽30 000 (≈ $160–$325). For the best deals, you can always compare options on Aviasales, where you can filter by price and airline to find the most convenient

Where to Stay

Istanbul’s neighborhoods each have a distinct personality. For first-timers, Sultanahmet is ideal—it’s the historic heart, with walking access to the Hagia Sophia, Blue Mosque, and Topkapi Palace. A mid-range hotel here costs about ₽4 500–₽9 000 (≈ $50–$100) per night. For nightlife and dining, Beyoğlu (near Taksim Square and Istiklal Street) offers boutique hotels and lively energy, with prices from ₽3 500–₽7 000 (≈ $40–$75).

Budget travelers love Kadıköy on the Asian side, where hostels start at ₽1 500 (≈ $16) per night. For luxury, consider the Bosphorus waterfront—the Four Seasons or Çırağan Palace can set you back ₽18 000–₽45 000 (≈ $200–$500) per night. Use Hotellook or Booking to compare rates and find the best

Top 15 Attractions

  1. Hagia Sophia – A masterpiece of Byzantine architecture, this former church and mosque is now a museum. Its massive dome and stunning mosaics are breathtaking. Ticket: ₽1 500 (≈ $16). Hours: Daily 9:00 AM–5:00 PM.
  2. Blue Mosque – Famous for its six minarets and blue Iznik tiles, this active mosque is free to enter. Dress modestly and remove shoes. Free entry. Hours: Daily 8:30 AM–6:00 PM (closed during prayer times).
  3. Topkapi Palace – The opulent home of Ottoman sultans, with courtyards, harem, and treasury. Ticket: ₽2 000 (≈ $22) + ₽1 000 (≈ $11) for Harem. Hours: Wed–Mon 9:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  4. Basilica Cistern – An underground water storage from the 6th century, with eerie columns and Medusa heads. Ticket: ₽1 200 (≈ $13). Hours: Daily 9:00 AM–7:00 PM.
  5. Grand Bazaar – One of the world’s largest covered markets, with over 4,000 shops selling carpets, jewelry, spices, and more. Free entry. Hours: Mon–Sat 9:00 AM–7:00 PM.
  6. Spice Bazaar – A smaller, fragrant market near the Galata Bridge, perfect for Turkish delight and saffron. Free entry. Hours: Mon–Sat 8:00 AM–7:30 PM.
  7. Galata Tower – A medieval stone tower offering panoramic views of the city. Ticket: ₽1 000 (≈ $11). Hours: Daily 8:30 AM–10:00 PM.
  8. Dolmabahçe Palace – A lavish 19th-century palace with European-style interiors and a crystal chandelier. Ticket: ₽2 500 (≈ $27). Hours: Tue–Sun 9:00 AM–4:00 PM.
  9. Süleymaniye Mosque – A stunning mosque designed by Mimar Sinan, set on a hill with serene gardens. Free entry. Hours: Daily 9:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  10. Bosphorus Cruise – A boat ride along the strait separating Europe and Asia, passing palaces, fortresses, and bridges. Ticket: ₽500–₽1 500 (≈ $5–$16) depending on duration. Hours: Multiple departures daily.
  11. Chora Church – A small church with exquisite Byzantine mosaics and frescoes. Ticket: ₽1 500 (≈ $16). Hours: Thu–Tue 9:00 AM–5:00 PM.
  12. Taksim Square & Istiklal Street – The modern heart of Istanbul, with shops, cafes, and the nostalgic tram. Free. Hours: Always open.
  13. Rüstem Pasha Mosque – A hidden gem with some of the finest Iznik tiles in the city. Free entry. Hours: Daily 9:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  14. Maiden's Tower – A small tower on an islet in the Bosphorus, with a restaurant and museum. Ticket: ₽1 000 (≈ $11) for ferry + entry. Hours: Daily 9:00 AM–7:00 PM.
  15. Miniatürk – A park with miniature models of Turkey’s famous landmarks. Great for families. Ticket: ₽600 (≈ $6.50). Hours: Daily 9:00 AM–7:00 PM.

Getting Around

Istanbul’s public transport is efficient and affordable. The metro (M1, M2, M4 lines) costs ₽15 (≈ $0.16) per ride with an Istanbulkart (refundable card for ₽70). Buses and trams are the same price. Taxis start at ₽70 (≈ $0.75) and cost about ₽20 per km—be sure the meter is running.

For airport transfers, the Havaist shuttle from IST costs ₽250 (≈ $2.70) to Taksim. You can also rent a car via local agencies for around ₽2 000 (≈ $22) per day, but traffic is heavy. For a hassle-free option, consider booking a private transfer through Kiwitaxi for a fixed price.

Where to Eat

Turkish cuisine is a highlight of any trip. For a budget meal, grab a döner kebab or lahmacun (Turkish pizza) for ₽250–₽500 (≈ $2.70–$5.40). Sit-down restaurants in Sultanahmet serve köfte (meatballs) or pide (Turkish flatbread) for ₽600–₽1 200 (≈ $6.50–$13).

For fine dining, try Mikla or Neolokal for modern Turkish cuisine—a full meal costs ₽3 500–₽7 000 (≈ $38–$75). Don’t miss balık ekmek (fish sandwich) by the Galata Bridge for ₽200 (≈ $2.20). For breakfast, a traditional kahvaltı spread at a café like Van Kahvaltı Evi runs ₽500–₽1 000 (≈ $5.40–$11). Always try çay (black tea) and baklava—they’re essential!

Tours & Excursions

To truly experience Istanbul, consider a guided tour. A half-day walking tour of Sultanahmet costs about ₽3 500 (≈ $38) per person. A full-day Bosphorus cruise with lunch is around ₽5 000 (≈ $54). For a unique experience, a food tour through Kadıköy market runs ₽4 000 (≈ $43). You can also take a day trip to the Princes' Islands (ferry: ₽200) or Gallipoli (₽7 000). Best Time to Visit

The best time to visit Istanbul is spring (April–May) and autumn (September–October), when temperatures are mild (15–25°C) and crowds are smaller. Summer (June–August) is hot and humid (30–35°C), with peak tourist season and higher hotel prices.

Winter (November–February) is chilly (5–10°C) and rainy, but you’ll find fewer tourists and lower prices—hotels can drop by 40%. For a balance of good weather and value, aim for late April or early October. Prices for flights and accommodation are typically 20–30% cheaper in winter compared to summer.

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens can visit Turkey visa-free for up to 60 days. You just need a valid passport (with at least 6 months validity from your entry date). No additional documents are required for tourism. For other nationalities, check the e-Visa system at www.evisa.gov.tr—it’s quick and costs around $50 for most countries. Always carry a printed copy of your flight itinerary and hotel booking.

Currency & Money

The local currency is the Turkish lira (TRY, symbol ₺). As of 2026, 1 USD ≈ 30 TRY. You can exchange money at banks, exchange offices (döviz), or ATMs, which are widely available. Avoid exchanging at airports for poor rates. Credit cards (Visa, Mastercard) are accepted in most hotels, restaurants, and shops, but small vendors and bazaars prefer cash. Bring about ₽10 000–₽15 000 (≈ $110–$160) in cash for a week to cover street food, taxis, and tips. Always have small bills for convenience.

Time Zone

Istanbul is in the Turkey Time Zone (UTC+3) year-round—it doesn’t observe daylight saving time. So, it’s the same as Moscow (no time difference), 2 hours ahead of London, 7 hours ahead of New York, and 1 hour behind Dubai. Adjust your schedule accordingly for flights and tours.

Travel Insurance

Travel insurance is not mandatory for entry to Turkey, but it’s highly recommended. It covers medical emergencies, trip cancellations, lost baggage, and theft. A basic 7-day policy costs about ₽2 500–₽5 000 (≈ $27–$54), while comprehensive plans with COVID-19 coverage are around ₽5 000–₽10 000 (≈ $54–$110).
