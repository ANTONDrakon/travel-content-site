15 Best Things to Do in Kusadasi 2026

Kusadasi has always been a favorite of mine for its unique blend of ancient history, stunning coastline, and vibrant resort energy. Perched on Turkey's Aegean coast, this port city offers more than just sunbathing—it's a gateway to some of the most remarkable archaeological sites in the world, like Ephesus, while also providing modern marina life, bustling bazaars, and pristine beaches.

Whether you're docking from a cruise ship or planning a week-long holiday, Kusadasi's attractions cater to history buffs, foodies, and adventure seekers alike. Let me guide you through the best experiences, with practical advice to make your 2026 trip seamless and memorable.

15 Best Attractions

1. Ephesus Ancient City

Walk in the footsteps of Romans at one of the best-preserved ancient cities in the Mediterranean. The Library of Celsus, the Great Theatre, and the marble streets will transport you back 2,000 years. It's a must-see for anyone interested in history.

Ticket price: ₽1 400 (≈ $15) per person.
Hours: 08:00–19:00 daily (summer), 08:00–17:00 (winter).
How to get there: Take a minibus (dolmuş) from Kusadasi bus station to Selçuk (₽100 / ≈ $1) then a short taxi ride, or join a tour.
Tip: Arrive early (before 09:00) to avoid cruise ship crowds.

2. House of the Virgin Mary

Perched on Mount Koressos, this small stone house is believed to be where Mary spent her final years. It's a peaceful pilgrimage site, visited by popes and travelers seeking spiritual solace.

Ticket price: ₽350 (≈ $4).
Hours: 08:00–18:00 (summer), 08:00–17:00 (winter).
How to get there: Located 7 km from Ephesus; take a taxi (₽700 / ≈ $8) or join a guided tour.
Tip: Bring a scarf for modesty; candles and water are sold at the entrance.

3. Kusadasi Castle (Pigeon Island)

This Byzantine fortress on a small island connected by a causeway offers panoramic views of the marina and bay. Inside, you'll find a small museum and a lovely café.

Ticket price: ₽200 (≈ $2.5).
Hours: 08:00–22:00 (summer), 08:00–17:00 (winter).
How to get there: Walk from the city center (10 minutes) via the causeway.
Tip: Visit at sunset for the best photo opportunities.

4. Ladies Beach (Kadınlar Plajı)

A popular sandy beach with calm waters, perfect for families and solo travelers. It's lined with restaurants, sunbeds, and water sports rentals.

Ticket price: Free (sunbed rental ₽150 / ≈ $2 per day).
Hours: Always open; facilities 08:00–20:00.
How to get there: Take bus #1 from the city center (₽30 / ≈ $0.30).
Tip: Try the fresh grilled fish at one of the beachfront meyhanes.

5. Dilek Peninsula National Park

A stunning nature reserve with hiking trails, secluded beaches (like İçmeler), and wild boars roaming freely. Ideal for a day of swimming and trekking.

Ticket price: ₽250 (≈ $3) per vehicle.
Hours: 07:00–19:00 (summer), 08:00–17:00 (winter).
How to get there: Take a dolmuş from Kusadasi to Güzelçamlı (₽60 / ≈ $0.70), then walk or taxi.
Tip: Bring insect repellent and plenty of water—the trails are steep.

6. Kusadasi Marina

A modern, upscale marina with luxury yachts, chic boutiques, and waterfront restaurants. It's a pleasant evening stroll spot with a lively atmosphere.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: 24/7; shops 10:00–22:00.
How to get there: Walk from the city center (15 minutes).
Tip: Enjoy a cocktail at one of the rooftop bars for sunset views.

7. Aqua Fantasy Aquapark

Turkey's largest water park, with over 30 slides, wave pools, and a lazy river. Perfect for family fun and adrenaline junkies.

Ticket price: ₽2 500 (≈ $30) per person.
Hours: 10:00–18:00 daily (summer only).
How to get there: Free shuttle from city center (check website); taxi ₽400 (≈ $5).
Tip: Buy tickets online via GetYourGuide for a discount and skip queues.

8. Şirince Village

A charming hillside village known for its Ottoman-Greek architecture, fruit wines, and olive oil soap. A peaceful escape from Kusadasi's crowds.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Shops 09:00–19:00.
How to get there: Take a dolmuş from Kusadasi to Selçuk (₽100 / ≈ $1), then a minibus to Şirince (₽40 / ≈ $0.50).
Tip: Sample local wines (especially peach and pomegranate) at the village taverns.

9. Kusadasi Bazaar (Grand Bazaar)

A labyrinth of shops selling carpets, leather, spices, and souvenirs. Haggling is expected and part of the fun.

Ticket price: Free entry.
Hours: 09:00–23:00 (summer), 09:00–20:00 (winter).
How to get there: Located in the city center, near the marina.
Tip: Start bargaining at 50% of the asking price—and walk away if needed.

10. Long Beach (Uzun Plajı)

A quieter, less developed stretch of sand ideal for relaxation and windsurfing. It's about 8 km from the center.

Ticket price: Free (sunbed rental ₽100 / ≈ $1).
Hours: Always open; facilities 08:00–19:00.
How to get there: Take bus #5 from the city center (₽40 / ≈ $0.50).
Tip: Bring your own snacks—fewer restaurants here.

11. Temple of Artemis

One of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, now reduced to a single marble column and a swampy site. It's more about the history than the visuals.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: 24/7.
How to get there: Located in Selçuk, 20 km from Kusadasi; take a dolmuş (₽100 / ≈ $1) then walk 10 minutes.
Tip: Combine with Ephesus visit—it's on the same road.

12. Kusadasi Golf & Spa

A luxury resort with an 18-hole golf course, spa treatments, and Turkish bath. A great option for a rainy day or a splurge.

Ticket price: ₽3 500 (≈ $40) for green fees; spa packages from ₽2 000 (≈ $23).
Hours: 07:00–20:00.
How to get there: Taxi from city center (₽500 / ≈ $6).
Tip: Book spa treatments in advance via Hotellook for better rates.

13. Selçuk Castle

A hilltop fortress offering sweeping views of Selçuk and the surrounding countryside. It's free and little-visited.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: 24/7 (exterior); interior closed for restoration as of 2026.
How to get there: Walk from Selçuk town center (15 minutes uphill).
Tip: Visit at golden hour for stunning photos.

14. Kusadasi Amphitheater

A small Roman-era amphitheater in the city center, often used for concerts and events. Not as grand as Ephesus, but convenient.

Ticket price: Free (event tickets vary).
Hours: Exterior always open; check local listings for events.
How to get there: Walk from the bazaar (5 minutes).
Tip: Check the Kusadasi tourism website for summer concert schedules.

15. Adaland Aquapark

A smaller water park with dolphin shows and slides, ideal for younger children. Less crowded than Aqua Fantasy.

Ticket price: ₽1 800 (≈ $20) per person.
Hours: 10:00–17:00 (summer only).
How to get there: Free shuttle from select hotels; taxi ₽300 (≈ $3.50).
Tip: Book dolphin encounter in advance—it sells out fast.

1-Day, 2-Day, 3-Day Itineraries

1-Day Itinerary

Start at Ephesus (08:00–11:00), then visit House of the Virgin Mary (11:30–12:30). Lunch at a Selçuk kebab house (₽400 / ≈ $5). Afternoon: Kusadasi Castle (14:00–15:30) and a stroll through the bazaar (16:00–17:30). End with sunset at Ladies Beach (18:00–19:00).

2-Day Itinerary

Day 1: Same as above. Day 2: Morning at Dilek Peninsula National Park (08:00–12:00), lunch at Güzelçamlı fish restaurants (₽600 / ≈ $7). Afternoon: Aqua Fantasy Aquapark (14:00–18:00). Evening: Marina walk and dinner (₽1 000 / ≈ $12).

3-Day Itinerary

Day 1 & 2: As above. Day 3: Morning in Şirince Village (09:00–12:00), tasting wines. Lunch there (₽500 / ≈ $6). Afternoon: Selçuk Castle and Temple of Artemis (14:00–16:00). Evening: Turkish bath experience at a local hammam (₽800 / ≈ $9).

Tours & Excursions

Several operators offer guided tours to Ephesus, Pamukkale, and even day trips to Greek islands like Samos (ferry from Kusadasi, ₽4 000 / ≈ $45). A typical Ephesus tour costs ₽2 500–₽4 000 (≈ $28–$45) per person, including transport and guide. Pamukkale tours run ₽3 500 (≈ $40) for a full day. Remember that tours can be booked via GetYourGuide for instant confirmation and free cancellation. For private transfers, check Kiwitaxi for airport pickups.

Book Tickets Online

To save money and time, book attraction tickets online. Ephesus and House of the Virgin Mary tickets can be purchased via the official Turkish museums website (Müzekart) or through Booking.com for bundled deals. Aquapark tickets are cheaper on Agoda or GetYourGuide. Remember that attraction tickets can be booked via Hotellook as part of package deals. Always check cancellation policies—most allow free cancellation up to 24 hours.

Free Attractions

  • Kusadasi Marina – Stroll and window-shop without spending a lira.
  • Ladies Beach – Free entry; bring your own towel.
  • Kusadasi Bazaar – Browsing is free; resist the sales pitch.
  • Temple of Artemis – A historical site with no entry fee.
  • Selçuk Castle – Free panoramic views.
  • Kusadasi Amphitheater – Free to see from outside.
  • Dilek Peninsula – Free if you walk in (no vehicle).

Practical Tips

Transportation

Minibuses (dolmuş) are the cheapest way to get around—routes to Selçuk, Ladies Beach, and Güzelçamlı cost ₽30–₽100 (≈ $0.30–$1). Taxis start at ₽200 (≈ $2.5) for short trips; agree on a price before riding. For airport transfers, pre-book via Kiwitaxi (₽2 000 / ≈ $23 from Izmir Airport). Consider renting a car from Aviasales for flexibility (₽1 500 / ≈ $17 per day).

Combo Tickets

The Müzekart (₽1 200 / ≈ $14) gives you access to Ephesus, Hierapolis, and 300+ Turkish museums for a year. Buy it at any museum ticket booth or online. For Kusadasi-specific savings, look for the "Kusadasi Pass" (₽2 000 / ≈ $23) which includes castle, aquapark, and a boat tour—available at tourist offices.

Other Tips

  • Best time to visit: April–June or September–October for pleasant weather and fewer crowds.
  • Cash vs. Card: Most places accept cards, but carry Turkish Lira for small purchases.
  • Sunscreen: Aegean sun is strong; reapply every two hours.
  • SIM card: Buy a local eSIM from Airalo (₽600 / ≈ $7 for 5GB) to avoid roaming fees.
  • Language: English is widely spoken in tourist areas, but learn a few Turkish phrases like "Teşekkür ederim" (thank you).

I hope this guide helps you plan an unforgettable trip to Kusadasi in 2026. Whether you're exploring ancient ruins or lounging on sandy beaches, this Turkish gem has something for everyone. Safe travels!

A guide is recommended for historical sites and complex routes. Self-guided travel is possible with audio guides and maps.
