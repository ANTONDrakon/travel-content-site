Nha Trang is Vietnam’s coastal gem, a city where turquoise waters meet golden sands and the pace of life slows to the rhythm of the waves. I remember my first morning here, sipping coconut coffee as the sun rose over Hon Chong promontory — the air smelled of salt, grilled seafood, and the faint sweetness of tropical flowers. This isn’t just a beach destination; it’s a place where you can dive among coral reefs one day, explore ancient Cham temples the next. and end with a bowl of bún chả cá under the stars.

Why is 2026 the year to visit? The city has matured beautifully. New beachfront parks, cleaner streets, and a growing food scene make it more than a stopover. You’ll find Russian menus in many restaurants, direct flights from several countries, and a relaxed vibe that appeals to families, couples, and solo travelers alike.

Average temperatures hover around 27°C year-round, and the sea is warm enough to swim in every month. Whether you’re here for the scuba diving (some of the best in Southeast Asia), the mud baths, or simply to read a book on a lounger, Nha Trang delivers without the crowds of Thailand’s islands.

The city has a gritty, real charm too. Motorbikes buzz along Tran Phu Street, vendors sell fresh sugarcane juice on corners, and the night market buzzes with chatter and sizzling grills. It’s not polished like Phuket — and that’s exactly why I love it. You’ll find five-star resorts next to family-run guesthouses, and luxury seafood restaurants beside street stalls selling bánh xèo for ₫20 000 (≈ ₽70). Nha Trang is a place where you can tailor your experience: luxury or backpacker, adventure or relaxation, or a bit of everything.

How to Get There

Cam Ranh International Airport (CXR) is the main gateway, located about 35 km south of Nha Trang city center. Direct flights arrive from Moscow (Aeroflot, Vietnam Airlines), Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, Bangkok, Seoul, and Shanghai.

From Moscow, expect a flight time of about 10 hours with fares starting around ₽45 000 (≈ $480) in low season, rising to ₽70 000 (≈ $750) during peak winter months. From Hanoi, flights cost around ₽5 000 (≈ $55) one-way; from Ho Chi Minh City, about ₽4 000 (≈ $45). You can check current prices and book flights on Aviasales to find the best deals for your dates.

Where to Stay

The city has three main accommodation zones. Tran Phu Beach Road is the heart of the action — high-rise hotels, beachfront resorts, and endless seafood restaurants. Mid-range options here cost ₽3 500–7 000 (≈ $38–75) per night. Nha Trang City Center (around Nguyen Thien Thuat Street) is budget-friendly with backpacker hostels from ₽700 (≈ $7.50) and guesthouses around ₽1 500 (≈ $16).

Long Beach (Bai Dai) is quieter, with luxury resorts like Amiana Resort (₽15 000+ per night, ≈ $160) and more space. I recommend staying near the beach but a block or two back for quieter nights. For a splurge, the Sheraton Nha Trang Hotel & Spa offers ocean-view rooms from ₽8 000 (≈ $85). You can compare prices and book on Hotellook or Booking to find your perfect fit.

Top 15 Attractions

  1. Po Nagar Cham Towers — Ancient Hindu temple complex dating to the 7th–12th centuries, perched on a hill overlooking the Cai River. Entry: ₫30 000 (≈ ₽105). Open 7:30–17:30 daily.
  2. Long Son Pagoda — Iconic white Buddha statue visible from afar. Climb 152 steps for panoramic city views. Free entry. Open 7:00–17:00.
  3. Nha Trang Cathedral — Gothic-style stone church built by French colonists in the 1930s. Free entry. Open 8:00–11:00, 14:00–16:30.
  4. Hon Chong Promontory — Rocky outcrop with strange rock formations and sea views. Entry: ₫22 000 (≈ ₽75). Open 6:00–18:00.
  5. VinWonders Nha Trang — Large amusement park on Hon Tre Island with a water park, aquarium, and roller coasters. Entry: ₫800 000–1 200 000 (≈ ₽2 800–4 200). Open 8:00–20:00.
  6. Nha Trang Beach — The main 6-km stretch of golden sand. Free access. Best for swimming and sunbathing.
  7. Bai Dai Beach (Long Beach) — Quieter, cleaner beach 15 km south of center. Free entry. Great for sunset walks.
  8. Institute of Oceanography — Vietnam’s largest marine research center with a living reef tank and preserved specimens. Entry: ₫40 000 (≈ ₽140). Open 7:00–17:00.
  9. Hon Mun Island — Prime snorkeling and diving spot with vibrant coral. Boat tours start at ₫500 000 (≈ ₽1 750).
  10. Yen Island (Swallow Island) — Protected island known for bird’s nest soup. Tours only via licensed operators, around ₫800 000 (≈ ₽2 800).
  11. Thap Ba Hot Springs — Mud baths, mineral pools, and spa treatments. Entry: ₫200 000–400 000 (≈ ₽700–1 400). Open 7:00–19:00.
  12. Nha Trang Night Market — Evening market on Tran Phu Street with souvenirs, clothes, and street food. Free entry. Open 17:00–23:00.
  13. Dam Market — Indoor market for local produce, dried seafood, and textiles. Free entry. Open 6:00–18:00.
  14. Alexandre Yersin Museum — Small museum dedicated to the Swiss-French doctor who founded Nha Trang’s Pasteur Institute. Entry: ₫30 000 (≈ ₽105). Open 8:00–11:00, 14:00–16:30.
  15. Hon Chong Beach — Rocky beach near the promontory, popular for swimming and photography. Free entry.

Getting Around

Nha Trang is compact and walkable along the beachfront. For longer distances, taxis are the best option — use Mai Linh (green) or Vinasun (white) to avoid scams. Flagfall is ₫12 000 (≈ ₽42) for the first 1.5 km, then ₫15 000/km (≈ ₽52). Grab (Southeast Asia’s Uber) works well; a ride across town costs ₫50 000–80 000 (≈ ₽175–280).

Motorbike rental is popular — expect ₫150 000–200 000 per day (≈ ₽525–700), plus ₫50 000 for fuel. You’ll need an International Driving Permit (IDP) and a helmet. Local buses (route 3, 4, 6) cost ₫7 000 (≈ ₽25) per ride but are infrequent. Cyclos (pedicabs) are touristy — negotiate to ₫30 000–50 000 for a short ride (≈ ₽105–175).

Where to Eat

Nha Trang’s food scene is a seafood lover’s paradise. Bún chả cá (fish cake noodle soup) is a local specialty — try it at Bún Chả Cá Hương (₫35 000, ≈ ₽120). Bánh xèo (crispy pancakes) with shrimp and bean sprouts cost ₫20 000 (≈ ₽70) at street stalls. For a sit-down meal, Lac Canh Restaurant is legendary for its charcoal-grilled beef and squid (₫150 000–250 000 per person, ≈ ₽525–875).

Lanterns Vietnamese Restaurant (Nguyen Thien Thuat) offers set menus from ₫200 000 (≈ ₽700). Street food along Tran Phu at night: grilled scallops with spring onions (₫10 000 each, ≈ ₽35), sugarcane shrimp (₫15 000, ≈ ₽52), and fresh spring rolls (₫10 000, ≈ ₽35). A full meal at a mid-range restaurant costs ₫200 000–400 000 (≈ ₽700–1 400). Budget travelers can eat well for ₫50 000–100 000 (≈ ₽175–350) per meal.

Tours & Excursions

The best day trips include Hon Mun Island snorkeling tours (₫500 000–800 000, ≈ ₽1 750–2 800, including lunch and gear), Ba Ho Waterfalls trekking (₫300 000, ≈ ₽1 050), and Nha Trang City Tour covering Po Nagar, Long Son Pagoda, and Dam Market (₫400 000, ≈ ₽1 400). For a unique experience, book a mud bath and spa day at Thap Ba or I-Resort (₫300 000–600 000, ≈ ₽1 050–2 100). Multi-day tours to Da Lat or Mui Ne are also available. You can browse and book tours on GetYourGuide to find options that fit your interests and budget.

Airport Transfer

Cam Ranh Airport is about a 45-minute drive from Nha Trang center. Taxi from the airport costs ₫300 000–400 000 (≈ ₽1 050–1 400) — use the official taxi counter inside arrivals. Public bus (route 18) runs every 30 minutes from 6:00 to 18:00 and costs ₫50 000 (≈ ₽175). The bus stops at Nha Trang bus station, then you’ll need a taxi to your hotel. For convenience, pre-book a private transfer on Kiwitaxi — prices start around ₽1 500 (≈ $16) for a car, with meet-and-greet service.

Connectivity

Wi-Fi is widespread and free in hotels, cafes, and restaurants — speeds are generally good (20–50 Mbps). For mobile data, local SIM cards from Viettel, Mobifone, or Vinaphone cost ₫50 000–100 000 (≈ ₽175–350) for 7–30 days with 1–4 GB data per day. You can buy them at the airport or any mobile shop. An even easier option is an eSIM — download and activate before you travel. Check out Airalo for Nha Trang eSIM plans starting at $5 (≈ ₽450) for 1 GB/7 days.

Best Time to Visit

Nha Trang has a tropical climate with two main seasons. Dry season (January–August): sunny, low humidity, sea calm — ideal for beach and diving. Temperatures: 25–32°C. Rainy season (September–December): higher rainfall, occasional typhoons, rougher seas. September–November sees the most rain. Prices: high season (December–March, July–August) sees hotel rates 30–50% higher. The best balance is February–May: warm, dry, and fewer crowds. I visited in March and had perfect weather every day.

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens can enter Vietnam visa-free for up to 45 days. Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months from the date of entry. No visa application is needed for stays under 45 days. For longer stays, you can apply for an e-visa (30–90 days) at the Vietnamese immigration website. Always carry a printed copy of your flight itinerary and hotel booking. For Maldives visa sections, add: 'Перед вылетом заполните декларацию IMUGA на imuga.immigration.gov.mv и получите QR-код — без него не посадят на рейс.'

Currency & Money

The local currency is the Vietnamese dong (VND), symbol ₫. Exchange rates (approximate): $1 ≈ ₫25 000, $1 (€1) ≈ ₫27 000, ₽1 ≈ ₫270. Cash is king in Nha Trang — many small shops, street stalls, and taxis don’t accept cards. Exchange money at banks (Vietcombank, BIDV) or gold shops for better rates — avoid airport exchange counters.

ATMs are everywhere; withdrawal fees are ₫30 000–50 000 (≈ ₽105–175) per transaction. Credit cards are accepted at larger hotels, restaurants, and tour operators. Bring about ₫5 000 000–10 000 000 (≈ ₽17 500–35 000) in cash for a week’s spending, depending on your style.

Time Zone

Nha Trang is in UTC+7 (Indochina Time). Time difference: Moscow is 4 hours behind (when it’s 12:00 in Nha Trang, it’s 08:00 in Moscow). Bangkok, Jakarta, and Phnom Penh are in the same time zone. No daylight saving time.
