15 Best Things to Do in Da Nang 2026

Da Nang is one of Vietnam’s most dynamic coastal cities, blending stunning natural landscapes with cultural depth and modern energy. I first visited Da Nang a few years ago, and it immediately won me over with its long sandy beaches, the dramatic Marble Mountains, and the friendly, laid-back vibe. Whether you’re a history buff, a foodie, or just looking for a beach escape, Da Nang delivers. In 2026, the city continues to grow, with new attractions and improved infrastructure, but its heart remains the same: warm, welcoming, and full of surprises. Here are the 15 best things to do in Da Nang, complete with practical tips to make your trip smooth and memorable.

15 Best Attractions

1. My Khe Beach (China Beach)

My Khe Beach is a long stretch of golden sand that’s perfect for swimming, sunbathing, or just strolling at sunrise. The water is calm and clean, making it a favorite for families and solo travelers alike. You’ll find plenty of beachside cafes and restaurants where you can grab a fresh coconut or a bowl of mì Quảng (a local noodle dish).

  • Ticket price: Free
  • Hours: Open 24/7; best visited early morning or late afternoon
  • How to get there: A short taxi or Grab ride from the city center (₽200–₽400, ≈ $2–$4)
  • Tip: Bring sunscreen and a towel; the sun can be intense by 10 a.m.

2. Marble Mountains (Ngũ Hành Sơn)

These five limestone and marble hills are dotted with caves, pagodas, and stunning viewpoints. You can climb to the top for panoramic views of Da Nang and the coast. The most popular mountain, Thủy Sơn, has a network of tunnels and Buddhist shrines to explore.

  • Ticket price: ₽400 (≈ $4) for Thủy Sơn; caves are extra (₽100–₽200, ≈ $1–$2)
  • Hours: 7:00 a.m. – 5:30 p.m.
  • How to get there: Bus #1 from Da Nang (₽50, ≈ $0.50) or taxi (₽500, ≈ $5)
  • Tip: Wear sturdy shoes; the stairs can be steep and slippery.

3. Bà Nà Hills & Golden Bridge

The Golden Bridge, held up by two giant stone hands, is one of the most iconic photo spots in Vietnam. The Bà Nà Hills complex also includes a French-style village, a cable car (one of the longest in the world), and amusement park rides. It’s a bit touristy but undeniably impressive.

  • Ticket price: ₽6 500 (≈ $70) for adults (includes cable car and most attractions)
  • Hours: 7:00 a.m. – 10:00 p.m. (cable car runs until 6:00 p.m.)
  • How to get there: Bus from Da Nang (₽300, ≈ $3) or taxi (₽1 500, ≈ $15)
  • Tip: Go early (by 8 a.m.) to avoid crowds; the cable car queue can be long.

4. Sơn Trà Peninsula (Monkey Mountain)

This nature reserve is home to the endangered red-shanked douc langur, often called the “queen of primates.” You can hike or drive through the lush jungle, visit the Linh Ứng Pagoda with its giant Lady Buddha statue. and enjoy breathtaking views of the coastline.

  • Ticket price: Free (park entry); ₽200 (≈ $2) for parking
  • Hours: Open 24/7; best visited early morning
  • How to get there: Scooter rental (₽500–₽700 per day, ≈ $5–$7) or taxi (₽400, ≈ $4)
  • Tip: Bring binoculars and a camera with a zoom lens for wildlife spotting.

5. Dragon Bridge (Cầu Rồng)

This modern bridge is shaped like a dragon and is a symbol of Da Nang. On weekend nights, the dragon breathes fire and water in a spectacular show. It’s a great spot for photos, especially when illuminated at night.

  • Ticket price: Free
  • Hours: Open 24/7; fire show at 9:00 p.m. on Saturdays and Sundays
  • How to get there: Walk from the city center (10–15 minutes)
  • Tip: Arrive by 8:30 p.m. to get a good viewing spot.

6. Hội An Ancient Town

Just 30 minutes south of Da Nang, Hội An is a UNESCO World Heritage site with well-preserved merchant houses, colorful lanterns, and a charming riverside atmosphere. It’s perfect for a day trip, with plenty of tailors, art galleries, and street food stalls.

  • Ticket price: Free to enter; ₽1 200 (≈ $12) for a combined ticket to 5 attractions
  • Hours: Open 24/7; best visited in the evening when lanterns are lit
  • How to get there: Bus from Da Nang (₽150, ≈ $1.50) or taxi (₽600, ≈ $6)
  • Tip: Take a lantern-making workshop (₽500, ≈ $5) for a unique souvenir.

7. Lady Buddha Statue at Linh Ứng Pagoda

Standing 67 meters tall, this white marble statue is one of the tallest Buddha statues in Vietnam. The pagoda complex is peaceful, with beautiful gardens and sea views. It’s a popular spot for locals to pray and for visitors to soak in the serenity.

  • Ticket price: Free
  • Hours: 6:00 a.m. – 8:00 p.m.
  • How to get there: Taxi from Da Nang (₽300, ≈ $3) or scooter
  • Tip: Dress modestly (cover shoulders and knees) as it’s a religious site.

8. Museum of Cham Sculpture

This museum houses the world’s largest collection of Cham artifacts, including intricate stone carvings from the 4th to 14th centuries. It’s a fascinating glimpse into the ancient Champa civilization that once ruled central Vietnam.

  • Ticket price: ₽600 (≈ $6)
  • Hours: 7:00 a.m. – 5:00 p.m. (closed Mondays)
  • How to get there: Walk from the city center (15 minutes)
  • Tip: Hire an audio guide (₽200, ≈ $2) for deeper context.

9. Bà Nà Hills Cable Car

Even if you skip the Golden Bridge, the cable car ride itself is an attraction. It holds several world records for length and altitude, offering stunning views of the jungle and mountains. The journey takes about 20 minutes each way.

  • Ticket price: Included in Bà Nà Hills entry (₽6 500, ≈ $70)
  • Hours: 7:00 a.m. – 6:00 p.m.
  • How to get there: Same as Bà Nà Hills
  • Tip: Sit on the left side for the best views on the way up.

10. Phạm Văn Đồng Beach

A quieter alternative to My Khe, this beach is popular with locals and offers a more relaxed vibe. It’s great for morning jogs, picnics, and watching fishing boats come in.

  • Ticket price: Free
  • Hours: Open 24/7
  • How to get there: Short taxi ride from the city center (₽150, ≈ $1.50)
  • Tip: Try the grilled seafood sold by vendors on the beach (₽200–₽500, ≈ $2–$5).

11. Helio Night Market

This lively night market is a food lover’s paradise, with dozens of stalls selling Vietnamese street food, seafood, and desserts. There’s also live music and a carnival-like atmosphere. It’s open every night and is a great place to experience local culture.

  • Ticket price: Free entry
  • Hours: 5:00 p.m. – 11:00 p.m.
  • How to get there: Walk from the city center (20 minutes) or taxi (₽100, ≈ $1)
  • Tip: Try the bánh xèo (Vietnamese crepe) and fresh sugarcane juice.

12. Hải Vân Pass

This scenic mountain pass connects Da Nang to Huế and offers one of the most thrilling drives in Vietnam. The road winds through lush hills with ocean views on one side. It’s a must-do for motorcycle enthusiasts.

  • Ticket price: Free (road access)
  • Hours: Open 24/7; best driven during daylight
  • How to get there: Rented scooter (₽500–₽700 per day, ≈ $5–$7) or private car (₽2 000, ≈ $20)
  • Tip: Stop at the summit for photos and a coffee at the small café.

13. Asia Park (Sun World Da Nang Wonders)

An amusement park with a giant Ferris wheel, roller coasters, and cultural zones representing different Asian countries. It’s fun for families and offers great views of the city from the top of the wheel.

  • Ticket price: ₽2 500 (≈ $25) for adults; ₽1 500 (≈ $15) for children
  • Hours: 3:00 p.m. – 10:00 p.m. (weekdays); 10:00 a.m. – 10:00 p.m. (weekends)
  • How to get there: Taxi from the city center (₽150, ≈ $1.50)
  • Tip: Visit in the late afternoon to enjoy the sunset from the Ferris wheel.

14. Mỹ Sơn Sanctuary

Another UNESCO site, Mỹ Sơn is a collection of abandoned Hindu temples built between the 4th and 14th centuries. It’s often called the “Angkor Wat of Vietnam” and is a peaceful, atmospheric place to explore.

  • Ticket price: ₽1 500 (≈ $15)
  • Hours: 6:00 a.m. – 5:00 p.m.
  • How to get there: Bus from Da Nang (₽200, ≈ $2) or organized tour (₽2 000, ≈ $20)
  • Tip: Go early (by 7 a.m.) to beat the heat and crowds.

15. Sông Hàn Bridge (Han River Bridge)

This unique swing bridge rotates 90 degrees to allow ships to pass. It’s a marvel of engineering and a great spot for a riverside walk. At night, the bridge is beautifully lit, and you can see the city skyline reflected in the water.

  • Ticket price: Free
  • Hours: Open 24/7; rotation happens around 1:00 a.m. (check local times)
  • How to get there: Walk from the city center (10 minutes)
  • Tip: Take a river cruise (₽500, ≈ $5) for a different perspective of the bridge.

1-Day, 2-Day, 3-Day Itineraries

1-Day Itinerary: Highlights Express

  • Morning (8:00 a.m. – 12:00 p.m.): Start at Marble Mountains (₽400, ≈ $4). Climb to the top for views, then visit a few caves. Taxi from city center (₽200, ≈ $2).
  • Lunch (12:00 p.m. – 1:00 p.m.): Grab bánh mì at a local stall near the mountains (₽100, ≈ $1).
  • Afternoon (1:00 p.m. – 4:00 p.m.): Head to Museum of Cham Sculpture (₽600, ≈ $6). Walk from lunch spot (15 minutes).
  • Evening (5:00 p.m. – 9:00 p.m.): Walk to Dragon Bridge for the 9:00 p.m. fire show. Then, have dinner at Helio Night Market (₽300–₽500, ≈ $3–$5).

2-Day Itinerary: Beach & Culture

  • Day 1: Follow the 1-day itinerary above.
  • Day 2 (8:00 a.m. – 6:00 p.m.): Take a day trip to Hội An Ancient Town. Bus from Da Nang (₽150, ≈ $1.50). Spend the morning exploring the old town (free entry; ₽1 200 for attractions). Lunch at a riverside restaurant (₽400, ≈ $4). In the afternoon, visit the Japanese Covered Bridge and take a lantern-making workshop (₽500, ≈ $5). Return to Da Nang by 6:00 p.m.

3-Day Itinerary: Full Experience

  • Day 1: Same as 1-day itinerary.
  • Day 2: Bà Nà Hills day trip. Bus from Da Nang (₽300, ≈ $3). Spend the morning at the Golden Bridge and French Village. Lunch at the park (₽800, ≈ $8). Afternoon cable car ride and amusement park. Return by 5:00 p.m.
  • Day 3 (8:00 a.m. – 5:00 p.m.): Rent a scooter (₽500–₽700 per day, ≈ $5–$7) and drive the Hải Vân Pass. Stop at the summit for photos. On the way back, visit Sơn Trà Peninsula for the Lady Buddha statue and monkey spotting. Dinner at My Khe Beach (₽400, ≈ $4).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do attractions cost in Da Nang?

Entrance fees vary: from free attractions to $20-30 for museums. Many venues have discount days.

Do I need a guide in Da Nang?

A guide is recommended for historical sites and complex routes. Self-guided travel is possible with audio guides and maps.
