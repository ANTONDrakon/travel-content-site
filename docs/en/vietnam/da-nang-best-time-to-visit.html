Best Time to Visit Da Nang 2026: Seasons & Weather

Da Nang, Vietnam’s vibrant coastal city, is a year-round destination that offers something for every type of traveler—whether you’re chasing sun-soaked beaches, cultural landmarks, or mouthwatering street food. But to make the most of your trip in 2026, timing is everything.

The city sits in a tropical monsoon zone, with distinct wet and dry seasons that shape your experience. I’ve spent weeks exploring Da Nang’s beaches, temples, and night markets, and I’ll help you navigate the weather so you can plan a trip that feels just right. Let’s dive into the seasons, monthly weather, and insider tips for 2026.

Da Nang enjoys a warm climate year-round, with average temperatures rarely dropping below 20°C (68°F). However, the key difference lies in rainfall and humidity. The dry season (January to August) is ideal for beach lovers and sightseers, while the rainy season (September to December) brings cooler temps and fewer crowds. Here’s a month-by-month breakdown to help you decide.

High Season

Da Nang’s high season runs from March to August, peaking during summer (June–August) when European and Vietnamese tourists flock to the coast. Why? The weather is glorious—think 30°C (86°F) days, clear skies, and calm seas perfect for swimming and snorkeling. This is also when the city hosts major events like the Da Nang International Fireworks Festival (April–June), which draws huge crowds.

Prices during high season can be steep. A mid-range hotel room that costs ₽3 500 (≈ $38) in low season might jump to ₽7 000 (≈ $76) per night. Flights from Moscow or Bangkok similarly spike—expect to pay ₽45 000 (≈ $490) for a round-trip ticket from Southeast Asian hubs. To lock in good rates, I recommend booking hotels early via Hotellook or Booking. For flights, check Aviasales for price alerts. Also, consider using Kiwitaxi for airport transfers to avoid surge pricing on taxis.

If you’re visiting during this period, book accommodations at least 2–3 months in advance, especially for beachfront resorts in My Khe or Non Nuoc. The city buzzes with energy, but it also means crowded beaches and longer queues at attractions like Ba Na Hills.

Low Season

The low season spans September to December, when the northeast monsoon brings heavy rains and occasional typhoons. This is the quietest time to visit Da Nang, with far fewer tourists and significantly lower prices. A beachfront room that costs ₽7 000 (≈ $76) in high season might drop to ₽3 500 (≈ $38) or less. Flight deals are plentiful too—you might snag a round-trip from Moscow for ₽30 000 (≈ $326) via Aviasales.

Pros: You’ll have beaches almost to yourself, and attractions like the Marble Mountains or Son Tra Peninsula feel peaceful. Cons: Rain can be relentless, especially in October and November, with daily downpours that last hours. Swimming is often unsafe due to rough seas and jellyfish. However, if you don’t mind getting wet, you can still enjoy Da Nang’s cafes, museums, and cooking classes. Just pack a rain jacket and waterproof shoes.

For excursions during low season, I suggest indoor activities like visiting the Cham Museum or taking a food tour. You can book these via GetYourGuide—many offer free cancellation if weather turns bad. And for staying connected, grab a local eSIM from Airalo to avoid roaming fees.

Best Time for Beach Vacation

If your dream Da Nang trip involves lounging on golden sands and swimming in turquoise waters, aim for March to August. During these months, the sea is calm, water temperatures hover around 26–30°C (79–86°F), and rainfall is minimal. My Khe Beach—often called “China Beach”—is at its finest in June and July, with gentle waves perfect for families. Non Nuoc Beach, near the Marble Mountains, offers a quieter vibe with the same stunning conditions.

For the best swimming, avoid September–November when strong currents and jellyfish are common. Even in December, the water can be choppy. But if you’re just sunbathing, early spring (March–April) is ideal—less humid, with temperatures around 28°C (82°F).

During beach season, book a hotel with direct beach access via Hotellook or Booking. Prices for a 4-star resort start at ₽7 000 (≈ $76) per night in March, rising to ₽12 000 (≈ $130) in July.

Best Time for Sightseeing

For exploring Da Nang’s cultural and natural wonders—like the Marble Mountains, Son Tra Peninsula, or the Golden Bridge at Ba Na Hills—the most comfortable months are February to April and September to October. Temperatures range from 22–30°C (72–86°F), making hiking and walking pleasant without the oppressive heat of summer.

February to April is particularly lovely: skies are clear, humidity is low, and crowds are thinner than in peak summer. You can climb the 156 stone steps to the top of Marble Mountain’s pagodas without breaking a sweat. September–October, though rainy, offers cooler temps (25–30°C) and lush green landscapes—just carry an umbrella.

For excursions, I recommend booking guided tours via GetYourGuide. A half-day trip to Ba Na Hills costs around ₽4 500 (≈ $49) and includes cable car tickets. For airport transfers to your hotel, use Kiwitaxi—it’s reliable and affordable, starting at ₽2 000 (≈ $22) from Da Nang Airport.

Rainy Season

Da Nang’s rainy season runs from September to December, with the heaviest downpours in October and November. Average precipitation during these months is 300–450 mm, and you can expect rain on 20+ days per month. Typhoons are possible, especially in October, but they usually pass within 24–48 hours. The city is well-prepared, with drainage systems and flood warnings in place.

Is it worth visiting during the rainy season? Absolutely—if you’re on a budget or prefer solitude. The rain often falls in short, intense bursts, leaving afternoons clear for exploring. Plus, hotel prices drop by 40–50%. You can find a clean guesthouse for ₽1 500 (≈ $16) per night. Just avoid outdoor activities like boat tours or hiking in the mountains, as trails can be slippery.

For indoor fun, visit the Da Nang Museum of Cham Sculpture (entry ₽600 ≈ $6.50) or take a Vietnamese cooking class (₽2 500 ≈ $27 via GetYourGuide). And don’t forget to check Airalo for a data eSIM to stay dry while planning your next move.

Events & Festivals

Da Nang’s calendar is packed with cultural events. Here are the major ones by month in 2026:

  • January–February (Tet Nguyen Dan): Vietnamese Lunar New Year. The city erupts with fireworks, parades, and family gatherings. Many shops close for 3–5 days, but it’s a magical time to experience local traditions. Hotels can be booked via Booking well in advance.
  • April–June (Da Nang International Fireworks Festival): A spectacular competition between teams from around the world, held over several weekends. The best views are from the Han River bridge area. Book a riverfront room early—prices start at ₽5 000 (≈ $54) per night.
  • August (Quan The Am Festival): A Buddhist celebration at the Marble Mountains, featuring processions, vegetarian food stalls, and cultural performances. Free entry.
  • September (Mid-Autumn Festival): Colorful lantern displays, mooncakes, and children’s parades. Held at the Da Nang Cathedral and along Bach Dang Street.
  • December (Da Nang Christmas & New Year): The city lights up with decorations, and hotels host festive dinners. Expect higher prices but a lively atmosphere.

For festival dates and tickets, check GetYourGuide for guided experiences.

Conclusion

So, when is the best time to visit Da Nang in 2026? It depends on your priorities:

  • For perfect beach weather: March to August (especially June–July).
  • For sightseeing without heat: February to April or September–October.
  • For budget travel: September to December (rainy but cheap).
  • For festivals: April–June (fireworks) or January–February (Tet).

Personally, I’d pick March or April—the weather is warm but not scorching, the beaches are uncrowded, and prices are reasonable. Flights for these dates can be booked via Aviasales, where you’ll find round-trip tickets from Moscow starting at ₽40 000 (≈ $435) or from Bangkok at ₽12 000 (≈ $130). For hotels, check Hotellook or Booking for early deals. And don’t forget to grab an eSIM from Airalo to stay connected from the moment you land.

Da Nang in 2026 awaits—pack your sunscreen for sunny days or a raincoat for cozy adventures. Either way, you’ll fall in love with this city’s charm, food, and friendly locals. Safe travels!
