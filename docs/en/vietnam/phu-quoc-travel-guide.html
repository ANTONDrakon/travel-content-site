Imagine an island where emerald jungles meet powder-white beaches, where the fish sauce is legendary, and where the pace of life slows to the rhythm of the tide. That’s Phu Quoc, Vietnam’s largest island, a place that feels like a secret paradise even though it’s increasingly on travelers’ radars.

In 2026, Phu Quoc is more accessible than ever, with a new international airport, world-class resorts, and a laid-back charm that’s hard to resist. Whether you’re here for the sunsets over the Gulf of Thailand, the buzzing night markets, or the untouched national park, this island offers a perfect blend of relaxation and adventure.

The atmosphere on Phu Quoc is a delightful mix of rustic and refined. On the west coast, you’ll find the main town of Duong Dong, where fishing boats bob in the harbor and the smell of grilled seafood wafts through the air. Drive just 20 minutes south, and you’re in a different world—luxury resorts, infinity pools, and private beaches.

What makes Phu Quoc special is its raw, natural beauty: the island is still 70% national park, with trails through dense forests leading to hidden waterfalls. It’s a place where you can spend the morning trekking and the afternoon sipping coconut water on a hammock. If you’re looking for a destination that’s both exotic and easy to love, Phu Quoc in 2026 is it.

What draws travelers here? Because the infrastructure has caught up with the dream. The new international terminal at Phu Quoc Airport (PQC) handles direct flights from major Asian hubs, and the road network is improving, making it easier to explore. Yet, the island hasn’t lost its soul.

You can still find empty beaches if you know where to look, and the local markets remain authentic. Plus, 2026 sees the completion of several eco-resorts and conservation projects, so your visit supports sustainability. Whether you’re a solo backpacker, a couple on a honeymoon, or a family with kids, Phu Quoc welcomes you with open arms—and a bowl of steaming pho.

How to Get There

Phu Quoc International Airport (PQC) is your gateway. From Moscow, you can fly with a layover in Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City (SGN) via Aeroflot or Vietnam Airlines. Direct flights from Bangkok, Kuala Lumpur, and Singapore are also available, taking about 1.5–2 hours. Average round-trip prices from Moscow start at ₽45 000 (≈ $490), while from Ho Chi Minh City, it’s around ₽8 000 (≈ $87). From Bangkok, expect ₽6 500 (≈ 2 600 THB). Where to Stay

Phu Quoc offers neighborhoods for every vibe. Duong Dong (the main town) is perfect for budget travelers and foodies—guesthouses start at ₽1 200 (≈ $13) per night, and you’re steps from the night market. Long Beach (southwest coast) is the sweet spot: mid-range resorts like Seashells Hotel cost ₽3 500–₽5 500 (≈ $38–$60) per night, with beach access and restaurants.

For luxury, head to Ong Lang Beach (northwest), where high-end villas at places like JW Marriott Phu Quoc start at ₽15 000 (≈ $163) per night. Ham Ninh (east coast) is quieter, with fishing villages and affordable homestays around ₽1 800 (≈ $20). To compare prices across these areas, use

Top 15 Attractions

  1. Sao Beach — A crescent of white sand and turquoise water, perfect for swimming and sunbathing. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  2. Phu Quoc National Park — A UNESCO Biosphere Reserve with hiking trails, waterfalls, and wildlife. Entry: ₽40 000 (≈ $1.70). Open 7 AM–5 PM.
  3. Duong Dong Night Market — A bustling market for seafood, souvenirs, and street food. Free entry. Open 5 PM–midnight.
  4. VinWonders Phu Quoc — A massive theme park with roller coasters, a water park, and a zoo. Tickets: ₽650 000 (≈ $28). Open 9 AM–8 PM.
  5. Fish Sauce Factory — Learn how Phu Quoc’s famous fish sauce is made. Free entry (donations welcome). Open 8 AM–5 PM.
  6. Ho Quoc Pagoda — A stunning Buddhist temple with ocean views. Free entry. Open 6 AM–6 PM.
  7. Phu Quoc Pearl Farm — See how pearls are cultivated and buy jewelry. Free entry. Open 8 AM–5 PM.
  8. Rach Vem Floating Village — A village on stilts with seafood restaurants and kayaking. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  9. Cau Temple — A historic temple at the mouth of the Duong Dong River, perfect for sunset. Free entry. Open 6 AM–8 PM.
  10. Bai Thom Beach — A secluded beach on the northeast coast, ideal for relaxation. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  11. Suoi Tranh Waterfall — A gentle waterfall and natural pool in the national park. Entry: ₽30 000 (≈ $1.30). Open 7 AM–5 PM.
  12. Phu Quoc Prison Museum — A historical site documenting the war. Entry: ₽10 000 (≈ $0.43). Open 7:30 AM–5 PM.
  13. Hon Thom Island — Accessible by cable car (the world’s longest overwater cable car). Round trip: ₽500 000 (≈ $22). Open 8 AM–5 PM.
  14. Dinh Cau Rock — A rocky outcrop with a lighthouse, great for sunsets. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  15. Ham Ninh Fishing Village — A traditional village with seafood markets and boat tours. Free entry. Open 6 AM–8 PM.

Getting Around

Phu Quoc has no public buses or metro. The best way to explore is by motorbike rental (₽150 000–₽250 000 per day, ≈ $1.60–$2.70). Taxis are available but pricey—short trips cost ₽50 000–₽100 000 (≈ $0.54–$1.10). For convenience, use Grab (Southeast Asia’s Uber) for fixed fares. If you’re not comfortable on two wheels, hire a private car and driver for around ₽800 000 (≈ $8.70) per day. Bicycles are also rentable for ₽50 000 (≈ $0.54) per day, but the island is large, so plan accordingly.

Where to Eat

Phu Quoc is a food lover’s paradise. For street food, head to Duong Dong Night Market where grilled squid, crab, and scallops cost ₽30 000–₽80 000 (≈ $0.32–$0.87) per dish. Bun Quay Phu Quoc (noodle soup) is a local specialty, found at stalls for ₽25 000 (≈ $0.27).

For sit-down meals, The Spice House at Long Beach offers Vietnamese classics for ₽150 000–₽300 000 (≈ $1.60–$3.25). Mid-range restaurants like Pepper Tree serve seafood platters for ₽400 000–₽600 000 (≈ $4.30–$6.50). A full meal with drinks averages ₽200 000–₽400 000 (≈ $2.20–$4.30) per person.

Best Time to Visit

Phu Quoc has two seasons: dry (November to April) and wet (May to October). The best time is December to March, when skies are blue, seas are calm, and temperatures average 28°C (82°F). This is peak season, so prices are higher. April–May is still good with fewer crowds. The wet season (June–October) brings rain and rougher seas, but also lower prices (hotels drop by 30–50%). July–August is the wettest, but you’ll find sunny mornings and afternoon downpours.

Visa & Documents

For Russian citizens, Phu Quoc offers a special visa exemption: you can stay without a visa for up to 45 days. Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months from entry. For longer stays or other nationalities, check with the Vietnamese embassy. No visa is needed for most Western countries for stays under 15 days. Currency & Money

The local currency is the Vietnamese dong (VND), symbol ₫. Exchange rates in 2026: 1 USD ≈ 23 500 VND. Exchange money at banks or gold shops (better rates than hotels). ATMs are widely available in Duong Dong and Long Beach, with a fee of ₽20 000–₽30 000 (≈ $0.22–$0.32) per withdrawal. Credit cards are accepted in major hotels, resorts, and upscale restaurants, but street food and markets are cash-only. Bring ₽5 000 000–₽10 000 000 (≈ $54–$108) in cash for a week, depending on your spending.

Time Zone

Phu Quoc is in UTC+7 (Indochina Time). It’s 4 hours ahead of Moscow (UTC+3), 1 hour behind Bangkok (UTC+8), and 12 hours ahead of New York (UTC-5). No daylight saving time.

10 Travel Tips

  1. Learn basic Vietnamese phrases — “Xin chào” (hello) and “Cảm ơn” (thank you) go a long way.
  2. Bargain at markets — Start at 50% of the asking price for souvenirs and clothes.
  3. Protect against mosquitoes — Use repellent, especially at dawn and dusk, to avoid dengue.
  4. Drink bottled water — Tap water is not safe. Buy large bottles at supermarkets.
  5. Rent a motorbike — It’s the best way to explore, but always wear a helmet and check the brakes.
  6. Visit during the dry season — November to April offers the best weather for beaches and activities.
  7. Try the local fish sauce — Phu Quoc’s fish sauce is world-famous; buy a bottle at the factory.
  8. Bring cash for street food — Most stalls don’t accept cards, and ATMs can be rare.
  9. Respect the environment — Don’t litter on beaches or in the national park; use reef-safe sunscreen.
