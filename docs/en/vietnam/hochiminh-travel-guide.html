Welcome to Ho Chi Minh City (HCMC), a vibrant metropolis where the energy of Vietnam's past and future collide in a whirlwind of neon lights, aromatic street food, and the constant hum of motorbikes. Formerly Saigon, this city of over 9 million people is the country's economic heart, pulsing with a relentless, infectious optimism. You'll find French colonial boulevards shaded by ancient trees, sleek skyscrapers piercing the sky, and narrow alleyways called hẻm hiding family-run pho stalls that have been simmering for generations.

Why is 2026 the year to visit? The city is in a constant state of thrilling evolution. The new metro line is beginning to reshape how locals and tourists navigate the city, while the food scene continues to earn global acclaim.

It’s a place where you can sip a ₫50 000 (≈ $2) iced coffee from a plastic stool on the sidewalk one moment. and dine at a world-class restaurant for ₫1 500 000 (≈ $60) the next. From the sobering history of the War Remnants Museum to the dazzling rooftop bars overlooking the Saigon River, HCMC offers a deeply authentic and unforgettable Asian adventure.

Whether you're a backpacker looking for cheap eats and bustling hostels, or a luxury traveler seeking five-star spas and private tours, Ho Chi Minh City welcomes you with open arms and a steaming bowl of noodles. This guide will give you all the practical details, honest prices, and local secrets you need to plan the perfect trip in 2026.

How to Get There

Your gateway is Tan Son Nhat International Airport (SGN), located just 8 km from the city center. It's one of Southeast Asia's busiest hubs, with direct flights from major cities worldwide. From Moscow (SVO), a direct flight with Aeroflot or Vietnam Airlines typically takes about 10 hours. From Bangkok, it's a short 1.5-hour hop. From London or Paris, expect flights with one stopover (often in Doha, Dubai, or Bangkok) to take around 12-15 hours.

Average round-trip ticket prices from Moscow in economy class start around ₽45 000 (≈ $490) during low season, rising to ₽75 000 (≈ $815) around New Year. From European capitals, expect to pay between ₽60 000 and ₽100 000 (≈ $650–$1085). To find the best deals on these routes, it's wise to use a flight aggregator. Where to Stay

Ho Chi Minh City offers a fantastic range of neighborhoods. For first-timers, District 1 is the heart of the action, with the most attractions, restaurants, and nightlife. You'll pay a premium here. For a more local, budget-friendly vibe with incredible street food, head to District 3 or District 4. District 2 (Thao Dien) is the expat hub, offering quiet streets, international schools, and upscale villas—perfect for families or those seeking a calmer base.

  • Budget Hostel: The Common Room Project (District 1) — Dorm beds from ₫250 000 (≈ $10) per night.
  • Mid-Range Hotel: Liberty Central Saigon Citypoint (District 1) — Double rooms from ₫1 800 000 (≈ $75) per night.
  • Luxury Hotel: Park Hyatt Saigon (District 1) — Suites from ₫8 000 000 (≈ $330) per night.

Top 15 Attractions

  1. War Remnants Museum — A sobering and essential visit. Three floors of exhibits documenting the Vietnam War (American War). Allow 2 hours. Ticket: ₫40 000 (≈ $1.60). Open daily 07:30–12:00 & 13:30–17:00.
  2. Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon — A beautiful red-brick French colonial cathedral in the heart of District 1. Currently under renovation but still worth seeing from outside. Free. Always accessible.
  3. Saigon Central Post Office — A stunningly preserved colonial building right next to the cathedral. Step inside to see the arched ceiling and giant portrait of Ho Chi Minh. Free. Open daily 07:00–19:00.
  4. Ben Thanh Market — The iconic, chaotic market. Great for souvenirs, clothes, and local snacks. Haggle hard! Free entry. Open daily 06:00–18:00 (night market outside from 18:00–23:00).
  5. Bitexco Financial Tower & Saigon Skydeck — For panoramic city views from the 49th floor. Ticket: ₫200 000 (≈ $8.50). Open 09:30–21:30.
  6. Nguyen Hue Walking Street — A wide, pedestrian-only boulevard in District 1, perfect for an evening stroll. Lined with fountains, street performers, and fancy cafes. Free. 24/7.
  7. Jade Emperor Pagoda — A stunningly ornate Taoist temple built in 1909. Full of intricate carvings and smoky incense. Free (donations welcome). Open 07:00–18:00.
  8. Cu Chi Tunnels — A vast underground tunnel network used by Viet Cong soldiers. A half-day trip from the city. Tickets (for the tunnels) range from ₫110 000 to ₫200 000 (≈ $4.50–$8.50), but most people book a tour.
  9. Museum of Ho Chi Minh City — Housed in a beautiful neoclassical building, this museum covers the city's history from ancient times to the present. Ticket: ₫30 000 (≈ $1.20). Open 08:00–17:00.
  10. Saigon Opera House (Municipal Theatre) — A gorgeous French colonial building. You can see a show (ballet, traditional music, or AO Show) or just admire the architecture. Show tickets from ₫500 000 (≈ $20).
  11. Binh Tay Market (Cholon) — The main market in the Chinatown district (District 5). More authentic and less touristy than Ben Thanh. Great for wholesale goods. Free entry. Open 06:00–18:00.
  12. Thien Hau Temple — A beautiful Chinese-style temple in Cholon dedicated to the sea goddess Mazu. Free. Open 08:00–16:30.
  13. Saigon Zoo and Botanical Gardens — One of the world's oldest zoos, established in 1865. A nice green escape. Ticket: ₫60 000 (≈ $2.50). Open 07:00–17:30.
  14. Independence Palace (Reunification Palace) — The historic site where the Vietnam War ended in 1975. You can walk through the preserved rooms and war command center. Ticket: ₫65 000 (≈ $2.70). Open 08:00–11:30 & 13:00–16:30.
  15. Suoi Tien Theme Park — A unique, quirky Buddhist-themed amusement park on the outskirts. A fun, bizarre day out. Ticket: ₫100 000 (≈ $4.10) for the park, extra for rides. Open 07:00–17:00.

Getting Around

HCMC's traffic is legendary chaos, but you'll get the hang of it. The new Metro Line 1 (Ben Thanh – Suoi Tien) finally opened in 2024! It's clean, fast, and air-conditioned. Tickets: ₫7 000–₫20 000 (≈ $0.30–$0.85) depending on distance. Buses are cheap (₫5 000–₫7 000 per ride) but can be slow and confusing for first-timers. Taxis are reliable if you use the right companies: Vinasun or Mai Linh.

Metered fares start at about ₫12 000. Avoid unmarked taxis. Ride-hailing apps like Grab (similar to Uber) are a lifesaver. Download it before you go. A typical short trip in District 1 costs ₫25 000–₫50 000 (≈ $1–$2). Motorbike taxis (xe om) via Grab are even cheaper. You can rent a motorbike for ₫150 000–₫200 000 (≈ $6–$8) per day, but only if you're a confident rider—traffic rules are more like suggestions here.

Where to Eat

Eating is the main event in HCMC. Street food is king. Look for a vendor with a long queue of locals. A bowl of pho costs ₫40 000–₫80 000 (≈ $1.60–$3.30). A bánh mì (Vietnamese baguette sandwich) is the ultimate cheap eat: ₫25 000–₫50 000 (≈ $1–$2). Bún chả (grilled pork with noodles) is another must-try.

For a sit-down meal, District 1's backpacker area (Bui Vien Street) has countless cheap restaurants. For a mid-range experience, try Secret Garden on Nguyen Hue Street (set dinner from ₫350 000). For fine dining, Anan Saigon (Michelin-starred) offers a tasting menu for ₫2 500 000 (≈ $100). Don't forget to try cà phê sữa đá (iced coffee with condensed milk) for about ₫25 000–₫40 000.

Tours & Excursions

The most popular day trip is the Cu Chi Tunnels, often combined with a visit to the Cao Dai Temple in Tay Ninh. These half-day tours start from ₫400 000 (≈ $16) for a group tour. For a different experience, take a Mekong Delta tour, where you'll cruise through floating markets, visit fruit orchards, and sample coconut candy.

Full-day tours start from ₫800 000 (≈ $33). Other great options include a street food tour by scooter (₫600 000–₫1 000 000 per person) or a cyclo tour of the city's back alleys. To see all available options, read reviews, and book your spot in advance, check out

Airport Transfer

Tan Son Nhat Airport is very close to the city center (about 20-45 minutes by car, depending on traffic). The cheapest option is public bus #109 which runs to the city center for ₫20 000 (≈ $0.80). A taxi from a reputable company like Vinasun or Mai Linh will cost around ₫100 000–₫150 000 (≈ $4–$6) to District 1. Always use the official taxi queue outside the arrivals hall.

For the most comfortable and stress-free option, pre-book a private transfer. A driver will meet you at the arrivals gate with a sign, help with luggage, and get you to your hotel. This is especially good for late-night arrivals or groups. A convenient private transfer can be booked in advance on

Connectivity

Most hotels, cafes, and restaurants in the city offer free Wi-Fi, which is generally fast and reliable. For constant connectivity on the go, you have two options. You can buy a local SIM card at the airport or at any mobile phone shop. The main providers are Viettel, Mobifone, and Vinaphone.

A 30-day data SIM with 30-50 GB costs around ₫100 000 (≈ $4). Alternatively, the easiest option for short trips is an eSIM. You can purchase and activate it online before your trip, so you have data the moment you land. An eSIM for your trip can be found and purchased on

Best Time to Visit

HCMC has a tropical climate with two main seasons: dry season (December–April) and wet season (May–November). The best time to visit is during the dry season, specifically from December to February, when temperatures are slightly cooler (25-32°C) and humidity is lower. This is also peak tourist season, so prices for flights and hotels are higher.

March and April are hot and dry, with temperatures reaching 38°C. The wet season means short, heavy downpours (usually in the afternoon), but it's also the low season, offering great deals on accommodation. It rarely rains all day, and the city stays green and lush. September and October are the wettest months.

Visa & Documents

Good news for Russian citizens: you can enter Vietnam visa-free for up to 45 days. Your passport must be valid for at least six months from your date of entry. You'll need a return or onward ticket. For longer stays, you can apply for an e-visa (90 days, single or multiple entry) online before you travel. Citizens of many other countries (including the US, UK, and most of the EU) also get a 45-day visa exemption. Always double-check the latest requirements with the Vietnamese embassy in your country before you travel.

Currency & Money

The local currency is the Vietnamese Dong (VND), symbol ₫. The exchange rate is roughly ₫23 000 to 1 USD. You'll see prices in thousands, like "50.000 đ" meaning ₫50 000. Cash is king in Vietnam, especially for street food, small shops, and taxis. Bring some USD or EUR to exchange at the airport or at gold shops and jewelry stores in the city (they often give better rates than banks).

ATMs are everywhere, but be aware of fees (usually ₫30 000–₫50 000 per withdrawal). Credit cards are accepted in mid-range and upscale hotels, restaurants, and larger stores, but not everywhere. It's wise to carry a mix of cash and card. For a week, bring about ₫5 000 000–₫10 000 000 (≈ $215–$430) in cash for daily expenses.

Time Zone

Ho Chi Minh City is in the Indochina Time Zone (ICT), UTC+7. It does not observe daylight saving time. The time difference from Moscow is +4 hours (when Moscow is 12:00, HCMC is 16:00). From London, it's +7 hours. From Bangkok, it's the same time. From Tokyo, it's -2 hours.
