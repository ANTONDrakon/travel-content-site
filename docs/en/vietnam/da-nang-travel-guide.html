Welcome to Da Nang, Vietnam’s most livable city and a rising star on Southeast Asia’s travel map. Nestled between the ancient charm of Hoi An and the imperial grandeur of Hue, Da Nang offers a perfect blend of modern energy, stunning natural beauty, and authentic Vietnamese culture. With its golden sandy beaches stretching for miles, the iconic Dragon Bridge breathing fire on weekend nights, and the misty Marble Mountains rising from the coast, this city feels like a dream you didn’t know you were dreaming.

Why visit Da Nang in 2026? Because it’s affordable, safe, and incredibly diverse. You can spend your morning hiking through the lush Son Tra Peninsula, your afternoon lounging on My Khe Beach (voted one of the world’s most beautiful). and your evening feasting on fresh seafood at a beachfront restaurant for less than ₽1 500 (≈ $16). The city is also a gateway to three UNESCO World Heritage Sites: Hoi An Ancient Town, My Son Sanctuary, and the Imperial Citadel of Hue. Da Nang’s international airport connects you to major Asian hubs, and the local hospitality is warm and genuine.

Whether you’re a budget backpacker, a digital nomad, or a luxury seeker, Da Nang welcomes you with open arms. The city’s infrastructure is modern, with wide boulevards, clean beaches, and a growing food scene that blends traditional Vietnamese flavors with international influences. In 2026, Da Nang continues to evolve, but its soul remains rooted in the rhythm of the Han River and the smile of its people.

How to Get There

Da Nang International Airport (DAD) is your gateway, located just 3 km from the city center. Direct flights connect Da Nang to major cities like Bangkok (₽8 000–₽15 000, ≈ $85–$160), Singapore (₽10 000–₽18 000, ≈ $105–$190), Seoul (₽15 000–₽25 000, ≈ $160–$265), and Kuala Lumpur (₽7 000–₽12 000, ≈ $75–$130). Where to Stay

Da Nang’s neighborhoods cater to every traveler. My Khe Beach is the most popular area, with high-rise hotels and beachfront resorts. For luxury, the InterContinental Danang Sun Peninsula Resort (₽25 000–₽50 000/night, ≈ $265–$530) offers world-class service. Mid-range options like Premier Village Danang Resort (₽8 000–₽15 000/night, ≈ $85–$160) are great for families. Budget travelers love Funtastic Beach Hostel (₽600–₽1 200/night, ≈ $6–$13). The Han River area is perfect for nightlife, with hotels like Novotel Danang Premier Han River (₽10 000–₽18 000/night, ≈ $105–$190). Top 15 Attractions

  • 1. My Khe Beach – A 20-km stretch of white sand and gentle waves. Free entry, open 24/7. Best for sunrise swimming.
  • 2. Marble Mountains – Five limestone hills with caves, pagodas, and panoramic views. Entry: ₽40 000 (≈ $1.70). Open 7:00–17:30.
  • 3. Son Tra Peninsula (Monkey Mountain) – Home to the Linh Ung Pagoda and a 67-m tall Lady Buddha statue. Free entry, open 6:00–18:00. Watch for wild monkeys.
  • 4. Dragon Bridge – A 666-m long steel dragon that breathes fire and water on Saturday and Sunday at 21:00. Free to view.
  • 5. Ba Na Hills – A hill station with the famous Golden Bridge held by giant stone hands. Cable car: ₽750 000 (≈ $32). Open 7:30–18:00.
  • 6. Han River Bridge – A unique swing bridge that rotates 90 degrees at midnight. Free to watch.
  • 7. Museum of Cham Sculpture – Houses the world’s largest collection of Cham artifacts. Entry: ₽60 000 (≈ $2.50). Open 7:00–17:00.
  • 8. Da Nang Cathedral – A pink Gothic church built in 1923. Free entry. Open 6:00–11:00, 14:00–17:00.
  • 9. Hai Van Pass – A scenic mountain road with ocean views. Best explored by motorbike or taxi (₽1 500 000, ≈ $64 for a private car). Free to drive.
  • 10. Non Nuoc Beach – A quieter, less developed beach near Marble Mountains. Free entry.
  • 11. Asia Park (Sun World Danang Wonders) – An amusement park with a 115-m tall Sun Wheel. Entry: ₽200 000 (≈ $8.50). Open 15:00–22:00.
  • 12. Phap Lam Pagoda – A serene Buddhist temple with intricate carvings. Free entry. Open 6:00–18:00.
  • 13. Da Nang Museum – Learn about the city’s history from prehistoric times to today. Entry: ₽20 000 (≈ $0.85). Open 7:30–17:00.
  • 14. Tien Sa Port – A scenic harbor with views of the East Sea. Free entry. Best visited at sunset.
  • 15. Hoa Khuong Lake – A peaceful reservoir 20 km west of the city. Popular for picnics and birdwatching. Free entry.

Getting Around

Da Nang doesn’t have a metro, but it’s easy to navigate. Taxis (Mai Linh, Vinasun) start at ₽12 000 (≈ $0.13) per km. Grab (Southeast Asia’s Uber) is cheaper: ₽15 000–₽30 000 (≈ $0.16–$0.32) for short rides. Public buses cost ₽5 000–₽10 000 (≈ $0.05–$0.11) per trip. Motorbike rental is the most popular option: ₽120 000–₽200 000/day (≈ $5–$8.50), plus ₽50 000 (≈ $2.10) for fuel. Bicycle rental is ₽50 000–₽80 000/day (≈ $2.10–$3.40). For airport transfers, book a Kiwitaxi from ₽1 000 (≈ $11) for a private car.

Where to Eat

Da Nang is a food lover’s paradise. Street food is king: try mì Quảng (turmeric noodles with pork and herbs) for ₽25 000–₽40 000 (≈ $1.05–$1.70), bánh xèo (crispy pancakes) for ₽20 000–₽35 000 (≈ $0.85–$1.50), and bún chả cá (fish cake noodle soup) for ₽30 000–₽50 000 (≈ $1.30–$2.10). Mid-range restaurants like Madame Lan (₽300 000–₽600 000, ≈ $13–$26) serve refined Vietnamese cuisine. Seafood at Be Man on My Khe Beach costs ₽200 000–₽500 000 (≈ $8.50–$21) for a feast of clams, prawns, and fish. International options include Italian at Waterfront (₽400 000–₽800 000, ≈ $17–$34) and Korean BBQ at Gogi House (₽300 000–₽600 000, ≈ $13–$26). Budget travelers can survive on ₽150 000–₽250 000/day (≈ $6.40–$10.60).

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens enjoy visa-free entry to Vietnam for up to 45 days (since 2023). Your passport must be valid for at least 6 months beyond your arrival date. No visa is needed for stays under 45 days. For longer trips, apply for an e-visa (₽1 500, ≈ $16) at evisa.xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn. Upon arrival, you’ll fill out a simple immigration form. No onward ticket is usually required, but it’s wise to have one. 10 Travel Tips

Don't forget travel insurance!

get travel insurance
  1. Learn a few Vietnamese phrases: “Xin chào” (hello), “Cảm ơn” (thank you), and “Bao nhiêu?” (how much?) go a long way.
  2. Use Grab for taxis: Avoid overcharging. Always agree on a price or use the app.
  3. Bargain respectfully: In markets, start at 50% of the asking price, but smile.
  4. Stay hydrated: Da Nang is hot and humid. Carry a reusable water bottle (tap water is not safe, but filtered water is widely available for ₽5 000, ≈ $0.05).
  5. Protect against mosquitoes: Dengue fever is present. Use repellent, especially at dawn and dusk.
  6. Try street food from busy stalls: The more locals, the better (and safer) the food.

  8. Get an eSIM for connectivity: Airalo offers Vietnam eSIMs from ₽500 (≈ $5.30) for 7 days of data.
  9. Respect local customs: Remove shoes before entering homes and temples. Dress modestly at religious sites.
  10. Plan for cash: While cards are accepted in hotels and larger restaurants, street food and markets are cash-only. ATMs charge ₽30 000–₽50 000 (≈ $1.30–$2.10) per withdrawal.
