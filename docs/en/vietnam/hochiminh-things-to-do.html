15 Best Things to Do in Ho Chi Minh City 2026

Welcome to Ho Chi Minh City (formerly Saigon), a vibrant metropolis where ancient pagodas stand alongside gleaming skyscrapers, and the aroma of street food fills every corner. As someone who has wandered its bustling streets and serene alleyways, I can tell you that this city offers an intoxicating blend of history, culture, and modern energy.

Whether you're a first-time visitor or returning for more, 2026 brings new attractions and timeless experiences. Let me guide you through the best things to do—from war remnants to rooftop bars, all with practical tips to make your trip smooth and memorable.

15 Best Attractions

Here are the must-see spots in Ho Chi Minh City, each with details to help you plan your visit.

1. War Remnants Museum

This museum offers a sobering but essential look at the Vietnam War through photographs, military equipment, and exhibits. It's a deeply moving experience that provides context to the country's history. The courtyard displays tanks, helicopters, and a guillotine, while indoor galleries focus on the human cost of war.

  • Ticket price: ₽400 000 (≈ $16)
  • Hours: 7:30 AM–5:30 PM daily
  • How to get there: Located at 28 Vo Van Tan Street, District 3. Take a taxi or Grab (app-based ride) from the city center—about 10 minutes.
  • Tip: Visit early to avoid crowds; allow 2 hours.

2. Cu Chi Tunnels

Explore the intricate underground network used by Viet Cong soldiers during the war. You can crawl through narrow tunnels, see trapdoors, and learn about guerrilla tactics. It's a thrilling, claustrophobic adventure that brings history to life.

  • Ticket price: ₽1 100 000 (≈ $45) for a half-day tour including transport
  • Hours: 7:00 AM–5:00 PM daily
  • How to get there: About 70 km northwest of the city. Book a tour via GetYourGuide or take a bus from Ben Thanh Market.
  • Tip: Wear comfortable shoes and insect repellent; bring a flashlight.

3. Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon

This iconic red-brick cathedral, built by French colonists, stands as a symbol of Saigon's colonial past. Though under renovation until 2027, you can still admire its twin bell towers and the statue of the Virgin Mary in the square. It's a peaceful spot in the bustling District 1.

  • Ticket price: Free (exterior only during renovation)
  • Hours: 24/7 for exterior
  • How to get there: At Paris Square, District 1. Walk from Ben Thanh Market (5 minutes).
  • Tip: Combine with a visit to the nearby Central Post Office.

4. Saigon Central Post Office

A stunning example of French colonial architecture, this still-functioning post office features a vaulted ceiling, vintage maps, and a giant portrait of Ho Chi Minh. You can send a postcard home or just marvel at the interior. It's a photographer's dream.

  • Ticket price: Free
  • Hours: 7:00 AM–7:00 PM daily
  • How to get there: Next to Notre-Dame Cathedral. Walk from District 1.
  • Tip: Visit in the morning for fewer tourists.

5. Ben Thanh Market

This bustling market is the heart of Saigon, selling everything from fresh produce to souvenirs. Haggling is expected, so practice your negotiation skills. It's a sensory overload—smells of pho, sounds of vendors, and colorful textiles. Great for local snacks and gifts.

  • Ticket price: Free entry
  • Hours: 6:00 AM–6:00 PM (day market); 6:00 PM–11:00 PM (night market)
  • How to get there: Central District 1, near the Reunification Palace. Walk from most hotels.
  • Tip: Bargain for 50-70% of the initial price.

6. Reunification Palace (Independence Palace)

This historic building was the site of the end of the Vietnam War in 1975. You can tour the preserved rooms, including the war command center and the presidential office. The architecture is a mix of 1960s modernism and Vietnamese style.

  • Ticket price: ₽650 000 (≈ $26)
  • Hours: 7:30 AM–11:00 AM, 1:00 PM–4:00 PM daily
  • How to get there: At 135 Nam Ky Khoi Nghia Street, District 1. Walk from Ben Thanh Market (10 minutes).
  • Tip: Book tickets online via Booking.com or Agoda to skip queues.

7. Bitexco Financial Tower (Saigon Skydeck)

For panoramic views of the city, head to the 49th floor of this skyscraper. The Skydeck offers a 360-degree view of Saigon's skyline, including the Saigon River. It's especially beautiful at sunset.

  • Ticket price: ₽800 000 (≈ $32)
  • Hours: 9:30 AM–9:30 PM daily
  • How to get there: At 2 Hai Trieu Street, District 1. Walk from Ben Thanh Market (5 minutes).
  • Tip: Go at sunset for golden hour photos.

8. Jade Emperor Pagoda

This Taoist temple is a serene escape from the city chaos. Built in 1909, it's adorned with intricate carvings, statues of deities, and incense-filled halls. The roof is covered in ceramic tiles depicting mythical creatures. It's a spiritual haven.

  • Ticket price: Free (donations appreciated)
  • Hours: 7:00 AM–6:00 PM daily
  • How to get there: At 73 Mai Thi Luu Street, District 1. Take a taxi or Grab (5 minutes from center).
  • Tip: Dress modestly—cover shoulders and knees.

9. Saigon Opera House (Municipal Theatre)

This elegant French colonial building hosts performances from ballet to contemporary shows. The interior is lavishly decorated with chandeliers and gold leaf. Even if you don't see a show, the exterior is worth a photo.

  • Ticket price: Free for exterior; show tickets from ₽1 500 000 (≈ $60)
  • Hours: Varies by performance; check schedule
  • How to get there: At 7 Lam Son Square, District 1. Walk from Ben Thanh Market (8 minutes).
  • Tip: Book show tickets on GetYourGuide for best seats.

10. Pham Ngu Lao Street (Backpacker Area)

This lively street is the hub for budget travelers, with hostels, bars, and street food stalls. It's chaotic but fun, especially at night when the sidewalks fill with plastic stools and beer. Great for meeting fellow travelers.

  • Ticket price: Free
  • Hours: 24/7 (livelier after 6 PM)
  • How to get there: District 1, near Ben Thanh Market. Walk or take a Grab.
  • Tip: Try a Bia Saigon (Vietnamese beer) for about ₽20 000 (≈ $0.80).

11. Museum of Fine Arts

Housed in a beautiful colonial building, this museum showcases Vietnamese art from ancient to contemporary. The collection includes lacquer paintings, sculptures, and folk art. It's a quieter alternative to the War Remnants Museum.

  • Ticket price: ₽300 000 (≈ $12)
  • Hours: 9:00 AM–5:00 PM, closed Mondays
  • How to get there: At 97A Pho Duc Chinh Street, District 1. Walk from Ben Thanh Market (10 minutes).
  • Tip: Visit the rooftop for a nice city view.

12. Chinatown (Cho Lon)

Explore the vibrant Chinese-influenced district with its temples, markets, and noodle shops. The Binh Tay Market is the main attraction, but don't miss the Thien Hau Temple (Pagoda of the Lady of the Sea). It's a cultural fusion.

  • Ticket price: Free (Binh Tay Market entry free)
  • Hours: 6:00 AM–6:00 PM (markets)
  • How to get there: District 5, about 20 minutes by taxi from center. Take a Grab for ₽100 000 (≈ $4).
  • Tip: Go in the morning for the freshest produce.

13. Saigon Zoo and Botanical Gardens

One of the oldest zoos in the world (founded in 1865), it houses a variety of animals and a lush botanical garden. It's a family-friendly spot with a playground and lake. The orchid collection is stunning.

  • Ticket price: ₽500 000 (≈ $20) for adults
  • Hours: 7:00 AM–5:30 PM daily
  • How to get there: At 2 Nguyen Binh Khiem Street, District 1. Walk from the center (20 minutes) or take a Grab.
  • Tip: Bring water and snacks; zoo food is limited.

14. Suoi Tien Theme Park

This unique theme park combines Buddhist mythology with water slides and roller coasters. It's a bit kitsch but fun for a day trip. Highlights include the dragon slide and the "Underwater World" aquarium.

  • Ticket price: ₽1 200 000 (≈ $48) for entrance and rides
  • Hours: 8:00 AM–5:30 PM daily
  • How to get there: About 20 km northeast. Take a taxi (₽300 000, ≈ $12) or bus 19 from Ben Thanh.
  • Tip: Go on weekdays to avoid crowds.

15. Saigon River Cruise

Enjoy a dinner cruise on the Saigon River, taking in the city lights from the water. Many cruises offer buffet dinners with live music. It's a romantic way to end your day.

  • Ticket price: ₽1 500 000 (≈ $60) for dinner cruise
  • Hours: Typically 6:30 PM–9:00 PM
  • How to get there: Board at Bach Dang Wharf, District 1. Walk from the center (10 minutes).
  • Tip: Book via GetYourGuide for discounts.

1-Day, 2-Day, 3-Day Itineraries

1-Day Itinerary: City Core

Start at Ben Thanh Market (8 AM), then walk to Notre-Dame Cathedral and Central Post Office (10 AM). Visit Reunification Palace (11 AM). Lunch at a nearby pho stall (₽50 000, ≈ $2). Afternoon: War Remnants Museum (1 PM). Evening: Bitexco Skydeck for sunset, then dinner at a rooftop bar.

2-Day Itinerary: History & Culture

Day 1: Same as above. Day 2: Morning at Cu Chi Tunnels (book a half-day tour). Afternoon: Jade Emperor Pagoda and Museum of Fine Arts. Evening: Saigon River cruise.

3-Day Itinerary: Full Immersion

Day 1: City core. Day 2: Cu Chi Tunnels + Chinatown (Cho Lon). Day 3: Suoi Tien Theme Park or a day trip to the Mekong Delta. Evening: Pham Ngu Lao Street for drinks.

Tours & Excursions

For a hassle-free experience, consider guided tours. Popular options include the Cu Chi Tunnels tour (₽1 100 000, ≈ $45), Mekong Delta day trip (₽2 500 000, ≈ $100), and Ho Chi Minh City walking tour (₽500 000, ≈ $20). These tours can be booked on GetYourGuide for instant confirmation and free cancellation. For private transfers, check Kiwitaxi for airport pickups.

Book Tickets Online

Save time and money by booking attraction tickets online. Use Booking.com or Agoda for combo deals (e.g., War Remnants Museum + Reunification Palace for ₽900 000, ≈ $36). These attraction tickets can be purchased via GetYourGuide with up to 20% discounts. Always check for e-tickets to skip queues.

Free Attractions

Explore without spending a dong: Notre-Dame Cathedral (exterior), Central Post Office, Jade Emperor Pagoda, Ben Thanh Market (entry), Pham Ngu Lao Street, and the Saigon River promenade. Also, visit Tao Dan Park for a peaceful walk among ancient trees.

Practical Tips

Transportation

Use Grab (app-based taxis) for cheap, reliable rides—average trip ₽50 000 (≈ $2). For airport transfers, pre-book Kiwitaxi at ₽600 000 (≈ $24). The new metro line (opening 2025) connects Ben Thanh to Suoi Tien, but taxis are easier for now. Rent a scooter for ₽200 000 (≈ $8)/day, but be cautious in traffic.

Combo Tickets

Save with combo tickets: War Remnants Museum + Reunification Palace (₽900 000, ≈ $36) or Bitexco Skydeck + Saigon Opera House (₽2 000 000, ≈ $80). Check Booking.com for hotel packages.

Visa & Entry

Most nationalities need a visa for Vietnam (e-visa available at ₽1 000 000, ≈ $40). Apply online at least 3 days before travel. For Maldives visa sections, add: 'Перед вылетом заполните декларацию IMUGA на imuga.immigration.gov.mv и получите QR-код — без него не посадят на рейс.'

Connectivity

Stay online with an eSIM from Airalo—plans start at ₽500 000 (≈ $20) for 5 GB. Alternatively, buy a local SIM at the airport for ₽200 000 (≈ $8).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do attractions cost in Ho Chi Minh City?

Entrance fees vary: from free attractions to $20-30 for museums. Many venues have discount days.

Do I need a guide in Ho Chi Minh City?

A guide is recommended for historical sites and complex routes. Self-guided travel is possible with audio guides and maps.
