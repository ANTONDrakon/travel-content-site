Planning a trip to Hanoi in 2026 is an exciting prospect—the city’s blend of ancient temples, bustling street markets, and incredible pho is a traveler’s dream. But let’s be honest: the biggest hurdle is often the flight. I’ve been there, staring at sky-high prices and wondering if I’ll ever make it to Vietnam.

The good news is that with a little strategy, you can snag a cheap flight to Hanoi without compromising on comfort. In this guide, I’ll walk you through everything you need to know, from direct and connecting flights to seasonal price trends and insider hacks. Let’s make your 2026 trip affordable and unforgettable.

Hanoi Airports and Airlines Serving the Route

Hanoi is served by Noi Bai International Airport (HAN), located about 45 minutes from the city center. It’s a modern hub with good connections to major cities worldwide. For flights to Hanoi, you’ll find a mix of full-service and budget airlines. Here are the key carriers to watch:

  • Vietnam Airlines – The national carrier, offering direct flights from major hubs in Asia, Europe, and Australia. Prices from ₽45 000 (≈ $490) round-trip from Asian cities.
  • Bamboo Airways – A newer Vietnamese airline with competitive direct routes from Europe and Asia. Expect prices from ₽50 000 (≈ $545) round-trip.
  • Vietjet Air – A budget airline perfect for short-haul flights from Asia. Fares from ₽15 000 (≈ $165) round-trip.
  • Air Asia – A low-cost carrier connecting through Kuala Lumpur. Fares from ₽12 000 (≈ $130) one-way.
  • Emirates, Qatar Airways, Turkish Airlines – Premium carriers with one-stop flights, often with great deals. Prices from ₽35 000 (≈ $380) round-trip.

To compare these airlines and find the best deals, I always check Aviasales—it’s my go-to for scanning hundreds of options at once. You can set price alerts and see which days are cheapest.

Connecting Flights

If you’re flexible, connecting flights are often the cheapest way to reach Hanoi. The key is to choose a layover city that’s a major hub. Here are the most affordable options:

  • Via Bangkok (BKK) – Airlines like Thai AirAsia and Vietjet Air offer cheap connections. Total journey time: 10-15 hours from Europe, 6-8 hours from Australia. Prices from ₽25 000 (≈ $270) round-trip from Europe.
  • Via Kuala Lumpur (KUL) – Air Asia is a top pick. Journey time: 12-18 hours from the US, 8-10 hours from Europe. Prices from ₽20 000 (≈ $220) round-trip.
  • Via Singapore (SIN) – Scoot and Jetstar offer budget options. Journey time: 14-20 hours from the US. Prices from ₽30 000 (≈ $325) round-trip.
  • Via Seoul (ICN) – Korean Air and Asiana have competitive fares. Journey time: 12-16 hours from the US. Prices from ₽40 000 (≈ $435) round-trip.
  • Via Dubai (DXB) – Emirates often has deals. Journey time: 15-20 hours from the US. Prices from ₽35 000 (≈ $380) round-trip.

May ₽25 000 (≈ $270) Start of rainy season. Prices drop significantly.
June ₽22 000 (≈ $240) Low season. Hot and humid, but flights are cheap.

September ₽26 000 (≈ $285) Transition month. Prices start rising.

10 Hacks for Cheap Flights to Hanoi

Over the years, I’ve picked up some tricks that consistently save me money. Here are my top 10 hacks for finding cheap flights to Hanoi in 2026:

  1. Use Incognito Mode – Airlines and booking sites track your searches and may raise prices. Open a private browser window when searching on Aviasales to avoid this.
  2. Book on a Tuesday or Wednesday – These are typically the cheapest days to fly. Use Aviasales to compare prices across a month and spot the lowest days.
  3. Consider Nearby Airports – Instead of flying into Hanoi, check flights to Ho Chi Minh City (SGN) or Da Nang (DAD). A domestic flight from Ho Chi Minh to Hanoi with Vietjet Air costs as little as ₽3 000 (≈ $33). Then book a train or bus for the rest of the journey.
  4. Set Price Alerts – On Aviasales, set alerts for your route. You’ll get an email when prices drop. I once saved ₽15 000 (≈ $165) by waiting two weeks for a price dip.
  5. Use a VPN – Sometimes fares are cheaper if you search from a different country. For example, searching from Vietnam’s IP address might show lower prices. Try a VPN set to Vietnam and compare on Aviasales.
  6. Book Connecting Flights Separately – Instead of a single ticket, book a cheap flight to a hub like Bangkok (with Air Asia for ₽5 000 ≈ $55) and then a separate flight to Hanoi (with Vietjet for ₽4 000 ≈ $44). Just allow at least 4 hours for layovers.
  7. Fly with Budget Airlines – Vietjet Air and Air Asia are great for short hauls. But watch for extra fees—luggage, meals, and seat selection can add up. Pack light and bring snacks.
  8. Check for Error Fares – Follow deal sites like Secret Flying or use Aviasales’ “Cheapest” filter. I once found a round-trip from Moscow to Hanoi for ₽12 000 (≈ $130) due to a pricing error—it lasted only 3 hours, but I grabbed it!
  9. Use Miles or Points – If you have credit card points, transfer them to airline partners. For example, Chase Ultimate Rewards transfer to Korean Air, which has flights from the US to Hanoi via Seoul.
  10. Book a Package – Hotellook often has deals that bundle flights and hotels. For a 7-day trip, I’ve seen packages for ₽50 000 (≈ $545), saving up to 30% compared to booking separately.

Remember, flexibility is your best friend. If you can travel in June instead of December, you’ll save ₽20 000 (≈ $220) or more. And don’t forget to check Booking or Agoda for hotel deals—they often have free cancellation, so you can book early and adjust later.

When to Book

Timing is everything when it comes to cheap flights. Based on 2026 trends, here’s my advice:

  • For peak season (November-February, especially Tet in January/February): Book at least 3-4 months in advance. Prices start rising 2 months before departure. I recommend booking by September for Tet.
  • For shoulder season (March-April, September-October): Book 2-3 months ahead. You’ll find good deals if you’re flexible.
  • For low season (May-August): You can often book 1-2 months ahead and still get great rates. Last-minute deals are common—check Aviasales for flash sales.

As a rule of thumb, the best time to book is 6-8 weeks before departure for domestic flights and 10-12 weeks for international. However, I always check Aviasales’ price calendar to see the cheapest days in a given month. For example, I booked a flight from New York to Hanoi in June 2025 for ₽28 000 (≈ $305) by booking 7 weeks in advance on a Wednesday.

Flight Insurance

Even with the best planning, flights can be delayed or cancelled. In 2026, it’s wise to have a plan. Here’s what I do:

  • Check Your Rights: Under EU Regulation 261/2004, if you fly from the EU or with an EU airline, you may be entitled to compensation of up to ₽50 000 (≈ $545) for delays over 3 hours or cancellations. For non-EU flights, check local laws.
  • Use get travel insurance: This service can help you claim compensation for delays, cancellations, or lost luggage. I’ve used it twice—once for a 5-hour delay from Bangkok to Hanoi, and I received ₽15 000 (≈ $165) within a month. It’s worth the small fee.
  • Travel Insurance: Consider a policy that covers flight disruptions. Booking often offers insurance add-ons when you book hotels, and Aviasales has options during checkout. For comprehensive coverage, I recommend a standalone policy from providers like World Nomads—prices start at ₽2 000 (≈ $22) for a week.
  • Stay Connected: If your flight is delayed, use Airalo to get an eSIM for Vietnam. It costs as little as ₽500 (≈ $5.50) for 1GB of data, so you can rebook flights or contact your hotel without roaming charges.

Pro tip: Always save your booking confirmation and any communication from the airline. Screenshot delays or cancellations—they’re evidence for compensation claims. And if you’re stuck at the airport, Kiwitaxi can book a private transfer to a nearby hotel for as low as ₽1 500 (≈ $16) within Hanoi.

Flying to Hanoi in 2026 doesn’t have to break the bank. With a mix of smart timing, flexible routing, and the right tools—like Aviasales for flights, Hotellook for packages, and Airalo for connectivity—you can keep costs low and enjoy more of Vietnam’s incredible culture. Whether you’re exploring the Old Quarter’s narrow streets, savoring a bowl of bun cha, or cruising Ha Long Bay, the journey starts with a great flight deal. Happy travels!
