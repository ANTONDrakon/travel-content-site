15 Best Things to Do in Olkhon Island 2026

Olkhon Island, the pearl of Lake Baikal in Siberia, is a place that feels like stepping into another world. I’ve been lucky enough to explore its rugged cliffs, sacred shamanic sites, and crystal-clear waters, and I can tell you—this island has a raw, spiritual energy that’s hard to find anywhere else. Whether you’re a nature lover, a photographer, or just someone craving an off-the-beaten-path adventure, Olkhon’s attractions will leave you breathless. In 2026, the island remains a must-visit for its untouched beauty, unique culture, and affordable travel options. Let me guide you through the best things to do, with practical tips to make your trip unforgettable.

15 Best Attractions on Olkhon Island

1. Cape Burkhan (Shaman Rock)

This iconic rock formation, with its twin peaks and sacred cave, is the spiritual heart of Olkhon. According to local Buryat legends, it’s home to the master spirit of Lake Baikal. You’ll see prayer ribbons tied to nearby trees, and the views of the lake at sunset are pure magic.

Ticket price: Free (open access).
Hours: 24/7.
How to get there: A 10-minute walk from Khuzhir village center (head toward the lake).
Tip: Go at sunrise for fewer crowds—you might have the whole place to yourself.

2. Khuzhir Village

The main settlement on Olkhon, Khuzhir is a charming mix of wooden houses, small cafes, and souvenir stalls. It’s your base for exploring the island, with a relaxed vibe that makes you want to linger over a cup of tea. Don’t miss the local fish market for fresh omul (smoked Baikal fish).

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Always open; shops and cafes typically 9:00–20:00.
How to get there: From the ferry dock, it’s a 5-minute walk into the village.
Tip: Try the omul at the market—₽300 (≈ $3.30) per piece, grilled or smoked.

3. Lake Baikal Shoreline

The shoreline around Olkhon is a photographer’s dream, with clear turquoise waters and dramatic cliffs. In summer, you can swim (water is chilly but refreshing), while in winter, the frozen lake forms stunning ice caves. It’s a perfect spot for a picnic or a quiet moment.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: 24/7.
How to get there: Walk from Khuzhir to the shore (5–10 minutes).
Tip: Bring sturdy shoes—the rocks can be slippery, especially near the water.

4. Peschanaya Bay

This secluded bay on the island’s west coast is known for its golden sand and calm, shallow waters. It’s a great spot for swimming and sunbathing, surrounded by pine forests. The hike here is moderate but rewarding, with panoramic views of the lake.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: 24/7.
How to get there: Take a 1-hour hike from Khuzhir (follow the trail along the coast).
Tip: Pack lunch and water—no cafes nearby.

5. Shamanic Rock Paintings (Pisanitsa)

Near Cape Burkhan, you’ll find ancient petroglyphs etched into the rocks by Buryat shamans centuries ago. These symbols depict animals, spirits, and rituals, offering a glimpse into the island’s mystical past. They’re subtle but fascinating.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: 24/7.
How to get there: Walk from Cape Burkhan (5 minutes south along the cliff).
Tip: Use a flashlight in the evening to see the carvings more clearly.

6. Olkhon Island Museum (Local Lore Museum)

This small museum in Khuzhir houses artifacts from the island’s history, including Buryat costumes, fishing tools, and Soviet-era memorabilia. It’s a quick but insightful stop to understand Olkhon’s culture and ecology.

Ticket price: ₽200 (≈ $2.20).
Hours: 10:00–18:00 (closed Mondays).
How to get there: Located on the main street in Khuzhir (look for the wooden building with a sign).
Tip: Combine with a visit to the nearby fish market for a full local experience.

7. Cape Khoboy

The northernmost point of Olkhon, Cape Khoboy is a dramatic rock formation resembling a dragon’s head. Legend says it’s a petrified shaman. The views of the lake and the surrounding cliffs are breathtaking, especially in winter when ice formations create a surreal landscape.

Ticket price: Free (but requires a permit for off-road driving—₽500 / ≈ $5.50).
Hours: 24/7.
How to get there: Drive or take a tour (30 km from Khuzhir; 1 hour by car).
Tip: Book a tour via GetYourGuide if you don’t have a 4x4—the road is rough.

8. Lake Shara-Nur

This small, turquoise salt lake is a hidden gem on the island’s east side. Its high mineral content makes the water buoyant, and the surrounding steppe feels like a desert oasis. It’s a peaceful spot for a swim or a hike.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: 24/7.
How to get there: Hike 2 hours from Khuzhir (trail starts near the village’s southern edge).
Tip: Bring a towel and change of clothes—the water leaves a salty residue.

9. Baikal Ice Caves (Winter Only)

In winter, the frozen lake forms stunning ice caves and grottoes along the coast, especially near Cape Khoboy. The blue ice glows like crystal, and you can walk inside some caves. It’s a bucket-list experience for photographers.

Ticket price: Free (but tour costs ₽2 000–₽4 000 / ≈ $22–$44).
Hours: December–March, 24/7.
How to get there: Join a winter tour from Khuzhir (check GetYourGuide for options).
Tip: Wear crampons—ice is slippery; rent them locally for ₽300 (≈ $3.30).

10. Uzury Village

A tiny settlement on the island’s west coast, Uzury is known for its hot springs and quiet beaches. It’s a great place to unwind, with a few guesthouses and a small pier. The water here is warmer than in Khuzhir.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: 24/7.
How to get there: Drive 15 km from Khuzhir (30 minutes) or hike 2 hours.
Tip: Visit the hot springs at the local guesthouse—₽500 (≈ $5.50) for a soak.

11. Olkhon Gates (Strait)

The narrow strait between Olkhon and the mainland is a stunning waterway with views of the lake and mountains. Ferries cross here, and the surrounding cliffs are popular for hiking. It’s a great spot for birdwatching.

Ticket price: Free (ferry crossing included in transport costs).
Hours: 24/7.
How to get there: Take the ferry from the mainland (₽100 / ≈ $1.10 per person).
Tip: Walk along the cliffs for panoramic photos—especially at sunset.

12. Mount Zhima

The highest point on Olkhon (1,274 meters), Mount Zhima offers a challenging hike with rewarding views of the entire island and Lake Baikal. The trail is steep but well-marked, passing through forests and meadows.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: 24/7 (best in summer).
How to get there: Hike 4 hours from Khuzhir (start early).
Tip: Bring plenty of water and snacks—no facilities on the trail.

13. Tagheran Steppe

This vast, grassy plain on the island’s east side is home to wild horses and herds of cattle. It’s a serene landscape with rolling hills and distant lake views, perfect for a peaceful walk or a picnic.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: 24/7.
How to get there: Drive 10 km from Khuzhir (20 minutes) or hike 1.5 hours.
Tip: Bring binoculars to spot eagles and falcons.

14. Sagan-Zaba Cliff

A dramatic cliff on the island’s west coast, Sagan-Zaba is known for its white limestone face and ancient petroglyphs. It’s a sacred site for Buryat shamans, and the energy here is palpable. The views of the lake are spectacular.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: 24/7.
How to get there: Hike 2 hours from Khuzhir (follow the coastal trail).
Tip: Visit in the late afternoon for golden light on the cliff face.

15. Olkhon Night Sky

With minimal light pollution, Olkhon offers incredible stargazing. In summer, the Milky Way is visible to the naked eye, and in winter, the aurora borealis sometimes appears. It’s a magical way to end your day.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Nighttime (best after midnight).
How to get there: Walk away from Khuzhir’s lights (10 minutes).
Tip: Bring a blanket and a star map app—I use “SkyView” for free.

1-Day, 2-Day, and 3-Day Itineraries

1-Day Itinerary (Best for a Quick Visit)

  • Morning: Start at Cape Burkhan for sunrise (6:00–7:00). Then explore Shamanic Rock Paintings (30 minutes).
  • Late Morning: Visit Olkhon Island Museum (1 hour). Grab omul for lunch at the Khuzhir market.
  • Afternoon: Hike to Lake Shara-Nur (3 hours round trip). Swim if it’s warm.
  • Evening: Sunset at Peschanaya Bay (1 hour). Dinner at a local cafe (try “Olkhon Cafe” for Buryat dumplings—₽400 / ≈ $4.40).

2-Day Itinerary (Optimal for Most Travelers)

  • Day 1: Morning: Cape Burkhan + Shamanic Rock Paintings. Afternoon: Olkhon Island Museum + Khuzhir market. Evening: Sunset at Peschanaya Bay.
  • Day 2: Morning: Hike to Mount Zhima (4 hours). Afternoon: Visit Uzury Village for hot springs (2 hours). Evening: Stargazing near Cape Khoboy.
  • Tip: Book a tour for Cape Khoboy via GetYourGuide—it’s ₽2 500 (≈ $28) per person and includes transport.

3-Day Itinerary (Full Island Experience)

  • Day 1: Same as 2-day Day 1.
  • Day 2: Morning: Cape Khoboy tour (half-day, ₽2 500 / ≈ $28). Afternoon: Tagheran Steppe walk (2 hours). Evening: Baikal shoreline picnic.
  • Day 3: Morning: Hike to Sagan-Zaba Cliff (3 hours). Afternoon: Visit Lake Shara-Nur again for a swim. Evening: Stargazing from Khuzhir’s outskirts.
  • Tip: Use Kiwitaxi for transfers from Irkutsk to Olkhon—₽3 000 (≈ $33) per person, shared.

Tours & Excursions

Olkhon Island offers a range of tours to help you explore its remote corners. Here are the best options:

  • Cape Khoboy Tour: Half-day, ₽2 500 (≈ $28) per person. Includes 4x4 transport and guide. Book on GetYourGuide.
  • Winter Ice Tour: Full-day, ₽4 000 (≈ $44) per person. Visit ice caves and frozen lake. Check GetYourGuide for dates.
  • Hiking Excursion: Full-day, ₽3 000 (≈ $33) per person. Covers Mount Zhima or Sagan-Zaba. Available via local guides at Khuzhir’s tourist office.
  • Private Custom Tour: From ₽5 000 (≈ $55) per person. Tailor-made itinerary. Ask at your guesthouse or use GetYourGuide.

Tours can be booked online via GetYourGuide or arranged locally in Khuzhir. Prices are approximate and vary by season.

Free Attractions

Olkhon is budget-friendly! Here’s what you can enjoy without spending a ruble:

  • Cape Burkhan and Shamanic Rock Paintings
  • Lake Baikal shoreline (swimming, picnics)
  • Peschanaya Bay
  • Tagheran Steppe (wild horses)
  • Stargazing (night sky)
  • Khuzhir village walks
  • Hiking trails (Mount Zhima, Sagan-Zaba, Lake Shara-Nur)

Transportation

  • Getting to Olkhon: From Irkutsk, take a bus to the ferry (₽800 / ≈ $8.80; 4 hours). Then ferry to Olkhon (₽100 / ≈ $1.10). Check Aviasales for flights to Irkutsk.
  • Getting around: Rent a bike (₽500 / ≈ $5.50 per day) or hire a local driver (₽2 000 / ≈ $22 per trip). Book a 4x4 tour for remote spots—use Hotellook for guesthouse deals.
  • Ferry schedule: Runs hourly from 7:00–21:00 in summer; winter crossing is via ice road (check conditions).

Combo Tickets & Discounts

  • No official combo ticket, but the Olkhon Island Museum offers a ₽300 (≈ $3.30) pass including a guided tour.
  • Some guesthouses offer package deals with tours—check Booking.com or Agoda

FAQ

How much do attractions cost in Olkhon Island?

Entrance fees vary: from free attractions to $20-30 for museums. Many venues have discount days.

Do I need a guide in Olkhon Island?

A guide is recommended for historical sites and complex routes. Self-guided travel is possible with audio guides and maps.
