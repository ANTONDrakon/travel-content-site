Welcome to Tbilisi, a city where ancient history meets vibrant modernity, nestled in the Caucasus Mountains. The moment you step into the old town, you're wrapped in a warm embrace of winding cobblestone streets, colorful wooden balconies, and the gentle hum of life. The atmosphere is a delightful chaos—a mix of sulfur baths, quirky street art, and the irresistible scent of fresh khachapuri drifting from every corner. Tbilisi isn't just a destination; it's a feeling of being part of a story that's been unfolding for centuries, with a modern twist that surprises you at every turn.

What makes this a great destination in 2026? For starters, it's one of Europe's most affordable capitals, offering incredible value for your ruble. You'll find world-class wine culture (Georgia is the cradle of winemaking!), a thriving arts scene, and a food scene that will make you rethink everything you know about hospitality. The city is safe, walkable, and full of hidden gems—from underground galleries to rooftop bars with panoramic views. Whether you're a history buff, a foodie, or an adventurer, Tbilisi has a way of pulling you in and making you want to stay longer.

Plus, Tbilisi is incredibly accessible for Russian travelers. With visa-free entry for up to a year, direct flights from Moscow and other cities, and a growing number of English-speaking locals, it's never been easier to explore this gem. The city's energy is contagious, and the locals—known for their legendary hospitality—will make you feel like family. Let's dive into everything you need to know for an unforgettable trip in 2026.

How to Get There

Your journey begins at Tbilisi International Airport (TBS), located about 17 km southeast of the city center. Direct flights from Moscow typically take around 2.5 hours, with airlines like Georgian Airways, Aeroflot, and S7 operating daily. From Saint Petersburg, expect 3.5-hour flights. Average round-trip prices from Moscow range from ₽15 000 to ₽30 000 (≈ $165 to $330), depending on the season and how early you book. From other major Russian cities like Yekaterinburg or Novosibirsk, prices can go up to ₽25 000–₽40 000 (≈ $275–$440). For the best deals, you can check flights on Aviasales

Where to Stay

Tbilisi offers neighborhoods for every vibe and budget. For first-timers, the historic Old Town (Kala) is perfect—you're steps from attractions like Narikala Fortress and sulfur baths. Mid-range hotels here cost around ₽4 000–₽8 000 per night (≈ $44–$88), while luxury stays in boutique hotels like Rooms Hotel Tbilisi can hit ₽10 000–₽20 000 (≈ $110–$220). For a more local feel, Vera and Sololaki are trendy with cafes and galleries, with guesthouses from ₽2 500–₽5 000 (≈ $28–$55). Budget travelers love Avlabari, where hostels start at ₽800–₽1 500 (≈ $9–$17) per night. You can browse and compare prices on Hotellook

Top 15 Attractions

  • 1. Narikala Fortress — A 4th-century fortress overlooking the city. Take the cable car (₽2.50, ≈ 0.50 GEL) or hike up for stunning views. Free entry; open 24/7.
  • 2. Sulfur Baths (Abanotubani) — Historic bathhouses in the old district. A private room costs ₽1 500–₽3 000 (≈ $17–$33) for 1 hour. Open daily 8 AM–11 PM.
  • 3. Sameba Cathedral — One of the world's largest Orthodox cathedrals. Free entry; open 7 AM–9 PM. Dress modestly.
  • 4. Rustaveli Avenue — The main artery with shops, theaters, and the Parliament building. Free to walk; explore the nearby museums.
  • 5. Georgian National Museum — Gold artifacts and Soviet history. Entry: ₽700 (≈ $8); open Tue–Sun 10 AM–6 PM.
  • 6. Bridge of Peace — A modern glass-and-steel bridge with LED lights at night. Free; best visited at sunset.
  • 7. Mtatsminda Park — An amusement park on a plateau with city views. Entry free, rides from ₽200–₽500 (≈ $2–$6). Open 11 AM–11 PM.
  • 8. Dry Bridge Market — A flea market for Soviet memorabilia, antiques, and art. Free entry; Sat–Sun 10 AM–5 PM. Bargain hard!
  • 9. Tbilisi Botanical Garden — 128 hectares of lush greenery near the fortress. Entry: ₽200 (≈ $2); open 9 AM–8 PM.
  • 10. Anchiskhati Basilica — The oldest church in Tbilisi (6th century). Free entry; open 9 AM–6 PM.
  • 11. Fabrika Tbilisi — A former Soviet sewing factory turned into a creative hub with bars, hostels, and street art. Free to explore; open 24/7.
  • 12. Funicular to Mtatsminda — A scenic ride up the mountain. Round trip: ₽350 (≈ $4); open 10 AM–10 PM.
  • 13. Gabriadze Theater — A puppet theater with a whimsical clock tower. Shows from ₽1 000 (≈ $11); check schedule online.
  • 14. Chronicles of Georgia — A massive stone monument on a hill outside the city. Free entry; open 24/7. Take a taxi (₽1 000–₽1 500, ≈ $11–$17).
  • 15. Turtle Lake — A serene lake perfect for a swim or picnic. Free entry; open 24/7. Reach by bus or taxi from the city center.

Getting Around

Tbilisi's public transport is efficient and cheap. The metro (two lines) costs ₽10 (≈ $0.11) per ride—buy a Metromoney card (₽70, ≈ $0.77) and top up. Buses and minibuses (marshrutkas) are also ₽10 (≈ $0.11) per ride. Taxis via apps like Bolt or Yandex start at ₽150–₽250 (≈ $1.65–$2.75) for short trips. For airport transfers, I recommend pre-booking with Kiwitaxi—a fixed price of ₽1 500–₽2 500 (≈ $17–$28) saves you from haggling. Car rentals from companies like Avis start at ₽2 000 per day (≈ $22), but parking in the old town is tough—stick to public transport.

Where to Eat

Georgian cuisine is a feast for the senses. Start with khachapuri (cheese bread) at Machakhela (₽400–₽600, ≈ $4.40–$6.60 per pie). For khinkali (dumplings), head to Zakhar Zakharich (₽150 each, ≈ $1.65). A full meal at a mid-range restaurant like Shavi Lomi costs ₽1 500–₽3 000 (≈ $17–$33) per person. Street food is a bargain: lobiani (bean-filled bread) from a bakery costs ₽100–₽200 (≈ $1.10–$2.20). Don't miss wine—Georgia's natural wines are world-renowned, with a glass at a wine bar like Wine Factory N1 starting at ₽300–₽500 (≈ $3.30–$5.50). For a budget meal, try Kebab House for shawarma (₽250–₽400, ≈ $2.75–$4.40).

Best Time to Visit

Tbilisi shines in spring (April–June) and autumn (September–October) when temperatures are mild (15–25°C) and crowds are thin. Summer (July–August) can be hot (30–35°C) but lively with festivals—prices for hotels rise by 20–30%. Winter (December–February) is chilly (0–5°C) and snowy, but the city is magical with Christmas lights; accommodation drops to ₽2 000–₽4 000 (≈ $22–$44) per night. For the best value, visit in late autumn (November) or early spring (March)—flights from Moscow can be as low as ₽12 000 (≈ $130) round trip.

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens can enter Georgia visa-free for up to 1 year—just bring your valid passport (must have at least 3 months' validity). No registration needed for stays under 90 days. If you're flying, note that direct flights from Russia to Georgia resumed in 2023, but check your airline's policies. 10 Travel Tips

  1. Learn a few Georgian phrases — "Gamarjoba" (hello), "Madloba" (thank you), and "Nakhvamdis" (goodbye) will earn you smiles.
  2. Carry cash — Many small shops and markets don't accept cards. ATMs are everywhere, but exchange rubles at banks for better rates.
  3. Use Bolt for taxis — It's cheaper than Yandex and avoids haggling. Never take unmetered taxis from the airport.
  4. Dress modestly at churches — Cover shoulders and knees for Sameba and other religious sites.
  5. Try the local wine — Visit a wine bar for a tasting flight (₽500–₽1 000, ≈ $5.50–$11). Georgia has 8,000 years of winemaking history!
  6. Bargain at the Dry Bridge Market — Start at half the asking price and negotiate politely.
  7. Stay connected — Buy a local SIM (Magti or Beeline) for ₽500–₽1 000 (≈ $5.50–$11) with data. Or get an eSIM from Airalo for convenience—just scan a QR code.
  8. Watch out for uneven sidewalks — Old town streets are charming but cobblestone and can be slippery. Wear comfortable shoes.
  9. Tap water is safe — Tbilisi's mountain spring water is drinkable, so bring a reusable bottle.
  10. Book attractions in advance — For popular spots like the Sulfur Baths, reserve a slot via GetYourGuide to avoid queues, especially in peak season.
