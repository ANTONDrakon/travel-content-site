Nestled along the banks of the Rioni River in western Georgia, Kutaisi is a city where ancient history meets vibrant modern life. With a population of around 150,000, it’s Georgia’s third-largest city but feels like a cozy, welcoming town. The air is filled with the scent of fresh khachapuri from street-side bakeries, and the sound of laughter spills from outdoor cafes. Kutaisi is a gateway to the stunning Imereti region, known for its lush forests, dramatic canyons, and the legendary Prometheus Cave.

Why is 2026 the year to visit? Because Kutaisi offers an authentic Georgian experience without the tourist crowds of Tbilisi or Batumi. You’ll wander through the UNESCO-listed Bagrati Cathedral, sip local wines at family-run maranis (wine cellars), and explore the Okatse Canyon’s sky-high walkway. The city is also a budget traveler’s dream—cheap eats, affordable transport, and a wealth of natural wonders just a short drive away. Whether you’re a history buff, a nature lover, or a foodie, Kutaisi has something special waiting for you.

Plus, 2026 marks a year of improved infrastructure—new direct flights, better road connections, and more English-friendly services. The city’s charm lies in its unpretentious vibe: locals will invite you for a toast with chacha (grape vodka), and you’ll leave with a full heart and a camera roll stuffed with photos of medieval stonework and emerald-green landscapes.

How to Get There

Kutaisi International Airport (KUT) is the city’s main hub, located about 20 kilometers west of the center. It’s a small but efficient airport, serving flights from major European and regional cities. Direct flights arrive from Moscow (Sheremetyevo) starting at around ₽15 000 (≈ $160), from Istanbul at ₽22 000 (≈ $240), and from Warsaw at ₽18 000 (≈ $195). Budget airlines like Wizz Air and Ryanair often have deals, especially in spring and autumn. Where to Stay

Kutaisi’s neighborhoods each have their own flavor. The city center, around Rustaveli Avenue, is perfect for first-timers—close to restaurants, shops, and main sights. For a quieter vibe, head to the Vake district, near the botanical garden, or stay in the historic quarter around Bagrati Cathedral, where cobblestone streets lead to cozy guesthouses.

Budget options: Hostels like “Kutaisi Hostel” start at ₽1 200 (≈ $13) per night for a dorm bed. Mid-range hotels, such as “Hotel Kutaisi Inn” or “Garden Inn,” offer double rooms from ₽3 500 (≈ $38) per night, often including a hearty Georgian breakfast. Top 15 Attractions

  1. Bagrati Cathedral — A UNESCO World Heritage site dating back to the 11th century, perched on a hilltop with panoramic views of the city. Free entry, open daily 9:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  2. Gelati Monastery — Another UNESCO site, 11 km from Kutaisi, known for its stunning mosaics and frescoes. Entry free, open 10:00 AM–5:00 PM.
  3. Prometheus Cave — A vast underground wonderland with stalactites, stalagmites, and an underground boat ride. Ticket: ₽1 500 (≈ $16), open 10:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  4. Okatse Canyon — A dramatic canyon with a 780-meter-long hanging walkway. Entry: ₽1 200 (≈ $13), open 9:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  5. Martvili Canyon — A stunning gorge with emerald-green water, perfect for boat tours. Entry: ₽1 000 (≈ $11), open 10:00 AM–5:00 PM.
  6. Sataplia Nature Reserve — Home to dinosaur footprints, a cave, and a glass-bottomed viewpoint. Entry: ₽800 (≈ $9), open 10:00 AM–5:00 PM.
  7. Kutaisi State Historical Museum — Exhibits on Georgian history, from ancient artifacts to Soviet-era items. Entry: ₽500 (≈ $5), open 10:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  8. White Bridge — A iconic pedestrian bridge over the Rioni River, built in 1852. Free, always open.
  9. Kutaisi Botanical Garden — A peaceful green space with exotic plants and a small waterfall. Entry: ₽300 (≈ $3), open 9:00 AM–8:00 PM.
  10. Motsameta Monastery — A serene 11th-century monastery on a cliff, 6 km from Kutaisi. Free, open 10:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  11. Niko Pirosmani Art Gallery — Showcasing works by Georgia’s famous primitive painter. Entry: ₽400 (≈ $4), open 10:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  12. Colchis Fountain — A modern fountain in the central square, featuring golden statues. Free, always open.
  13. Kutaisi Market — A bustling bazaar for local produce, spices, and souvenirs. Free entry, open 8:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  14. Kinkhada Waterfall — A hidden gem 30 km from Kutaisi, accessible by a short hike. Free, best visited in spring.
  15. Kutaisi Synagogue — A historic Jewish site with beautiful stained glass. Free, open by appointment.

Getting Around

Kutaisi is a walkable city, but for longer distances, you have several options. Buses (marshrutkas) are the cheapest—fares start at ₽30 (≈ $0.30) per ride. Taxis are affordable: a ride across town costs ₽500–₽1 000 (≈ $5–$11), but always agree on the price upfront. For day trips to canyons or caves, rent a car from local agencies like “Car Rental Kutaisi” for ₽2 500 (≈ $27) per day, or book a private transfer via Kiwitaxi for around ₽3 000 (≈ $33) per trip. There’s no metro in Kutaisi, but the city’s compact size makes it easy to navigate.

Where to Eat

Georgian cuisine is a feast for the senses. Start with khachapuri (cheese-filled bread) at “Khachapuri House” for ₽400 (≈ $4). For a full meal, try “Restaurant Palaty” where a three-course dinner (including khinkali dumplings and grilled meat) costs ₽1 500–₽2 500 (≈ $16–$27). Street food is everywhere—try lobiani (bean-filled bread) from a market stall for ₽100 (≈ $1). For a splurge, “Restaurant Tsar” offers fine dining with wine pairings for ₽3 000 (≈ $33) per person. Budget eaters can survive on ₽1 000 (≈ $11) a day, while mid-range dining runs ₽2 500 (≈ $27) per meal.

Best Time to Visit

Kutaisi has a humid subtropical climate. Spring (March–May) brings mild temperatures (15–25°C) and blooming flowers—perfect for hiking. Summer (June–August) is hot, reaching 35°C, but ideal for canyon boat tours. Autumn (September–November) is pleasant (20–25°C) with lower prices and fewer tourists. Winter (December–February) is cool (5–10°C) and rainy, but Gelati Monastery looks magical in the mist. Peak season is July–August, when hotel prices double. For the best value, visit in May or September—temperatures are comfortable, and crowds are thin.

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens can enter Georgia visa-free for up to one year. You’ll need a valid passport (at least 6 months beyond your stay) and proof of accommodation. No additional visa is required for Georgia for most nationalities, but check your country’s requirements. Register with the Georgian Ministry of Justice if staying over 90 days.
