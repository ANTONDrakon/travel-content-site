Batumi, Georgia's vibrant Black Sea gem, has evolved into one of the most exciting travel destinations in the Caucasus. By 2026, the city's hotel scene will be more diverse than ever—from ultra-modern towers with sea views to charming boutique guesthouses tucked away in the old town. Whether you're here for the pebble beaches, the buzzing nightlife, or the stunning mountain backdrop, finding the right place to stay can make or break your trip.

Hotels in Batumi fall into three main neighborhoods: the Boulevard area (prime sea front, most expensive), the Old Town (historic, mid-range, close to attractions), and the New Boulevard (modern high-rises, great value, slightly quieter). Budget Hotels (under $50/night)

1. Hotel Lile

Location: Near Batumi Boulevard, 10-minute walk to the beach
Price range: ₽1 800–₽3 500 per night (≈ $20–$38)
Rating: 8.2/10

A no-frills family-run hotel that's been a backpacker favorite for years. Rooms are simple but spotless, with basic furniture, air conditioning, and free Wi-Fi. The highlight is the rooftop terrace where you can see the sea and the mountains. Breakfast is a modest spread of bread, cheese, jam, and tea—nothing fancy, but it fills you up.

Pros: Very affordable, friendly staff, close to the beach and casino area.
Cons: No elevator (rooms on upper floors), thin walls, no parking.

2. Guesthouse Gantiadi

Location: Old Town, 5 minutes from Europe Square
Price range: ₽1 500–₽2 800 per night (≈ $16–$30)
Rating: 8.5/10

A charming guesthouse with a lush courtyard garden. The owner, a retired teacher, grows her own vegetables and often shares homemade wine with guests. Rooms are cozy, with vintage furniture and colorful textiles. The shared kitchen is well-equipped for self-catering. This is the kind of place where you'll feel like part of the family.

Pros: Authentic Georgian hospitality, garden, free parking, great value.
Cons: Shared bathroom in some rooms, no air conditioning in older rooms, limited hot water during peak hours.

3. Hostel Batumi

Location: New Boulevard, steps from the beach
Price range: ₽700–₽1 500 per night (≈ $8–$16 for dorm beds)
Rating: 7.9/10

If you're on a shoestring budget, this modern hostel delivers. Dorms have privacy curtains, individual reading lights, and lockers. The common area is lively, with a pool table and a bar that serves cheap local beer. The staff organize pub crawls and day trips to nearby attractions like the Botanical Garden.

Pros: Cheapest option, social atmosphere, clean facilities, 24-hour reception.
Cons: Noisy at night, limited kitchen space, no breakfast included.

Mid-Range Hotels ($50-150/night)

4. Hotel Old Batumi

Location: Old Town, on Gogebashvili Street
Price range: ₽4 500–₽8 000 per night (≈ $49–$87)
Rating: 8.8/10

A beautifully restored 19th-century building with high ceilings, wooden floors, and a small internal courtyard. Each room is uniquely decorated with local art and antique furniture. The rooftop bar offers panoramic views of the port and the old town. Breakfast is a generous buffet with khachapuri, fresh fruit, and local cheeses.

Highlights: Historic charm, excellent location for sightseeing, rooftop terrace, free wine tasting in the evening.

5. Hotel Intourist Palace

Location: Batumi Boulevard, directly on the seafront
Price range: ₽6 000–₽12 000 per night (≈ $65–$130)
Rating: 8.4/10

A Soviet-era landmark that's been fully modernized. The rooms are spacious, with floor-to-ceiling windows and balconies overlooking the Black Sea. The hotel has an indoor pool, a spa, and a casino. The restaurant serves both Georgian and European cuisine. It's not the most stylish hotel, but it's reliable and well-run.

Highlights: Prime sea front location, large rooms, indoor pool, great value for the location.

6. Hotel Kolkhi

Location: New Boulevard, near the Ferris wheel
Price range: ₽5 000–₽9 000 per night (≈ $54–$98)
Rating: 8.6/10

A sleek, modern hotel with a focus on wellness. The rooms are minimalist and bright, with smart TVs and rain showers. The rooftop infinity pool is the star attraction—it's heated and offers stunning sunset views. The spa includes a sauna, steam room, and massage treatments. The breakfast is exceptional, with a live cooking station for omelets and pancakes.

Highlights: Rooftop pool, excellent spa, modern design, great breakfast.

7. Hotel Panorama

Location: Hill above the Old Town, 15-minute walk to the beach
Price range: ₽7 000–₽14 000 per night (≈ $76–$152)
Rating: 8.9/10

Perched on a hill, this hotel offers what many consider the best view in Batumi—the entire bay, the old town, and the mountains. Rooms are contemporary with floor-to-ceiling windows. The infinity pool seems to merge with the sea. The restaurant specializes in Adjarian cuisine, and the wine list features excellent local vintages.

Highlights: Breathtaking views, infinity pool, romantic atmosphere, free shuttle to the beach.

Luxury Hotels ($150+/night)

8. Hilton Batumi

Location: Batumi Boulevard, central
Price range: ₽15 000–₽30 000 per night (≈ $163–$326)
Rating: 9.0/10

The flagship luxury property in Batumi. The lobby is a grand atrium with a glass ceiling. Rooms are spacious and plush, with marble bathrooms and Nespresso machines. The hotel has a full-service spa, a large outdoor pool, and several restaurants, including a rooftop lounge. Service is professional and attentive, with concierge staff who can arrange private tours and transfers.

Features: World-class spa, multiple dining options, casino, kids' club, private beach area.

9. Sheraton Batumi Hotel

Location: New Boulevard, near the dolphinarium
Price range: ₽18 000–₽35 000 per night (≈ $195–$380)
Rating: 9.1/10

A striking modern tower with a distinctive wave-like design. The interiors are sleek and contemporary, with floor-to-ceiling windows and smart home technology. The spa is one of the best in the city, with a thermal suite, hammam, and a 25-meter indoor pool. The signature restaurant serves fusion cuisine with a focus on local ingredients. The rooftop bar is a popular spot for cocktails and live music.

Features: Award-winning spa, infinity edge pool, butler service, helipad, private yacht charter available.

10. Villa Riva

Location: Old Town, on the waterfront
Price range: ₽20 000–₽45 000 per night (≈ $217–$489)
Rating: 9.3/10

A boutique luxury hotel with only 12 suites, housed in a restored 19th-century mansion. Each suite is individually designed, with antiques, original artworks, and private terraces overlooking the sea. The service is discreet and personalized—the staff will arrange anything from a private boat trip to a cooking class with a local chef. The restaurant is a destination in itself, with a tasting menu that changes daily.

Features: Intimate atmosphere, private terrace, Michelin-starred restaurant, wine cellar, personal butler.

How to Choose the Right Area

For beach lovers: Stick to the Boulevard area (Hotels Intourist Palace, Hilton, Sheraton). You'll be steps from the pebble beach, the promenade, and the best beach clubs. The sea is cleanest here, and you'll have easy access to water sports. Expect higher prices and more crowds in summer.

For sightseeing: The Old Town (Hotel Old Batumi, Villa Riva, Guesthouse Gantiadi) is your base. You can walk to Europe Square, the Astronomical Clock, the Piazza, and the Botanical Garden (take a short bus ride). The streets are narrow and charming, with plenty of cafes and souvenir shops. It's quieter at night, which is nice for sleeping.

For nightlife: The New Boulevard (Hotel Kolkhi, Hostel Batumi, Hotel Panorama) is where the party is. This area has the most bars, clubs, and casinos. The Ferris wheel area is particularly lively, with open-air concerts and street performers. You'll find good value hotels here, but it can be noisy until 3 AM.

Booking Tips

When to book: For summer travel (June–August), book at least 3 months in advance—the best hotels sell out fast. For winter, you can book a week ahead and often get last-minute deals. Shoulder seasons (May, September, October) offer the best balance of good weather and reasonable prices.

How to save: Consider booking a hotel with a kitchenette and cooking some meals—local markets are cheap and fresh. Look for hotels that include breakfast in the rate (many mid-range ones do). If you're flexible, check prices for Sunday–Thursday stays, which are often 20-30% cheaper than weekends.
