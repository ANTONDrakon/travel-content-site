Best Time to Visit Tbilisi 2026: Seasons & Weather

Planning a trip to Tbilisi in 2026? You’re in for a treat. Georgia’s vibrant capital sits at the crossroads of Europe and Asia, offering a mix of ancient history, modern energy, and stunning landscapes. But when should you go? The answer depends on what you want—warm city strolls, snowy mountain escapes, or lively festivals. Let me walk you through Tbilisi’s seasons and weather, so you can pick the perfect time for your adventure.

Tbilisi has a humid subtropical climate, with hot summers and chilly winters. Spring and autumn are mild and beautiful, making them ideal for exploring. The city’s elevation (about 450 meters) means it’s cooler than Georgia’s coastal areas but warmer than the mountains. Expect rain throughout the year, but it varies by season. Here’s a month-by-month breakdown to help you decide.

High Season

When: May to September. This is Tbilisi’s peak tourist period, with warm weather, long days, and a buzz of activity. July and August are the busiest, with temperatures often hitting 32°C—perfect for outdoor cafes and rooftop bars.

Why it’s popular: The city comes alive with festivals, street performances, and al fresco dining. Nearby mountains and vineyards are also accessible for day trips. Prices for flights and accommodation spike, so budget accordingly.

Prices: Expect hotel rates to double or triple. A mid-range hotel room might cost ₽8 000–15 000 (≈ $85–160) per night. During high season, it’s worth booking hotels early via Hotellook or Booking to secure the best deals. I recommend reserving at least 2–3 months in advance.

Low Season

When: November to February. Winter in Tbilisi is cold but charming, with fewer crowds and lower prices. December and January see temperatures around 0–8°C, with occasional snow that dusts the old town rooftops.

Pros: You’ll have attractions like Narikala Fortress and the sulfur baths almost to yourself. Hotel prices drop by 40–50%, with budget options starting at ₽2 500–4 000 (≈ $27–43) per night. Flights are cheaper too.

Cons: Days are short (sunset around 5:30 PM), and some outdoor cafes close. Rain or snow can make cobblestone streets slippery. But if you don’t mind bundling up, it’s a cozy time to explore.

Best Time for Beach Vacation

Tbilisi itself doesn’t have beaches—it’s an inland city. But if you’re dreaming of swimming, head to the Black Sea coast (Batumi or Kobuleti) about 4–5 hours away by car or train. The best months for a beach trip are June to September, when sea temperatures reach 23–25°C. July and August are peak beach season, with water warm enough for long swims. If you’re combining Tbilisi with a beach escape, plan for late June or early September to avoid the biggest crowds.

For a day trip from Tbilisi, try the local “beaches” along the Mtkvari River or at Turtle Lake (just outside the city). These are best in July and August, with water temperatures around 20–23°C—refreshing but not tropical.

Best Time for Sightseeing

For comfortable sightseeing without extreme heat or cold, aim for April to June or September to October. During these months, temperatures range from 15–25°C, making walking tours of the old town, climbing to the Narikala Fortress, or exploring the Open Air Museum of Ethnography a pleasure.

Spring (April–May) brings blooming jasmine and wisteria, while autumn (September–October) offers golden leaves and crisp air. Rain is possible but usually brief. I’d say May and September are the sweet spots—sunny, warm, and less crowded than peak summer.

Rainy Season

Tbilisi doesn’t have a true rainy season like tropical destinations, but precipitation is highest in April to June and October to November. Expect around 50–60 mm of rain per month during these periods, with occasional heavy showers. July and August are driest, with only 35–40 mm.

Is it worth visiting during the rainier months? Absolutely. Showers are usually short-lived (15–30 minutes), and the city looks lush and green. Just pack an umbrella and waterproof shoes. The sulfur baths are a perfect rainy-day activity—soak in warm, mineral-rich waters for ₽1 500–3 000 (≈ $16–32) per hour.

Events & Festivals

Tbilisi’s calendar is packed with cultural events. Here are the major ones by month:

  • January: New Year celebrations (Dec 31–Jan 2) with fireworks and street parties. Orthodox Christmas on January 7.
  • March/April: Tbilisi International Film Festival (usually in early April). Easter (dates vary) with church services and feasts.
  • May: Tbilisi Jazz Festival (mid-May) brings international musicians. Independence Day on May 26 with parades.
  • June: Tbilisi Open Air (rock/electronic music festival) in late June. Art-Gene Festival celebrating wine and culture.
  • July: Tbilisi International Theatre Festival. Tbilisoba (city festival) in early October—watch for local food and crafts.
  • August: Tbilisi International Music Festival (classical).
  • September: Tbilisi Marathon (mid-September). Rtveli (grape harvest) in nearby vineyards.
  • October: Tbilisoba (first weekend) with folk music, dance, and wine. Tbilisi International Photography Festival.
  • November: Tbilisi International Short Film Festival.
  • December: Christmas markets and New Year’s Eve celebrations.

For tickets to events, check GetYourGuide for tours and festival passes.

Conclusion

So, what’s the best time to visit Tbilisi in 2026? If you want warm weather and vibrant energy, May and September are unbeatable—sunny, mild, and packed with festivals. For budget travelers, November to February offer cozy charm and low prices. Beach lovers should target July and August for Black Sea swims, while sightseers thrive in April–June or September–October.

No matter when you go, Tbilisi will welcome you with open arms, warm khachapuri, and a glass of amber wine. Safe travels!
