15 Best Things to Do in Kutaisi 2026

Updated: July 2026 • Expert guide • ⏱ 5 min read

If you’re planning a trip to Georgia in 2026, let me tell you why Kutaisi deserves a top spot on your list. This ancient city, one of the oldest continuously inhabited in the world, offers a perfect blend of history, nature, and culture—without the crowds of Tbilisi. From stunning UNESCO-listed cathedrals to lush parks and vibrant markets, Kutaisi feels like a hidden gem that’s just waiting to be explored. Plus, it’s incredibly affordable, with most attractions costing under ₽1 000 (≈ $11). Let me walk you through the best things to do, including practical tips to make your trip unforgettable.

15 Best Attractions

Here are my top picks for Kutaisi’s must-see spots, each with details to help you plan your visit. Remember, all prices are approximate estimates based on typical market rates—always double-check locally.

1. Bagrati Cathedral

This 11th-century cathedral, a UNESCO World Heritage site, sits atop Ukimerioni Hill and offers panoramic views of Kutaisi. The interior is simple but majestic, with restored frescoes and a peaceful atmosphere. It’s a symbol of Georgian unity and medieval architecture.

Ticket price: Free (donations welcome).
Hours: Daily 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM.
How to get there: A 15-minute uphill walk from the city center (Rustaveli Avenue) or take a taxi for about ₽300 (≈ $3).
Tip: Visit at sunset for golden-hour photos over the city.

2. Gelati Monastery

Just 7 km east of Kutaisi, this 12th-century monastery is a masterpiece of medieval Georgian art, known for its vibrant mosaics and frescoes. It’s also a UNESCO site and was once a major center of learning. The grounds are serene, with gardens and views of the surrounding hills.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Daily 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM.
How to get there: Take marshrutka #1 from the central bus station (₽20, ≈ $0.20) or a taxi for ₽700 (≈ $8).
Tip: Combine with a visit to Motsameta Monastery, just 3 km away.

3. Motsameta Monastery

Perched on a cliff above the Tskaltsitela River, this 11th-century monastery is dedicated to saints David and Constantine. Legend says the saints’ remains were carried here by lions. The setting is dramatic, with a small church and peaceful courtyard.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Daily 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM.
How to get there: Walk 30 minutes from Gelati Monastery along a scenic path, or take a taxi from Kutaisi for ₽500 (≈ $6).
Tip: Visit early morning to avoid crowds and enjoy the quiet.

4. Kutaisi State Historical Museum

This museum houses over 100,000 artifacts, including ancient Colchian gold, medieval manuscripts, and folk costumes. It’s a deep dive into Georgia’s rich history, from the Bronze Age to modern times. The exhibits are well-curated, with English descriptions.

Ticket price: ₽500 (≈ $6).
Hours: Tuesday–Sunday, 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM; closed Mondays.
How to get there: Located on Rustaveli Avenue, a 5-minute walk from the central square.
Tip: Check for temporary exhibitions on Georgian art.

5. Colchis Fountain

This modern fountain in the central square features golden statues of mythical creatures and historical figures from Colchis, the ancient kingdom. It’s a popular meeting spot and great for photos, especially lit up at night. The square itself is lively with cafes and street performers.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Always open.
How to get there: Central location, easily walkable from anywhere in the city center.
Tip: Visit in the evening when the fountain is illuminated.

6. Sataplia Nature Reserve

This protected area is famous for its dinosaur footprints, caves, and lush forest trails. The reserve features a glass-bottomed platform over a canyon, offering breathtaking views. It’s a perfect half-day trip from the city.

Ticket price: ₽1 500 (≈ $16) for adults, including cave entrance.
Hours: Daily 10:00 AM – 6:00 PM (last entry 5:00 PM).
How to get there: Take marshrutka #10 from the central bus station (₽30, ≈ $0.30) or a taxi for ₽800 (≈ $9).
Tip: Wear sturdy shoes—trails can be slippery after rain.

7. Prometheus Cave

One of Georgia’s largest caves, Prometheus features stunning stalactites, stalagmites, and underground rivers. A guided tour takes you through illuminated chambers, and you can take a boat ride on the subterranean lake. It’s a magical experience.

Ticket price: ₽2 000 (≈ $22) for adults, including boat ride.
Hours: Daily 10:00 AM – 6:00 PM (last tour 5:00 PM).
How to get there: Take a taxi from Kutaisi for ₽1 000 (≈ $11) or join a tour from the city center.
Tip: Book online via GetYourGuide to skip queues.

8. Okatse Canyon

This dramatic canyon features a 780-meter-long cliffside walkway with glass platforms, offering vertigo-inducing views of the gorge. The trail ends at a waterfall, and the surrounding forest is teeming with wildlife. It’s a must for adventure lovers.

Ticket price: ₽1 200 (≈ $13) for the walkway.
Hours: Daily 10:00 AM – 6:00 PM.
How to get there: Take a taxi from Kutaisi for ₽1 500 (≈ $16) or join a tour via GetYourGuide.
Tip: Visit early to avoid crowds—the walkway can get busy by noon.

9. Martvili Canyon

This emerald-green canyon offers boat rides through narrow gorges and past waterfalls. The scenery is stunning, with limestone cliffs and lush vegetation. It’s a popular spot for swimming in summer (bring a swimsuit).

Ticket price: ₽1 500 (≈ $16) for boat ride; ₽500 (≈ $6) for walking trail.
Hours: Daily 10:00 AM – 5:00 PM.
How to get there: Take a taxi from Kutaisi for ₽1 800 (≈ $20) or join a tour via GetYourGuide.
Tip: Combine with Okatse Canyon for a full day trip—book a private taxi for around ₽3 000 (≈ $33).

10. Kutaisi Botanical Garden

This peaceful garden on the banks of the Rioni River features exotic plants, shaded paths, and a small waterfall. It’s perfect for a relaxing stroll or a picnic. The garden is well-maintained and less crowded than other attractions.

Ticket price: ₽200 (≈ $2).
Hours: Daily 9:00 AM – 7:00 PM.
How to get there: A 10-minute walk from the central square, near the river.
Tip: Bring a book and enjoy the quiet benches.

11. White Bridge

This iconic pedestrian bridge over the Rioni River was built in the 19th century and offers great views of the city. It’s a popular spot for evening walks and photos, especially during sunset. The bridge connects the old town to newer districts.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Always open.
How to get there: Walk 5 minutes from the central square.
Tip: Visit at dusk for a romantic atmosphere.

12. Kutaisi Central Market

This bustling market is a sensory overload—fresh produce, spices, cheese, and local crafts fill the stalls. You’ll find everything from churchkhela (Georgian candy) to handmade souvenirs. It’s a great place to interact with locals and taste authentic flavors.

Ticket price: Free entry; budget ₽500–₽1 000 (≈ $6–$11) for shopping.
Hours: Daily 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM.
How to get there: A 10-minute walk from the central square, near the bus station.
Tip: Try the fresh sulguni cheese and khachapuri from a bakery stall.

13. Kutaisi Synagogue

Built in the 19th century, this synagogue is one of the largest in Georgia and reflects the city’s historic Jewish community. The interior is ornate, with colorful stained glass and a peaceful atmosphere. It’s a unique cultural site.

Ticket price: Free (donations welcome).
Hours: By appointment; contact the local Jewish community center.
How to get there: A 5-minute walk from the central square, on Leselidze Street.
Tip: Call ahead to arrange a visit—it’s not always open to the public.

14. Bagrati Cathedral Park

The park surrounding Bagrati Cathedral offers walking paths, benches, and panoramic views of Kutaisi and the Rioni River. It’s a great spot for a picnic or a quiet afternoon. The park is well-maintained and family-friendly.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Always open.
How to get there: Same as Bagrati Cathedral—uphill walk or taxi.
Tip: Bring snacks and enjoy a sunset picnic.

15. Rioni River Walk

This scenic path along the Rioni River offers a peaceful escape from the city center. You’ll see locals fishing, kids playing, and beautiful views of the hills. It’s perfect for a morning jog or an evening stroll.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Always open.
How to get there: Start from the White Bridge and follow the river south.
Tip: Look for the small cafes along the path for cheap coffee.

1-Day, 2-Day, 3-Day Itineraries

Here are optimal city routes to make the most of your time in Kutaisi. These itineraries focus on walking and public transport.

1-Day Itinerary

  • Morning (9:00 AM – 12:00 PM): Start at Bagrati Cathedral and its park (free). Walk down to the Colchis Fountain for photos.
  • Lunch (12:00 PM – 1:00 PM): Eat at a local café near the fountain—try khinkali for around ₽500 (≈ $6).
  • Afternoon (1:00 PM – 4:00 PM): Visit Kutaisi State Historical Museum (₽500, ≈ $6). Then walk to the White Bridge and Rioni River Walk.
  • Evening (4:00 PM – 6:00 PM): Explore Kutaisi Central Market for souvenirs. End with a taxi to Sataplia Nature Reserve (₽1 500, ≈ $16) for a quick cave visit.
  • Dinner (7:00 PM): Enjoy dinner at a traditional Georgian restaurant like Palaty, budget ₽1 000 (≈ $11).

2-Day Itinerary

Day 1: Follow the 1-day itinerary above, but skip Sataplia and add a visit to Motsameta Monastery (free) in the afternoon via taxi (₽500, ≈ $6).

Day 2:

  • Morning (9:00 AM – 12:00 PM): Take a marshrutka to Gelati Monastery (free). Spend time exploring the frescoes and gardens.
  • Lunch (12:00 PM – 1:00 PM): Eat at a café near Gelati—try lobiani for ₽300 (≈ $3).
  • Afternoon (1:00 PM – 5:00 PM): Visit Sataplia Nature Reserve (₽1 500, ≈ $16). Walk the trails and see the dinosaur footprints.
  • Evening (6:00 PM): Relax at Kutaisi Botanical Garden (₽200, ≈ $2). Dinner at a riverside restaurant.

3-Day Itinerary

Day 1: Same as Day 1 of the 2-day itinerary.

Day 2: Same as Day 2 of the 2-day itinerary.

Day 3:

  • Morning (8:00 AM – 12:00 PM): Book a taxi for a combined trip to Prometheus Cave (₽2 000, ≈ $22) and Okatse Canyon (₽1 200, ≈ $13). The taxi will cost around ₽3 000 (≈ $33) round trip.
  • Lunch (12:00 PM – 1:00 PM): Pack a picnic or eat at a café near Okatse.
  • Afternoon (1:00 PM – 4:00 PM): Visit Martvili Canyon (₽1 500, ≈ $16) for a boat ride. Return to Kutaisi by 5:00 PM.
  • Evening (6:00 PM): Explore the Kutaisi Synagogue (free, by appointment) and enjoy a farewell dinner.

FAQ

How much do attractions cost in Kutaisi?

Entrance fees vary: from free attractions to $20-30 for museums. Many venues have discount days.

Do I need a guide in Kutaisi?

A guide is recommended for historical sites and complex routes. Self-guided travel is possible with audio guides and maps.
