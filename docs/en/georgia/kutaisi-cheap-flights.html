If you’re dreaming of exploring Georgia’s ancient cities, lush vineyards, and rugged Caucasus mountains without breaking the bank, Kutaisi is your gateway. This charming city, home to the UNESCO-listed Bagrati Cathedral and the stunning Prometheus Cave, has become a budget-friendly hub for travelers. As someone who’s spent countless hours hunting for flight deals, I’m here to share everything you need to know about snagging cheap flights to Kutaisi in 2026. Let’s dive in—your adventure starts with a smart booking strategy.

Kutaisi Airport and Airlines

Kutaisi is served by Kutaisi International Airport (KUT), a compact but efficient hub located about 15 km west of the city center. It’s the main low-cost gateway to Georgia, with a handful of airlines offering both direct and connecting flights. The airport is small, so expect quick check-ins and minimal queues—perfect for budget travelers. Key airlines serving Kutaisi include:

  • Wizz Air – The primary low-cost carrier, connecting Kutaisi to numerous European cities.
  • Ryanair – Offers seasonal and year-round routes from select European bases.
  • Georgian Airways – Provides limited direct flights from Tbilisi and a few international destinations.
  • FlyOne – A Moldovan carrier with occasional budget options via Chisinau.
  • Pegasus Airlines – Connects via Istanbul Sabiha Gökçen for affordable layovers.

Most flights are operated by low-cost carriers, so expect add-ons for luggage, seat selection, and meals. But with the right timing, you can score incredible deals.

Direct Flights

Direct flights to Kutaisi are limited but growing. In 2026, expect these routes from Wizz Air and Ryanair:

  • From Europe: Wizz Air offers direct flights from cities like Budapest, Warsaw, Vienna, Milan (Bergamo), Rome (Ciampino), Berlin (Brandenburg), London (Luton), Paris (Beauvais), and Barcelona. Prices start at around ₽7 000 (≈ $75) for one-way tickets during sales, but average round-trip fares range from ₽18 000 to ₽35 000 (≈ $195–$380).
  • From the Middle East: Ryanair occasionally operates from Tel Aviv and Abu Dhabi, with one-way fares from ₽9 000 (≈ $100).
  • From Eastern Europe: Wizz Air connects Kutaisi from Kyiv (when operational), Chisinau, and Larnaca, with prices from ₽5 500 (≈ $60).

Direct flights are the fastest option—typically 3–5 hours from Europe—but they book up quickly. To find the best deals, I recommend checking Aviasales for real-time price comparisons across all airlines.

Connecting Flights

If direct flights are sold out or too pricey, connecting flights are your best bet. They’re often cheaper, especially if you’re flexible with layovers. Here are the cheapest options for 2026:

  • Via Istanbul (IST or SAW): Turkish Airlines and Pegasus offer connections from North America, Asia, and Europe. A round-trip from New York or Toronto can cost ₽45 000–₽70 000 (≈ $490–$760), with layovers of 3–10 hours. From London, prices drop to ₽20 000–₽30 000 (≈ $220–$330) via Pegasus.
  • Via Dubai (DXB): Flydubai connects from Asia and Australia. A round-trip from Bangkok starts at ₽25 000 (≈ $270), with a 4–8 hour layover.
  • Via Moscow (SVO or VKO): Aeroflot and S7 Airlines offer connections from CIS countries. Prices from Almaty are around ₽15 000 (≈ $160), but check visa requirements.
  • Via Chisinau (RMO): FlyOne and Wizz Air connect from Eastern Europe. From Warsaw, a one-way can be as low as ₽4 500 (≈ $50).

10 Hacks for Cheap Flights

After years of trial and error, I’ve compiled my top 10 hacks for scoring cheap flights to Kutaisi. These aren’t just tips—they’re strategies I use every time I book.

  1. Use Price Comparison Sites: Start with Aviasales to compare hundreds of airlines and dates. Set up price alerts for your route—they’ll email you when fares drop. For example, a London–Kutaisi flight can go from ₽25 000 (≈ $270) to ₽15 000 (≈ $160) overnight.
  2. Book Connecting Flights via Istanbul: Istanbul is a major hub for Kutaisi. Pegasus Airlines often has sales, with round-trips from ₽12 000 (≈ $130) from European cities. Check Aviasales for multi-city options too.
  3. Fly on Tuesdays or Wednesdays: Mid-week flights are consistently cheaper. For instance, a Tuesday departure from Berlin can save you ₽5 000–₽8 000 (≈ $55–$90) compared to a Friday.
  4. Use Incognito Mode: Airlines and booking sites track your searches. Open a private browser window to avoid price hikes based on your history.
  5. Consider Nearby Airports: If Kutaisi is expensive, check Tbilisi (TBS) or Batumi (BUS). A bus from Tbilisi to Kutaisi costs ₽1 000 (≈ $11) and takes 3 hours. Use Aviasales to compare all Georgian airports.
  6. Book with Wizz Air’s Discount Club: For frequent travelers, the Wizz Air Discount Club offers 10% off fares and priority boarding. A round-trip from Warsaw drops from ₽14 000 (≈ $150) to ₽12 600 (≈ $137).
  7. Use Kiwitaxi for Airport Transfers: After landing, Kiwitaxi offers fixed-price transfers from Kutaisi Airport to the city center for ₽1 500 (≈ $16)—cheaper than taxis and stress-free.
  8. Pack Light to Avoid Fees: Low-cost carriers charge for checked luggage. A carry-on with Wizz Air costs ₽2 000–₽4 000 (≈ $22–$44) if added online, but ₽6 000 (≈ $65) at the airport. Pack a backpack to save.
  9. Use Airalo for eSIM Data: Stay connected without roaming fees. Airalo offers a Georgia eSIM for ₽500 (≈ $5.50) for 1 GB—perfect for navigating Kutaisi.
  10. Book Tours via GetYourGuide: Once you’re there, GetYourGuide has affordable day trips to Prometheus Cave or Svaneti. Book in advance for discounts up to 20%.

When to Book

Timing is everything for cheap flights. Based on 2026 data, here’s my advice:

  • Optimal Booking Window: Book 6–8 weeks before departure for the best prices. For peak summer (July–August), book 3–4 months in advance. For low season (January–February), 4–6 weeks is fine.
  • Best Time of Year: February is the cheapest month, with round-trips from ₽13 000 (≈ $140). Avoid July and August, when prices double.
  • Day of the Week: Tuesday and Wednesday departures are cheapest. Avoid Friday and Sunday.
  • Sales and Alerts: Wizz Air holds flash sales every few months—sign up for their newsletter. Set alerts on Aviasales and Hotellook to catch drops.
  • Last-Minute Deals: If you’re flexible, check 2–3 weeks before departure. I’ve snagged a ₽9 000 (≈ $100) one-way from Milan by waiting.

Remember, Aviasales is your best friend for comparing prices across months. For example, a flight from London in February might cost ₽14 000 (≈ $150), but the same route in July is ₽40 000 (≈ $435). Plan accordingly.
