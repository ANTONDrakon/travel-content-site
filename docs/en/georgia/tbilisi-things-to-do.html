15 Best Things to Do in Tbilisi 2026

I first visited Tbilisi on a whim, and it completely stole my heart. Nestled in a dramatic river gorge and framed by the Caucasus Mountains, this city blends ancient history with a vibrant, artsy soul. From sulfur baths to modernist architecture, every corner feels like a story waiting to be told. In 2026, Tbilisi is more accessible than ever, with new cafes, galleries, and experiences popping up. Here’s my personal guide to the 15 best things to do, plus itineraries, tours, and practical tips to help you plan your trip.

Why Visit Tbilisi for Its Attractions

Tbilisi isn’t just a city—it’s a living museum where you can wander from a 4th-century fortress to a futuristic bridge in 20 minutes. The attractions here range from ancient Orthodox monasteries to quirky Soviet-era sculptures, and the best part? Many are completely free. Whether you’re soaking in natural hot springs, sampling khinkali dumplings, or riding a cable car up to a hilltop statue, Tbilisi offers a sensory overload that’s both chaotic and charming. The city’s affordability (a metro ride costs about ₽30 ≈ $0.35) makes it easy to explore without breaking the bank.

15 Best Attractions

1. Narikala Fortress

Perched on a hill overlooking the Mtkvari River, Narikala Fortress is the soul of Tbilisi. Dating back to the 4th century, it offers panoramic views of the colorful Old Town and modern districts. The fortress has been rebuilt multiple times, but its weathered stone walls and towers still feel ancient and mysterious. Ticket price: Free. Hours: Open 24/7. How to get there: Take the cable car from Rike Park (₽200 ≈ $2.50 round trip) or hike up from the Abanotubani district. Tip: Visit at sunset when the golden light hits the city—it’s magical.

2. Sulfur Baths (Abanotubani)

No trip to Tbilisi is complete without a soak in the sulfur baths, which have been a tradition since the 17th century. The district of Abanotubani is dotted with domed brick bathhouses, each with private rooms where you can relax in warm, mineral-rich water. The experience is both rejuvenating and deeply local. Ticket price: ₽1 500–₽4 000 (≈ $17–$45) per hour for a private room, depending on the bathhouse. Hours: Most open 9:00 AM–11:00 PM. How to get there: Walk from Freedom Square down to the river; the baths are clustered near the mosque. Tip: Book a basic scrub and massage package at Chreli Abano for a full experience.

3. The Bridge of Peace

This futuristic glass-and-steel bridge connects Rike Park to the Old Town, creating a striking contrast with the ancient fortress behind it. Designed by Italian architect Michele De Lucchi, it’s illuminated by thousands of LEDs at night, making it a favorite spot for evening strolls. Ticket price: Free. Hours: Open 24/7. How to get there: Cross from the Metekhi Bridge or walk from the Avlabari metro station. Tip: Come after dark to see the bridge’s light show—it changes colors every hour.

4. Sameba Cathedral

One of the largest Orthodox cathedrals in the world, Sameba (Holy Trinity) is a stunning example of modern Georgian architecture. Built in the early 2000s, its golden dome dominates the skyline, and the interior is adorned with intricate frescoes and icons. Ticket price: Free. Hours: 7:00 AM–8:00 PM daily. How to get there: Take bus #122 from Freedom Square or a 15-minute walk from the Avlabari metro station. Tip: Dress modestly—women should bring a scarf to cover their heads.

5. Tbilisi Old Town (Dzveli Tbilisi)

Wandering through the narrow, cobblestone streets of the Old Town is like stepping into a fairy tale. Colorful wooden balconies, hidden courtyards, and vine-covered cafes make every corner photogenic. The area is compact and easy to explore on foot. Ticket price: Free to wander; some museums cost ₽200–₽500 (≈ $2–$6). Hours: Always open. How to get there: Start at Freedom Square and walk toward the river. Tip: Look for the “Peace” mural on the side of a building near Sioni Street—it’s a local Instagram favorite.

6. Freedom Square (Tavisuplebis Moedani)

The central hub of Tbilisi, Freedom Square is dominated by a towering statue of St. George slaying a dragon. It’s surrounded by elegant 19th-century buildings, including the City Hall and the National Museum. The square hosts festivals and markets throughout the year. Ticket price: Free. Hours: Open 24/7. How to get there: It’s the main square—just look for the gold statue. Tip: Grab a coffee at Cafe Leila nearby and people-watch for an hour.

7. National Museum of Georgia

This museum houses the country’s most important archaeological treasures, including the famous “Golden Fleece” artifacts and prehistoric gold jewelry. The exhibits cover Georgian history from the Stone Age to the Soviet era. Ticket price: ₽700 (≈ $8) for adults. Hours: 10:00 AM–6:00 PM, closed Mondays. How to get there: On Rustaveli Avenue, a 5-minute walk from Freedom Square. Tip: Don’t miss the Soviet Occupation Hall—it’s a sobering but essential part of Georgian history.

8. Mtatsminda Park

Perched on a mountain above the city, Mtatsminda is an amusement park with a retro vibe. The main attraction is the Ferris wheel, which offers jaw-dropping views of Tbilisi. The park also has a restaurant, a small zoo, and a funicular that’s an attraction itself. Ticket price: Funicular round trip: ₽400 (≈ $5); park rides: ₽200–₽500 (≈ $2–$6) each. Hours: Funicular runs 9:00 AM–midnight; park open 10:00 AM–10:00 PM. How to get there: Take the funicular from the lower station near Rustaveli Avenue. Tip: Visit on a clear day for the best views—the park is quieter on weekdays.

9. Dry Bridge Market

This open-air flea market is a treasure trove of Soviet-era memorabilia, antiques, and handmade crafts. You’ll find everything from old cameras to enamel pins, and haggling is expected. It’s a great place to pick up unique souvenirs. Ticket price: Free to browse. Hours: Weekends 10:00 AM–5:00 PM; smaller selection on weekdays. How to get there: Walk from the Avlabari metro station or cross the Dry Bridge from the Old Town. Tip: Bring cash in small bills—many vendors don’t accept cards.

10. Fabrika Hostel & Courtyard

Once a Soviet sewing factory, Fabrika is now a vibrant hostel and cultural hub with a courtyard filled with cafes, bars, and street art. It’s a favorite spot for young travelers and digital nomads. Even if you’re not staying here, the courtyard is worth a visit. Ticket price: Free. Hours: Courtyard open 24/7; cafes open 9:00 AM–late. How to get there: On Egnate Ninoshvili Street, a 10-minute walk from Marjanishvili metro station. Tip: Try the craft beer at the Fabrika bar—it’s locally brewed.

11. Tbilisi Botanical Garden

Spread over 128 hectares, the botanical garden is a lush escape from the city’s noise. It features a waterfall, a greenhouse with tropical plants, and winding paths through forests and flower gardens. Ticket price: ₽200 (≈ $2.50). Hours: 9:00 AM–8:00 PM daily (hours may vary in winter). How to get there: Walk from the Narikala Fortress or enter from the Sololaki district. Tip: Pack a picnic—there are plenty of shady spots near the waterfall.

12. Rike Park

This modern park on the riverbank is a popular spot for families and couples. It has a playground, a concert hall, and a cable car station. The park’s futuristic design, with its curving pathways and light installations, makes it a pleasant place to relax. Ticket price: Free. Hours: Open 24/7. How to get there: Cross the Bridge of Peace from the Old Town. Tip: Rent a bike from the park’s rental station (₽300 ≈ $3.50 per hour) and ride along the river.

13. Anchiskhati Basilica

This is the oldest surviving church in Tbilisi, dating back to the 6th century. Its simple brick exterior belies a serene interior with ancient icons and frescoes. The church is still active, so you may hear chanting during services. Ticket price: Free. Hours: 9:00 AM–6:00 PM daily. How to get there: On Ioane Shavteli Street, a short walk from the Old Town. Tip: Visit during a weekday morning to avoid crowds.

14. Tbilisi Opera and Ballet Theatre

An architectural gem in the heart of the city, the opera house hosts world-class performances in a stunning neo-Moorish interior. Even if you don’t attend a show, the building’s facade is worth a photo. Ticket price: Shows from ₽1 000–₽5 000 (≈ $11–$56) depending on seat. Hours: Box office open 11:00 AM–7:00 PM; performances usually at 7:00 PM. How to get there: On Rustaveli Avenue, near Freedom Square. Tip: Book tickets in advance through the theatre’s website—popular shows sell out.

15. Turtle Lake

A serene lake just outside the city center, Turtle Lake is perfect for a half-day escape. You can hike around the lake, rent a paddleboat, or simply enjoy the views. The name comes from the turtles that live in the water. Ticket price: Free; paddleboat rental ₽500 (≈ $6) per hour. Hours: Open 24/7. How to get there: Take bus #61 from the Vake district or a taxi (₽500–₽800 ≈ $6–$9). Tip: Bring a swimsuit in summer—the lake is clean and swimmable.

1-Day, 2-Day, 3-Day Itineraries

1-Day Itinerary: Tbilisi Highlights

  • Morning (9:00 AM–12:00 PM): Start at Freedom Square, then walk to the Old Town. Explore Anchiskhati Basilica and the sulfur bath district (Abanotubani).
  • Lunch (12:00–1:00 PM): Grab khinkali at a local spot like Pasanauri (₽500 ≈ $6 for 5 dumplings).
  • Afternoon (1:00–5:00 PM): Take the cable car to Narikala Fortress (₽200 round trip), then walk down to the Botanical Garden (₽200 entry). End at Rike Park and cross the Bridge of Peace.
  • Evening (6:00–9:00 PM): Visit Fabrika courtyard for drinks, then enjoy dinner at a traditional Georgian restaurant like Shavi Lomi (₽1 500–₽2 500 ≈ $17–$28 per person).

2-Day Itinerary: Culture & Relaxation

  • Day 1: Follow the 1-day itinerary above.
  • Day 2 Morning (9:00 AM–1:00 PM): Visit Sameba Cathedral and the National Museum (₽700 entry).
  • Day 2 Afternoon (1:00–5:00 PM): Head to the Dry Bridge Market (free to browse), then take the funicular to Mtatsminda Park (₽400 round trip).
  • Day 2 Evening: Book a sulfur bath at Chreli Abano (₽3 000 ≈ $34 for a 1-hour session), then have a late dinner at Cafe Leila.

3-Day Itinerary: Full Exploration

  • Day 1 & 2: Follow the 2-day itinerary.
  • Day 3 Morning (9:00 AM–12:00 PM): Take a taxi to Turtle Lake (free entry, ₽500 for paddleboat).
  • Day 3 Afternoon (12:00–4:00 PM): Explore the Tbilisi Opera House (book a tour if available, ₽500 ≈ $6), then walk through the Sololaki district for street art.
  • Day 3 Evening: Catch a performance at the opera (₽1 000–₽5 000) or enjoy a sunset drink at a rooftop bar like Stamba (₽800 ≈ $9 for a cocktail).

Free Attractions

Tbilisi is incredibly budget-friendly. Here are the top free attractions:

  • Narikala Fortress: Free to explore (cable car costs ₽200).
  • Bridge of Peace: Free, best at night.
  • Sameba Cathedral: Free entry.
  • Old Town Streets: Free to wander; no ticket needed.
  • Freedom Square: Free public space.
  • Dry Bridge Market: Free to browse (bring cash for purchases).
  • Fabrika Courtyard: Free to enter and enjoy the street art.
  • Rike Park: Free, with river views.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do attractions cost in Tbilisi?

Entrance fees vary: from free attractions to $20-30 for museums. Many venues have discount days.

Do I need a guide in Tbilisi?

A guide is recommended for historical sites and complex routes. Self-guided travel is possible with audio guides and maps.
