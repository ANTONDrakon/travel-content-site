Kutaisi, Georgia’s ancient capital and third-largest city, is a gem of the Caucasus that blends rich history with a relaxed, modern vibe. Nestled along the Rioni River and surrounded by lush hills, it offers a more authentic, less crowded alternative to Tbilisi. The city’s hotel scene has blossomed in recent years, with options ranging from cozy family-run guesthouses to sleek boutique properties and even a few luxury escapes. Whether you’re here to explore the UNESCO-listed Gelati Monastery, soak in the sulfur baths, or use Kutaisi as a base for hiking in the nearby Svaneti region, finding the right accommodation can make or break your trip.

The city’s neighborhoods each have their own character. The historic center, around the main avenue and old town, is perfect for sightseeing, with restaurants, cafes, and attractions within walking distance. The area near the Kutaisi International Airport (about 20 minutes from the center) is quieter and more residential, ideal for early flights. Prices are generally affordable—budget rooms start around ₽3 500 (≈ 380 GEL / $40), mid-range options hover between ₽7 000 and ₽14 000 (≈ 760–1,520 GEL / $80–170), and luxury suites can reach ₽25 000 (≈ 2,700 GEL / $300) per night. To get the best deals, you can compare hotel prices in Kutaisi on Hotellook or check Booking.com for flexible cancellation options. Hotel prices in Kutaisi can be surprisingly low compared to other European cities, making it a great value destination for 2026.

Budget Hotels (under $50/night)

1. Hotel Old Town Kutaisi

This charming guesthouse sits right in the historic heart of Kutaisi, just a 5-minute walk from the famous Colchis Fountain and the central market. Rooms are simple but spotless, with traditional Georgian textiles and wooden furniture. The staff is incredibly welcoming—they often offer homemade wine and churchkhela (a local candy) upon arrival. Breakfast is included and features fresh khachapuri, cheeses, and seasonal fruits.

Price: ₽3 500–₽5 000 (≈ 380–545 GEL / $40–55) per night.

  • Pros: Central location, free Wi-Fi, friendly hosts, authentic atmosphere.
  • Cons: No elevator (stairs only), basic bathrooms, street noise in some rooms.

2. Guesthouse Lile

A family-run guesthouse in a quiet residential area about 10 minutes from the city center. The property has a lovely garden with grapevines and a terrace where guests can relax. Rooms are spacious and include a small kitchenette, making it a good choice for longer stays or families. The owners are known for their hospitality, often sharing local tips and arranging taxi transfers.

Price: ₽4 000–₽6 000 (≈ 435–655 GEL / $45–70) per night.

  • Pros: Kitchenette, garden, quiet neighborhood, free parking.
  • Cons: No breakfast included, a bit far from main attractions, limited public transport.

3. Hostel Kutaisi

A modern hostel with both dormitory beds and private rooms, located near the central bus station. It’s popular with backpackers and solo travelers for its social vibe—there’s a common room, rooftop terrace, and organized pub crawls. Private rooms offer good value for couples or friends who want privacy on a budget. The staff speaks English and can help with tour bookings.

Price: ₽1 200–₽2 500 (≈ 130–272 GEL / $14–28) per night for dorms; ₽3 000–₽4 500 (≈ 327–490 GEL / $35–52) for private rooms.

  • Pros: Cheap dorms, social atmosphere, free breakfast, lockers.
  • Cons: Noisy at night, shared bathrooms, limited privacy in dorms.

Mid-Range Hotels ($50-150/night)

4. Kutaisi Inn Hotel

A stylish boutique hotel located on the main avenue, a 10-minute walk from the old town. The design blends modern minimalism with Georgian accents—think exposed brick walls, local art, and warm lighting. Rooms feature comfortable beds, smart TVs, and rain showers. The on-site restaurant serves excellent Georgian cuisine, and the bar offers a good selection of local wines. It’s a favorite among business travelers and couples.

Price: ₽7 000–₽10 000 (≈ 760–1,090 GEL / $80–120) per night.

  • Highlights: Great restaurant, soundproof windows, free parking, 24-hour reception.

5. Hotel Terrace Kutaisi

Perched on a hill overlooking the city, this hotel offers panoramic views of Kutaisi and the surrounding mountains. The rooms are spacious and modern, with large windows and balconies. The highlight is the rooftop terrace, perfect for sunset drinks. The hotel also has a small spa with a sauna and massage services. It’s about a 15-minute walk from the center, but a bit of a climb on foot.

Price: ₽8 500–₽12 000 (≈ 925–1,305 GEL / $100–140) per night.

  • Highlights: Rooftop terrace, spa, free breakfast, stunning views.

6. Argo Palace Hotel

A well-established hotel in a quiet area near the Rioni River, a 20-minute walk from the old town. The property has a classic, slightly old-fashioned charm with marble floors, chandeliers, and a grand staircase. Rooms are large and include sitting areas, mini-bars, and tea/coffee makers. The hotel has a decent fitness center and a restaurant serving both Georgian and European dishes. It’s a solid choice for families or travelers seeking comfort without frills.

Price: ₽6 000–₽9 000 (≈ 653–980 GEL / $70–105) per night.

  • Highlights: Spacious rooms, fitness center, river views, free parking.

7. Hotel Tergi

A modern hotel located near the central market, within walking distance of the Bagrati Cathedral and old town. The rooms are sleek and minimalist, with neutral tones and wooden accents. The hotel offers a good buffet breakfast with both Georgian and continental options. The staff is professional and can arrange airport transfers or city tours. It’s a great value for the quality.

Price: ₽7 500–₽11 000 (≈ 816–1,200 GEL / $85–130) per night.

  • Highlights: Central location, modern design, free Wi-Fi, good breakfast.

Luxury Hotels ($150+/night)

8. Hotel Paliani

The most prestigious hotel in Kutaisi, located in a restored 19th-century mansion near the city center. It offers a boutique luxury experience with only 12 suites, each uniquely decorated with antique furniture, chandeliers, and original artworks. The property features a lush garden, a wine cellar with over 200 Georgian wines, and a fine-dining restaurant serving modern Georgian cuisine. There’s also a spa with a hammam, sauna, and massage rooms. The service is impeccable, with a concierge who can arrange private tours, helicopter rides, or even a private chef.

Price: ₽20 000–₽35 000 (≈ 2,180–3,810 GEL / $230–400) per night.

  • Features: Historic mansion, wine cellar, spa, garden, private tours.

9. Kutaisi Marriott Hotel

Part of the international Marriott chain, this hotel opened in 2024 and quickly became a top choice for business travelers and luxury seekers. It’s located on the main avenue, with easy access to shops, restaurants, and attractions. The rooms are spacious and contemporary, with floor-to-ceiling windows, premium bedding, and marble bathrooms. Amenities include a rooftop pool with city views, a fitness center, a full-service spa, and multiple dining options, including a steakhouse and a lounge bar. The staff is multilingual and highly professional.

Price: ₽18 000–₽28 000 (≈ 1,960–3,050 GEL / $200–320) per night.

  • Features: Rooftop pool, spa, multiple restaurants, business center.

10. Hotel Balcony Kutaisi

A boutique luxury hotel with a focus on design and local culture, located in a renovated building near the old town. Each room has a private balcony overlooking the city or the Rioni River. The interiors blend industrial elements (exposed pipes, concrete walls) with warm textures (wool rugs, linen curtains). The hotel has a small art gallery, a rooftop bar, and a restaurant that sources ingredients from local farms. The staff can organize cooking classes, wine tastings, or hiking trips to the nearby Okatse Canyon.

Price: ₽15 000–₽22 000 (≈ 1,630–2,400 GEL / $170–250) per night.

  • Features: Private balconies, rooftop bar, art gallery, local experiences.

How to Choose the Right Area

For Sightseeing (History & Culture)

Stay in the old town (around Colchis Fountain and Bagrati Cathedral). This area is compact and walkable, with most attractions—Gelati Monastery (a short taxi ride), the Kutaisi State Historical Museum, and the central market—within easy reach. Hotels like Hotel Old Town Kutaisi or Hotel Tergi are ideal. You’ll also find plenty of restaurants and cafes for breaks between sightseeing.

For Nature & Outdoor Activities

If you’re planning to explore the surrounding nature—such as the Prometheus Cave, Okatse Canyon, or the Sataplia Nature Reserve—choose a hotel near the airport road or the eastern outskirts of the city. These areas offer quicker access to the main highways leading to natural attractions. Guesthouse Lile or Hotel Terrace Kutaisi are good options, as they have parking and are close to nature trails.

For Nightlife & Dining

The main avenue (Rustaveli Avenue) and the area around the central square are where most bars, clubs, and restaurants are concentrated. Hotels like Kutaisi Inn Hotel or Kutaisi Marriott Hotel put you right in the action. For a quieter evening, the old town has cozy wine bars and live music venues, but they close earlier. If you prefer a lively scene, stay near the avenue.

  • Check cancellation policies: Free cancellation is common on Booking.com and Agoda. Book a refundable rate, then rebook if a better deal appears closer to your stay.
