Welcome to Paphos, a sun-drenched coastal gem on the southwestern coast of Cyprus where ancient mythology meets modern Mediterranean living. As the legendary birthplace of Aphrodite, the goddess of love, this UNESCO World Heritage city offers a magical blend of archaeological wonders, turquoise waters, and a laid-back vibe that instantly makes you feel at home.

Walking through the marina, you'll catch the scent of salt and jasmine, while the distant sound of waves lapping against ancient harbour walls sets the rhythm of the day. Paphos isn't just a destination; it's a feeling—one of warmth, history, and unhurried joy.

Why is 2026 the year to visit? Because this is the year the city is truly coming into its own. New boutique hotels have opened along the coast, the promenade has been beautifully revamped, and the local food scene is buzzing with farm-to-table restaurants. Yet Paphos retains its authentic Cypriot soul—you'll still find grandmothers selling handmade lace in the old market and tavernas where the owner brings you a complimentary dessert just because.

Whether you're a history buff eager to explore Tombs of the Kings, a beach lover craving golden sands, or a foodie ready to dive into halloumi and souvlaki, Paphos delivers with a smile. Plus, with direct flights from dozens of European cities and a warm climate almost year-round, it's an easy, affordable escape.

This guide is your personal roadmap to making the most of your trip. I've lived and travelled in Paphos extensively, and I'll share insider tips, honest prices, and practical advice to help you plan like a pro. From finding the best hotel for your budget to discovering hidden tavernas and booking unforgettable excursions, consider this your best friend in digital form. Let's dive in.

How to Get There

The main gateway is Paphos International Airport (PFO), located about 15 km southeast of the city centre. It's a modern, easy-to-navigate airport serving flights from major hubs like London, Moscow, Berlin, Dubai, and Tel Aviv. Average round-trip fares from Moscow start at around ₽45 000 (≈ $514 (€450)) in low season, rising to ₽65 000 (≈ $743 (€650)) in summer.

Where to Stay

Paphos is divided into two main areas: Kato Paphos (Lower Paphos) near the harbour and archaeological sites, and Ktima (Upper Paphos), the old town with local shops and cafes. For first-timers, staying in Kato Paphos is ideal—you're walking distance to the waterfront, castle, and most attractions. Budget travellers love the Pyramos Hotel (around ₽4 500 per night, ≈ $51 (€45)) for its clean rooms and central location.

Mid-range options include the King's Palace Hotel (₽10 000 per night, ≈ $114 (€100)), which has a lovely pool and sea views. Luxury seekers should check out the Elysium Hotel (₽25 000 per night, ≈ $286 (€250)), a five-star resort with private beach access and world-class spa. For apartments, the Coral Bay area is quieter and cheaper, with studios from ₽3 500 per night (≈ $40 (€35)). Always compare prices on Hotellook or Booking to snag the best rates. Top 15 Attractions

  • 1. Paphos Archaeological Park — A sprawling UNESCO site with stunning Roman mosaics, including the House of Dionysus. You'll need at least two hours to explore. Ticket: ₽8 500 (≈ $10 (€8.50)). Open daily 8:30 AM–7:30 PM (summer), 8:30 AM–5 PM (winter).
  • 2. Tombs of the Kings — An ancient necropolis carved from solid rock, dating back to the 4th century BC. It's eerie and awe-inspiring. Ticket: ₽4 500 (≈ $5 (€4.50)). Open 8:30 AM–7:30 PM (summer), 8:30 AM–5 PM (winter).
  • 3. Paphos Harbour & Castle — The iconic medieval fortress by the sea. Climb to the top for panoramic views. Free to walk around; castle entrance ₽4 000 (≈ $5 (€4)). Open 8:30 AM–7:30 PM.
  • 4. Aphrodite's Rock (Petra tou Romiou) — A legendary sea stack where Aphrodite is said to have risen from the foam. Best visited at sunset. Free access, 24/7.
  • 5. Paphos Mosaics (House of Theseus) — Part of the Archaeological Park, these intricate floor mosaics depict scenes from Greek mythology. Included in the park ticket.
  • 6. Kato Paphos Harbour Promenade — A lovely walk lined with cafes, restaurants, and souvenir shops. Perfect for a evening stroll. Free.
  • 7. Agia Solomoni Catacombs — An underground Christian catacomb with a sacred tree at the entrance. Free entry, open 24/7.
  • 8. Paphos Medieval Castle — Also known as the Fort of Paphos, it offers great views of the harbour. Ticket: ₽4 000 (≈ $5 (€4)).
  • 9. Paphos Aphrodite Waterpark — A fun day out for families with slides, pools, and lazy rivers. Ticket: ₽35 000 (≈ $40 (€35)) for adults, ₽20 000 (≈ $23 (€20)) for kids. Open 10 AM–6 PM (summer only).
  • 10. Paphos District Museum — A small but fascinating museum with artefacts from the Neolithic to medieval periods. Free entry. Open 8 AM–5 PM (closed Mondays).
  • 11. Coral Bay Beach — A stunning sandy beach with clear water, sunbeds, and water sports. Free entry; sunbeds ₽2 500 (≈ $3 (€2.50)) per day.
  • 12. Paphos Zoo — Home to over 400 animals, including rare birds and big cats. Ticket: ₽18 000 (≈ $21 (€18)) for adults, ₽10 000 (≈ $11 (€10)) for kids. Open 9 AM–6 PM.
  • 13. Paphos Old Town (Ktima) — Explore the local market, traditional houses, and authentic tavernas. Free entry.
  • 14. Byzantine Museum of Paphos — Houses a collection of icons and religious art from the 12th to 19th centuries. Ticket: ₽5 000 (≈ $6 (€5)). Open 9 AM–5 PM (closed Sundays).
  • 15. Paphos Lighthouse — A charming 19th-century lighthouse near the harbour. Great photo spot. Free access.

Getting Around

Paphos is compact, and many attractions in Kato Paphos are walkable. For longer distances, public buses are reliable and cheap. The main bus station is near the harbour. A single ticket costs ₽1 500 (≈ $2 (€1.50)), a day pass ₽5 000 (≈ $6 (€5)). Taxis are plentiful but pricey—expect ₽1 000–₽1 500 (≈ $11 (€10)–15) for a 10-minute ride.

Always agree on the fare upfront or use the meter. Renting a car gives you freedom to explore the Akamas Peninsula and Troodos Mountains. Rentals start at ₽2 500 per day (≈ $29 (€25)) from local agencies. Check Booking for car hire deals. Bicycles and scooters can be rented for around ₽1 500 per day (≈ $17 (€15)).

Where to Eat

Cypriot cuisine is a delicious mix of Greek, Turkish, and Middle Eastern influences. Start with a traditional meze (a spread of small dishes) at Ta Piatakia in the old town—a feast for two costs around ₽6 000 (≈ $69 (€60)). For fresh seafood, head to Mylos Fish Tavern near the harbour, where grilled octopus is ₽1 500 (≈ $17 (€15)).

Street food lovers must try souvlaki from O Gyros Tou Pafou—a generous pita wrap for ₽500 (≈ $6 (€5)). Budget meals (souvlaki, salads) cost ₽500–₽1 000 (≈ $6 (€5)–10). Mid-range restaurant dinners (meze with wine) run ₽3 000–₽5 000 (≈ $34 (€30)–50). High-end dining at places like Muse Café Kitchen can reach ₽8 000 (≈ $91 (€80)) per person. Don't miss local desserts like loukoumades (honey doughnuts) for ₽300 (≈ $3 (€3)).

Tours & Excursions

To truly experience Cyprus, book a few guided tours. A Troodos Mountains Wine Tour takes you to traditional villages and vineyards, with tastings included (₽8 000, ≈ $91 (€80)).

Airport Transfer

Getting from Paphos Airport to the city is straightforward. The cheapest option is bus route 612, which runs every 30 minutes from the airport to the harbour (₽1 500, ≈ $2 (€1.50) 30 minutes). A taxi costs a fixed ₽2 500 (≈ $29 (€25)) to Kato Paphos, but always confirm the price before getting in.

For a stress-free, door-to-door service, pre-book a private transfer. I always use Kiwitaxi—they meet you at arrivals with a name sign, and prices start at ₽2 000 (≈ $23 (€20)) for a car. Best Time to Visit

Paphos enjoys a Mediterranean climate with over 300 sunny days a year. The best time is April–June and September–October, when temperatures are 25–30°C, crowds are thinner, and prices are moderate. July and August are scorching (35°C+) and packed with tourists—hotels can cost double. Winter (November–March) is mild (15–20°C) but can be rainy; it's great for sightseeing and budget travel. Visa & Documents

Russian citizens can enter Cyprus visa-free for up to 90 days within a 180-day period. You need a valid passport (at least 6 months from entry date), proof of accommodation, return flight, and travel insurance. Currency & Money

Cyprus uses the Euro (EUR, symbol €). Banknotes come in denominations of 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, and 500 euros. Coins are 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 cents, and 1, 2 euros. Exchange money at banks or official exchange offices—avoid street changers. ATMs are everywhere, and most places accept Visa and Mastercard.

However, some small tavernas and market stalls are cash-only. I recommend bringing about ₽10 000–₽15 000 (≈ $114 (€100)–150) in cash for incidentals, but you can pay by card for most things. Tipping is not mandatory, but rounding up the bill or leaving 5–10% is appreciated.

Time Zone

Paphos is in Eastern European Time (UTC+2). During summer (last Sunday in March to last Sunday in October), clocks go forward to UTC+3. The time difference from Moscow is -1 hour (Moscow is UTC+3). From London, it's +2 hours in winter, +3 in summer. From Dubai, it's -2 hours (same time zone in winter).

Weekly Trip Budget

Here's an approximate weekly budget for one person in Paphos (7 days):

  • Accommodation: ₽28 000–₽70 000 (≈ $320 (€280)–700) for a mid-range hotel or apartment.
  • Food: ₽21 000–₽35 000 (≈ $240 (€210)–350) for meals (mix of street food and sit-down dinners).
  • Transport: ₽7 000–₽14 000 (≈ $80 (€70)–140) for bus passes, a few taxi rides, or car rental.
  • Entertainment: ₽14 000–₽28 000 (≈ $160 (€140)–280) for entrance fees, one tour, and a waterpark visit.
  • Total: ₽70 000–₽147 000 (≈ $800 (€700)–1,470) per person, excluding flights.

Travel Insurance

Travel insurance is not mandatory for Cyprus, but it's highly recommended. It covers medical emergencies, trip cancellations, lost luggage, and flight delays. A basic policy for a week starts at around ₽2 000 (≈ $23 (€20)). I strongly suggest getting comprehensive coverage—it's cheap peace of mind.
