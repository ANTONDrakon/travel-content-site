Limassol is Cyprus’s vibrant coastal gem, where ancient history meets modern energy. Strolling along the palm-lined promenade, you’ll feel the warm Mediterranean breeze mix with the buzz of cafes, yacht clubs, and centuries-old castles.

This city isn’t just a beach destination—it’s a living tapestry of Greek, Roman, and Byzantine influences, all wrapped in a laid-back Cypriot charm. Whether you’re sipping Commandaria wine in a old-town taverna or exploring the archaeological ruins of Kourion, Limassol offers a rich, layered experience that feels both timeless and fresh.

Why is 2026 the year to visit? The city is constantly evolving, with new waterfront developments, cultural festivals, and a food scene that’s gaining international acclaim. You’ll find fewer crowds than in Ayia Napa or Paphos, yet more authentic local life—think family-run bakeries, bustling markets, and hidden coves just a short drive away. Limassol is also a fantastic base for day trips to the Troodos Mountains or the wild Akamas Peninsula, making it perfect for travelers who want a mix of relaxation and adventure.

From the historic Limassol Castle to the dazzling marina, every corner has a story. The city’s dual personality—traditional and cosmopolitan—means you can spend the morning hiking ancient trails and the evening dining at a Michelin-starred restaurant. It’s this blend of old and new that makes Limassol unforgettable, and I’m here to help you plan every step.

How to Get There

The main gateway is Larnaca International Airport (LCA), about 60 km from Limassol. Direct flights connect from major European hubs like London, Moscow, and Berlin, with average round-trip fares from Moscow ranging from ₽25 000 to ₽45 000 (≈ $270–$490) in economy. During peak summer (July–August), prices can spike to ₽55 000 (≈ $600), while shoulder seasons (April–May, September–October) offer better deals. For the best options, check flights on Aviasales, where you can compare dozens of airlines and dates to snag the lowest fare.

Where to Stay

Limassol’s neighborhoods cater to every traveler. For beach lovers, the coastal strip near the Old Port and Dasoudi Beach has mid-range hotels like the Poseidonia Beach Hotel, where double rooms start at ₽8 000 (≈ $87) per night. If you want luxury, the marina district boasts five-star resorts like the Amara, with prices from ₽25 000 (≈ $270) per night.

Budget travelers should look at guesthouses in the old town, like Liana Hotel, with basic doubles for ₽4 000 (≈ $43). For a wide selection, compare hotel prices on Hotellook or Booking.com—you’ll find everything from hostels to villas.

Top 15 Attractions

  • Limassol Castle – A medieval fortress housing the Cyprus Medieval Museum. Explore the dungeon, ramparts, and nearby old town. Ticket: ₽500 (≈ $5.40). Open daily 9:00–17:00 (until 19:00 in summer).
  • Kourion Archaeological Site – Ancient city with a stunning Greco-Roman theater overlooking the sea. Don’t miss the mosaic floors. Ticket: ₽500 (≈ $5.40). Open daily 8:30–19:30 (summer), 8:30–17:00 (winter).
  • Limassol Marina – A modern waterfront with luxury yachts, shops, and restaurants. Free entry, open 24/7. Perfect for sunset strolls.
  • Fasouri Watermania – Cyprus’s largest water park with slides, wave pools, and lazy rivers. Ticket: ₽3 500 (≈ $38). Open May–October, 10:00–18:00.
  • Kolossi Castle – A 13th-century Crusader fortress with a wine press and sweet Commandaria history. Ticket: ₽250 (≈ $2.70). Open daily 8:30–19:30 (summer), 8:30–17:00 (winter).
  • Limassol Zoo – Small but charming zoo with local and exotic animals. Ticket: ₽300 (≈ $3.25). Open daily 9:00–17:00 (winter), 9:00–19:00 (summer).
  • Amathus Ruins – Ancient city ruins with a Roman agora, baths, and a basilica. Free entry (donation suggested). Open daily 8:30–19:30 (summer), 8:30–17:00 (winter).
  • Limassol Municipal Art Gallery – Contemporary Cypriot art in a restored mansion. Ticket: ₽200 (≈ $2.15). Open Tuesday–Sunday 10:00–18:00.
  • Governor’s Beach – Beautiful pebble beach with crystal-clear water and dramatic cliffs. Free entry. Open year-round.
  • Limassol Old Town – A maze of narrow streets with boutiques, bakeries, and street art. Free to explore. Best visited in the morning.
  • Cyprus Wine Museum – Learn about 5,000 years of winemaking, with tastings. Ticket: ₽500 (≈ $5.40). Open Monday–Saturday 9:00–17:00.
  • Limassol Promenade (Molos) – A 2-km seaside walkway with sculptures, playgrounds, and cafes. Free. Open 24/7.
  • Kourion Beach – Sandy beach near the archaeological site, with shallow waters. Free entry. Open year-round.
  • Panagia Katholiki Church – A Byzantine-era church with stunning frescoes. Free entry. Open during service hours.
  • Limassol Market (Dimarchou) – A vibrant market selling fresh produce, spices, and local crafts. Free to browse. Open Monday–Saturday 7:00–14:00.

Getting Around

Limassol is easy to navigate. Buses (EMEL) cost ₽150 (≈ $1.60) per ride, with day passes at ₽500 (≈ $5.40). Taxis start at ₽500 (≈ $5.40) for short trips; use apps like Bolt or TaxiLimassol. For flexibility, rent a car from ₽2 500 (≈ $27) per day—check Booking.com for deals. Biking is also popular along the flat promenade, with rentals at ₽1 000 (≈ $11) per day.

Where to Eat

Limassol’s food scene is a Mediterranean dream. For traditional meze (a feast of small dishes), head to Ta Piatia in the old town—set menus from ₽2 000 (≈ $22) per person. Street food lovers should try souvlaki at O Gyros for ₽500 (≈ $5.40). Fine dining at Pyrgos offers sea views and modern Cypriot cuisine, with mains from ₽3 500 (≈ $38). Budget meals at bakeries like Zorpas cost ₽300 (≈ $3.25) for a pastry and coffee.

Tours & Excursions

Top excursions include a day trip to the Troodos Mountains (₽6 000, ≈ $65), with waterfalls and painted churches; a wine tour of the Commandaria region (₽5 000, ≈ $54); and a catamaran cruise along the coast (₽8 000, ≈ $87). For a unique experience, try a sunset jeep safari in Akamas. Browse and book tours via GetYourGuide, where you can read reviews and compare prices for dozens of activities.

Airport Transfer

From Larnaca Airport, the easiest option is a pre-booked taxi or private transfer, costing ₽6 000–₽8 000 (≈ $65–$87) for the 45-minute drive. Public bus (Intercity Buses) runs every hour, costing ₽400 (≈ $4.30) per person, but takes 90 minutes. For convenience, book a reliable transfer on Kiwitaxi, where you can choose your vehicle type and pay in advance—no surprises.

Connectivity

Wi-Fi is widely available in hotels, cafes, and public areas. For a local SIM, buy a prepaid card from CYTA or PrimeTel at the airport for ₽1 000 (≈ $11) with 10 GB data. An even easier option is an eSIM from Airalo, which you can install before departure—plans start at ₽700 (≈ $7.60) for 5 GB, valid for 30 days, and you’ll be online the moment you land.

Best Time to Visit

Limassol shines from April to October. Spring (April–May) has pleasant 20–25°C weather and lower prices. Summer (June–August) is hot (30–35°C) and crowded, with peak hotel rates. Autumn (September–October) offers warm seas and fewer tourists. Winter (November–March) is mild (15–20°C) but rainy—perfect for sightseeing at off-peak rates.

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens can enter Cyprus visa-free for up to 90 days within any 180-day period. You need a valid passport (at least 6 months beyond your stay) and proof of accommodation. For the Maldives visa section, note: Перед вылетом заполните декларацию IMUGA на imuga.immigration.gov.mv и получите QR-код — без него не посадят на рейс.

Currency & Money

The local currency is the Euro (EUR, symbol €). Exchange rates at banks or ATMs are best—avoid airport kiosks. Cards (Visa, Mastercard) are widely accepted, but carry ₽5 000–₽10 000 (≈ $54–$108) cash for small purchases, markets, and taxis. ATMs are plentiful in the city.

Time Zone

Limassol is in Eastern European Time (UTC+2), with daylight saving (UTC+3) from March to October. Moscow is +1 hour ahead in winter, +0 in summer; London is +2 hours; New York is +7 hours.

While not mandatory for Russian citizens, travel insurance is highly recommended for medical emergencies, trip cancellations, and lost luggage. A basic policy covers up to ₽1 500 000 (≈ $16300) in medical expenses, costing around ₽2 000 (≈ $22) per week. For comprehensive coverage, get insurance via Booking.com or your preferred provider—it’s a small price for peace of mind.

10 Travel Tips

Don't forget travel insurance!

  1. Book ahead: Summer hotels fill up fast—reserve 2–3 months in advance.
  2. Rent a car: It’s the best way to explore the Troodos Mountains and hidden beaches.
  3. Learn basic Greek: “Yassou” (hello) and “Efharisto” (thank you) go a long way.
  4. Try Commandaria: This sweet wine is a UNESCO-recognized tradition—visit a winery for a tasting.
  5. Stay hydrated: Cypriot summers are dry; carry a reusable water bottle.
  6. Respect dress codes: Cover shoulders and knees when entering churches.
  7. Use sunscreen: The Mediterranean sun is strong even in spring.
  8. Bargain at markets: Haggling is acceptable at street stalls, not in shops.
  9. Check opening hours: Many attractions close for lunch (13:00–15:00) in summer.
  10. Embrace the siesta: Shops often close from 14:00–17:00—plan your day around it.
