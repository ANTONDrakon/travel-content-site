15 Best Things to Do in Limassol 2026

Limassol is often called the heart of Cyprus’s Riviera, and for good reason. It’s a city that blends ancient history with modern glamour, where you can explore a Crusader castle in the morning and sip cocktails by a marina in the evening.

I’ve spent many afternoons wandering its old town and coastal paths, and every visit reveals something new. In 2026, Limassol feels more vibrant than ever — with restored landmarks, trendy cafes, and a lively cultural scene. Whether you’re here for a weekend or a week, these 15 attractions will show you the very best of this Cypriot gem.

15 Best Attractions

1. Limassol Marina

A sleek, modern marina lined with luxury yachts, upscale restaurants, and boutique shops. It’s the perfect spot for a sunset stroll or a leisurely coffee by the water. The marina is also home to the Limassol Marina Museum, which displays artifacts from the city’s maritime history. Entry is free, but dining costs extra.

Ticket price: Бесплатно (free)

Hours: Open 24/7

How to get there: A 15-minute walk from the old town or a short taxi ride (₽600 / ≈ $7).

Tip: Visit at golden hour for the best photos of the yachts against the sunset.

2. Limassol Castle (Cyprus Medieval Museum)

This 13th-century castle, built on the site of a Byzantine fortress, now houses the Cyprus Medieval Museum. Inside, you’ll find suits of armor, pottery, and tombstones that tell the story of the island’s medieval past. It’s also where Richard the Lionheart is said to have married Berengaria of Navarre in 1191. The view from the top is worth the climb.

Ticket price: ₽450 (≈ $5)

Hours: 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM (closed on public holidays)

How to get there: Located in the old town center; easily reachable on foot from most hotels.

Tip: Combine your visit with a walk through the adjacent old town market for a full morning.

3. Kourion Archaeological Site

One of the most impressive ancient sites on the island, Kourion features a Greco-Roman theater, stunning mosaics, and the remains of a Roman villa. The site sits on a cliff overlooking the Mediterranean, offering breathtaking views. You can easily spend 2–3 hours exploring the ruins and the on-site museum. It’s a must-see for history buffs.

Ticket price: ₽900 (≈ $10)

Hours: 8:00 AM – 7:30 PM (summer), 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM (winter)

How to get there: Take bus #30 from Limassol city center (₽200 / ≈ $2.30) or a taxi (₽1 500 / ≈ $17).

Tip: Arrive early to beat the crowds and the midday heat — the site is mostly uncovered.

4. Limassol Old Town (Laiki Geitonia)

Wander through narrow, winding streets filled with traditional Cypriot houses, artisan shops, and cozy tavernas. The old town is the soul of Limassol, where you can buy handmade lace, sample local halloumi, or just soak in the atmosphere. Don’t miss the colorful street art on Anexartisias Street. It’s free to explore.

Ticket price: Бесплатно (free)

Hours: Shops and cafes generally open 9:00 AM – 8:00 PM

How to get there: Walk from the castle or take a bus to the “Old Town” stop.

Tip: Stop for a meze lunch at a traditional taverna like “To Katoi” — you’ll get a feast for ₽2 000 (≈ $23).

5. Limassol Zoo and Municipal Gardens

A small but well-maintained zoo featuring over 300 animals, including lions, monkeys, and flamingos. The adjacent Municipal Gardens are perfect for a picnic or a relaxing afternoon. It’s a hit with families, and the entrance fee is very affordable. The gardens also have a playground and a small cafe.

Ticket price: ₽300 (≈ $3.50)

Hours: 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM (summer), 9:00 AM – 4:00 PM (winter)

How to get there: A 10-minute walk from the old town or a short bus ride (bus #1 or #2).

Tip: Visit in the morning when the animals are most active.

6. Amathus Archaeological Site

An ancient city-kingdom with ruins dating back to 1100 BC, including a sanctuary of Aphrodite and a Roman agora. The site is less crowded than Kourion but equally fascinating. You can see the remains of a basilica, baths, and a necropolis. It’s a quieter, more contemplative experience.

Ticket price: ₽450 (≈ $5)

Hours: 8:00 AM – 7:30 PM (summer), 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM (winter)

How to get there: Bus #30 from the city center (₽200 / ≈ $2.30) or a 20-minute walk from the marina.

Tip: Bring water and sunscreen — there’s little shade on site.

7. Fasouri Waterpark

One of the largest waterparks in Cyprus, with over 20 slides, a wave pool, and a lazy river. It’s a fantastic place to cool off on a hot summer day. The park is surrounded by lush gardens and has several food outlets. It’s especially popular with families and groups of friends.

Ticket price: ₽3 500 (≈ $40) for adults, ₽2 500 (≈ $29) for children

Hours: 10:00 AM – 6:00 PM (May–October)

How to get there: A 15-minute drive from the city center; take a taxi (₽1 200 / ≈ $14) or the free shuttle from the marina.

Tip: Book tickets online via GetYourGuide to skip the queue — often 10% cheaper.

8. Cyprus Wine Museum

Located in the village of Erimi, just outside Limassol, this museum traces the history of Cypriot winemaking from ancient times to today. You’ll see traditional wine presses, amphorae, and a modern tasting room. The museum offers a guided tour and a tasting of three local wines. It’s a wonderful introduction to Cyprus’s wine culture.

Ticket price: ₽600 (≈ $7) including tasting

Hours: 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM (closed Sundays and public holidays)

How to get there: Take bus #30 to Erimi (₽200 / ≈ $2.30) or a taxi (₽1 500 / ≈ $17).

Tip: Pair your visit with a trip to a local winery like “Vouni Panayia” for a deeper dive.

9. Molos Promenade

A beautiful 2.5-km seaside walkway stretching from the old port to the marina. It’s dotted with sculptures, fountains, and benches, making it a favorite for joggers, cyclists, and families. The promenade also features a skate park and a children’s playground. It’s a free and easy way to enjoy the Mediterranean breeze.

Ticket price: Бесплатно (free)

Hours: Open 24/7

How to get there: Accessible from anywhere along the coast; park at the marina or old port.

Tip: Rent a bike from the city’s bike-sharing scheme (₽300 / ≈ $3.50 per hour) for a fun ride.

10. Carob Mill Museum

A restored 19th-century carob processing factory turned into a museum showcasing the history of carob trade in Cyprus. You’ll see original machinery, photographs, and learn how carob was used in everything from food to animal feed. The museum is small but charming, with a gift shop selling local products. Entry is free.

Ticket price: Бесплатно (free)

Hours: 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM (closed Sundays)

How to get there: In the old town, a 5-minute walk from the castle.

Tip: Try the carob syrup at the shop — it’s a local delicacy.

11. Limassol Archaeological Museum

This museum houses artifacts from the Neolithic to the Roman periods, including pottery, jewelry, and sculptures. It’s a quieter alternative to the larger museums in Nicosia, but it’s packed with treasures. The museum is set in a beautiful colonial-era building with a courtyard. It’s a great spot for a rainy day.

Ticket price: ₽450 (≈ $5)

Hours: 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM (closed Mondays)

How to get there: On the edge of the old town, a 10-minute walk from the castle.

Tip: Combine with a visit to the nearby Municipal Gardens for a half-day outing.

12. Lady’s Mile Beach

A long, sandy beach stretching for miles, perfect for swimming, sunbathing, and water sports. It’s less crowded than the city beaches and has several beach bars and restaurants. The water is clear and shallow, making it safe for children. It’s a favorite among locals for weekend getaways.

Ticket price: Бесплатно (free); sunbeds and umbrellas from ₽600 (≈ $7) per set

Hours: Open 24/7; facilities open 9:00 AM – 7:00 PM

How to get there: A 15-minute drive from the city; take bus #30 to the “Lady’s Mile” stop, then walk 10 minutes.

Tip: Arrive before 10 AM on weekends to secure a good spot.

13. Governor’s Beach

A stunning beach with white pebbles and turquoise water, located about 20 km east of Limassol. It’s known for its dramatic cliffs and clear waters, ideal for snorkeling. There are a few tavernas and a small hotel nearby. It’s a peaceful escape from the city.

Ticket price: Бесплатно (free)

Hours: Open 24/7

How to get there: Take bus #30 to the “Governor’s Beach” stop (₽250 / ≈ $2.90) or a taxi (₽2 000 / ≈ $23).

Tip: Bring water shoes — the pebbles can be sharp.

14. Kolossi Castle

A 15th-century fortress built by the Knights of St. John, known for producing the sweet dessert wine Commandaria. The castle is small but well-preserved, with a tower offering views of the surrounding vineyards. It’s a quick stop but rich in history. Entry is very affordable.

Ticket price: ₽250 (≈ $3)

Hours: 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM (summer), 8:00 AM – 4:00 PM (winter)

How to get there: A 10-minute drive west of Limassol; take bus #30 (₽200 / ≈ $2.30) or a taxi (₽1 000 / ≈ $12).

Tip: Visit the nearby Commandaria winery for a tasting after your castle tour.

15. Limassol Carnival

If you’re visiting in February or March, the Limassol Carnival is a spectacle not to be missed. It’s the largest carnival in Cyprus, with parades, costumes, and street parties that last for weeks. The highlight is the Grand Parade through the city center. It’s a vibrant celebration of Cypriot culture.

Ticket price: Бесплатно (free to watch); parade participation costs vary

Hours: Various events over 2–3 weeks; check local listings for 2026 dates.

How to get there: The parade route runs along the coastal road; easy to access on foot.

Tip: Book accommodation well in advance — hotels fill up fast during carnival season.

1-Day, 2-Day, 3-Day Itineraries

1-Day Itinerary: Limassol Highlights

Start your day at Limassol Castle (9:00 AM – 10:30 AM). Walk through the old town to Carob Mill Museum (10:45 AM – 11:30 AM). Have lunch at a traditional taverna in Laiki Geitonia (₽1 500 / ≈ $17). Afternoon: visit Kourion (1:00 PM – 3:30 PM). Evening: stroll along Molos Promenade and dine at a marina restaurant (₽2 500 / ≈ $29).

2-Day Itinerary: History and Relaxation

Day 1: Follow the 1-day itinerary above. Day 2: Morning at Amathus (9:00 AM – 11:00 AM). Lunch at a seaside cafe. Afternoon: Lady’s Mile Beach for swimming and sunbathing (12:30 PM – 4:00 PM). Evening: visit Cyprus Wine Museum (5:00 PM – 6:30 PM). Dinner at a winery restaurant.

3-Day Itinerary: Full Immersion

Day 1: As above. Day 2: As above. Day 3: Morning at Kolossi Castle (9:00 AM – 10:30 AM). Midday: Fasouri Waterpark (11:00 AM – 4:00 PM). Late afternoon: Limassol Zoo (4:30 PM – 6:00 PM). Evening: explore the old town’s nightlife.

Tours & Excursions

For a deeper experience, consider a guided tour. The “Limassol City Tour” covers the castle, old town, and marina for ₽2 500 (≈ $29) per person. A “Kourion & Wine Tasting” half-day tour costs ₽4 000 (≈ $46). For a full-day excursion, the “Troodos Mountains & Kykkos Monastery” tour is ₽6 000 (≈ $69). These tours can be booked through GetYourGuide, which offers a wide selection and often discounts for early booking. Tours can also be booked through local operators at the marina.

A guide is recommended for historical sites and complex routes. Self-guided travel is possible with audio guides and maps.
