Best Time to Visit Paphos 2026: Seasons & Weather

When I first started planning trips to Cyprus, Paphos quickly became one of my favorite destinations. There’s something magical about this coastal city on the southwest coast—its blend of ancient ruins, stunning beaches, and laid-back charm draws travelers year-round. But choosing the best time to visit Paphos depends on what you’re after: sun-soaked beach days, comfortable sightseeing, or budget-friendly escapes. Let me walk you through the seasons and weather patterns for 2026, so you can plan your perfect trip.

Paphos enjoys a Mediterranean climate, with hot, dry summers and mild, wet winters. The sea breezes keep things pleasant even in peak heat, while spring and autumn offer near-perfect conditions. Below, I’ll break down everything month by month, highlight the high and low seasons, and share tips for making the most of your visit.

High Season

The high season in Paphos runs from June to August, peaking in July and August when schools are out across Europe. This is when the city buzzes with energy—beaches are lively, restaurants are full, and the atmosphere is festive. The weather is hot, with daytime temperatures often hitting 30-33°C, perfect for swimming and sunbathing. Water temperatures are equally inviting, hovering around 27-28°C.

Why visit during high season? If you love vibrant nightlife, beach parties, and a bustling vibe, this is your time. Prices, however, reflect the demand. Accommodation costs can double compared to shoulder seasons, with a standard double room in a mid-range hotel costing around ₽10 000–₽15 000 (≈ $110–$165) per night. Flights also spike, especially from European hubs.

Pro tip: During high season, it’s worth booking hotels early. Check options on Hotellook or Booking to secure the best rates and availability. For flights, Aviasales is a great place to compare prices across airlines. I’ve found that booking at least 2-3 months ahead saves a lot of hassle.

Low Season

The low season spans November to February, with December and January being the quietest months. This is when Paphos slows down—tourism drops, many beachside cafes close, and the weather turns cooler and wetter. Daytime temperatures range from 12-18°C, with sea temperatures around 16-17°C (too chilly for most swimmers). Rain is more frequent, with December and January averaging 80mm of precipitation.

Pros of low season: You’ll find incredible deals. Accommodation prices can drop by 50% or more, with a double room costing as little as ₽4 000–₽6 000 (≈ $44–$66) per night. Flights are cheaper too, and attractions like the Paphos Archaeological Park are uncrowded. It’s a great time for a peaceful getaway, hiking in the nearby Troodos Mountains, or exploring museums without queues.

Cons: Some restaurants and shops have reduced hours or close entirely. The weather can be unpredictable—you might get a sunny 18°C day or a week of drizzle. If you’re okay with layering up and don’t mind occasional rain, low season offers a cozy, authentic side of Paphos.

Best Time for Beach Vacation

For a classic beach vacation—swimming, snorkeling, and lounging on the sand—the best months are June through September. Sea temperatures are warmest from July to September, hitting 26-28°C, while air temperatures are hot but manageable with sea breezes. May and October are also good options if you prefer milder heat, though the water is cooler (21-23°C).

My personal favorite? September. The crowds thin out after August, the sea is still bath-warm, and the sun isn’t as scorching. Beaches like Coral Bay and Lara Beach are stunning during this time. Just pack sunscreen—the UV index is high even in late summer.

Best Time for Sightseeing

If you’re more about history and culture than swimming, you’ll want to avoid the peak summer heat. The best months for sightseeing are April, May, and October. Temperatures range from 20-26°C, making it comfortable to walk around ancient sites like the Tombs of the Kings, the Paphos Mosaics, and the Paphos Castle. Spring brings blooming wildflowers, while autumn offers golden light for photography.

During these months, you can explore without feeling rushed by the heat. For excursions, check options on GetYourGuide for guided tours to the Troodos Mountains, Aphrodite’s Rock, or the Akamas Peninsula. I recommend a half-day tour to the Baths of Aphrodite—it’s a lovely hike with a mythological twist.

Rainy Season

The rainy season in Paphos runs from November to March, with the heaviest rainfall in December and January. During these months, you can expect around 70-80mm of rain per month, often falling in short, heavy bursts followed by sunshine. It’s not a monsoon-like deluge—Cyprus has a Mediterranean climate, so rain is sporadic rather than constant.

Is it worth visiting during the rainy season? Absolutely, if you’re flexible. Rainy days can be spent visiting the Paphos Archaeological Museum, the Byzantine Museum, or enjoying a cozy meal at a taverna. The countryside turns lush and green, offering beautiful hiking conditions when the skies clear. Just bring a waterproof jacket and comfortable shoes.

Events & Festivals

Paphos has a rich calendar of events that add color to your visit. Here are the major ones by month:

  • January: New Year’s celebrations and the Epiphany ceremony (January 6) at the harbor, where a cross is thrown into the sea.
  • February: Carnival season kicks off with parades and costume parties, leading up to Lent.
  • March: Green Monday (Clean Monday) marks the start of Lent with outdoor picnics and kite flying.
  • April: Easter is a major event in Cyprus, with religious processions and festive feasts (dates vary).
  • May: The Paphos Aphrodite Festival (mid-May) features opera performances at the medieval castle.
  • June: The Paphos Beer Festival (early June) offers live music and local brews.
  • July: The Paphos Summer Festival includes concerts, dance, and theater events throughout the month.
  • August: Assumption Day (August 15) is a public holiday with religious celebrations.
  • September: The Paphos Wine Festival (early September) showcases Cypriot wines and traditional food.
  • October: The Paphos International Film Festival (mid-October) draws cinema lovers.
  • November: The Paphos Marathon (early November) attracts runners with scenic coastal routes.
  • December: Christmas markets and festive lights brighten the city, with a New Year’s Eve fireworks display.

For a cultural experience, try to align your visit with Easter or the Wine Festival. Both are deeply rooted in Cypriot traditions and offer a taste of local life.

Conclusion

So, what’s the best time to visit Paphos in 2026? It really depends on your priorities:

  • For beach lovers: June to September offer the warmest sea and sunniest days. September stands out for its balance of warmth and fewer crowds.
  • For sightseers: April, May, and October provide comfortable temperatures and vibrant landscapes. You’ll enjoy exploring ruins and hiking without overheating.
  • For budget travelers: November to February deliver the lowest prices, though you’ll need to pack for rain and cooler weather.
  • For festival enthusiasts: Align your trip with Easter (April), the Wine Festival (September), or the Summer Festival (July).

Flights for those dates can be checked on Aviasales to find the best deals from your departure city. If you’re flying from Russia, note that direct flights from Moscow to Larnaca (about 1.5 hours from Paphos) are common. For other routes, compare options to save. And for accommodation, Hotellook or Booking are reliable for finding hotels, apartments, or villas. For transfers from Larnaca or Paphos airports, Kiwitaxi offers private car services that are convenient and affordable.

No matter when you choose to go, Paphos has a way of capturing your heart. Whether you’re soaking up the sun on a golden beach, wandering through ancient ruins, or savoring a glass of Commandaria wine at a seaside taverna, this Cypriot gem promises an unforgettable escape. Happy travels!
