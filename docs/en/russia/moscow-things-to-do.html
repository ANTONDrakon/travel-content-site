15 Best Things to Do in Moscow 2026

Moscow in 2026 is a city that pulses with history, art, and an energy that’s hard to describe. I remember my first visit—standing on Red Square at dusk, the Kremlin’s ruby stars glowing against a deep blue sky, feeling like I’d stepped into a living fairy tale.

But Moscow isn’t just about its iconic landmarks; it’s a place where centuries-old cathedrals sit next to cutting-edge galleries, where Soviet-era metro stations are underground palaces, and where every corner offers a new surprise. Whether you’re a first-time traveler or returning for more, this guide will help you discover the best things to do in Moscow in 2026, with practical tips to make your trip smooth and memorable.

15 Best Attractions

1. Red Square and St. Basil’s Cathedral

Red Square is the heart of Moscow, a sprawling cobblestone plaza that has witnessed centuries of history. The candy-colored domes of St. Basil’s Cathedral, built in the 16th century, are an absolute must-see—each onion dome is unique, and the interior is a maze of narrow corridors and intricate frescoes. You can’t miss the Lenin Mausoleum nearby, where you can pay respects to the embalmed leader (free but with strict queues).

  • Ticket price: St. Basil’s Cathedral: ₽1 000 (≈ $11). Lenin Mausoleum: free.
  • Hours: St. Basil’s: daily 10:00–18:00 (closed Wednesdays). Mausoleum: Tue–Thu, Sat 10:00–13:00.
  • How to get there: Metro to Ploshchad Revolyutsii (red line) or Okhotny Ryad (green line).
  • Tip: Arrive early (before 9:30 AM) to avoid crowds at the Mausoleum—queues can stretch for hours.

2. The Kremlin and Armoury Chamber

The Kremlin is a fortified complex that houses Russia’s government buildings, cathedrals, and museums. The Armoury Chamber is a treasure trove of Fabergé eggs, royal carriages, and coronation robes—it’s like wandering through a history book made of gold. Don’t miss the Ivan the Great Bell Tower for panoramic views of the city.

  • Ticket price: Kremlin grounds: ₽700 (≈ $8). Armoury Chamber: ₽1 000 (≈ $11). Combined ticket: ₽1 500 (≈ $16).
  • Hours: Daily 10:00–17:00 (closed Thursdays).
  • How to get there: Metro to Aleksandrovsky Sad (dark blue line) or Borovitskaya (gray line).
  • Tip: Book Armoury tickets online via the Kremlin website at least a week in advance—they sell out fast.

3. Tretyakov Gallery

This gallery is Russia’s premier art museum, showcasing a vast collection of Russian art from medieval icons to 20th-century avant-garde. The highlight is Andrei Rublev’s “Trinity” icon, but the halls of Repin, Levitan, and Kandinsky will leave you breathless. The building itself, a charming 19th-century mansion, adds to the experience.

  • Ticket price: ₽600 (≈ $7).
  • Hours: Tue–Sun 10:00–18:00 (closed Mondays).
  • How to get there: Metro to Tretyakovskaya (yellow line) or Novokuznetskaya (green line).
  • Tip: Visit on a weekday morning to avoid school groups—the gallery can get packed.

4. Gorky Park

Gorky Park is Moscow’s central green oasis, perfect for a leisurely stroll, bike ride, or picnic. In summer, you can rent a boat on the pond, and in winter, the park turns into a giant ice-skating rink. The park also hosts open-air art exhibitions and a modern “Garage” museum of contemporary art.

  • Ticket price: Free entry. Skate rentals: ₽500 (≈ $5) per hour.
  • Hours: Open 24/7.
  • How to get there: Metro to Park Kultury (red line) or Oktyabrskaya (orange line).
  • Tip: Rent a bike from Velobike stations (₽150 per hour) and cycle along the Moskva River embankment.

5. Moscow Metro Tour

The Moscow Metro is more than just a transit system—it’s a museum of Soviet art and architecture. Stations like Komsomolskaya, Mayakovskaya, and Novoslobodskaya are adorned with chandeliers, mosaics, and marble columns. A self-guided tour is easy, but a guided tour adds context to the history.

  • Ticket price: Single ride: ₽70 (≈ $0.75). Guided tour: ₽1 500 (≈ $16) per person via GetYourGuide.
  • Hours: Metro runs 5:30 AM–1:00 AM.
  • How to get there: Start at Ploshchad Revolyutsii station for the most ornate stops.
  • Tip: Download the “Moscow Metro” app for offline maps—WiFi is spotty underground.

6. Bolshoi Theatre

The Bolshoi Theatre is one of the world’s most famous opera and ballet venues, with a stunning neoclassical facade and a gilded auditorium. Even if you don’t catch a performance, you can take a guided tour of the building. The acoustics and artistry are legendary—seeing “Swan Lake” here is a bucket-list experience.

  • Ticket price: Tours: ₽1 500 (≈ $16). Performances: from ₽3 000 (≈ $33).
  • Hours: Tours daily 10:00–17:00 (check schedule).
  • How to get there: Metro to Teatralnaya (green line) or Okhotny Ryad.
  • Tip: Book tickets on the Bolshoi website 3 months in advance—popular shows sell out instantly.

7. VDNKh (Exhibition of Achievements of National Economy)

VDNKh is a sprawling park and exhibition complex that feels like a Soviet theme park. The futuristic pavilions, fountains, and the iconic “Worker and Kolkhoz Woman” statue are perfect for photos. Don’t miss the “Cosmonautics Museum” inside, which celebrates Russia’s space achievements.

  • Ticket price: Park: free. Cosmonautics Museum: ₽500 (≈ $5).
  • Hours: Park open 24/7. Museum: Tue–Sun 10:00–19:00.
  • How to get there: Metro to VDNKh (orange line).
  • Tip: Visit on a weekend for the food market—try pelmeni and blini from local vendors.

8. Novodevichy Convent and Cemetery

This UNESCO-listed convent is a serene complex of white-walled churches and gardens, founded in the 16th century. The adjacent cemetery is the final resting place of famous Russians like Chekhov, Gogol, and Khrushchev. The atmosphere is peaceful and reflective.

  • Ticket price: Convent: ₽500 (≈ $5). Cemetery: free.
  • Hours: Convent: daily 10:00–18:00. Cemetery: daily 9:00–17:00.
  • How to get there: Metro to Sportivnaya (red line), then a 10-minute walk.
  • Tip: Combine with a visit to the adjacent Novodevichy Park for a picnic.

9. Arbat Street

Arbat is Moscow’s most famous pedestrian street, lined with souvenir shops, street performers, and cafes. It’s touristy but charming, especially in the evening when musicians play. The historic building at No. 53 was once home to the poet Alexander Pushkin.

  • Ticket price: Free to walk.
  • Hours: Always open.
  • How to get there: Metro to Arbatskaya (dark blue line) or Smolenskaya.
  • Tip: Avoid buying souvenirs here—prices are inflated. Shop at Izmailovo Market instead.

10. State Historical Museum

Located on Red Square, this museum covers Russian history from prehistoric times to the 20th century. The red-brick building is as impressive as the exhibits, with ornate halls and a massive collection of artifacts. It’s a deep dive into the nation’s past.

  • Ticket price: ₽700 (≈ $8).
  • Hours: Wed–Mon 10:00–18:00 (closed Tuesdays).
  • How to get there: Metro to Ploshchad Revolyutsii.
  • Tip: Rent an audio guide (₽500) for detailed commentary—English exhibits are limited.

11. Cathedral of Christ the Saviour

This colossal cathedral, rebuilt in the 1990s after being destroyed by Stalin, is a symbol of Russia’s religious revival. The interior is covered in gold leaf and intricate frescoes. You can also visit the observation deck for panoramic city views.

  • Ticket price: Free. Observation deck: ₽300 (≈ $3).
  • Hours: Daily 7:00–19:00.
  • How to get there: Metro to Kropotkinskaya (red line).
  • Tip: Dress modestly—women must cover their heads, and men should avoid shorts.

12. Izmailovo Kremlin and Market

This whimsical complex is a modern recreation of a traditional Russian fortress, with colorful wooden buildings and a massive souvenir market. It’s the best place to buy matryoshka dolls, fur hats, and Soviet memorabilia. The market is chaotic but fun.

  • Ticket price: Market: free. Kremlin grounds: ₽500 (≈ $5).
  • Hours: Market: daily 10:00–20:00. Kremlin: daily 10:00–19:00.
  • How to get there: Metro to Partizanskaya (dark blue line).
  • Tip: Haggle gently—vendors expect it, but be respectful.

13. Sparrow Hills and Moscow State University

Sparrow Hills offers the best panoramic view of Moscow, with the Moskva River winding below and the city skyline stretching to the horizon. The nearby Moscow State University building is a stunning example of Stalinist architecture. It’s a favorite spot for sunset.

  • Ticket price: Free.
  • Hours: Open 24/7.
  • How to get there: Metro to Universitet (red line), then a 15-minute walk.
  • Tip: Bring a picnic and watch the sun set behind the city—it’s magical.

14. Museum of Cosmonautics

Dedicated to Russia’s space program, this museum features real spacecraft, spacesuits, and a replica of the Sputnik satellite. The highlight is a full-scale model of the Mir space station you can walk through. It’s inspiring for kids and adults alike.

  • Ticket price: ₽500 (≈ $5).
  • Hours: Tue–Sun 10:00–19:00.
  • How to get there: Metro to VDNKh.
  • Tip: Check for temporary exhibitions—they often feature astronaut memorabilia.

15. Zaryadye Park

This modern park near Red Square is a marvel of landscape design, with four climate zones (tundra, steppe, forest, and wetland) in one space. The “Floating Bridge” extends over the Moskva River, offering incredible views. The underground museum and concert hall are bonuses.

  • Ticket price: Free. Floating bridge: free.
  • Hours: Park open 24/7. Museum: daily 10:00–20:00.
  • How to get there: Metro to Kitay-Gorod (purple line).
  • Tip: Visit at golden hour for stunning photos of the Kremlin from the bridge.

1-Day, 2-Day, 3-Day Itineraries

1-Day Itinerary: Moscow’s Heart

Start at Red Square (7:00 AM) for a quiet photo of St. Basil’s. Visit Lenin’s Mausoleum (8:00 AM), then explore the Kremlin and Armoury Chamber (9:00 AM–12:00 PM). Lunch at a nearby cafe (try “Moscow” restaurant for borscht). Afternoon: Tretyakov Gallery (1:00 PM–4:00 PM). Evening: walk Arbat Street, then dinner at a Georgian restaurant (like “Khachapuri”). End with a night view from Sparrow Hills.

2-Day Itinerary: Add Culture

Day 1: Same as above. Day 2: Morning: Moscow Metro tour (start at Komsomolskaya station). Midday: Bolshoi Theatre tour (book in advance). Afternoon: Gorky Park for a stroll and boat ride. Evening: VDNKh for the Cosmonautics Museum and dinner at the food market.

3-Day Itinerary: Deep Dive

Day 1: Red Square, Kremlin, Tretyakov Gallery. Day 2: Metro tour, Bolshoi, Gorky Park. Day 3: Morning: Novodevichy Convent. Midday: Izmailovo Market for souvenirs. Afternoon: Zaryadye Park and the Floating Bridge. Evening: catch a ballet at the Bolshoi (if tickets available).

Tours & Excursions

Guided tours can save you time and provide insider knowledge. For the Kremlin, a 2-hour tour costs around ₽2 500 (≈ $27) via GetYourGuide. A Moscow Metro tour with a guide is ₽1 500 (≈ $16) per person. For a full-day city tour covering Red Square, Arbat, and Sparrow Hills, expect ₽5 000 (≈ $54). You can also book private excursions, like a Soviet history walk (₽3 000) or a river cruise (₽2 000). Tours can be booked online through services like GetYourGuide, which offers free cancellation.

Free Attractions

Moscow has plenty of free gems. Red Square is always free, as is the exterior of St. Basil’s. Gorky Park and Zaryadye Park cost nothing to enter. The Cathedral of Christ the Saviour is free (except the observation deck). Sparrow Hills offers stunning views without a ticket. The Moscow Metro is cheap (₽70 per ride) but you can ride for hours just admiring the stations. Walk along the Moskva River embankment for a peaceful escape.

Practical Tips

Transportation

The Moscow Metro is your best friend—it’s efficient, cheap, and beautiful. Buy a “Troika” card (₽200 deposit) for unlimited rides (₽70 per trip). Taxis via Yandex Go are reliable; a 15-minute ride costs about ₽500. Avoid driving—traffic is notorious. For airport transfers, consider Kiwitaxi for pre-booked private cars (from ₽2 500).

Combo Tickets

Save money with combo tickets. The Kremlin + Armoury Chamber ticket is ₽1 500 (≈ $16) instead of ₽1 700 separately. The “Moscow CityPass” (₽3 500 for 3 days) includes entry to 30+ museums and free metro rides—check Hotellook for deals. Book accommodation via Booking or Agoda for the best rates.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do attractions cost in Moscow?

Entrance fees vary: from free attractions to $20-30 for museums. Many venues have discount days.

Do I need a guide in Moscow?

A guide is recommended for historical sites and complex routes. Self-guided travel is possible with audio guides and maps.
