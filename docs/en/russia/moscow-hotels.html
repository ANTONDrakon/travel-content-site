Budget Hotels (under $50/night)

1. Godzillas Hostel Moscow

Tucked away on Bolshaya Sadovaya Street, this iconic hostel has been a backpacker favorite for years. It's clean, friendly, and just a 15-minute walk from the Bolshoi Theatre. Dorm beds start at ₽1 200 (≈ $13), while private doubles are ₽3 500 (≈ $38). The common area is buzzing with travelers swapping stories, and the staff speaks excellent English. Pros: Central location, affordable, great social vibe. Cons: No breakfast included, shared bathrooms can get busy.

2. Hotel Vega Izmailovo

Part of the massive Izmailovo complex near the famous Vernissage market, this hotel offers incredible value. A standard double room costs ₽3 800 (≈ $41). Rooms are basic but clean, with Soviet-era charm and modern updates. The metro station is a 5-minute walk, taking you to Red Square in 20 minutes. Pros: Cheap, close to a major market, good transport links. Cons: Area feels a bit isolated at night, decor is dated.

3. Mini Hotel on Tverskaya

This tiny gem on Moscow's main street is a steal. Private rooms with shared bathroom start at ₽4 200 (≈ $45). It's a no-frills option, but the location is unbeatable—right opposite the Kremlin. The staff are incredibly helpful, and there's a 24-hour reception. Pros: Prime location, very cheap for the area. Cons: Thin walls, no elevator, shared facilities.

Mid-Range Hotels ($50-150/night)

4. ibis Moscow Centre Bakhrushina

A reliable chain hotel near Paveletsky Station. Rooms are compact but modern, with soundproof windows and comfy beds. Doubles go for ₽7 500 (≈ $80) including breakfast. The metro is a 3-minute walk, and you can reach Red Square in 10 minutes. Highlights: Free Wi-Fi, 24-hour bar, great value for business travelers.

5. Mercure Arbat Moscow

On the historic Arbat Street, this hotel blends old-world charm with modern comfort. A standard double is ₽10 000 (≈ $108). Rooms feature elegant decor with touches of Russian art. The neighborhood is perfect for strolling—street musicians, souvenir shops, and cafes line the pedestrian street. Highlights: Excellent breakfast buffet, rooftop terrace with city views, friendly multilingual staff.

6. Hampton by Hilton Moscow St. Petersburg

Located near the Leningradsky Station, this hotel is ideal for travelers arriving by train. Doubles cost ₽9 000 (≈ $97) with free hot breakfast. The rooms are spotless, and the gym is a nice bonus. It's a 15-minute metro ride to the Kremlin. Highlights: Free Wi-Fi, 24-hour convenience store, consistent quality.

7. Hotel National Moscow (Affordable Wing)

Yes, the famous National Hotel has a more budget-friendly wing! While the main building is luxury, the "Economy" rooms offer the same prime address opposite Red Square for ₽13 000 (≈ $140). You get access to the hotel's facilities, including the stunning restaurant with Kremlin views. Highlights: Unbeatable location, historic ambiance, excellent service.

Luxury Hotels ($150+/night)

8. Four Seasons Hotel Moscow

Overlooking Red Square and the Kremlin, this is the epitome of Moscow luxury. Suites start at ₽45 000 (≈ $485), but even the standard rooms are palatial. The spa is world-class, with a 20-meter pool and Russian banya. The concierge can arrange private tours of the Kremlin Armory. Features: Michelin-starred restaurant, butler service, direct access to the Okhotny Ryad metro.

9. The Ritz-Carlton, Moscow

Just steps from the Bolshoi Theatre, this hotel offers old-world grandeur with modern amenities. Rooms from ₽38 000 (≈ $410). The rooftop O2 Lounge is legendary for cocktails and panoramic views. The spa includes a hammam and a 24-hour fitness center. Features: Exclusive shopping arcade, private dining, personalized city guides.

10. Baltschug Kempinski Moscow

Directly across the Moskva River from the Kremlin, this hotel has been a landmark since the 1990s. Doubles start at ₽35 000 (≈ $378). The rooms are classic and spacious, with many offering river views. The restaurant serves excellent Russian-European cuisine. Features: Indoor pool, sauna, 24-hour room service, free parking.

How to Choose the Right Area

For sightseeing: Stay in Tverskoy or Kitay-Gorod districts. You'll be within walking distance of Red Square, St. Basil's Cathedral, and the GUM department store. Hotels here are pricier, but you save on transport and time. The Hotel National or Four Seasons are top picks if your budget allows.

For nightlife: Head to the "Moscow City" business district in Presnensky or the trendy streets around Chistye Prudy. The area is packed with rooftop bars, clubs, and late-night restaurants. The Mercure Arbat is a good mid-range option here, while the ibis near Paveletsky gives you easy metro access to nightlife spots.

For a quiet, local experience: Choose Patriarch Ponds or the area around Ostozhenka Street. These leafy neighborhoods are full of cafes, boutiques, and charming architecture. The Baltschug Kempinski is a luxurious choice with a serene atmosphere, while the Mini Hotel on Tverskaya offers budget-friendly proximity to the action without the noise.

Booking Tips

Book early for the best rates. Moscow's hotel prices spike during summer (June–August) and around New Year's Eve. I recommend booking at least 3–4 months in advance for peak seasons. Use Hotellook or Booking to compare prices—they often have exclusive deals. For last-minute bookings, Agoda sometimes offers discounts on unsold rooms.

Consider package deals. Some hotels partner with GetYourGuide for tours—you might get a discount on a Kremlin visit when you book a room. Also, check if your hotel offers airport transfers via Kiwitaxi; it's often cheaper than hailing a taxi at the airport.

Don't forget connectivity. Moscow has excellent Wi-Fi, but if you want a local SIM for data, grab an Airalo eSIM before you travel. It's cheap and works instantly—no need to hunt for a store.
