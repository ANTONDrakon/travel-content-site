Welcome to Saint Petersburg — Russia’s cultural heart, a city of canals, imperial palaces, and white nights. Built by Peter the Great as a “window to Europe,” this northern capital pulses with art, history, and an unmistakable atmosphere of grandeur. Walking along Nevsky Prospekt, you’ll pass baroque cathedrals, art nouveau mansions, and cozy courtyards where time seems to stand still. The city’s soul is in its bridges — 342 of them — that rise at night to let ships pass, creating a romantic spectacle you won’t forget.

Why visit? Because Saint Petersburg is a living museum. The Hermitage alone holds over 3 million works of art. The Church of the Savior on Spilled Blood dazzles with 7,500 square meters of mosaics. And in summer, the “White Nights” (from late May to mid-July) bathe the city in a magical twilight that never quite turns dark. Locals celebrate with festivals, street performances, and boat rides along the Neva. It’s a city that rewards curiosity — every bridge and canal hides a story.

Whether you’re a first-time visitor or returning for the 10th time, Saint Petersburg in 2026 is more accessible than ever. New flight connections, improved visa processes, and a growing food scene make it a perfect destination for culture lovers, romantics, and budget travelers alike. Let’s dive into everything you need to know.

How to Get There

Pulkovo Airport (LED) is the main gateway, located about 20 km south of the city center. Direct flights from Moscow take about 1.5 hours and cost from ₽4 000 (≈ $45). From European hubs like London, Paris, or Berlin, expect flights from ₽15 000 (≈ $165). From Dubai, flights start at ₽20 000 (≈ $220). Where to Stay

Saint Petersburg offers neighborhoods for every vibe. For first-timers, stay near Nevsky Prospekt — the main artery with endless cafes and shops. The historic center (Admiralteysky District) puts you steps from the Hermitage and Palace Square. Budget travelers love Vasilyevsky Island for its student energy and cheaper hostels. Top 15 Attractions

  1. The State Hermitage Museum — One of the world’s greatest art museums, housed in the Winter Palace. See works by da Vinci, Rembrandt, and Picasso. Ticket: ₽800 (≈ $9). Open: Tue–Sun 10:30–18:00 (Wed until 21:00). Closed Mon.
  2. Church of the Savior on Spilled Blood — A stunning onion-domed church with over 7,500 sq m of mosaics inside. Ticket: ₽450 (≈ $5). Open: daily 10:30–18:00 (Thu until 21:00).
  3. Peterhof Palace & Gardens — The “Russian Versailles” with 150 fountains and golden statues. Ticket: ₽1 000 (≈ $11). Open: daily 09:00–20:00 (fountains run May–Oct).
  4. St. Isaac’s Cathedral — Climb the 262 steps to the colonnade for panoramic city views. Ticket: ₽500 (≈ $5.50). Open: daily 10:30–18:00 (colonnade until 22:00 in summer).
  5. State Russian Museum — The largest collection of Russian art, from icons to avant-garde. Ticket: ₽700 (≈ $8). Open: daily 10:00–18:00 (closed Tue).
  6. Nevsky Prospekt — The city’s main street, perfect for walking, shopping, and people-watching. Free entry.
  7. Peter and Paul Fortress — The original citadel of the city, with the Peter and Paul Cathedral (burial place of Romanovs). Ticket: ₽750 (≈ $8). Open: daily 10:00–18:00.
  8. Catherine Palace (Tsarskoye Selo) — Famous for the Amber Room, a baroque masterpiece in Pushkin town. Ticket: ₽1 200 (≈ $13). Open: daily 10:00–17:00 (closed Tue).
  9. Yusupov Palace — A lavish palace on the Moika River, where Rasputin was murdered. Ticket: ₽800 (≈ $9). Open: daily 11:00–18:00.
  10. Kazan Cathedral — A majestic neoclassical cathedral with a 96-column colonnade. Free entry. Open: daily 07:00–20:00.
  11. Fabergé Museum — Houses 9 imperial Easter eggs and decorative arts in the Shuvalov Palace. Ticket: ₽1 000 (≈ $11). Open: daily 10:00–20:45.
  12. Mariinsky Theatre — World-renowned opera and ballet venue. Tickets from ₽2 000 (≈ $22). Book in advance on GetYourGuide.
  13. Rivers & Canals Boat Tour — See the city from the water, passing under low bridges. Ticket: ₽1 500 (≈ $16). 1-hour tours run daily in summer.
  14. Erarta Museum of Contemporary Art — Russia’s largest private contemporary art museum. Ticket: ₽600 (≈ $7). Open: daily 10:00–22:00 (closed Tue).
  15. Summer Garden — A formal park with marble statues and fountains, created by Peter the Great. Free entry. Open: daily 10:00–22:00 (May–Oct).

Getting Around

The metro is the fastest and cheapest way to travel. A single ride costs ₽70 (≈ $0.77) — buy a reusable Troika card (₽100 deposit) or use contactless cards. The system is deep and clean, with stations like Avtovo and Ploshchad Vosstaniya decorated like palaces. Buses and trams cost ₽60 (≈ $0.66) per ride. Taxis via Yandex Go start at ₽200 (≈ $2.20) for short trips. Car rentals from ₽2 500 (≈ $28) per day. For airport transfers, pre-book a private car on Kiwitaxi — reliable and fixed price.

Where to Eat

Russian cuisine is hearty and comforting. Try borscht (beet soup), pelmeni (dumplings), and blini (pancakes) with sour cream. For a classic Soviet-style meal, visit Chainaya Lozhka — a chain with blini from ₽150 (≈ $1.65). Mid-range restaurants like Probka (Italian-Russian fusion) offer mains from ₽800 (≈ $9). Fine dining at Birch (Michelin-starred) starts at ₽3 500 (≈ $38). Street food: try pirozhki (stuffed buns) from street stalls for ₽100 (≈ $1.10). Don’t miss kvass (fermented rye drink) from barrels in summer — ₽50 (≈ $0.55).

Best Time to Visit

Summer (June–August) is peak season — White Nights, long days (up to 19 hours of daylight), and temperatures around 20–25°C. Prices are highest. Spring (May) and early autumn (September) are pleasant with fewer crowds. Winter (November–March) is cold (-10°C average) but magical — frozen canals, Christmas markets, and cheaper hotels. Avoid November for grey skies. In July, the Scarlet Sails festival draws huge crowds. Book hotels and flights early for summer using

Visa & Documents

Most foreign visitors need a Russian visa. Apply at least 4 weeks before travel. For a 30-day tourist visa, you’ll need a visa support letter (invitation) from a hotel or tour operator. Cost: ₽3 000–₽6 000 (≈ $33–$66) depending on processing speed. Citizens of some countries (e.g., Israel, South Korea) enjoy visa-free stays up to 60 days. Always carry your passport and migration card while in the city. For domestic travel within Russia, you need a valid Russian internal passport or foreign passport with a valid visa — no extra permits needed.

10 Travel Tips

Don't forget travel insurance!

get travel insurance
  1. Learn basic Russian phrases — “Spasibo” (thank you) and “Zdravstvuyte” (hello) go a long way. Many locals under 40 speak English, but not all.
  2. Dress for the weather — Summer: layers + rain jacket. Winter: thermal underwear, hat, gloves, and waterproof boots.
  3. Buy a Troika card — It works on metro, buses, and trams. Top up at machines in stations.
  4. Watch your belongings — Petty theft happens in crowded metro and tourist spots. Keep bags zipped and phones secure.
  5. Book museum tickets online — The Hermitage and Peterhof have long queues in summer. Use GetYourGuide for skip-the-line options.
  6. Try local drinks — Kvass, mors (berry juice), and Russian tea from a samovar. Avoid tap water.
  7. Visit during White Nights — Late May to mid-July for 24-hour twilight. Book accommodation months ahead.
  8. Use Wi-Fi carefully — Public Wi-Fi requires registration (SMS). Buy a local SIM or eSIM from Airalo for data.
  9. Check bridge schedules — Drawbridges open nightly from 1:25 AM to 4:55 AM. If you stay on Vasilyevsky Island after that, you’re stuck until morning.
  10. Carry cash — While cards are accepted everywhere, some small stalls and taxis prefer rubles. ATMs are plentiful.
