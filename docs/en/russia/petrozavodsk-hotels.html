Top 10 Hotels in Petrozavodsk 2026: Budget to Luxury

Petrozavodsk, the capital of Karelia, is a gem on the shores of Lake Onega, blending Soviet-era charm with modern Russian hospitality. Whether you're visiting for the stunning Kizhi Island, the vibrant waterfront, or the local culture, finding the right hotel can make or break your trip. I’ve explored this city extensively, and in 2026, the hotel scene offers something for everyone—from frugal backpackers to luxury seekers.

The city’s neighborhoods each have a distinct vibe: the historic center near Lenin Square buzzes with cafes and museums, while the quieter Gagarin District offers proximity to the lake. Hotel infrastructure here is solid, with most properties offering free Wi-Fi, breakfast, and friendly English-speaking staff, though amenities like pools are rare outside top-tier spots.

Prices in Petrozavodsk are surprisingly reasonable compared to Moscow or St. Petersburg, but they do spike during the summer (June–August) and major holidays. A budget room can start as low as ₽2 500 (≈ $27), while mid-range options hover around ₽5 000–₽12 000 (≈ $54–$130). Luxury properties push ₽15 000+ (≈ $162+).

For the best deals, I recommend booking early—especially for July’s White Nights Festival. As a quick reference, hotel prices in Petrozavodsk can be ₽2 500–₽18 000 per night, depending on the season and category. Let’s dive into the top 10 picks for 2026, broken down by budget to luxury.

Budget Hotels (under $50/night)

These options are perfect for solo travelers, students, or anyone looking to save without sacrificing comfort. Expect clean rooms, basic amenities, and central locations—just don’t expect fancy extras.

1. Hotel Severnaya

A classic Soviet-era property recently renovated, Hotel Severnaya sits right on Lenin Square, steps from the National Museum and embankment. Rooms are compact but spotless, with wooden floors and simple furnishings. The on-site café serves hearty Karelian breakfasts (try the fish pie!). Pros: unbeatable location, friendly staff, free parking. Cons: thin walls, no elevator, and some rooms face a busy street. Prices start at ₽3 200 (≈ $35) per night for a standard single.

2. Hostel Karelia

For the ultimate budget experience, Hostel Karelia offers dorms and private rooms near the train station. It’s a social hub with a common kitchen, free tea, and a cozy lounge. The staff organize walking tours to Kizhi in summer. Pros: cheap as chips, great for meeting people, lockers provided. Cons: basic mattresses, shared bathrooms, and it’s a 15-minute walk to the lake. Dorm beds from ₽1 200 (≈ $13), private rooms from ₽2 800 (≈ $30).

3. Mini-Hotel Na Gertsena

Tucked on a quiet street near the main pedestrian zone, Mini-Hotel Na Gertsena feels like a home away from home. Each of its 10 rooms has a unique theme—think Karelian forest murals or Soviet memorabilia. The owner bakes fresh pastries for breakfast. Pros: personal touch, quiet area, free Wi-Fi. Cons: no 24-hour reception, limited parking. Prices from ₽3 500 (≈ $38) per night.

Mid-Range Hotels ($50-150/night)

These hotels balance comfort and value, ideal for couples, families, or business travelers. Expect private bathrooms, better soundproofing, and often a restaurant or bar on-site.

4. Park Inn Petrozavodsk

A reliable chain option, Park Inn is located on the waterfront with panoramic views of Lake Onega. Rooms are modern and spacious, with work desks and flatscreen TVs. The breakfast buffet is generous—think blini, fresh fruit, and local cheeses. Highlights: fitness center, sauna, and a stylish lobby bar. Prices range from ₽5 500–₽9 000 (≈ $59–$97) per night. It’s a 10-minute walk to the city center.

5. Hotel Karelia

This Soviet-era giant has been fully revamped, and Hotel Karelia now boasts sleek interiors, a business center, and a popular restaurant serving Karelian cuisine (try the salmon soup). Rooms are large with lake or city views. Highlights: indoor pool (rare in Petrozavodsk!), free parking, and a kids’ play area. Prices from ₽6 000–₽10 000 (≈ $65–$108) per night. It’s a 5-minute drive from the train station.

6. Boutique-Hotel Onega

For a romantic stay, Boutique-Hotel Onega offers just 15 rooms in a historic building near the National Theater. Each room has exposed brick, vintage furniture, and a rain shower. The rooftop terrace is a summer highlight. Highlights: complimentary wine in the evening, bike rentals, and a library. Prices from ₽7 500–₽12 000 (≈ $81–$130) per night. Book early—it fills up fast.

7. Hotel Severny

A newer property in the Gagarin District, Hotel Severny is a 20-minute walk to the lake but close to the city’s main shopping center. Rooms are minimalist but comfortable, with soundproof windows and plush bedding. Highlights: 24-hour reception, on-site café, and a small gym. Prices from ₽5 000–₽8 000 (≈ $54–$86) per night. It’s a good choice for those who prefer peace over location.

Luxury Hotels ($150+/night)

If you’re celebrating a special occasion or just want to indulge, these top-tier hotels offer impeccable service, premium amenities, and unforgettable experiences.

8. Radisson Blu Petrozavodsk

The city’s only international luxury brand, Radisson Blu sits on the embankment with floor-to-ceiling windows overlooking Lake Onega. Rooms are elegantly appointed with marble bathrooms, smart TVs, and Nespresso machines. Features: a full-service spa, indoor pool, fine-dining restaurant (the Karelian tasting menu is a must), and a cigar lounge. Prices from ₽15 000–₽25 000 (≈ $162–$270) per night. It’s the go-to for VIPs and honeymooners.

9. Art-Hotel Karelia

Blending art and luxury, Art-Hotel Karelia showcases local paintings and sculptures throughout its 20 suites. Each suite has a private sauna, Jacuzzi, and a balcony with city views. Features: a gourmet breakfast with champagne, butler service, and a private library. It’s located on the main square, so everything is walkable. Prices from ₽18 000–₽30 000 (≈ $194–$324) per night. Perfect for connoisseurs.

10. Villa on Onego

For ultimate privacy, Villa on Onego is a standalone mansion on the lake’s edge, a 15-minute drive from the city center. It has 5 suites, each with a fireplace, a private terrace, and a hot tub. Features: a private chef (available for hire), a boat dock, and a Finnish sauna. The property is surrounded by pine forest. Prices from ₽25 000–₽40 000 (≈ $270–$432) per night. Ideal for groups or romantic escapes.

How to Choose the Right Area

Petrozavodsk’s neighborhoods cater to different travel styles, so pick wisely:

  • Beach lovers: Stay near the Lake Onega embankment (around Radisson Blu or Park Inn). The “beach” is more a grassy park with swimming spots, but it’s perfect for summer sunsets. Hotels here offer lake views and easy access to boat tours.
  • Sightseeing: The city center (Lenin Square, Kirov Street) is best for history buffs. You’ll be steps from the National Museum, Cathedral, and Kizhi ferry terminal. Budget and mid-range hotels like Severnaya or Onega dominate here.
  • Nightlife: Head to the Gagarin District or the area around Prospekt Lenina. Bars and clubs like “Bar 21” and “Karelia Club” are lively. Hotels like Severny or Karelia are nearby, offering a quieter base after late nights.

Airport Transfers

Petrozavodsk’s Besovets Airport (PES) is about 12 km from the city center. Taxis are available at the terminal, costing around ₽1 500–₽2 000 (≈ $16–$22) for a 20-minute ride. Buses (route 101) run every hour for ₽30 (≈ $0.33) but drop you at the train station—then you’ll need a local bus or walk.

For convenience, I recommend pre-booking a transfer through Kiwitaxi—they offer fixed prices, meet-and-greet service, and child seats. A convenient transfer can be ₽2 500 (≈ $27) for a private car to most hotels. This saves stress after a long flight, especially if you’re arriving late.

Booking Tips

To get the best value in Petrozavodsk, follow these tips:

  • Book early: Summer (June–August) and New Year’s see prices double. Reserve 2–3 months ahead via Hotellook or Booking for the best rates. For budget stays, Agoda often has hostel deals.
  • Use price comparison: You can check prices on Aviasales or Hotellook to compare rates across platforms—this can save up to 20%.
  • Consider package deals: For luxury properties, booking via Booking with free cancellation is smart. For mid-range, Agoda sometimes offers loyalty discounts.
  • Local tips: Pay in cash (rubles) at check-in for potential discounts—some small hotels avoid card fees. Also, ask about “Karelian breakfast” upgrades—they’re often worth ₽500 (≈ $5).
  • Seasonal hacks: Visit in September for lower prices (₽3 000–₽10 000 per night) and stunning autumn colors. White Nights in July are magical but pricey—book by April.

For a seamless trip, consider buying a local SIM from Airalo for eSIM data—it’s cheap and works instantly. And if you’re planning day trips (like to Kizhi or Valaam), check GetYourGuide for tours that include hotel pickup.
