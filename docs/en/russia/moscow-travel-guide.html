Moscow is a city that grabs you by the shoulders and refuses to let go. It’s a sprawling, contradictory metropolis where golden onion domes rise next to brutalist Soviet towers, and centuries-old cathedrals stand in the shadow of glass skyscrapers. The energy here is electric — young Muscovites sip craft coffee in former factory lofts, while babushkas sell pickles at open-air markets.

You’ll feel the pulse of history on every street: from the cobblestones of Red Square to the leafy boulevards of the Patriarch’s Ponds, where Bulgakov set his devilish tales. Whether you’re a history buff, a foodie, or a nightlife seeker, Moscow offers a depth that few cities can match.

Why is 2026 the year to visit? For one, the city is constantly reinventing itself. New parks, pedestrian zones, and modern art spaces keep popping up, while classic attractions like the Kremlin and the Bolshoi Theatre remain world-class. The metro alone — a palace of marble, mosaics, and chandeliers — is worth the trip.

And after a day of sightseeing, you can warm up with a bowl of borscht or dance until dawn in a hidden basement club. Moscow is not a cheap destination, but it offers incredible value for its scale and grandeur. Just be prepared for extremes: freezing winters, dazzling summers, and a city that never sleeps.

How to Get There

Moscow is served by three major international airports: Sheremetyevo (SVO), Domodedovo (DME), and Vnukovo (VKO). Direct flights arrive from most European capitals, Dubai, Istanbul, Beijing, and many other global hubs. From New York, expect a flight time of about 10–11 hours; from London, around 4 hours; from Dubai, 5 hours.

Average round-trip economy fares from Europe range from ₽25 000 (≈ $270) to ₽50 000 (≈ $540), while from Asia or the Middle East, you might find deals from ₽30 000 (≈ $325) to ₽60 000 (≈ $650). From North America, prices are higher — typically ₽70 000 (≈ $760) to ₽120 000 (≈ $1300). Where to Stay

Moscow’s neighborhoods each have a distinct flavor. Tverskoy (city center) is the heart of the action — Red Square, the Kremlin, and GUM are all walkable. Luxury hotels like the Four Seasons Moscow (from ₽30 000/≈ $325 per night) dominate, but you can find mid-range options like the Mercure Arbat (₽8 000–12 000/≈ $87–$130). Arbat is touristy but charming, with street musicians and souvenir shops — budget hostels start at ₽2 000 (≈ $22) per night.

Patriarch’s Ponds is upscale and quiet, perfect for foodies. Zamoskvorechye (across the river) offers a more authentic, less crowded vibe with boutique hotels from ₽5 000 (≈ $54). For a Soviet-era experience, try VDNKh area near the Exhibition Center — hotels here are cheaper (₽3 000–6 000/≈ $33–$65) but farther from the center. Top 15 Attractions

  1. Red Square & St. Basil’s Cathedral — The iconic cobblestone square and the candy-colored cathedral with nine onion domes. Free entry to the square; St. Basil’s interior: ₽1 000 (≈ $11). Open daily 10:00–18:00.
  2. Kremlin & Armoury Chamber — The historic fortress and seat of Russian power. Tickets: Kremlin grounds ₽700 (≈ $8); Armoury Chamber ₽1 500 (≈ $16). Open 10:00–17:00, closed Thursdays.
  3. Lenin’s Mausoleum — See the embalmed body of the Soviet leader. Free entry, but expect long queues. Open Tue–Thu, Sat–Sun 10:00–13:00.
  4. Bolshoi Theatre — World-renowned opera and ballet. Tickets from ₽2 000 (≈ $22) for balcony seats to ₽15 000 (≈ $163) for stalls. Check schedule online.
  5. Tretyakov Gallery — The finest collection of Russian art, from icons to avant-garde. Tickets: ₽600 (≈ $7). Open Tue–Sun 10:00–18:00.
  6. Gorky Park — A massive green space along the Moscow River, with bike rentals, ice skating in winter, and modern art at the Garage Museum. Free entry; activities extra.
  7. VDNKh (Exhibition of Achievements of National Economy) — A sprawling Soviet-era park with futuristic pavilions, the giant Worker and Kolkhoz Woman statue, and the Ostankino Tower. Free entry to grounds; pavilion tickets from ₽500 (≈ $5).
  8. Moscow Metro Tour — Visit the most ornate stations like Komsomolskaya, Mayakovskaya, and Kievskaya. A single ride costs ₽70 (≈ $0.76). Guided tours from ₽1 500 (≈ $16).
  9. Cathedral of Christ the Saviour — The tallest Orthodox church in the world, rebuilt after Soviet destruction. Free entry. Open daily 8:00–20:00.
  10. Arbat Street — A historic pedestrian street with souvenir stalls, street performers, and cafes. Free to walk. Open 24/7.
  11. Moscow City (Business Center) — A cluster of futuristic skyscrapers with observation decks. Tickets to the 56th floor of the Federation Tower: ₽1 200 (≈ $13). Open 10:00–22:00.
  12. Novodevichy Convent & Cemetery — A UNESCO World Heritage site with beautiful architecture, plus the resting place of Chekhov, Gogol, and Yeltsin. Convent tickets: ₽500 (≈ $5). Open daily 9:00–17:00.
  13. Museum of Cosmonautics — Dedicated to the Soviet space program, with real spacecraft and exhibits. Tickets: ₽500 (≈ $5). Open Tue–Sun 10:00–19:00.
  14. Tsaritsyno Palace & Park — A stunning Catherine-era palace complex with a large park and museum. Tickets: ₽500 (≈ $5). Park free. Open 10:00–18:00.
  15. Bunker 42 (Cold War Museum) — A former secret nuclear bunker 65 meters underground. Guided tours: ₽2 500 (≈ $27). Book in advance.

Getting Around

The Moscow Metro is the fastest and cheapest way to travel. A single ride costs ₽70 (≈ $0.76); a 60-ride pass is ₽2 300 (≈ $25). The system is clean, efficient, and runs from 5:30 AM to 1:00 AM. Buses, trams, and trolleybuses cover the rest — fares are the same as metro, and you can use the Troika card (a rechargeable smart card) for all public transport.

Taxis are widely available via Yandex Go app — typical city rides cost ₽500–1 500 (≈ $5–$16). Avoid hailing cabs on the street; always use the app for fixed prices. Rental cars start at ₽3 000 (≈ $33) per day, but traffic is notorious, and parking is expensive (up to ₽500/≈ $5 per hour in the center). For airport transfers, you can pre-book a comfortable ride on Kiwitaxi — prices from SVO to city center start at ₽2 500 (≈ $27).

Where to Eat

Moscow’s food scene ranges from Soviet-era canteens to Michelin-starred restaurants. Local cuisine must-tries: borscht (beetroot soup), pelmeni (dumplings), blini (pancakes), shchi (cabbage soup), and beef stroganoff. For a classic Soviet experience, try Stolovaya No. 57 in GUM (meals from ₽500/≈ $5). Mid-range restaurants like Mari Vanna (home-style Russian) or Black Market Kitchen (modern Russian) serve full meals for ₽1 500–3 000 (≈ $16–$33).

High-end dining at White Rabbit (panoramic views) or Selfie costs ₽4 000–8 000 (≈ $43–$87) per person. For street food, grab a cheburek (fried pastry with meat) at a market stall for ₽200 (≈ $2) or a shashlik (kebab) from a street vendor for ₽400 (≈ $4). Don’t miss Danilovsky Market for a food hall experience with dozens of cuisines — meals from ₽800 (≈ $9).

Best Time to Visit

Summer (June–August) is peak season: warm (20–30°C), long days, and festivals. Prices are highest — hotels can double. Winter (December–February) is brutally cold (-10 to -20°C), but magical with snow-covered Red Square and Christmas markets. Prices drop significantly.

Spring (March–May) is muddy and unpredictable, but April–May brings cherry blossoms and fewer crowds. Autumn (September–October) offers golden leaves and mild weather, perfect for walking — also shoulder season with moderate prices. For budget travelers, visit in late January or February; for the best weather, aim for mid-May to early June or September.

Visa & Documents

Most foreign nationals need a Russian visa to enter Moscow. Apply at least 4–6 weeks in advance through the Russian embassy or visa center in your country. You’ll need a valid passport (6+ months), a visa invitation (from a hotel or tour agency), and a completed application form. Tourist visas cost around ₽5 000–10 000 (≈ $54–$109) depending on processing speed.

Citizens of several CIS countries (e.g., Belarus, Kazakhstan) do not need a visa. For domestic travel within Russia, you only need your Russian internal passport (if you have one) or your foreign passport with a valid visa — no additional permits are required for Moscow itself. Always carry your passport with you; police may ask for identification.

10 Travel Tips

  1. Learn basic Russian phrases — English is not widely spoken outside tourist areas. “Spasibo” (thank you) and “Zdravstvuyte” (hello) go a long way.
  2. Carry cash — While cards are accepted in most places, some markets and small cafes still prefer rubles. ATMs are everywhere.
  3. Dress for the weather — Winters require thermal layers, a warm coat, hat, and gloves. Summers are warm but pack a light jacket for evenings.
  4. Use the metro — It’s fast, cheap, and an attraction in itself. Download the Yandex Metro app for offline maps.
  5. Beware of pickpockets — Especially on crowded metro trains and in tourist spots like Arbat Street. Keep valuables in a zipped bag.
  6. Visit museums on weekdays — Many museums are closed on Mondays (check hours). Weekends are crowded.
  7. Try the local beer and kvass — Russian craft beer is excellent, and kvass (fermented rye drink) is a refreshing non-alcoholic option.
  8. Get a Troika card — Available at metro kiosks for ₽50 (≈ $0.54) deposit. It works on metro, buses, and trams, and saves you time.
  9. Book popular attractions in advance — The Armoury Chamber, Bolshoi Theatre, and Bunker 42 sell out quickly. Use GetYourGuide or official sites to reserve.
  10. Stay connected with a local SIM — Buy an eSIM from Airalo before you arrive for affordable data (from ₽500/≈ $5 for 1 GB). Alternatively, get a physical SIM at the airport from providers like MTS or Beeline.

Moscow is a city of contrasts — overwhelming at first, but endlessly rewarding. Take your time, wander off the main streets, and let its rhythm pull you in. Whether you’re marveling at the Kremlin’s towers or sipping hot chocolate at a cozy café, you’ll leave with stories that last a lifetime.
