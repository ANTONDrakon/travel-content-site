15 Best Things to Do in Bangkok 2026

Updated: July 2026 · Expert guide · ⏱ 5 min read

Bangkok in 2026 is still the same beautiful chaos it’s always been, but with a few shiny new metro lines and cleaner canals. I first visited ten years ago and fell in love with the street food steam rising at dawn and the golden spires catching the sunset. Now, with even better transport and more green spaces, it’s the perfect time to explore. Whether you’re a first-timer or a returning traveler, here’s my personal guide to the best things to do this year.

15 Best Attractions

1. The Grand Palace & Wat Phra Kaew

The glittering heart of Bangkok. The Temple of the Emerald Buddha (Wat Phra Kaew) is the most sacred site in Thailand, with a jade Buddha statue dressed in seasonal robes. The palace grounds are a maze of golden stupas, murals, and ornate halls. Allow at least two hours to wander respectfully.

Ticket price: ₽3 500 (≈ $38) for foreigners.
Hours: Daily 8:30 AM – 3:30 PM.
How to get there: Take the BTS Skytrain to Saphan Taksin, then a Chao Phraya Express Boat to Tha Chang Pier (₽65 / ≈ $0.70). Or take a taxi from central Bangkok (₽300–500 / ≈ $3–5).
Tip: Dress modestly — shoulders and knees covered. If you forget, buy a sarong from the stalls outside for about ₽500 (≈ $5).

2. Wat Pho (Temple of the Reclining Buddha)

Home to a 46-meter-long golden reclining Buddha that fills an entire hall. The temple complex is also the birthplace of traditional Thai massage. You can get a foot massage at the on-site school for around ₽1 200 (≈ $13) for 30 minutes.

Ticket price: ₽1 000 (≈ $11).
Hours: Daily 8:00 AM – 6:30 PM.
How to get there: A 10-minute walk from the Grand Palace. Or take bus 508 from Siam (₽40 / ≈ $0.40).
Tip: Go early (before 10 AM) to avoid tour groups and the midday heat.

3. Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn)

A stunning riverside temple covered in colorful porcelain. You can climb the steep central prang (spire) for a panoramic view of the Chao Phraya River. It looks magical at sunset when the lights reflect off the water.

Ticket price: ₽500 (≈ $5.50).
Hours: Daily 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM.
How to get there: Take the BTS to Saphan Taksin, then a ferry from Sathorn Pier to Wat Arun (₽65 / ≈ $0.70).
Tip: Cross the river to the opposite bank for the best photo spot at sunset.

4. Chatuchak Weekend Market

One of the largest weekend markets in the world, with over 15,000 stalls. You can find everything from vintage clothes and antiques to live plants and street food. It’s easy to get lost, but that’s part of the fun. Bring cash and a reusable bag.

Ticket price: Free entry.
Hours: Saturday & Sunday 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM (some stalls open Friday).
How to get there: BTS Mo Chit station (exit 1) or MRT Chatuchak Park station. Both are a short walk.
Tip: Visit the northern section (Section 7–9) for affordable Thai street food like pad thai and coconut ice cream.

5. Khao San Road

The backpacker hub of Bangkok. By day, it’s a sleepy street with guesthouses; by night, it transforms into a neon-lit party zone with bars, street food, and souvenir stalls. It’s loud, chaotic, and utterly iconic.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Bars open until 2:00 AM.
How to get there: Take a taxi from central Bangkok (₽250–400 / ≈ $3–4) or the Chao Phraya Express Boat to Tha Phra Athit (₽65 / ≈ $0.70).
Tip: Try a fried scorpion from a street cart (₽200 / ≈ $2) for the Instagram shot.

6. Jim Thompson House Museum

The former home of an American silk entrepreneur who vanished mysteriously in 1967. The museum showcases traditional Thai architecture, art, and a stunning silk collection. The guided tour (included in ticket) is excellent.

Ticket price: ₽1 200 (≈ $13).
Hours: Daily 10:00 AM – 6:00 PM (last tour at 5:00 PM).
How to get there: BTS National Stadium station (exit 2), then a 5-minute walk.
Tip: Visit the on-site restaurant for a quiet lunch in the garden (₽600–900 / ≈ $6–10).

7. Chao Phraya River Cruise

A boat ride along the "River of Kings" is the best way to see Bangkok’s temples, skyscrapers, and floating villages. You can take a public express boat (cheap) or a dinner cruise (romantic). For 2026, new electric ferries are quieter and cleaner.

Ticket price: Public boat: ₽65–200 (≈ $0.70–2). Dinner cruise: ₽2 500–5 000 (≈ $27–55).
Hours: Public boats run 6:00 AM – 8:00 PM. Dinner cruises depart 7:00 PM.
How to get there: Board at Sathorn Pier (BTS Saphan Taksin).
Tip: Book a dinner cruise through GetYourGuide for a buffet and live music — prices start at ₽3 000 (≈ $33).

8. Asiatique The Riverfront

A large night market and entertainment complex on the river. It’s cleaner and more organized than Khao San Road, with a Ferris wheel, restaurants, and shops. The Calypso Cabaret show is a hit with families.

Ticket price: Free entry.
Hours: Daily 4:00 PM – midnight.
How to get there: Free shuttle boat from Sathorn Pier (every 15 minutes). Or take a taxi (₽300 / ≈ $3).
Tip: Visit the food court on the second floor for affordable local dishes (₽200–400 / ≈ $2–4).

9. Yaowarat Road (Chinatown)

Bangkok’s Chinatown is a sensory overload of gold shops, incense, and the best street food in the city. Yaowarat Road comes alive at night with stalls selling seafood, dim sum, and roasted duck. Don’t miss the grilled seafood platters.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Street food stalls from 5:00 PM to midnight.
How to get there: MRT Wat Mangkon station (exit 1).
Tip: Try the oyster omelet at Nai Mong Hoi Thod (₽250 / ≈ $3) and the mango sticky rice at Kor Panich (₽150 / ≈ $1.50).

10. Lumpini Park

Bangkok’s "Central Park" — a green oasis in the middle of skyscrapers. You can jog, rent a paddleboat on the lake, or watch monitor lizards sunbathing. It’s a peaceful escape from the city noise.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: Daily 4:30 AM – 9:00 PM.
How to get there: MRT Silom station (exit 3) or BTS Sala Daeng station.
Tip: Join the free outdoor aerobics class at 6:00 PM — locals love it.

11. Wat Saket (Golden Mount)

A man-made hill topped with a golden chedi. Climbing the 318 steps is a mini-pilgrimage, and the view from the top is worth every step. The temple is especially beautiful during the November Loy Krathong festival.

Ticket price: ₽500 (≈ $5.50).
Hours: Daily 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM.
How to get there: Take a taxi from Khao San Road (₽150 / ≈ $1.50) or walk 20 minutes.
Tip: Go at sunset for golden light and fewer crowds.

12. Bangkok Art and Culture Centre (BACC)

A contemporary art museum with rotating exhibitions, a rooftop sculpture garden, and a cafe. It’s free to enter and a great spot to cool down. The galleries showcase Thai and international artists.

Ticket price: Free (special exhibitions may cost ₽500 / ≈ $5.50).
Hours: Tuesday–Sunday 10:00 AM – 9:00 PM (closed Monday).
How to get there: BTS Siam station (exit 3), connected via skywalk.
Tip: Check the schedule for free film screenings on weekends.

13. Siam Square & Siam Paragon

The shopping heart of Bangkok. Siam Square is a maze of indie boutiques and street food, while Siam Paragon is a luxury mall with a basement aquarium (Sea Life Bangkok). The aquarium is one of the largest in Southeast Asia.

Ticket price: Shopping: free. Sea Life: ₽2 500 (≈ $27) online.
Hours: Malls: 10:00 AM – 10:00 PM.
How to get there: BTS Siam station (direct access).
Tip: Book Sea Life tickets on GetYourGuide to skip the line — often 20% cheaper than at the door.

14. Erawan Shrine

A small but incredibly popular Hindu shrine in the middle of a busy intersection. It’s dedicated to Brahma, and you’ll see locals and tourists making offerings of flowers, incense, and wooden elephants. The traditional Thai dance performances are captivating.

Ticket price: Free (offerings cost extra).
Hours: 24 hours daily.
How to get there: BTS Chit Lom station (exit 4).
Tip: Buy a garland of marigolds from the vendors (₽100 / ≈ $1) for a blessing.

15. Khlong Toei Market

Bangkok’s largest fresh market, where you can see (and smell) the raw energy of the city. It’s not for the squeamish — there are live frogs, bloodied meat, and piles of durian. But it’s an authentic glimpse into local life. Go early in the morning.

Ticket price: Free.
Hours: 4:00 AM – 10:00 AM.
How to get there: MRT Khlong Toei station (exit 1).
Tip: Bring a face mask — the fish section is pungent. Try the fresh coconut water (₽50 / ≈ $0.50).

1-Day, 2-Day, 3-Day Itineraries

1-Day Itinerary: The Classics

  • Morning (8:00 AM – 12:00 PM): Visit the Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew. Then walk to Wat Pho (10 minutes).
  • Lunch (12:00 PM – 1:30 PM): Eat at a riverside restaurant near Tha Tien Pier — try tom yum goong (₽300 / ≈ $3).
  • Afternoon (1:30 PM – 4:00 PM): Take a ferry to Wat Arun for photos. Then BTS to Siam for shopping at Siam Paragon.
  • Evening (5:00 PM – 9:00 PM): Take a Chao Phraya dinner cruise. Book via GetYourGuide (₽3 500 / ≈ $38).

2-Day Itinerary: Temples + Markets

  • Day 1: Same as 1-day itinerary, but skip the cruise. End the evening at Khao San Road for street food and people-watching.
  • Day 2 Morning (9:00 AM – 12:00 PM): Chatuchak Weekend Market (if Saturday/Sunday) or Jim Thompson House Museum (if weekday).
  • Day 2 Afternoon (1:00 PM – 4:00 PM): Lumpini Park for a paddleboat ride (₽200 / ≈ $2).
  • Day 2 Evening (5:00 PM – 10:00 PM): Yaowarat Road (Chinatown) for the best street food in Bangkok. Try grilled squid and mango sticky rice.

3-Day Itinerary: Full Immersion

  • Day 1: Same as 1-day itinerary.
  • Day 2: Same as 2-day itinerary, Day 2.
  • Day 3 Morning (8:00 AM – 11:00 AM): Khlong Toei Market for a raw local experience. Then Wat Saket for the view.
  • Day 3 Afternoon (12:00 PM – 3:00 PM): BACC art museum + lunch at a cafe in Siam Square.
  • Day 3 Evening (4:00 PM – 9:00 PM): Asiatique for the Ferris wheel (₽500 / ≈ $5.50) and a Calypso Cabaret show (₽2 000 / ≈ $22).

Tours & Excursions

If you want a guided experience, Bangkok has excellent tours that save you time and hassle. Popular options include:

  • Half-day Grand Palace & Wat Pho tour: ₽2 500 (≈ $27) — includes a guide and skip-the-line entry.
  • Chao Phraya River dinner cruise: ₽3 500–5 000 (≈ $38–55) — buffet, live music, and skyline views.
  • Floating market tour (Damnoen Saduak): ₽4 000 (≈ $44) — includes hotel pickup and a long-tail boat ride.
  • Ayutthaya day trip: ₽6 000 (≈ $65) — train or minivan to the ancient capital, with temple visits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do attractions cost in Bangkok?

Entrance fees vary: from free attractions to $20-30 for museums. Many venues have discount days.

Do I need a guide in Bangkok?

A guide is recommended for historical sites and complex routes. Self-guided travel is possible with audio guides and maps.
