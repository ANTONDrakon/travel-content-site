15 Best Things to Do in Bangkok 2026

Bangkok is a city that never sleeps, blending ancient temples with neon-lit skyscrapers, chaotic street markets with serene canals. Whether you're a first-time visitor or a seasoned traveler, the Thai capital offers an endless list of experiences.

From the glittering Grand Palace to hidden alleyway food stalls, each attraction tells a story. In this guide, I'll walk you through the 15 best things to do in Bangkok in 2026, with practical tips on prices, transport, and how to make the most of your time. Let's dive in!

15 Best Attractions

1. The Grand Palace

The Grand Palace is the heart of Bangkok's royal history. Built in 1782, this sprawling complex houses the revered Wat Phra Kaew (Temple of the Emerald Buddha). The intricate gold detailing, mirrored mosaics, and towering spires will leave you speechless. It’s a must-see for anyone wanting to understand Thai culture.

Ticket price: ₽1 500 (≈ $15) per person. Hours: Daily 8:30 AM – 3:30 PM. How to get there: Take the BTS Skytrain to Saphan Taksin station, then a Chao Phraya Express Boat to Tha Chang pier (about ₽60 / ≈ $0.60). Alternatively, a taxi from central Bangkok costs around ₽400 (≈ $4). Tip: Go early (before 10 AM) to avoid crowds and the midday heat. Dress modestly – knees and shoulders must be covered.

2. Wat Pho (Temple of the Reclining Buddha)

Just a short walk from the Grand Palace, Wat Pho is home to a 46-meter-long gold-plated Reclining Buddha. The temple also serves as Thailand’s first public university, with traditional massage school. The peaceful courtyards and hundreds of Buddha images make it a serene escape.

Ticket price: ₽600 (≈ $6). Hours: Daily 8:00 AM – 6:30 PM. How to get there: Same as Grand Palace – boat to Tha Tien pier (₽60 / ≈ $0.60). Tip: Try a 30-minute Thai massage at the on-site school for ₽800 (≈ $8) – a true bargain.

3. Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn)

Across the river from Wat Pho, Wat Arun is known for its stunning central prang (spire) decorated with colorful porcelain. Climb the steep steps for panoramic views of the Chao Phraya River and Bangkok skyline. It’s especially magical at sunset.

Ticket price: ₽200 (≈ $2). Hours: Daily 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM. How to get there: Take a cross-river ferry from Tha Tien pier (₽15 / ≈ $0.15). Tip: Visit in late afternoon for golden hour photos – the light hits the temple perfectly.

4. Chatuchak Weekend Market

One of the largest weekend markets in the world, Chatuchak has over 15,000 stalls selling everything from vintage clothes to live plants. It’s a shopper’s paradise, but also a feast for the senses – smell sizzling street food, hear bargaining shouts, and see colorful textiles. Plan to spend at least 3-4 hours here.

Ticket price: Free entry. Hours: Saturday & Sunday 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM (some stalls open Friday). How to get there: BTS Skytrain to Mo Chit station (exit 1), or MRT to Chatuchak Park station. Tip: Bring cash – most stalls don’t accept cards. Wear comfortable shoes; it’s huge!

5. Khao San Road

The backpacker hub of Bangkok, Khao San Road is a lively strip of bars, street food, and budget hostels. By day, it’s a place to grab a cheap pad Thai or a coconut ice cream; by night, it transforms into a party scene with music blaring from every bar. It’s chaotic, but that’s the charm.

Ticket price: Free to walk around. Hours: Shops and bars open from 10:00 AM until late (often 2-3 AM). How to get there: Taxi or tuk-tuk from central Bangkok (around ₽300 / ≈ $3). No direct BTS or MRT. Tip: Bargain hard for souvenirs – start at half the asking price.

6. Jim Thompson House Museum

This beautiful teakwood house was the home of American silk entrepreneur Jim Thompson, who mysteriously disappeared in 1967. The museum showcases his art collection and Thai architecture, set in lush tropical gardens. It’s a quiet oasis in the middle of the city.

Ticket price: ₽800 (≈ $8). Hours: Daily 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM (last tour at 5:00 PM). How to get there: BTS to National Stadium station (exit 2) – a 5-minute walk. Tip: Join the guided tour (included in ticket) to learn the fascinating story behind the house.

7. Asiatique The Riverfront

Asiatique combines a night market with a riverside mall, offering shopping, dining, and entertainment. Ride the giant Ferris wheel for skyline views, or catch a traditional Thai puppet show. It’s less chaotic than other markets, making it great for families.

Ticket price: Free entry. Ferris wheel: ₽800 (≈ $8). Hours: Daily 11:00 AM – 11:00 PM (shops open from 4:00 PM). How to get there: Free shuttle boat from Saphan Taksin BTS station (every 15 minutes). Tip: Visit after 6 PM when the lights come on – it’s gorgeous.

8. Wat Saket (Golden Mount)

Wat Saket is a temple built on an artificial hill, offering 360-degree views of Bangkok from its golden chedi. The climb up 318 steps is lined with bells and statues, making it a meditative experience. It’s especially popular during the Loy Krathong festival.

Ticket price: ₽150 (≈ $1.50). Hours: Daily 8:00 AM – 7:00 PM. How to get there: MRT to Sam Yot station, then a 10-minute walk. Tip: Go at sunset for the best photos – the city glows below.

9. Siam Paragon & Siam Square

For luxury shopping, Siam Paragon is Bangkok’s premier mall, with high-end brands, a huge aquarium (Siam Ocean World), and a food court. Nearby Siam Square is full of trendy boutiques and cafes. It’s a great place to escape the heat.

Ticket price: Free entry. Siam Ocean World: ₽2 500 (≈ $25). Hours: Daily 10:00 AM – 10:00 PM. How to get there: BTS to Siam station (directly connected). Tip: Check out the basement food court for affordable Thai eats – a plate of pad see ew costs about ₽200 (≈ $2).

10. Chinatown (Yaowarat)

Bangkok’s Chinatown is a sensory overload of gold shops, incense, and street food. Yaowarat Road comes alive at night with sizzling seafood stalls. Try the grilled river prawns, dim sum, or mango sticky rice. It’s a foodie’s dream.

Ticket price: Free to explore. Hours: Best visited from 6 PM – midnight (street food stalls). How to get there: MRT to Wat Mangkon station (exit 1). Tip: Bring cash and an empty stomach – portions are generous and cheap.

11. Lumpini Park

Bangkok’s largest green space, Lumpini Park is perfect for a morning jog, a paddleboat ride, or just people-watching. You’ll see monitor lizards lounging by the lake, and locals practicing tai chi. It’s a peaceful break from the city chaos.

Ticket price: Free. Hours: Daily 4:30 AM – 9:00 PM. How to get there: BTS to Sala Daeng station, or MRT to Lumphini station. Tip: Rent a paddleboat for ₽100 (≈ $1) per hour – a fun, cheap activity.

12. Bangkok Art and Culture Centre (BACC)

This contemporary art space hosts rotating exhibitions of Thai and international artists. The spiral ramp galleries are free to explore, and there’s a cafe and gift shop. It’s a cool, air-conditioned spot for art lovers.

Ticket price: Free. Hours: Tuesday-Sunday 10:00 AM – 9:00 PM (closed Monday). How to get there: BTS to Siam station (exit 3) – a 5-minute walk. Tip: Check their website for current exhibitions – some are world-class.

13. Vimanmek Mansion

Once the world’s largest golden teakwood mansion, Vimanmek was a royal residence. It’s now a museum filled with antiques and royal memorabilia. The architecture is stunning, with intricate carvings and a peaceful garden setting.

Ticket price: ₽500 (≈ $5) – often combined with Grand Palace ticket. Hours: Daily 9:00 AM – 4:00 PM (last entry 3:15 PM). How to get there: Taxi from central Bangkok (₽400 / ≈ $4) or bus 503 from Mo Chit. Tip: Guided tours are mandatory and run every 30 minutes – they’re informative and included.

14. Ratchada Train Night Market

This vibrant night market is famous for its retro stalls, vintage goods, and seafood towers. The colorful vendor tents create a photo-worthy scene from above. It’s a great place to buy quirky souvenirs and eat grilled seafood.

Ticket price: Free entry. Hours: Thursday-Sunday 5:00 PM – midnight. How to get there: MRT to Thailand Cultural Centre station (exit 3). Tip: Go to the nearby Esplanade mall’s parking garage (4th floor) for the iconic overhead view.

15. Floating Markets (Damnoen Saduak)

About 1.5 hours from Bangkok, Damnoen Saduak is the most famous floating market. Women in traditional hats sell fruit, noodles, and souvenirs from wooden boats. It’s touristy but unforgettable. You can also visit Amphawa for a more local vibe.

Ticket price: Free entry. Boat ride: ₽500-1 500 (≈ $5-15) depending on length. Hours: Best visited 7:00 AM – noon (market ends early). How to get there: Book a tour or take a minivan from Mo Chit or Khao San Road (₽800 / ≈ $8 round trip). Tip: Arrive by 8 AM to avoid the worst crowds – it gets packed.

1-Day, 2-Day, 3-Day Itineraries

1-Day Itinerary: Temple & Market Blitz

Start at the Grand Palace and Wat Pho (morning). Cross to Wat Arun for lunch at a riverside café. In the afternoon, take a taxi to Khao San Road for a quick wander, then head to Chinatown for dinner (Yaowarat). Finish with a nightcap at a rooftop bar like Lebua for skyline views. Total walking: about 6 km. Best for: travelers with limited time who want the highlights.

2-Day Itinerary: Culture & Shopping

Day 1: Morning at Grand Palace + Wat Pho. Afternoon at Jim Thompson House and Siam Square shopping. Evening at Asiatique for dinner and the Ferris wheel. Day 2: Morning at Chatuchak Market (if weekend). Afternoon at Lumpini Park and BACC. Evening at Ratchada Train Night Market. Total cost for entry fees: about ₽3 000 (≈ $30). Best for: couples and culture lovers.

3-Day Itinerary: Full Immersion

Day 1: Temples (Grand Palace, Wat Pho, Wat Arun). Day 2: Markets (Chatuchak + Chinatown) and Jim Thompson House. Day 3: Day trip to Damnoen Saduak floating market (morning), then relax at a Thai massage parlor (e.g., Health Land for ₽1 000 / ≈ $10 for 2 hours). Evening at a rooftop bar. Total cost: about ₽5 000 (≈ $50) for entry fees and transport. Best for: first-time visitors wanting a complete experience.

Tours & Excursions

Bangkok’s tours save you time and hassle. Popular options include guided walking tours of the historic Rattanakosin Island (₽3 500 / ≈ $35 for 4 hours), food tours of Chinatown (₽4 000 / ≈ $40), and day trips to Ayutthaya’s ancient ruins (₽6 000 / ≈ $60 including transport and lunch). For floating markets, half-day tours cost around ₽4 500 (≈ $45). You can book tours and excursions through GetYourGuide, where you’ll find reviews and flexible cancellation policies.

Book Tickets Online

Buying tickets online saves time and often money. For popular attractions like the Grand Palace, Wat Pho, and Wat Arun, you can skip the queues by booking via Hotellook or Booking.com activities. For Siam Ocean World, online tickets are ₽2 200 (≈ $22) vs. ₽2 500 at the door. GetYourGuide also offers combo deals – for example, a Grand Palace + Wat Pho + Wat Arun tour for ₽2 500 (≈ $25). Always check for discounts on attraction tickets online.

Free Attractions

Bangkok has plenty of free things to do. Walk through Lumpini Park, explore the Bangkok Art and Culture Centre (BACC), or wander the grounds of Wat Saket (entry is free if you don’t climb the mount). The flower market (Pak Khlong Talat) is open 24/7 and costs nothing to visit.

For a spectacular view, head to the rooftop of the King Power Mahanakhon building’s free observation deck (after buying a drink at the bar). You can also watch monks receive alms at dawn near Wat Pho – a serene, free cultural experience.

Practical Tips

Transportation

Bangkok’s BTS Skytrain and MRT subway are the fastest ways to avoid traffic. A single BTS ride costs ₽60-200 (≈ $0.60-2). Get a Rabbit Card (₽500 / ≈ $5 deposit) for convenience. Taxis start at ₽100 (≈ $1) – always insist on the meter.

Tuk-tuks are fun but negotiate the price before hopping in (expect ₽200-400 / ≈ $2-4 for short trips). For airport transfers, consider Kiwitaxi for fixed prices – from Suvarnabhumi to central Bangkok, about ₽1 200 (≈ $12). To stay connected, buy a local SIM or use Airalo eSIM (₽400 / ≈ $4 for 7 days).

Combo Tickets

Save money with combo tickets. The Grand Palace ticket (₽1 500) includes entry to Vimanmek Mansion and Wat Phra Kaew. The Siam Ocean World + Madame Tussauds combo costs ₽3 000 (≈ $30) vs. ₽4 000 separately. Many tours on GetYourGuide bundle multiple attractions – check for deals before you go.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do attractions cost in Bangkok?

Entrance fees vary: from free attractions to $20-30 for museums. Many venues have discount days.

Do I need a guide in Bangkok?

A guide is recommended for historical sites and complex routes. Self-guided travel is possible with audio guides and maps.
