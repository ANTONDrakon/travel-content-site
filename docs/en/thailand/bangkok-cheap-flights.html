How to Find Cheap Flights to Bangkok 2026

Bangkok is a city that never sleeps—golden temples, buzzing street markets, and food that will make your taste buds sing. But before you can dive into a bowl of tom yum goong or haggle at Chatuchak Market, you need to get there without breaking the bank. I’ve spent years tracking flight prices, and I’m here to share everything I know about scoring cheap flights to Bangkok in 2026.

Bangkok is served by two main airports: Suvarnabhumi Airport (BKK), the primary international hub, and Don Mueang International Airport (DMK), which handles many budget carriers. Airlines flying to Bangkok from around the world include full-service carriers like Aeroflot, Emirates, Qatar Airways, and Thai Airways, as well as low-cost options like AirAsia, Scoot, and Nok Air. The key is knowing which airports and airlines suit your budget and route.

Let’s break it down step by step, so you can book with confidence and save rubles for the fun stuff.

Direct Flights

If you value time over money, direct flights are your best bet. In 2026, several airlines offer non-stop service to Bangkok from major cities worldwide. Here’s a snapshot of what to expect:

  • Aeroflot: Direct flights from Moscow (SVO) to BKK. Prices start around ₽45 000 (≈ $490) for economy in low season. Flight time is about 9 hours.
  • Thai Airways: Non-stop from Moscow (SVO) to BKK, with fares from ₽55 000 (≈ $600). Great service and included meals.
  • Emirates: Direct from Dubai (DXB) to BKK, with prices from ₽30 000 (≈ $330) for a one-way. Dubai is a common stopover for travelers from Europe and Africa.
  • Qatar Airways: Non-stop from Doha (DOH) to BKK, starting at ₽35 000 (≈ $380). Often includes a stopover deal.
  • Air Astana: Direct from Almaty (ALA) to BKK, with fares around ₽25 000 (≈ $270) for economy.

Direct flights are convenient but can be pricier. For the best deals, check Aviasales to compare all airlines and dates. They aggregate options from both BKK and DMK, so you can filter by airport.

Connecting Flights

Connecting flights are where the real savings hide. In 2026, the cheapest options often involve a layover in a hub city. Here’s what I’ve found works best:

  • Via Dubai: Fly with flydubai or Emirates from Moscow to Dubai (₽20 000–₽30 000 / ≈ $220–$330), then connect to Bangkok. Total time: 12–15 hours. Prices as low as ₽35 000 (≈ $380) round-trip.
  • Via Doha: Qatar Airways offers stopovers in Doha. Flights from Moscow to Doha start at ₽15 000 (≈ $165), then onward to BKK. Total: ₽40 000 (≈ $435).
  • Via Istanbul: Turkish Airlines from Moscow to Istanbul (₽18 000 / ≈ $195), then to Bangkok. Total around ₽45 000 (≈ $490). Longer layovers, but you can explore Istanbul for a day.
  • Via Beijing: Air China or China Southern from Moscow to Beijing (₽12 000 / ≈ $130), then to BKK. Total: ₽30 000–₽35 000 (≈ $330–$380). Flight time: 15–18 hours.
  • Via Kuala Lumpur: AirAsia from Moscow to KL (₽25 000 / ≈ $270), then to Bangkok. Total: ₽28 000 (≈ $305). Budget-friendly but longer.

Connecting flights can be searched on Aviasales and prices compared across dates. The key is flexibility—midweek departures (Tuesday, Wednesday) are often 20–30% cheaper than weekend flights. Use the "cheapest month" view on Aviasales to spot trends.

10 Hacks for Cheap Flights

I’ve tested these tricks over years of travel, and they consistently save me 20–50% on flights. Here are my top 10 hacks for Bangkok in 2026:

  1. Use incognito mode: Airlines and booking sites track your searches. Open a private browser window to prevent price hikes based on your history.
  2. Fly to DMK instead of BKK: Don Mueang Airport serves budget carriers like AirAsia and Nok Air. Flights from Moscow to DMK via connecting hubs can be ₽5 000–₽10 000 cheaper. Check Aviasales for both airports.
  3. Book a "hacker fare": Mix airlines—fly out with one carrier and return with another. For example, outbound on Aeroflot (₽25 000) and return on AirAsia (₽15 000). Use Aviasales to combine.
  4. Set price alerts: On Aviasales, enable alerts for your route. You’ll get an email when prices drop. I’ve snagged deals 30% below average this way.
  5. Consider stopover programs: Qatar Airways and Turkish Airlines offer free stopovers in Doha or Istanbul. Book a flight with a 1–2 day layover, and you get a free hotel. It’s like two trips for one price.
  6. Use credit card points: Many Russian banks offer miles on travel purchases. Transfer points to airline partners like Aeroflot Bonus or S7 Priority. A round-trip to Bangkok can cost just ₽10 000 in taxes.
  7. Fly on Tuesday or Wednesday: These are the cheapest days to depart. I’ve seen prices drop by ₽8 000–₽12 000 compared to Friday flights.
  8. Book 6–8 weeks in advance: For international flights, the sweet spot is 45–60 days before departure. Last-minute deals are rare for Bangkok.
  9. Check budget airlines for connections: Fly from Moscow to Almaty with Air Astana (₽12 000), then to Bangkok with AirAsia (₽10 000). Total ₽22 000 (≈ $240). Use Aviasales to build this route.
  10. Use a VPN: Sometimes, booking from a different country (e.g., Thailand or India) shows lower prices. Set your VPN to Thailand and compare on Hotellook for hotels too.

When to Book

Timing is everything. Based on 2026 trends, here’s my advice:

  • For peak season (December–January): Book by August–September. Prices rise 20–30% after October.
  • For low season (May–September): Book 4–6 weeks ahead. Sales often appear 2 months before.
  • For shoulder season (March–April, October–November): Book 6–8 weeks out. Watch for flash sales on Aviasales.

I recommend checking prices every Tuesday evening—airlines often release deals then. Also, use the "calendar" view on Aviasales to see the cheapest dates in a month.

Flight Insurance

Even with the best planning, delays and cancellations happen. In 2026, Bangkok’s airports can be busy during monsoons (May–October). Here’s what to do:

  • Check your rights: Under Russian law, airlines must compensate you for delays over 3 hours. This includes meals, hotel stays, and rebooking. For EU carriers, EU Regulation 261/2004 applies—compensation up to $686 (€600) (≈ ₽55 000).
  • Use a service: If your flight is delayed or cancelled, you can claim compensation via services like Compensair or AirHelp. They handle the paperwork for a fee (typically 25% of the payout).
  • Travel insurance: I always buy a policy that covers flight disruptions. Check Aviasales for add-on insurance when booking. For just ₽1 500 (≈ $16), you get coverage for delays, lost luggage, and medical emergencies.

Pro tip: Save your boarding pass and take screenshots of any delay notifications. This makes the claims process smoother.
