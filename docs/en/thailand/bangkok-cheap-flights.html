How to Find Cheap Flights to Bangkok 2026

Bangkok, the vibrant capital of Thailand, is one of the most popular travel destinations in Southeast Asia, and for good reason—it's a city that never sleeps, with stunning temples, bustling markets, and mouthwatering street food. If you're planning a trip in 2026, finding affordable flights is key to keeping your budget in check.

I've been traveling to Bangkok for years, and I've learned a few tricks to snag the best deals. In this guide, I'll walk you through everything you need to know, from direct and connecting flights to monthly price trends and insider hacks. Let's dive in!

Bangkok is served by two major airports: Suvarnabhumi Airport (BKK), which handles most international flights, and Don Mueang International Airport (DMK), a hub for low-cost carriers and domestic routes. Depending on where you're flying from, you might arrive at either.

Airlines serving the route include full-service carriers like Thai Airways, Emirates, Qatar Airways, and Singapore Airlines, as well as budget options like AirAsia, Nok Air, and Scoot. For the best prices, I recommend checking Aviasales—it's my go-to for comparing all airlines in one place.

Direct Flights

Direct flights to Bangkok are the most convenient option, especially if you're short on time or want to avoid layovers. In 2026, several airlines operate non-stop routes from major cities worldwide. Here's a breakdown by region:

  • From Moscow (SVO, DME, VKO) to BKK: Aeroflot and Thai Airways offer direct flights, with prices starting at ₽45 000 (≈ $490) round-trip in low season. During peak months, expect to pay ₽70 000 (≈ $760).
  • From London (LHR) to BKK: British Airways, Thai Airways, and EVA Air operate non-stop services. Prices range from ₽55 000 (≈ $600) to ₽85 000 (≈ $925), depending on the season.
  • From New York (JFK) to BKK: Thai Airways and Emirates have direct flights (Emirates via Dubai, but some are marketed as direct). Round-trip fares start at ₽65 000 (≈ $710) and can go up to ₽100 000 (≈ $1090).
  • From Dubai (DXB) to BKK: Emirates and Thai Airways fly direct, with prices as low as ₽25 000 (≈ $270) in off-peak times.
  • From Singapore (SIN) to BKK: Singapore Airlines and Thai Airways have frequent direct flights, costing ₽15 000 (≈ $160) to ₽30 000 (≈ $330) round-trip.

For the most accurate and up-to-date prices, I always use Aviasales—it's a fantastic tool that lets you filter by airline, duration, and price. You can also set price alerts to catch sales.

Connecting Flights

If you're flexible with time, connecting flights can save you a significant amount of money. The cheapest options often involve a layover in a major hub like Dubai, Doha, Istanbul, or Kuala Lumpur. Here are some popular routes:

  • Via Dubai (DXB): Fly with Emirates or flydubai from cities like Moscow, London, or New York. Total travel time is 12-16 hours, with prices starting at ₽35 000 (≈ $380) round-trip.
  • Via Doha (DOH): Qatar Airways offers excellent service, with layovers of 2-5 hours. Fares from Europe or North America begin at ₽40 000 (≈ $435).
  • Via Istanbul (IST): Turkish Airlines is a popular choice, with flights from major cities worldwide. Prices range from ₽38 000 (≈ $415) to ₽60 000 (≈ $655).
  • Via Kuala Lumpur (KUL): AirAsia and Malaysia Airlines offer budget-friendly connections from Australia, China, and India. Fares can be as low as ₽20 000 (≈ $220).

10 Hacks for Cheap Flights

Over the years, I've developed a set of strategies to score the cheapest flights to Bangkok. Here are my top 10 hacks:

  1. Use Incognito Mode: When searching for flights, always use your browser's incognito or private mode. Airlines and booking sites track your searches and may raise prices if they see repeated interest. I've tested this myself—prices can jump by 10-15% after a few searches.
  2. Set Price Alerts on Aviasales: Aviasales lets you set alerts for specific routes. You'll get an email or push notification when prices drop. I once caught a deal from Moscow to Bangkok for ₽28 000 (≈ $305)—half the usual price!
  3. Book Connecting Flights: As mentioned, layovers can save you up to 40%. For example, flying via Doha or Dubai often costs less than direct flights. Just be prepared for longer travel times.
  4. Fly on Weekdays: Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday are typically the cheapest days to fly. I avoid weekends, especially Fridays and Sundays, when prices are highest.
  5. Consider Nearby Airports: If you're flying from Europe, check flights to Phuket (HKT) or Chiang Mai (CNX) instead of Bangkok. From there, you can take a budget flight or train to Bangkok for ₽2 000 (≈ $22) to ₽5 000 (≈ $55).
  6. Use Budget Airlines for Short Hauls: For regional flights from nearby countries like Malaysia, Singapore, or Vietnam, use AirAsia, Nok Air, or Scoot. Fares can be as low as ₽3 000 (≈ $33) one-way.
  7. Book in Advance: For peak season (November-February), book at least 3-4 months ahead. For low season, 1-2 months is fine. I use Hotellook to compare hotel prices alongside flights—it's a great way to plan a budget trip.
  8. Check for Error Fares: Sometimes airlines accidentally list prices at a fraction of the normal rate. Follow deal sites like Secret Flying or use Aviasales' "Cheapest" filter to spot these gems.
  9. Use Miles or Points: If you have loyalty points with airlines like Aeroflot, Emirates, or Thai Airways, redeem them for flights. For example, 30,000 miles can get you a round-trip ticket from Moscow to Bangkok.
  10. Book a Package Deal: Sometimes bundling flights and hotels through Booking or Agoda can save you money. I once saved ₽12 000 (≈ $130) by booking a flight+hotel package for a week in Bangkok.

For tours and activities in Bangkok, I recommend GetYourGuide—they have great deals on temple tours, cooking classes, and river cruises. And for airport transfers, Kiwitaxi offers reliable private cars from BKK and DMK to your hotel.

When to Book

Timing is everything when it comes to cheap flights. Based on my experience and data from Aviasales, here's the optimal booking window for Bangkok in 2026:

  • Low Season (May-October): Book 1-2 months in advance. Prices are already low, so last-minute deals are rare. For example, a flight in September might cost ₽37 000 (≈ $405) if booked 6 weeks ahead.
  • Shoulder Season (March-April, November): Book 2-3 months ahead. April's Songkran festival can drive up prices, so early booking is key.
  • High Season (December-February): Book 4-6 months in advance. Christmas and New Year's flights from Moscow can hit ₽80 000 (≈ $870), but early birds might snag them for ₽60 000 (≈ $655).

I also recommend booking on a Tuesday or Wednesday, as airlines often release sales early in the week. Use Aviasales' calendar view to see the cheapest days to fly—it's a lifesaver!

Flight Insurance

Flight delays and cancellations can happen, especially during monsoon season (May-October) or peak travel periods. That's why I always recommend getting flight insurance. If your flight is delayed by more than 3 hours or cancelled, you may be entitled to compensation under EU Regulation 261/2004 (if flying from Europe) or similar laws in other regions. For example, a delay of 4 hours could get you ₽25 000 (≈ $270) in compensation.
