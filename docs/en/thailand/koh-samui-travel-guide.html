Koh Samui is Thailand’s second-largest island, a tropical paradise that blends pristine beaches with lush jungle interiors. The atmosphere here is wonderfully relaxed—think coconut palms swaying over white sand, warm turquoise waters, and a gentle pace of life that invites you to slow down. Unlike the party-heavy vibe of Phuket, Samui offers a more laid-back charm, with luxurious resorts, rustic beach bungalows, and a vibrant local culture centered around fishing villages and Buddhist temples.

Why is 2026 the year to visit? The island continues to evolve while preserving its natural beauty. New wellness retreats, farm-to-table dining, and eco-friendly tours have emerged, making it a top destination for both adventurers and those seeking serenity.

Whether you’re snorkeling around coral reefs, hiking to waterfalls, or simply lounging on Chaweng Beach, Koh Samui delivers a slice of heaven that feels both exotic and accessible. Plus, the local cuisine—bursting with fresh seafood, spicy curries, and tropical fruits—is a journey in itself.

This guide is your practical companion for planning a trip to Koh Samui in 2026. I’ve included real prices, honest tips, and all the details you need to navigate the island with confidence. Let’s dive in!

How to Get There

The main gateway is Samui International Airport (USM), a charming open-air airport with thatched-roof terminals. Direct flights from Bangkok take about 1 hour, with airlines like Bangkok Airways offering frequent service. From major Asian hubs like Singapore, Hong Kong, or Kuala Lumpur, you can find direct flights averaging ₽15 000–₽25 000 (≈ 6 000–10 000 THB).

From Europe or the US, expect to fly via Bangkok or Phuket, with total costs ranging ₽40 000–₽70 000 (≈ 16 000–28 000 THB) for a round trip. For the best deals, check flights on Aviasales, which compares hundreds of airlines to find the cheapest dates for your trip.

Where to Stay

Koh Samui offers neighborhoods for every vibe and budget. Chaweng is the liveliest area, with nightlife, shopping, and a long beach—perfect for first-timers. Mid-range hotels here cost ₽3 500–₽7 000 per night (≈ 1 400–2 800 THB), while luxury resorts like the Library start at ₽12 000 (≈ 4 800 THB). Lamai is quieter and more affordable, with bungalows from ₽1 500–₽3 000 (≈ 600–1 200 THB) and a relaxed atmosphere.

Bophut (Fisherman’s Village) charms with its wooden shophouses and Friday night market—rooms average ₽2 500–₽5 000 (≈ 1 000–2 000 THB). For ultimate seclusion, Mae Nam and Choeng Mon offer peaceful beaches and high-end villas from ₽6 000 (≈ 2 400 THB) per night. You can compare hotel prices and read reviews on Hotellook or Booking to find the best fit for your budget.

Top 15 Attractions

  1. Big Buddha Temple (Wat Phra Yai) – A 12-meter golden Buddha statue on a small island. Free entry, open 8:00–18:00.
  2. Wat Plai Laem – A colorful Chinese-Thai temple with a 18-arm Guanyin statue. Free, open 8:00–18:00.
  3. Grandfather and Grandmother Rocks (Hin Ta Hin Yai) – Natural rock formations shaped like genitalia. Free, open 24/7.
  4. Na Muang Waterfalls – Two scenic waterfalls in the jungle. Entry ₽100 (≈ 40 THB), open 8:00–17:00.
  5. Secret Buddha Garden – A hilltop garden with Buddha statues and panoramic views. Entry ₽200 (≈ 80 THB), open 8:00–17:00.
  6. Ang Thong National Marine Park – A stunning archipelago for kayaking, snorkeling, and hiking. Tours from ₽1 500 (≈ 600 THB) per person, including lunch.
  7. Samui Elephant Sanctuary – Ethical sanctuary where you can observe rescued elephants. Entry ₽2 000 (≈ 800 THB), open 9:00–16:00.
  8. Chaweng Beach – The island’s most popular beach, perfect for swimming and sunbathing. Free.
  9. Lamai Beach – Quieter than Chaweng, with good snorkeling. Free.
  10. Fisherman’s Village Walking Street – A Friday night market with food, crafts, and live music. Free entry.
  11. Koh Samui Aquarium and Tiger Zoo – Marine life and animal exhibits. Entry ₽500 (≈ 200 THB), open 9:00–17:00.
  12. Samui Butterfly Garden – A lush garden with hundreds of butterflies. Entry ₽300 (≈ 120 THB), open 8:30–17:00.
  13. Magic Alambic Rum Distillery – A working rum distillery with tastings. Tours ₽400 (≈ 160 THB), open 10:00–18:00.
  14. Khao Hua Jook Pagoda – A hilltop pagoda with panoramic views of the island. Free.
  15. Samui Night Market – Rotating night markets (Sunday at Lamai, Monday at Chaweng) for street food and souvenirs. Free entry.

Getting Around

The island has no metro or train system. Songthaews (shared pickup trucks) are the main public transport—they run fixed routes for ₽50–₽100 (≈ 20–40 THB) per ride. Taxis are plentiful but expensive; short trips cost ₽200–₽400 (≈ 80–160 THB), while longer journeys like Chaweng to Lamai are ₽500–₽700 (≈ 200–280 THB).

Motorbike rental is the most popular option—₽250–₽500 (≈ 100–200 THB) per day, but you’ll need an international driving permit and a helmet. Car rental starts at ₽1 500 (≈ 600 THB) per day. For airport transfers, a pre-booked Kiwitaxi is hassle-free with fixed prices and English-speaking drivers.

Where to Eat

Koh Samui is a food lover’s paradise. Street food at night markets is cheap and delicious—pad thai, mango sticky rice, and grilled seafood cost ₽100–₽200 (≈ 40–80 THB) per dish. For local Thai cuisine, try Krua Bophut or Sabeinglae (both in Bophut), where a full meal is ₽300–₽600 (≈ 120–240 THB) per person.

Mid-range restaurants like The Jungle Club (hilltop with views) or Tree Tops (fine dining) cost ₽1 000–₽2 500 (≈ 400–1 000 THB) per person. Don’t miss the Fisherman’s Village Walking Street on Fridays for an unforgettable food crawl.

Tours & Excursions

The best way to explore beyond the beaches is by joining a tour. Popular options include a full-day Ang Thong Marine Park tour (₽2 500–₽4 000, ≈ 1 000–1 600 THB) with snorkeling and kayaking, a sunset dinner cruise (₽3 000–₽5 000, ≈ 1 200–2 000 THB), or a jungle safari (₽2 000–₽3 500, ≈ 800–1 400 THB) visiting waterfalls and viewpoints.

For a unique experience, try a cooking class (₽1 500–₽2 500, ≈ 600–1 000 THB). You can browse and book tours on GetYourGuide, which offers verified reviews and free cancellation.

Airport Transfer

From Samui Airport, you have several options. Taxis are readily available—a ride to Chaweng costs ₽400–₽600 (≈ 160–240 THB), to Lamai ₽500–₽700 (≈ 200–280 THB), and to Bophut ₽300–₽500 (≈ 120–200 THB). Songthaews also run from the airport but are less frequent. For a stress-free experience, book a private transfer via Kiwitaxi, which offers fixed prices, meet-and-greet service, and comfortable vehicles—ideal after a long flight.

Connectivity

Most hotels and cafes offer free Wi-Fi, but speeds can vary. For reliable internet on the go, buy a local SIM card at the airport or 7-Eleven—AIS, TrueMove, and dtac offer tourist SIMs with 10–30 GB data for ₽300–₽600 (≈ 120–240 THB). Alternatively, get an eSIM before your trip from Airalo, which lets you activate a data plan instantly without swapping physical SIMs. Prices start at ₽250 (≈ 100 THB) for 1 GB over 7 days.

Best Time to Visit

The best weather is from December to March, when it’s sunny and dry with temperatures around 28–32°C (82–90°F). This is peak season, so expect higher prices for flights and hotels. April to June is hot but less crowded, with occasional showers—still a good time for budget travelers. July to October is the rainy season, with heavy downpours and rough seas, but you’ll find great deals on accommodation. The Samui Regatta (May/June) and Full Moon Party (on nearby Koh Phangan) are popular events.

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens can enter Thailand visa-free for up to 60 days. You’ll need a passport valid for at least 6 months from your arrival date, a return ticket, and proof of accommodation. For the Maldives section of your trip (if combining with Samui), remember: Перед вылетом заполните декларацию IMUGA на imuga.immigration.gov.mv и получите QR-код — без него не посадят на рейс. Always check the latest visa rules on the Thai embassy website before traveling.

Currency & Money

The local currency is the Thai baht (THB), symbol ฿. Exchange rates as of 2026: 1 USD ≈ 35 THB, 1 EUR ≈ 38 THB. Where to exchange: Avoid airport kiosks with poor rates—use exchange booths in Chaweng or Lamai (e.g., SuperRich) or ATMs (withdraw ₽5 000–₽10 000, ≈ 200–400 THB at a time).

Can you pay by card? Yes, major hotels, restaurants, and shops accept credit cards, but street vendors and markets are cash-only. Bring at least ₽10 000–₽15 000 (≈ 400–600 THB) in cash for small purchases and tips. Note: Thai ATMs charge a ₽220 (≈ 8.8 THB) fee per withdrawal.

Time Zone

Koh Samui is in UTC+7 (Indochina Time). This is 4 hours behind Sydney (AEDT), 1 hour behind Hong Kong/Singapore, 5 hours ahead of Moscow, and 12 hours ahead of New York (EST). No daylight saving time.

Travel Insurance

Travel insurance is not mandatory for entering Thailand, but it’s highly recommended. A good policy covers medical emergencies, trip cancellation, lost luggage, and activities like snorkeling or motorbike rental. Costs vary: a week-long policy starts at ₽1 500–₽3 000 (≈ 60–120 THB) for basic coverage, and ₽4 000–₽7 000 (≈ 160–280 THB) for comprehensive plans with adventure sports. 10 Travel Tips

  1. Respect local customs: Dress modestly when visiting temples (cover shoulders and knees), and remove shoes before entering.
  2. Bargain politely: In markets, haggling is expected—start at 50% of the asking price and negotiate with a smile.
  3. Stay hydrated: The tropical heat is intense—carry a reusable water bottle and refill at hotels (tap water is not drinkable).
  4. Use sunscreen and insect repellent: The sun is strong, and mosquitoes can carry dengue—apply both regularly.
  5. Learn basic Thai: “Sawadee krap/ka” (hello), “khob khun krap/ka” (thank you), and “mai pen rai” (no worries) go a long way.
  6. Rent a motorbike carefully: Roads can be steep and poorly lit—wear a helmet, carry your license, and check for insurance coverage.
  7. Avoid the rainy season for beach activities: July–October brings rough seas and jellyfish—check conditions before swimming.
  8. Book tours in advance: Popular excursions like Ang Thong sell out—book via GetYourGuide to secure your spot.
  9. Pack light and breathable clothing: Cotton, linen, and quick-dry fabrics are best—don’t forget a light jacket for air-conditioned buses or temples.
  10. Stay connected: Get an eSIM from Airalo before you arrive to avoid roaming fees—it’s a lifesaver for maps and translation apps.
