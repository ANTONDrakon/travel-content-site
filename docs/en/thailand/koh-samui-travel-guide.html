Koh Samui is Thailand’s second-largest island, a tropical gem in the Gulf of Thailand that balances laid-back beach vibes with a surprising dose of luxury. Imagine palm-fringed shores, warm turquoise waters, and a jungle-clad interior dotted with waterfalls and coconut groves. The atmosphere here is effortlessly relaxed — you can spend your days swinging in a hammock, exploring hidden coves, or indulging in world-class spa treatments without ever feeling rushed.

Why is 2026 the year to visit? The island has matured beautifully: infrastructure is excellent, yet it retains a charming, unhurried character. Unlike the party-centric islands, Samui offers something for everyone — from families splashing in the shallow bays at Chaweng to couples sipping cocktails at sunset in Bophut. The local culture is vibrant too, with glittering temples like Wat Plai Laem and the iconic Big Buddha statue reminding you that you’re in Thailand, not just a resort bubble.

Whether you’re a first-time visitor to Southeast Asia or a seasoned traveler seeking a reliable slice of paradise, Koh Samui delivers. The food scene is incredible, the people are warm, and the natural beauty is genuinely breathtaking. It’s the kind of place where a week easily turns into two — and you’ll already be planning your return before you leave.

How to Get There

The main gateway is Samui International Airport (USM), a charming open-air airport with thatched-roof buildings and tropical gardens. Direct flights arrive from Bangkok (1h15m), Phuket, Singapore, Hong Kong, and Kuala Lumpur.

From Bangkok, a one-way ticket costs around ₽6 500–15 000 (≈ $70–160), while flights from Singapore average ₽10 000–20 000 (≈ 400–800 SGD). Prices vary hugely by season — book early for the best deals. You can easily compare and book flights on Aviasales to find the most affordable options for your dates.

Where to Stay

Koh Samui’s neighborhoods each have a distinct personality. Chaweng is the bustling heart — perfect for first-timers who want nightlife, shopping, and the widest beach. Lamai is slightly quieter but still lively, with great value accommodation. Bophut (Fisherman’s Village) offers a charming, boutique vibe with excellent restaurants. Mae Nam and Choeng Mon are serene, ideal for couples and families seeking peace.

Budget travelers can find clean guesthouses from ₽1 800/night (≈ $20), mid-range resorts cost ₽5 500–14 000 (≈ $60–150), and luxury five-star properties range from ₽18 000–55 000 (≈ $200–600). For a wide selection of hotels and villas, check prices on Hotellook or Booking — they often have free cancellation options. In Asia, Agoda is particularly strong for local deals.

Top 15 Attractions

  1. Big Buddha Temple (Wat Phra Yai) — A 12-meter golden Buddha statue on a small island connected by a causeway. The views from the base are spectacular. Free entry, open daily 6:00–18:00.
  2. Wat Plai Laem — A colorful Chinese-Thai temple complex with a striking 18-arm Guanyin statue. Free entry, open daily 8:00–17:00.
  3. Na Muang Waterfalls — Two jungle waterfalls (Na Muang 1 and 2) with natural pools perfect for a refreshing dip. Free entry, open daily 8:00–17:00.
  4. Ang Thong National Marine Park — A stunning archipelago of 42 islands accessible by day trip. Kayak through emerald lagoons and hike to viewpoints. Park fee ₽1 200 (≈ $13), plus tour costs ₽2 800–5 500 (≈ $30–60).
  5. Hin Ta & Hin Yai Rocks — Famous rock formations shaped like male and female genitalia, with a legend behind them. Free entry, open 24/7.
  6. Fisherman’s Village Walking Street (Bophut) — Every Friday evening, the old wooden shophouses turn into a lively market with food, crafts, and music. Free entry, 17:00–22:00.
  7. Secret Buddha Garden — A hidden hillside garden filled with whimsical statues created by a local farmer. Entry ₽550 (≈ $6), open 8:00–17:00.
  8. Samui Elephant Sanctuary — An ethical sanctuary where rescued elephants roam freely. No riding, just observing and feeding. Entry ₽4 600 (≈ $50), includes guided tour, open 8:00–16:00.
  9. Chaweng Beach — The island’s most famous stretch of white sand, lined with bars and water sports. Free access.
  10. Lamai Beach — A beautiful, slightly quieter beach with good swimming and a relaxed atmosphere. Free access.
  11. Overlap Stone — A dramatic viewpoint where a large boulder perches precariously over the sea. Entry ₽275 (≈ $3), open 8:00–17:00.
  12. Grandfather’s and Grandmother’s Rocks (Hin Ta Hin Yai) — Already mentioned above, but worth a separate visit for the quirky photo op.
  13. Samui Cultural Center and Fine Art of Saeng Thong — A museum showcasing Thai culture, dance performances, and local art. Entry ₽1 100 (≈ $12), open 9:00–17:00.
  14. Khao Hua Jook Pagoda — A lesser-known hilltop pagoda with panoramic views of the island. Free entry.
  15. Silver Beach (Thong Tanote) — A tiny, secluded beach near Lamai with crystal-clear water and great snorkeling. Free entry.

Getting Around

Koh Samui has no metro or train. The main options are:

  • Songthaews (shared taxis): Red pickup trucks that run fixed routes. Fares ₽55–180 (≈ $0.60–2) per person depending on distance. Flag them down anywhere.
  • Private taxis: Expensive and negotiable — a 15-minute ride costs ₽550–1 100 (≈ $6–12). Always agree on the price before getting in.
  • Scooter rental: The most popular option. Rentals from ₽460–920/day (≈ $5–10). Requires an international driving permit (IDP) — police checkpoints are common.
  • Car rental: From ₽2 300–4 600/day (≈ $25–50). Book via local agencies or online.

Where to Eat

Thai food on Samui is a revelation. Street food stalls serve pad thai, som tam (papaya salad), and grilled seafood for ₽180–550 (≈ $2–6). For a sit-down meal at a local restaurant, expect ₽550–1 400 (≈ $6–15) per person.

Must-try dishes: tom yum goong (spicy shrimp soup), green curry, and mango sticky rice for dessert. Fisherman’s Village in Bophut has excellent waterfront dining — try Ocean 11 or The Hut. For upscale dining, Dining on the Rocks at SIX SENSES offers a tasting menu for ₽9 200+ (≈ $100+).

Tours & Excursions

The best way to explore Samui is through organized tours. Popular options include:

  • Ang Thong Marine Park day trip: ₽2 800–5 500 (≈ $30–60) — includes snorkeling, kayaking, lunch, and transfers.
  • Samui Island Tour: ₽2 300–3 700 (≈ $25–40) — covers Big Buddha, Wat Plai Laem, waterfalls, and viewpoints.
  • Snorkeling trip to Koh Tao & Koh Nang Yuan: ₽4 600–7 400 (≈ $50–80) — full-day speedboat trip with lunch.
  • Cooking class: ₽2 300–3 700 (≈ $25–40) — learn to make Thai classics.
  • Sunset dinner cruise: ₽4 600–9 200 (≈ $50–100) — romantic evening on the water.

For a wide selection of tours with free cancellation, check options on GetYourGuide — they have everything from elephant sanctuaries to private boat charters. You can browse and book excursions online.

Airport Transfer

Samui Airport is just 2 km from Chaweng and 6 km from Bophut. Options:

  • Taxi: Fixed prices from the airport counter — ₽1 100–2 300 (≈ $12–25) to most beaches.
  • Shared minivan: ₽550–920 (≈ $6–10) per person — slower but cheaper.
  • Public songthaew: Rare from the airport — better to walk 10 minutes to the main road and flag one.
  • Private transfer: Pre-booked cars start at ₽1 100 (≈ $12) for a standard sedan.

For a stress-free arrival, pre-book a private transfer on Kiwitaxi — they meet you at arrivals with a name sign, and prices are fixed. It’s especially worth it for families or late-night flights.

Connectivity

Wi-Fi is widely available in hotels, cafes, and restaurants, but speeds can vary. For reliable internet on the go, get a local SIM at the airport or 7-Eleven: AIS and TrueMove offer tourist SIMs with 10–30 GB data for ₽920–1 800 (≈ $10–20). An even easier option is an eSIM — you can buy and activate it before you travel. I recommend Airalo for affordable eSIM plans starting from ₽825 (≈ $9) for 5 GB valid 30 days. No need to swap physical SIMs.

Best Time to Visit

Koh Samui has a tropical monsoon climate with three distinct seasons:

  • Dry season (December–February): Best weather — sunny, low humidity, temperatures 25–30°C. Peak tourist season, prices are highest.
  • Hot season (March–August): Still mostly dry, with occasional rain. Temperatures 28–35°C. Lower prices, fewer crowds.
  • Rainy season (September–November): Heaviest rainfall, especially October. Many hotels offer discounts of 30–50%. Some tours may be cancelled.

For the best balance of good weather and reasonable prices, visit in late January–March or June–August.

Visa & Documents

Citizens of Russia, Ukraine, and most European countries enjoy visa-free entry to Thailand for up to 60 days (extendable for another 30 days at immigration). You need a passport valid for at least 6 months from the date of entry, a return ticket, and proof of accommodation (hotel booking). Immigration may ask for proof of funds (at least ₽92 000/≈ $1000 per person).

Currency & Money

Local currency: Thai baht (THB), symbol ฿. Exchange rates as of 2026: 1 USD ≈ 35 THB, 1 RUB ≈ 0.38 THB. Exchange money at authorized booths (rates are better than at hotels) or withdraw from ATMs (fee ₽920/≈ $10 per transaction). Credit cards are accepted at hotels, larger restaurants, and tour agencies, but many street vendors and local shops are cash-only. Bring at least ₽18 000–28 000 (≈ $200–300) in cash for a week, plus a card for backup. Avoid exchanging at the airport — rates are poor.

Time Zone

Koh Samui is in Indochina Time (ICT, UTC+7). Time difference: Moscow is 4 hours behind (when it’s 12:00 in Samui, it’s 08:00 in Moscow), London is 6 hours behind, New York is 12 hours behind. No daylight saving time.

Travel Insurance

Travel insurance is not mandatory for entry to Thailand, but it is highly recommended. Medical costs in private hospitals (like Bangkok Hospital Samui) can be steep — a simple consultation costs ₽4 600–7 400 (≈ $50–80).

A good policy covers medical expenses, trip cancellation, lost luggage, and emergency evacuation. Expect to pay ₽1 800–4 600 (≈ $20–50) for a week of comprehensive coverage. You can get reliable travel insurance quickly via GetYourGuide or specialized providers — just enter your dates and destination.

  2. Bargain respectfully: Negotiate at markets and with taxi drivers, but keep it friendly — a smile goes a long way.
