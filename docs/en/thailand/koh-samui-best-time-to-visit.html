Best Time to Visit Koh Samui 2026: Seasons & Weather

Planning a trip to Koh Samui in 2026? You're in for a treat. This Thai island in the Gulf of Thailand is famous for its palm-fringed beaches, coconut groves, and vibrant nightlife. But timing your visit right can make all the difference between a dreamy escape and a drizzly disappointment. Let me walk you through the seasons, month by month, so you can pick the perfect window for your adventure.

Koh Samui has a tropical monsoon climate, but unlike much of Thailand, its rainy season peaks later—from October to early December—while the rest of the country gets drenched from June to October. That means you can often find sunny skies here when Phuket or Krabi are under clouds. Temperatures stay warm year-round, hovering between 26°C and 34°C (79°F–93°F). The water is bathwater-warm, rarely dipping below 27°C (81°F).

High Season

When: Mid-December to February, and also July to August (European summer holidays).

Why it's popular: The weather is at its best—blue skies, low humidity, and calm seas. December and January bring festive vibes with Christmas and New Year celebrations on the beach. February is especially lovely, with minimal rain and comfortable temperatures around 30°C (86°F).

Prices: Expect a significant jump. Hotel rates can double or triple compared to low season. A standard beachfront bungalow that costs ₽3 500 (≈ $38) in May might go for ₽8 000 (≈ $87) in January. Flights from Europe or Russia also spike—think ₽45 000 (≈ $490) round trip from Moscow versus ₽25 000 (≈ $270) in low season.

During high season, it's worth booking hotels early via Hotellook or Booking.com to secure your ideal spot. Properties like The Library or Banyan Tree Samui fill up months in advance. For a mid-range option, check out Agoda for deals on resorts in Chaweng or Lamai.

Low Season

When: May to October, with the wettest months being October and November.

Pros: Huge discounts—hotels can be 40–60% cheaper. Fewer tourists mean quieter beaches and more personal service. You'll also find bargains on tours and spa treatments.

Cons: Rain is frequent, especially from September onward. Some beachfront restaurants and water sports operators shut down. The sea can be rough, making swimming less pleasant.

Weather: Expect afternoon downpours that last an hour or two, then clear up. Temperatures remain warm (27–32°C / 81–90°F), but humidity is high. Sunny days are fewer—only 12–15 per month from June to August.

Prices: A double room at a 4-star hotel might cost ₽2 500 (≈ $27) per night. Flights drop too—₽20 000 (≈ $217) from Moscow with a layover.

If you're on a budget and don't mind some rain, low season is a steal. Just pack a light rain jacket and plan indoor activities for afternoons.

Best Time for Beach Vacation

For swimming and sunbathing, aim for January to April. The sea is calm, warm (27–29°C / 81–84°F), and crystal clear. February and March are the absolute sweet spot—almost no rain, low humidity, and water temperatures perfect for floating all day.

May through August can still be good for swimming, but expect occasional waves and more cloudy days. October and November are risky—heavy rain and strong currents often lead to red flags on beaches. If you're set on a beach holiday, avoid those months unless you're okay with indoor backup plans.

Best Time for Sightseeing

December to February offers the most comfortable conditions for exploring. Temperatures hover around 27–30°C (81–86°F), and humidity is low. You'll enjoy hiking to Na Muang Waterfalls, visiting the Big Buddha (Wat Phra Yai), or touring the Secret Buddha Garden without breaking a sweat.

March and April are hotter (up to 34°C / 93°F), so plan sightseeing for early morning or late afternoon. Excursions can be booked via GetYourGuide—they offer guided tours to Ang Thong Marine Park, snorkeling trips to Koh Tao, and cultural walks in Nathon Town. Prices are reasonable year-round, but booking ahead in high season ensures availability.

For a unique experience, hire a private driver through Kiwitaxi for a half-day tour of the island's temples and viewpoints—it's about ₽3 500 (≈ $38) for three hours, split among friends.

Rainy Season

When: October to early December. This is the island's main monsoon period, driven by the northeast winds.

Intensity: October and November are the wettest, with 240–260 mm of rain per month. Expect daily downpours, often heavy and lasting several hours. Flooding can occur in low-lying areas like Chaweng or Lamai. December improves, with rain tapering off to 100 mm and more sunny intervals.

Is it worth visiting? If you're flexible and don't mind some wet days, yes—especially for the low prices and empty beaches. But if your heart is set on sunbathing and snorkeling, skip these months. The sea is often too rough for boat trips to Ang Thong or Koh Tao. Many tour operators suspend activities, so check availability before booking.

Pro tip: If you visit in rainy season, stay at a resort with good amenities—pools, spas, and restaurants—so you have options when the skies open. Bring a Kindle or a deck of cards for lazy afternoons.

Events & Festivals

Koh Samui's calendar is packed with cultural and fun events. Here are the highlights by month:

  • January: New Year's Eve beach parties (especially at Chaweng and Lamai). Many resorts host countdown events with fireworks.
  • February: Chinese New Year (movable date) – lanterns, dragon dances, and special menus at Chinese restaurants.
  • April: Songkran (Thai New Year, April 13-15) – massive water fights on the streets. Be prepared to get soaked!
  • May: Royal Ploughing Ceremony (early May) – a Buddhist ritual marking the start of the rice season. Observed at temples.
  • June: Samui Regatta – a week-long yacht racing event with parties in Chaweng. Great for sailing enthusiasts.
  • July: Asahna Bucha Day (movable) – Buddhist holy day; temples hold candlelit processions.
  • August: Mother's Day (August 12) – celebrations honoring the Queen; temple fairs and cultural shows.
  • September: Vegetarian Festival (movable, usually September/October) – nine days of vegan food, parades, and fire-walking at Chinese shrines.
  • October: Ok Phansa (end of Buddhist Lent) – boat races and merit-making at temples.
  • November: Loy Krathong (full moon night) – floating decorated baskets on rivers and beaches; magical atmosphere.
  • December: Christmas and New Year's Eve – beach parties, fireworks, and festive buffets at resorts.

For the best festival experience, visit during Songkran (April) or Loy Krathong (November)—both offer unique cultural immersion. Note that Loy Krathong falls in the rainy season, so bring an umbrella, but the celebrations are still enchanting.

Conclusion

So, when should you go to Koh Samui in 2026? Here's my take:

  • Best overall: January to March – perfect weather, calm seas, and plenty of sun. Ideal for beach lovers and sightseers alike.
  • Best for budget travelers: May to August – lower prices, fewer crowds, and still decent weather (just expect some rain).
  • Best for festivals: April (Songkran) or November (Loy Krathong) – unique cultural experiences, though November is wet.
  • Avoid if possible: October to early December – heavy rain and rough seas limit outdoor activities.
