Planning a trip to Koh Samui in 2026? You’re in for a treat. This Thai island paradise offers everything from quiet coconut-palm-fringed beaches to lively nightlife spots, and the hotel scene is just as diverse.

Whether you’re a backpacker on a shoestring, a couple seeking romance, or a family wanting five-star pampering, Koh Samui has a place that feels like it was made for you. I’ve spent countless hours exploring the island’s nooks and crannies, and I’m excited to share my top 10 picks for 2026—covering budget-friendly stays, mid-range gems, and luxury escapes.

Koh Samui’s hotel infrastructure is well-developed, with most accommodations concentrated along the eastern and northern coasts. The main neighborhoods—Chaweng, Lamai, Bophut, and Maenam—each have their own vibe. Chaweng is the bustling heart with endless dining and party options, Lamai offers a slightly more laid-back but still vibrant atmosphere, Bophut (Fisherman’s Village) charms with boutique shops and seaside restaurants, and Maenam is perfect for those seeking tranquility.

Prices vary widely: you can find a basic bungalow for as little as ₽2 800 (≈ $30) per night, while a luxury villa might set you back ₽18 500 (≈ $200) or more. To get the best deals, you can check Hotellook to compare prices across platforms like Booking and Agoda.

Budget Hotels (under $50/night)

If you’re watching your wallet but still want comfort and character, these three budget-friendly options deliver. They’re clean, well-located, and often include breakfast or kitchenettes to save even more.

1. The Cosy Villa (Lamai Beach)

Description: Tucked away in a quiet lane just a 5-minute walk from Lamai Beach, The Cosy Villa is a family-run gem with 12 rooms. Each room is simple but spotless, with air conditioning, a mini-fridge, and a private balcony. The garden is lush, and the owners are incredibly welcoming—they’ll even help you rent a scooter or book tours. It’s not fancy, but it feels like home.

Price: From ₽2 800 (≈ $30) per night.

Pros: Very affordable, friendly staff, walking distance to restaurants and beach.
Cons: No pool, basic amenities, can be a bit noisy from nearby bars on weekends.

2. Baan Samui Resort (Chaweng)

Description: Don’t let the “resort” name fool you—this is a budget-friendly spot right in the heart of Chaweng. The rooms are compact but functional, with tiled floors and modern bathrooms. There’s a small pool, and the beach is just across the street. It’s ideal for travelers who want to be in the middle of the action without breaking the bank.

Price: From ₽3 200 (≈ $35) per night.

Pros: Great location on Chaweng Beach Road, pool access, 24-hour front desk.
Cons: Rooms are small, street noise can be bothersome, breakfast not included.

3. Samui Homestay (Maenam)

Description: For a truly local experience, this homestay in Maenam is unbeatable. You’ll stay in a traditional Thai wooden house with a shared kitchen and garden. The host, Auntie Noi, cooks homemade breakfasts (included) and gives insider tips on hidden beaches and markets. It’s a 15-minute walk to Maenam Beach, but the peaceful rural setting is worth it.

Price: From ₽2 300 (≈ $25) per night.

Pros: Authentic Thai hospitality, free breakfast, very cheap.
Cons: No air conditioning (only fans), far from nightlife, shared bathroom in some rooms.

Mid-Range Hotels ($50-150/night)

This sweet spot offers excellent value—think comfortable rooms, good pools, and often beachfront locations. Here are four mid-range hotels that consistently impress.

4. The Library (Chaweng)

Description: A design-forward hotel with a literary theme, The Library is a standout on Chaweng Beach. The rooms are minimalist and white, with floor-to-ceiling windows and outdoor rain showers. The famous red-tiled pool is Instagram gold. It’s just steps from the sand and surrounded by Chaweng’s best cafes and bars. Perfect for couples and solo travelers who appreciate aesthetics.

Price: From ₽9 200 (≈ $100) per night.

Highlights: Unique design, red pool, beachfront location, excellent breakfast buffet.

5. Anantara Bophut Resort & Spa (Bophut)

Description: Set on the peaceful northern coast, this resort blends Thai elegance with modern comfort. Rooms are spacious with four-poster beds and private balconies overlooking tropical gardens. The infinity pool faces the sea, and the spa offers traditional Thai massages. It’s a 10-minute walk to Fisherman’s Village, so you get both serenity and convenience.

Price: From ₽11 100 (≈ $120) per night.

Highlights: Infinity pool, on-site spa, free shuttle to Fisherman’s Village, family-friendly.

6. Baan Hin Sai Resort (Lamai)

Description: Perched on a hillside between Chaweng and Lamai, this resort offers stunning ocean views and a more secluded feel. The rooms are in traditional Thai-style bungalows with wooden furnishings and outdoor seating. There’s a large pool, a private beach cove, and a restaurant serving fresh seafood. It’s a bit of a walk to main roads, but the peace is priceless.

Price: From ₽6 500 (≈ $70) per night.

Highlights: Panoramic sea views, private beach, quiet atmosphere, good for couples.

7. Samui Verticolor (Maenam)

Description: A colorful, boutique hotel that’s big on personality. Each room is painted in bright hues and decorated with local art. The rooftop bar and pool offer sunset views over the Gulf of Thailand. It’s a 5-minute walk to the beach and close to local markets. Great for young travelers and digital nomads who want a social vibe without the party noise.

Price: From ₽5 500 (≈ $60) per night.

Highlights: Rooftop pool and bar, artistic decor, free Wi-Fi, bike rental available.

Luxury Hotels ($150+/night)

For those who want the best of the best—private pools, butler service, and world-class dining—these three luxury hotels deliver unforgettable experiences.

8. Four Seasons Resort Koh Samui (Maenam)

Description: Perched on a lush hillside, this resort is the epitome of tropical luxury. All villas have private infinity pools and panoramic ocean views. The service is impeccable—staff remember your name and preferences. There’s a private beach, three restaurants, a spa, and a kids’ club. It’s ideal for honeymooners and families who want total indulgence.

Price: From ₽46 000 (≈ $500) per night.

Features: Private infinity pools, butler service, Michelin-star dining (Koh Thai Kitchen), free yoga classes.

How to Choose the Right Area

Your choice of neighborhood can make or break your trip. Here’s a quick guide based on your travel style:

  • For beach lovers: Stay in Maenam or Bophut. Maenam has long, quiet beaches perfect for sunbathing and swimming. Bophut’s Fisherman’s Village area offers a charming beachfront with calm waters and sunset views. Both are less crowded than Chaweng.
  • For sightseeing and culture: Lamai is your best bet. It’s close to landmarks like the Big Buddha Temple (Wat Phra Yai), Hin Ta and Hin Yai rocks, and the Secret Buddha Garden. You’ll also find local markets and cooking classes within walking distance.
  • For nightlife and dining: Chaweng is the party hub. The main strip is packed with clubs, bars, and international restaurants. If you want a slightly more refined night out, Bophut’s Fisherman’s Village has live music and beachfront dining.

Airport Transfers

Koh Samui Airport (USM) is small but efficient. Most hotels offer airport transfers for a fee, but you can also book a private car or taxi at the airport. A convenient transfer can be pre-booked through Kiwitaxi for around ₽1 800 (≈ $20) to Chaweng, or ₽2 300 (≈ $25) to Maenam.

Taxis from the airport are slightly more expensive—expect ₽2 800 (≈ $30) to Chaweng. For budget travelers, minivans are available for about ₽900 (≈ $10) per person, but they wait until full. If you’re staying at a luxury resort, many include complimentary transfers in their packages—check when booking.

Booking Tips

To get the best value for your Koh Samui hotel, keep these tips in mind:

  • Book early for peak season: December to March is high season (dry and sunny). Prices spike, so book 3-6 months ahead. Use Hotellook to compare rates across Booking, Agoda, and others.
  • Consider shoulder months: April, May, and November offer good weather with lower prices. You can often snag mid-range rooms for ₽4 600 (≈ $50) instead of ₽7 400 (≈ $80).
  • Check for hidden fees: Some budget hotels add charges for air conditioning, towels, or late check-out. Read the fine print on Booking or Agoda.
  • Use cash for local bookings: If you’re staying more than a week, negotiate directly with smaller hotels for discounts (10-20% off).
  • Don’t forget connectivity: Stay online with an eSIM from Airalo—plans start at ₽460 (≈ $5) for 1 GB.
