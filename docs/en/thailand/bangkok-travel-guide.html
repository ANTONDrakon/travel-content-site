Bangkok is a city that never sleeps, a pulsating metropolis where golden temples tower over neon-lit streets and the scent of sizzling street food mingles with the fragrance of incense. The Chao Phraya River snakes through the heart of the city, carrying long-tail boats past glittering palaces and humble stilt houses.

This is a place of beautiful chaos—where a tuk-tuk ride can feel like a rollercoaster and a quiet alley might hide a world-class café. The energy is infectious, and the warmth of the Thai people makes every visitor feel welcome.

Why is 2026 the year to visit? Because Bangkok is evolving faster than ever. New metro lines have made getting around easier, while the city's creative scene—from rooftop bars to independent galleries—is booming.

You'll find incredible value here: a luxurious hotel room might cost you what a hostel would in Europe, and a bowl of the best pad Thai you've ever tasted can be yours for less than a dollar. Whether you're a history buff, a foodie, or a shopaholic, Bangkok delivers experiences that stay with you long after you've left.

This guide is your practical companion. I've packed it with real prices, honest tips, and the kind of details that turn a good trip into an unforgettable one. Let's dive in.

How to Get There

Bangkok is served by two major airports: Suvarnabhumi (BKK), the main international hub, and Don Mueang (DMK), which handles many budget airlines and domestic routes. From Moscow, a direct flight to BKK takes about 9 hours, with average round-trip prices ranging from ₽45 000 to ₽75 000 (≈ $490 to $820) depending on the season. From St.

Petersburg, expect similar fares, though you might need a connection. European cities like London or Paris offer flights from ₽30 000 (≈ $377 (€330)) one-way. Asian hubs like Singapore or Hong Kong have frequent connections from ₽8 000 (≈ $90). Always check multiple dates and airlines — you can compare options and find the best deals on Aviasales

Where to Stay

Bangkok's neighborhoods each have a distinct personality. For first-timers, Sukhumvit is the most convenient: packed with hotels, restaurants, and BTS Skytrain stations. A decent mid-range room here costs ₽3 500–₽6 000 per night (≈ $38–$65). Khao San Road is the backpacker hub, with dorm beds from ₽500 (≈ $5.50) and private rooms from ₽1 500 (≈ $16).

For luxury, Riverside offers stunning views and five-star properties like the Mandarin Oriental, with rooms from ₽15 000 (≈ $165). If you want local life, Bang Rak (the old business district) has charming boutique hotels. Use Hotellook or Booking.com

Top 15 Attractions

  • Grand Palace – The former royal residence, a dazzling complex of temples and halls. Don't miss the Emerald Buddha. Entry: ₽1 800 (≈ $20). Open daily 8:30 AM–3:30 PM.
  • Wat Pho – Home to the 46-meter-long Reclining Buddha. Also has a traditional Thai massage school. Entry: ₽900 (≈ $10). Open 8 AM–6:30 PM.
  • Wat Arun (Temple of Dawn) – A stunning riverside temple with a central prang (spire) you can climb for views. Entry: ₽450 (≈ $5). Open 8 AM–6 PM.
  • Chatuchak Weekend Market – Over 15,000 stalls selling everything from vintage clothes to live plants. Best on Saturday or Sunday. Free entry. Open 9 AM–6 PM.
  • Jim Thompson House – A beautiful museum of traditional Thai silk and the home of an American architect. Entry: ₽900 (≈ $10). Open 9 AM–6 PM.
  • Khao San Road – The backpacker epicenter, buzzing with bars, street food, and cheap souvenirs. Free to walk. Active until late.
  • Wat Traimit (Golden Buddha) – Houses a 5.5-ton solid gold Buddha statue. Entry: ₽450 (≈ $5). Open 8 AM–5 PM.
  • Damnoen Saduak Floating Market – A classic tourist experience: boats piled with fruit and souvenirs. Entry free, boat rides from ₽900 (≈ $10). Open 6 AM–noon.
  • Lumphini Park – Bangkok's green lung, perfect for jogging or watching monitor lizards. Free entry. Open 4:30 AM–9 PM.
  • Asiatique The Riverfront – A night market and entertainment complex by the river. Free entry. Open 4 PM–midnight.
  • Erawan Shrine – A Hindu shrine in the heart of the city, famous for its traditional dance performances. Free entry. Open 6 AM–11 PM.
  • Bangkok Art and Culture Centre (BACC) – Contemporary art exhibitions and a cool café. Free entry. Open 10 AM–9 PM (closed Mondays).
  • Wat Saket (Golden Mount) – A hilltop temple with panoramic city views. Entry: ₽450 (≈ $5). Open 8 AM–7 PM.
  • Yaowarat (Chinatown) – A sensory overload of gold shops, Chinese temples, and incredible street food. Free to walk. Best visited in the evening.
  • Skywalk at King Power Mahanakhon – Thailand's tallest observation deck with a glass-floor skywalk. Entry: ₽2 200 (≈ $24). Open 10 AM–midnight.

Getting Around

Bangkok's traffic is legendary, so avoid taxis during rush hour (7–9 AM, 5–7 PM). The BTS Skytrain and MRT Metro are fast and air-conditioned. A single ride costs ₽45–₽130 (≈ $0.50–$1.40), depending on distance. You can buy a Rabbit Card (BTS) or a stored-value card (MRT) for convenience. Taxis start at ₽90 (≈ $1) for the first kilometer — always insist on the meter.

Tuk-tuks are fun for short trips but negotiate the price beforehand (usually ₽100–₽200 (≈ $1.10–$2.20)). Buses are dirt cheap (₽15–₽30 (≈ $0.15–$0.30)) but confusing for first-timers. Grab (Southeast Asia's Uber) is reliable and shows fixed prices. You can also rent a scooter (from ₽700/day (≈ $8)), but driving in Bangkok is not for the faint of heart.

Where to Eat

Bangkok is a food lover's paradise. Street food stalls serve dishes like pad Thai (₽100–₽150 (≈ $1.10–$1.65)), tom yum goong (spicy shrimp soup, ₽200 (≈ $2.20)), and mango sticky rice (₽100 (≈ $1.10)). For a sit-down meal, head to Thip Samai for legendary pad Thai (around ₽250 (≈ $2.75) per plate).

Raan Jay Fai is a Michelin-starred street food spot, famous for its crab omelet — expect queues and prices around ₽800 (≈ $9) per dish. For upscale dining, Bo.lan offers a modern take on Thai cuisine (set menus from ₽3 500 (≈ $38)). Chinatown (Yaowarat) is a must for seafood and roasted duck. A typical budget for food: ₽500–₽1 000/day (≈ $5.50–$11) for street food, or ₽2 000–₽4 000 (≈ $22–$44) for mid-range restaurants.

Tours & Excursions

To go beyond the basics, consider a guided tour. A half-day tour of the Grand Palace and Wat Pho costs around ₽2 500 (≈ $27). A full-day trip to the ancient capital of Ayutthaya (UNESCO site) runs ₽4 500–₽6 000 (≈ $49–$65), including transport and guide. For a unique experience, try a Bangkok food tour through Chinatown (₽3 000 (≈ $33)) or a long-tail boat tour of the canals (₽1 500 (≈ $16) per person). Airport Transfer

From Suvarnabhumi (BKK), the fastest option is the Airport Rail Link to Phaya Thai station (connecting to BTS). A trip costs ₽130–₽180 (≈ $1.40–$2) and takes 30 minutes. A taxi to the city center costs ₽500–₽800 (≈ $5.50–$8.70) including tolls and airport surcharge — make sure the driver uses the meter.

From Don Mueang (DMK), taxis are similar in price, but there's no direct rail link; you can take a shuttle bus to Mo Chit BTS station (₽100 (≈ $1.10)). For a stress-free door-to-door service, book a private transfer in advance via Kiwitaxi

Connectivity

Free Wi-Fi is available in most hotels, cafes, and shopping malls, but speeds can vary. For reliable internet on the go, buy a local SIM card at the airport (AIS, TrueMove, or DTAC). A 7-day tourist SIM with unlimited data costs ₽400–₽600 (≈ $4.40–$6.60). An even more convenient option is an eSIM — you can activate it before you leave home and avoid swapping physical cards. Best Time to Visit

Bangkok has a tropical climate with three seasons. Cool season (November–February) is the most pleasant, with temperatures around 25–30°C (77–86°F) and low humidity. This is peak tourist season, so hotel prices can be 30–50% higher. Hot season (March–May) sees temperatures soaring to 35–40°C (95–104°F) — it's still manageable if you stay hydrated and plan indoor activities for midday.

Rainy season (June–October) brings daily downpours, often short but heavy, and lower prices. I recommend visiting in November or February for the best balance of weather and crowds. The annual Songkran Festival (Thai New Year, April 13–15) is a massive water fight — fun but chaotic.

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens can enter Thailand visa-free for up to 60 days for tourism purposes. You need a passport valid for at least six months from your date of entry. On arrival, you'll receive a stamp — don't lose it, as overstaying incurs a fine of ₽1 800 (≈ $20) per day. For stays longer than 60 days, you can apply for a 30-day extension at any immigration office (cost ₽3 600 (≈ $40)).

Always carry a printed copy of your hotel reservation and return flight ticket, as immigration may ask to see them. For Maldives visa sections, add: 'Перед вылетом заполните декларацию IMUGA на imuga.immigration.gov.mv и получите QR-код — без него не посадят на рейс.'

Currency & Money

Thailand's currency is the Thai baht (THB), symbol ฿. Banknotes come in 20 (≈ $0.55), 50 (≈ $1.40), 100 (≈ $2.75), 500 (≈ $13.70), and 1,000 (≈ $27.50) denominations. Coins are 1, 2, 5, and 10 baht. Cash is king in Bangkok — many street stalls, taxis, and small shops don't accept cards.

Exchange money at SuperRich kiosks (found at airports and in the city) for the best rates; avoid airport exchange counters which have poor rates. ATMs charge a fee of ₽650 (≈ $7) per withdrawal, so take out larger amounts to minimize fees. Credit cards are accepted in upscale hotels, restaurants, and malls. I recommend carrying ₽10 000–₽15 000 (≈ $110–$165) in cash for a week's spending, plus a card for emergencies.

Time Zone

Bangkok operates on Indochina Time (ICT), which is UTC+7. There is no daylight saving time. The time difference from Moscow is +4 hours (when Moscow is on standard time). From London, it's +7 hours; from New York, +12 hours; from Sydney, -3 hours (during Australian winter).
