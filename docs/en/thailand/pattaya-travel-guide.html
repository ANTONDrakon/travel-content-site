Welcome to Pattaya — a city that never quite sleeps, where the Gulf of Thailand meets a whirlwind of neon lights, golden temples, and sandy shores. Once known primarily as a party destination, Pattaya has reinvented itself into a multifaceted hub that welcomes families, couples, and solo travelers alike.

Stroll along the famous Walking Street at dusk, explore floating markets by day, or simply unwind on Jomtien Beach with a fresh coconut in hand. The atmosphere here is electric, chaotic, and surprisingly charming — a place where you can sip a cocktail by the sea one moment and haggle for silk scarves the next.

What makes this a great destination in 2026? For starters, it offers incredible value for money compared to other Thai hotspots like Phuket or Koh Samui. You’ll find world-class dining, theme parks, and cultural sites without the premium price tag. The city also serves as a gateway to nearby islands like Koh Larn, reachable by a short ferry ride.

Whether you’re seeking adrenaline-pumping water sports, serene Buddhist sanctuaries, or just a plate of pad Thai that costs less than your morning coffee, Pattaya delivers. It’s a destination that rewards curiosity — and your rubles go a long way here.

Travelers from Russia will feel especially welcome, as Pattaya has long been a favorite among Russian-speaking tourists. Many signs, menus, and even hotel staff cater to this audience, making it a comfortable first stop in Thailand. The vibe is relaxed yet energetic, with a tropical climate that beckons year-round. How to Get There

The nearest airport is U-Tapao Rayong-Pattaya International Airport (UTP), located about 45 minutes from the city center. Direct flights from major Russian cities like Moscow (SVO) and Saint Petersburg (LED) are available, with average prices ranging from ₽35 000 to ₽55 000 (≈ $380–$600) for a round-trip economy ticket during off-peak seasons.

In high season (December–February), expect prices to rise by 20–30%. Alternatively, you can fly into Bangkok’s Suvarnabhumi Airport (BKK) and take a bus or taxi to Pattaya — a journey of about two hours. Where to Stay

Pattaya offers neighborhoods for every vibe and budget. For beach lovers, Jomtien Beach is quieter and family-friendly, with mid-range hotels like D Varee Jomtien Beach starting at ₽3 500 (≈ 1 400 THB) per night. Central Pattaya is the heart of the action — close to Walking Street, shopping malls, and nightlife — with budget hostels from ₽1 000 (≈ 400 THB) and luxury options like Hilton Pattaya from ₽9 000 (≈ 3 600 THB).

For a more upscale, tranquil experience, Pratumnak Hill offers panoramic sea views and high-end resorts. Use Hotellook or Booking to compare prices and read guest reviews — you can often snag deals by booking a month in advance.

Top 15 Attractions

  1. Sanctuary of Truth — A stunning wooden temple by the sea, intricately carved with Hindu and Buddhist motifs. Tickets: ₽1 500 (≈ 500 THB). Open daily 8:00–18:00.
  2. Walking Street — The iconic nightlife strip with bars, clubs, and street performances. Free entry; drinks from ₽300 (≈ 100 THB). Open 18:00–late.
  3. Pattaya Beach — The main city beach, perfect for people-watching and water sports. Free access; jet ski rental ~₽1 200 (≈ 400 THB) per hour.
  4. Koh Larn Island — A paradise island with white sand and clear water. Ferry: ₽300 (≈ 100 THB) round trip. Open daily.
  5. Nong Nooch Tropical Garden — A sprawling botanical garden with cultural shows. Tickets: ₽1 000 (≈ 350 THB). Open 8:00–18:00.
  6. Art in Paradise — A 3D interactive museum with optical illusions. Tickets: ₽800 (≈ 280 THB). Open 9:00–21:00.
  7. Pattaya Floating Market — Traditional Thai market on water, with food and crafts. Entry: ₽600 (≈ 200 THB). Open 9:00–20:00.
  8. Big Buddha Hill (Wat Phra Yai) — A massive golden Buddha with panoramic city views. Free entry. Open dawn to dusk.
  9. Underwater World Pattaya — An aquarium with a walk-through tunnel. Tickets: ₽900 (≈ 300 THB). Open 9:00–18:00.
  10. Mini Siam — Miniature replicas of world landmarks. Tickets: ₽700 (≈ 250 THB). Open 8:00–22:00.
  11. Ripley’s Believe It or Not! — A quirky attraction with oddities and a 4D theater. Tickets: ₽1 200 (≈ 400 THB). Open 10:00–22:00.
  12. Pattaya Elephant Village — Ethical elephant encounters and shows. Tickets: ₽1 500 (≈ 500 THB). Open 8:00–17:00.
  13. Jomtien Beach — A quieter alternative to Pattaya Beach, ideal for families. Free access; parasailing ~₽1 500 (≈ 500 THB).
  14. Wat Yansangwararam — A peaceful temple complex with a giant Buddha statue. Free entry. Open 6:00–18:00.
  15. Pattaya Park Tower — A revolving observation deck with a bungee jump. Tickets: ₽600 (≈ 200 THB). Open 10:00–22:00.

Getting Around

Pattaya’s main public transport is the songthaew (shared pickup truck), which follows fixed routes. Fares are ₽30–60 (≈ 10–20 THB) per ride — just hop on and press the bell to stop. For flexibility, rent a scooter from ₽600 (≈ 200 THB) per day (you’ll need an international driver’s permit). Taxis and tuk-tuks are pricier, starting at ₽300 (≈ 100 THB) for short trips. The Pattaya Monorail is under development but not yet operational; for now, stick to songthaews or ride-hailing apps like Grab.

Where to Eat

Street food is king in Pattaya. Try pad Thai from a stall for ₽180 (≈ 60 THB) or som tam (papaya salad) for ₽150 (≈ 50 THB). For sit-down meals, head to Rimpa Lapin on Pratumnak Hill for seafood with a view (mains ~₽600–900 (≈ 200–300 THB)).

Mum Aroi serves authentic Thai dishes in a casual setting (mains ~₽450–750 (≈ 150–250 THB)). International cuisine abounds — from Russian restaurants like St. Petersburg to Italian at Pizza King. Budget for food: ₽1 500–2 500 (≈ 500–800 THB) per day for three meals.

Tours & Excursions

Popular excursions include a day trip to Koh Larn (snorkeling and lunch included, ~₽3 000 (≈ 1 000 THB)), a visit to the Elephant Sanctuary (~₽4 500 (≈ 1 500 THB)), or a guided tour of the Sanctuary of Truth (~₽2 500 (≈ 800 THB)). For a full-day experience, consider a Pattaya city tour covering temples, markets, and viewpoints. You can browse and book tours on GetYourGuide, which offers free cancellation on many options.

Airport Transfer

From U-Tapao Airport, the cheapest option is a public bus to Pattaya Bus Station (₽450 (≈ 150 THB)). A private taxi costs ₽1 800–2 500 (≈ 600–800 THB) and takes 45 minutes. From Bangkok’s Suvarnabhumi, you can take a direct bus (₽600 (≈ 200 THB)) or book a private transfer. For convenience and reliability, use Kiwitaxi, where you can pre-book a car with a meet-and-greet service.

Connectivity

Free Wi-Fi is available in most hotels and cafes, but for reliable connectivity on the go, get a local SIM card from AIS or TrueMove at the airport or 7-Eleven — plans start from ₽600 (≈ 200 THB) for 15 GB. An even easier option is an eSIM, which you can activate before you travel. Check Airalo for Thailand eSIM plans starting at ₽800 (≈ 260 THB) for 7 days of data.

The best time is November to February, with pleasant weather and clear skies. However, prices for flights and hotels are 30–50% higher. For budget travelers, the rainy season (June–October) offers discounts, though afternoon showers are common. March–May is scorching but less crowded.

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens can enter Thailand visa-free for up to 60 days (extendable by 30 days at an immigration office for ₽5 700 (≈ 1 900 THB)). You need a passport valid for at least 6 months from your entry date, a return ticket, and proof of accommodation. No visa application is needed for stays under 60 days. Перед вылетом заполните декларацию IMUGA на imuga.immigration.gov.mv и получите QR-код — без него не посадят на рейс.

Currency & Money

The local currency is the Thai baht (THB), symbol ฿. Exchange rubles at SuperRich or bank kiosks in Pattaya — rates are better than in Russia. Avoid exchanging at airports. Credit cards are accepted in hotels and larger restaurants, but street markets and small shops require cash. Bring around ₽15 000–20 000 (≈ 5 000–6 500 THB) per week for daily expenses. ATMs charge a fee of ₽660 (≈ 220 THB) per withdrawal, so take out larger amounts.

Time Zone

Pattaya is on Indochina Time (UTC+7). It is 4 hours ahead of Moscow (Moscow UTC+3) and 2 hours behind Bangkok (same zone). No daylight saving time.

Travel Insurance

Travel insurance is not mandatory for Russian citizens entering Thailand, but it is highly recommended. It covers medical emergencies (including COVID-19 treatment), trip cancellations, and lost baggage. A standard policy for a one-week trip costs around ₽1 500–3 000 (≈ $15–$30). 10 Travel Tips

  1. Respect the monarchy — Never speak ill of the king or royal family; it’s a legal offense.
  2. Dress modestly at temples — cover shoulders and knees; remove shoes before entering.
  3. Bargain politely at markets — start at 50% of the asking price.
  4. Drink bottled water — tap water is not safe.
  5. Use sunscreen (SPF 50+) — the sun is intense year-round.
  6. Learn basic Thai — “Sawasdee” (hello) and “Khob khun” (thank you) go a long way.
  7. Watch your belongings on crowded beaches and Walking Street.
  8. Try local fruits — mango, durian, and rambutan are cheap and delicious.
  9. Book tours in advance for better prices, especially during high season.
  10. Stay hydrated — carry a refillable water bottle and use 7-Eleven for ice.

Pattaya awaits with its unique blend of chaos and calm. Whether you’re here for the beaches, the temples, or the street food, this guide should help you navigate with confidence. Safe travels!
