Imagine stepping off a plane into a world where the air smells of salt, jasmine, and sizzling street food. Phuket, Thailand's largest island, is a place where turquoise waters meet dramatic limestone cliffs, and ancient Buddhist temples stand beside bustling night markets.

It's not just a destination; it's a feeling—a vibrant, chaotic, and utterly addictive blend of natural beauty, rich culture, and modern comfort. Whether you're a first-time visitor to Southeast Asia or a seasoned traveler, Phuket offers something for everyone: from serene beachfront retreats to adrenaline-pumping jungle adventures.

What makes this a great destination in 2026? The island is constantly evolving, with new eco-friendly resorts, improved infrastructure, and a deeper focus on sustainable tourism. You can still find the legendary nightlife of Patong, but you'll also discover quiet coves, world-class diving spots like the Similan Islands. and a thriving food scene that goes far beyond pad thai.

The warm hospitality of the Thai people, the affordable luxury, and the sheer diversity of experiences make it a place you'll want to return to again and again. Plus, with direct flights from many Russian cities, getting here has never been easier.

In this guide, I'll walk you through everything you need to know for a perfect trip—from booking flights and finding the right neighborhood to eating like a local and navigating the island. Let's dive in, shall we?

How to Get There

Phuket International Airport (HKT) is your gateway to the island. It's a modern hub with direct flights from major Russian cities like Moscow (SVO, DME, VKO), St. Petersburg (LED), and Novosibirsk (OVB), as well as from Asian hubs like Bangkok, Singapore, and Dubai. Average flight prices from Moscow in 2026 range from ₽35 000 to ₽55 000 (≈ $380–$600) for a round-trip economy ticket, depending on the season and airline.

From St. Petersburg, expect to pay around ₽40 000 to ₽65 000 (≈ $435–$705). Airlines like Aeroflot, S7, and Thai Airways offer direct routes, while budget carriers like AirAsia and Scoot provide cheaper options with layovers. To find the best deals, you can check prices on Aviasales—it's a great tool for comparing flights from hundreds of airlines.

Where to Stay

Phuket's neighborhoods each have their own personality. For first-timers, Patong is the heart of the action: think neon lights, beachside bars, and endless shopping. A mid-range hotel here costs ₽3 500–₽6 000 (≈ $38–$65) per night. If you prefer tranquility, head to Kata or Karon—these southern beaches offer calm waters and a relaxed vibe, with hotels from ₽2 500–₽5 000 (≈ $27–$54).

For luxury, Bang Tao and Kamala host five-star resorts with private pools and beach access, starting at ₽8 000 (≈ $87) per night. Budget travelers love Phuket Town, where boutique hostels and guesthouses cost ₽1 000–₽2 000 (≈ $11–$22) per night, and you're surrounded by Sino-Portuguese architecture and amazing street food. You can compare hotel prices on Hotellook or Booking.com—they often have exclusive deals.

Top 15 Attractions

  1. Big Buddha (Wat Phra Sing) — A 45-meter-tall marble statue atop Nakkerd Hill, offering panoramic views of the island. Free entry, open daily 6:00 AM–6:30 PM.
  2. Wat Chalong — The most important Buddhist temple in Phuket, with intricate architecture and a revered monk's statue. Free, open 7:00 AM–5:00 PM.
  3. Patong Beach — The iconic party beach, with soft sand, jet skis, and a lively promenade. Free, open 24/7.
  4. Phi Phi Islands — A stunning archipelago with emerald waters, limestone cliffs, and Maya Bay (made famous by "The Beach"). Day tours from ₽3 000 (≈ $33), typically 8:00 AM–5:00 PM.
  5. James Bond Island (Khao Phing Kan) — A dramatic limestone karst featured in "The Man with the Golden Gun." Tours from ₽2 500 (≈ $27), usually 8:00 AM–4:00 PM.
  6. Similan Islands — World-class snorkeling and diving with crystal-clear visibility. Day trips from ₽5 000 (≈ $54), open November to April.
  7. Old Phuket Town — A charming area with colorful Sino-Portuguese shophouses, cafes, and street art. Free to explore, shops open 10:00 AM–8:00 PM.
  8. Phang Nga Bay — A serene bay with mangrove forests, sea caves, and floating villages. Kayak tours from ₽2 000 (≈ $22), 8:00 AM–5:00 PM.
  9. Karon Viewpoint — A scenic overlook with a three-beach panorama, perfect for sunrise. Free, open 24/7.
  10. Bangla Road (Patong) — The epicenter of Phuket's nightlife, with clubs, go-go bars, and live music. Free entry, active from 6:00 PM to 2:00 AM.
  11. Elephant Sanctuary — Ethical sanctuaries where you can feed and bathe rescued elephants. Half-day visits from ₽4 000 (≈ $43), 8:00 AM–12:00 PM.
  12. Promthep Cape — The southernmost tip of Phuket, famous for sunset views. Free, best visited 5:00 PM–6:30 PM.
  13. Phuket FantaSea — A cultural theme park with a dinner show, carnival games, and Thai mythology. Tickets from ₽2 500 (≈ $27), open 5:30 PM–11:30 PM.
  14. Nai Harn Beach — A quieter, pristine beach with clear water and lush hills. Free, open 24/7.
  15. Gibbon Rehabilitation Centre — A sanctuary for rescued gibbons, offering educational tours. Donation-based (₽300–₽500 suggested), open 9:00 AM–4:00 PM.

Getting Around

Phuket's public transport is limited, but you have options. Songthaews (shared pickup trucks) are the cheapest way to travel between beaches—fares are ₽40–₽60 (≈ $0.43–$0.65) per ride. Local buses connect Phuket Town to Patong, Kata, and Karon for ₽50 (≈ $0.54). Taxis are expensive: short trips cost ₽300–₽500 (≈ $3.25–$5.40), while longer journeys from the airport to Patong are ₽800–₽1 200 (≈ $8.70–$13).

Renting a scooter is the most popular option—prices start at ₽300 (≈ $3.25) per day, but you'll need an international driving permit (IDP). For safety, consider using the Grab app (like Uber) for fixed fares. Alternatively, pre-book a private car via Kiwitaxi for airport transfers or day trips.

Where to Eat

Phuket's food scene is a feast for the senses. Street food is king: try pad thai (₽100–₽150), tom yum goong (₽150–₽250), and mango sticky rice (₽80–₽120) at night markets like Phuket Weekend Market or Chillva Market. For sit-down meals, Raya Restaurant in Phuket Town serves authentic Thai cuisine (₽300–₽600 per dish).

Kan Eang@Pier in Chalong offers fresh seafood with ocean views (₽500–₽1 000 per dish). Budget travelers can eat well for ₽300–₽500 (≈ $3.25–$5.40) per meal, while mid-range restaurants cost ₽800–₽1 500 (≈ $8.70–$16). Don't miss khao soi (curry noodle soup) and som tam (papaya salad). For a splurge, book a table at Suay Restaurant for creative Thai fusion (₽1 500–₽3 000 per person).

Tours & Excursions

Phuket is a hub for island-hopping and adventure. Popular excursions include a Phi Phi Islands day trip (₽3 000–₽5 000, includes lunch and snorkeling), a James Bond Island tour (₽2 500–₽4 000), and a Similan Islands liveaboard (₽8 000–₽15 000 for 2 days).

For culture lovers, a Phuket City and Temple Tour costs around ₽1 500 (≈ $16). Adventure seekers can try white-water rafting (₽3 000) or ATV jungle rides (₽2 500). You can browse and book these tours on GetYourGuide—they offer free cancellation and verified reviews.

Airport Transfer

Getting from Phuket Airport (HKT) to your hotel is straightforward. The cheapest option is the Airport Bus to Phuket Town (₽100–₽150, 45 minutes) or Patong (₽180–₽200, 1 hour). Taxis at the airport charge fixed rates: ₽800 to Patong, ₽1 000 to Kata, ₽1 200 to Bang Tao. For convenience, pre-book a private transfer via Kiwitaxi—prices start at ₽1 500 (≈ $16) for a car with a meet-and-greet service. This is especially useful if you arrive late or have lots of luggage.

Connectivity

Staying connected in Phuket is easy. Most hotels, cafes, and restaurants offer free Wi-Fi. For on-the-go access, buy a local SIM card at the airport or 7-Eleven: AIS, TrueMove, and dtac offer tourist SIMs with 15–30 days of data (₽300–₽800, ≈ $3.25–$8.70). Alternatively, get an eSIM before you travel—it's instant and saves you from swapping physical cards. Best Time to Visit

Phuket has a tropical monsoon climate. The high season (November–April) is the best time: dry, sunny, and calm seas, with temperatures around 28–32°C. Prices are highest then. The low season (May–October) brings rain and rough seas, but also lower prices and fewer crowds. June–September is the wettest, though you'll still get sunny breaks. For diving, November–April is ideal for the Andaman Sea. Budget travelers love the shoulder months (May and October) for good weather and discounts.

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens can enter Thailand visa-free for up to 60 days (extendable by 30 days at immigration). You need a passport valid for at least 6 months from your entry date and a return ticket.

For the Maldives visa section: 'Перед вылетом заполните декларацию IMUGA на imuga.immigration.gov.mv и получите QR-код — без него не посадят на рейс.' (Note: This applies if you're transiting or visiting the Maldives as part of a multi-stop trip.) No other visa is needed for Phuket for stays under 60 days.

Currency & Money

The local currency is the Thai Baht (THB), symbol ฿. ₽1 ≈ 0.40 THB (as of 2026). Exchange money at superrich kiosks (best rates) or banks in Phuket Town—avoid airport counters. ATMs are everywhere, but charge a fee of $7 (220 ฿) (₽550) per withdrawal. Credit cards are accepted at hotels, restaurants, and shops, but cash is king for street food, taxis, and markets. Bring ₽15 000–₽20 000 (≈ $163–$217) in cash for a week, plus a card for emergencies.

Time Zone

Phuket is in UTC+7 (Indochina Time). It's 4 hours ahead of Moscow (when Moscow is UTC+3), 3 hours ahead of St. Petersburg, and 1 hour behind Bangkok. No daylight saving time.
