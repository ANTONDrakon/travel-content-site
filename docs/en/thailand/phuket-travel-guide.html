Welcome to Phuket, Thailand's largest island and a true gem of the Andaman Sea. With its powdery white-sand beaches, lush tropical hills, and vibrant culture, Phuket offers something for every traveler—whether you're seeking a serene escape or an action-packed adventure.

The atmosphere here is a delightful blend of laid-back island charm and energetic tourism hubs, from the bustling streets of Patong to the quiet coves of the north. In 2026, Phuket continues to evolve with improved infrastructure and sustainable tourism initiatives, making it more accessible and enjoyable than ever.

What draws travelers here? Imagine waking up to the sound of waves, spending your days snorkeling in crystal-clear waters, and savoring world-class Thai cuisine as the sun sets over the horizon. Beyond the beaches, you'll discover ancient temples, lively night markets, and jungle waterfalls.

The island's warm hospitality and year-round sunshine make it a top destination for couples, families, and solo travelers alike. Whether you're a first-time visitor or a returning fan, Phuket always has new surprises in store—like the upcoming Phuket Light Festival in 2026, which promises dazzling displays across the island.

For those planning a trip, I've crafted this guide with all the essential details: from flights and accommodation to top attractions and insider tips. All prices mentioned are approximate estimates based on typical market rates—they're meant to give you a ballpark, not exact quotes from specific providers. Ready to explore? Let's dive in!

How to Get There

Phuket International Airport (HKT) is your gateway to the island, located about 30 kilometers north of Patong Beach. Direct flights connect Phuket to major cities across Asia, Europe, and the Middle East. From Moscow, a round-trip flight in economy class typically costs between ₽45 000 (≈ $490) and ₽75 000 (≈ $815), depending on the season.

From Bangkok, budget airlines like AirAsia and Thai Lion Air offer one-way tickets from ₽3 000 (≈ $36 (1200 ฿)) to ₽6 000 (≈ $71 (2400 ฿)). Where to Stay

Phuket's neighborhoods each have their own personality. For nightlife and shopping, Patong Beach is the epicenter—expect crowds and energy. Kata and Karon beaches offer a quieter vibe with family-friendly resorts. For luxury and seclusion, head to Bang Tao or Kamala, where five-star hotels line the coast. Budget travelers love the guesthouses in Phuket Town, which also boasts a charming old quarter with Sino-Portuguese architecture.

Accommodation prices vary widely. A budget guesthouse in Phuket Town costs from ₽1 200 (≈ $15 (500 ฿)) per night, while a mid-range hotel in Kata might run ₽4 500 (≈ $53 (1800 ฿)) to ₽9 000 (≈ $107 (3600 ฿)). Top 15 Attractions

  • 1. Patong Beach – The most famous beach, bustling with activities, restaurants, and nightlife. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  • 2. Big Buddha – A 45-meter-tall marble statue atop Nakkerd Hill, offering panoramic views. Free entry (donations welcome). Open daily 8:00 AM–7:30 PM.
  • 3. Wat Chalong – The largest and most revered Buddhist temple in Phuket. Free entry. Open 7:00 AM–5:00 PM.
  • 4. Phi Phi Islands – A stunning archipelago accessible by ferry or tour. Day trips cost ₽2 500–₽5 000 (≈ $30 (1000 ฿)–$59 (2000 ฿)). Tours typically run 8:00 AM–4:00 PM.
  • 5. James Bond Island – Famous from "The Man with the Golden Gun," with dramatic limestone karsts. Tours cost ₽2 000–₽4 000 (≈ $24 (800 ฿)–$48 (1600 ฿)). Duration: 6–8 hours.
  • 6. Phuket Old Town – A historic area with colorful shop-houses, cafes, and street art. Free to explore. Open 24/7, but shops open 10:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  • 7. Promthep Cape – The southernmost point, famous for sunset views. Free entry. Best visited 5:00 PM–6:30 PM.
  • 8. Bangla Road – Patong's nightlife hub, packed with bars and clubs. Free entry. Active from 7:00 PM–2:00 AM.
  • 9. Elephant Sanctuary – Ethical sanctuaries like Phuket Elephant Sanctuary offer half-day visits. Prices from ₽5 000 (≈ $59 (2000 ฿)). Open 8:00 AM–4:00 PM.
  • 10. Similan Islands – A world-class diving and snorkeling destination. Day trips cost ₽6 000–₽10 000 (≈ $71 (2400 ฿)–$119 (4000 ฿)). Season: November–April.
  • 11. Khao Sok National Park – A rainforest with Cheow Lan Lake, hiking, and wildlife. Entry fee ₽600 (≈ $7 (240 ฿)). Open 8:00 AM–5:00 PM.
  • 12. Kata Viewpoint – A scenic overlook of Kata and Karon beaches. Free entry. Open 24/7.
  • 13. Splash Jungle Water Park – A family-friendly water park with slides and pools. Tickets ₽2 000 (≈ $24 (800 ฿)). Open 10:00 AM–6:00 PM.
  • 14. Phuket FantaSea – A cultural theme park with shows, buffet dinner, and carnival games. Tickets ₽4 000 (≈ $48 (1600 ฿)). Show starts 7:30 PM.
  • 15. Coral Island – A quiet island with snorkeling and beach bars. Day trips from ₽1 500 (≈ $18 (600 ฿)). Tours run 9:00 AM–4:00 PM.

Getting Around

Phuket's public transport is limited, but options exist. Tuk-tuks (private, colorful vehicles) are common for short trips—expect to pay ₽200–₽500 (≈ $2 (80 ฿)–$6 (200 ฿)) for a 10-minute ride. Songthaews (shared pickup trucks) run fixed routes like Phuket Town to Patong for ₽60 (≈ $1 (24 ฿)) per person. Taxis are more expensive, starting at ₽500 (≈ $6 (200 ฿)) for short distances.

Renting a scooter is popular and costs ₽350–₽600 (≈ $4 (140 ฿)–$7 (240 ฿)) per day—be sure to wear a helmet and carry an international driver's permit. Car rentals start at ₽2 000 (≈ $24 (800 ฿)) per day. For airport transfers and longer journeys, consider pre-booking a reliable service like Kiwitaxi for fixed prices and comfort.

Where to Eat

Phuket's food scene is a feast for the senses. Street food stalls in Phuket Town offer classics like pad thai (₽100–₽200, ≈ $1 (40 ฿)–$2 (80 ฿)) and mango sticky rice (₽80–₽150, ≈ $1 (32 ฿)–$2 (60 ฿)). Local specialties include "mee hokkien" (stir-fried noodles) and "tom yum goong" (spicy shrimp soup).

Mid-range restaurants in Patong serve Thai and international dishes for ₽400–₽1 000 (≈ $5 (160 ฿)–$12 (400 ฿)) per meal. For fine dining, head to resorts like Trisara or Banyan Tree, where tasting menus cost ₽3 000–₽6 000 (≈ $36 (1200 ฿)–$71 (2400 ฿)). Don't miss the Sunday Walking Street Market in Phuket Town—a paradise for foodies with dishes from ₽50 (≈ $1 (20 ฿)) each.

Best Time to Visit

Phuket has a tropical monsoon climate. The best time is from November to April (dry season), with sunny days and calm seas—perfect for beach activities and island tours. Peak season (December–February) brings higher prices and crowds. The rainy season (May–October) sees frequent downpours but lower hotel rates (up to 50% off) and fewer tourists. Average temperatures range from 25°C to 33°C year-round. For a balance of good weather and affordability, consider visiting in November or March.

Visa & Documents

Russian citizens can enter Thailand visa-free for up to 60 days (as of 2025–2026). You need a passport valid for at least 6 months from your arrival date and proof of onward travel (return or onward ticket). No visa application is required for stays under 60 days. For longer stays, contact the Thai embassy in Moscow or Saint Petersburg. Note: Always check the latest entry requirements before traveling, as policies can change.

Currency & Money

The local currency is the Thai baht (THB), symbolized as ฿. Exchange rates fluctuate, but as of early 2025, ₽1 ≈ $0 (0.40 ฿) (or $0 (1 ฿) ≈ ₽2.5). You can exchange rubles, dollars, or euros at banks, exchange booths (look for "SuperRich" for best rates). and the airport—but avoid street changers.

Credit cards are accepted at hotels, large restaurants, and shops, but street food stalls and markets prefer cash. Bring enough cash for daily expenses: ₽3 000–₽5 000 (≈ $36 (1200 ฿)–$59 (2000 ฿)) per day for mid-range travel. ATMs charge a fee of 220 THB (≈ ₽550) per withdrawal, so withdraw larger amounts to minimize costs.

Time Zone

Phuket operates on Indochina Time (ICT), which is UTC+7. There is no daylight saving time. Time difference from Moscow: Moscow is UTC+3, so Phuket is 4 hours ahead (e.g., 12:00 PM in Moscow = 4:00 PM in Phuket). From Bangkok, it's the same time zone. From London (UTC+0), Phuket is 7 hours ahead; from New York (UTC-5), it's 12 hours ahead.

Travel Insurance

Travel insurance is not mandatory for entering Thailand, but it's highly recommended. It covers medical emergencies, trip cancellations, lost baggage, and more. A basic policy for a week in Phuket costs around ₽1 500–₽3 000 (≈ $16–$33). For comprehensive coverage (including COVID-19, adventure sports like scuba diving), expect ₽3 000–₽6 000 (≈ $33–$65). You can get insurance via get travel insurance providers like Tripinsurance or Sravni, where you can compare policies and buy online in minutes.
