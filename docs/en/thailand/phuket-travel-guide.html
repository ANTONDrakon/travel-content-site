Welcome to Phuket, Thailand's largest and most famous island, where emerald-green waters meet golden sands and lush jungle-clad hills. The atmosphere here is a vibrant blend of tropical paradise and bustling tourist hub — think longtail boats bobbing in turquoise bays, the scent of sizzling pad thai from street stalls, and the gentle hum of motorbikes weaving through palm-fringed roads.

Whether you're seeking serene beach days, adrenaline-pumping water sports, or cultural immersion in ornate temples, Phuket delivers an unforgettable escape.

What makes this a great destination in 2026? The island has evolved beautifully, balancing modern comforts with authentic Thai charm. You'll find world-class resorts alongside family-run guesthouses, chic beach clubs next to lively night markets, and untouched corners like the northern beaches that still feel like a secret.

The food scene is a revelation — from fiery southern curries to fresh seafood grilled right on the sand. Plus, the island's infrastructure keeps improving: new roads, better public transport, and more direct flights make it easier than ever to explore. Whether you're a solo backpacker, a couple on a romantic getaway, or a family with kids, Phuket has something special waiting for you.

This guide is your all-in-one companion for planning a trip to Phuket in 2026. I'll walk you through everything: how to get there, where to stay, top attractions, local eats, practical tips, and budgets. Let's dive in and make your Phuket adventure truly magical.

How to Get There

Phuket International Airport (HKT) is your gateway, located about 30 kilometers north of Patong Beach. It's a modern hub with direct flights from major cities across Asia, Europe, and the Middle East. From Moscow, you can find direct flights with Aeroflot or connecting options via Dubai, Doha, or Istanbul, with average prices ranging from ₽40 000 to ₽80 000 (≈ $430–$870) for a round trip in economy.

From Bangkok, budget airlines like AirAsia and Thai Lion Air offer flights from as low as ₽3 500 (≈ $38) one way. For the best deals, I recommend checking flight options on Aviasales, where you can compare prices across hundreds of airlines and set price alerts.

Where to Stay

Phuket's neighborhoods each have a distinct personality. For first-timers, Patong is the epicenter of nightlife and shopping, with hotels like the 4-star Holiday Inn Resort Patong (from ₽5 500/≈ $60 per night) or budget-friendly Lub d Phuket Patong (from ₽1 800/≈ $20). Karon and Kata beaches offer a quieter vibe with family-friendly resorts like the Kata Beach Resort (from ₽4 500/≈ $49).

Rawai and Nai Harn in the south are perfect for couples and long-stay travelers, with upscale options like The Nai Harn (from ₽12 000/≈ $130). For luxury seekers, Bang Tao boasts sprawling resorts like Angsana Laguna Phuket (from ₽15 000/≈ $163). Budget travelers love Phuket Town for its cultural charm and hostels from ₽800 (≈ $9) per night. You can compare prices and book your perfect stay on Hotellook or Booking.com.

Top 15 Attractions

  • 1. Big Buddha (Wat Phra Sing) — A 45-meter-tall marble statue atop Nakkerd Hill offering panoramic views of the island. Free entry, open daily 8:00 AM–7:30 PM.
  • 2. Wat Chalong — The most revered Buddhist temple in Phuket, famous for its intricate architecture and the Grand Pagoda housing a relic of Buddha. Free entry, open 7:00 AM–5:00 PM.
  • 3. Phi Phi Islands — A stunning archipelago with crystal-clear waters and limestone cliffs. Day tours from ₽2 500 (≈ $27) per person, including snorkeling gear and lunch. Boats depart at 8:00 AM, return by 5:00 PM.
  • 4. James Bond Island (Khao Phing Kan) — Made famous by the 1974 film "The Man with the Golden Gun." Tours from ₽2 000 (≈ $22) per person, typically 8:00 AM–4:00 PM.
  • 5. Similan Islands — A world-class diving and snorkeling destination with vibrant coral reefs. Day tours from ₽4 500 (≈ $49) per person, open November to April only.
  • 6. Old Phuket Town — A charming area with Sino-Portuguese architecture, boutique shops, and street art. Free to explore, best visited in the morning.
  • 7. Phang Nga Bay — A dramatic seascape of limestone karsts and mangrove forests. Kayak tours from ₽2 200 (≈ $24) per person, 8:00 AM–5:00 PM.
  • 8. Patong Beach — The most famous beach, perfect for sunbathing, jet skiing, and nightlife. Free entry, open 24/7.
  • 9. Promthep Cape — The southernmost point of Phuket, known for spectacular sunsets. Free entry, best visited 5:00 PM–7:00 PM.
  • 10. Tiger Kingdom Phuket — An interactive zoo where you can get close to tigers. Entry from ₽1 200 (≈ $13) per person, open 9:00 AM–5:00 PM.
  • 11. Phuket Elephant Sanctuary — An ethical sanctuary where you can observe rescued elephants. Tours from ₽4 500 (≈ $49) per person, 6:30 AM–12:30 PM.
  • 12. Kata Viewpoint — A three-point viewpoint offering stunning vistas of Kata Noi, Kata, and Karon beaches. Free entry, open 24/7.
  • 13. Bangla Road — The heart of Patong's nightlife, with bars, clubs, and street performances. Free to walk, open from 6:00 PM to 4:00 AM.
  • 14. Coral Island — A peaceful escape with white sands and clear waters for snorkeling. Day tours from ₽1 800 (≈ $20) per person, 9:00 AM–4:00 PM.
  • 15. Splash Jungle Water Park — A fun-filled water park with slides and a lazy river. Entry from ₽1 500 (≈ $16) per person, open 10:00 AM–6:00 PM.

Getting Around

Phuket's public transport is limited but improving. Songthaews (shared pick-up trucks) run along main routes, costing ₽50–100 (≈ $0.55–$1.10) per trip. Local buses connect major beaches and Phuket Town, with fares from ₽30 (≈ $0.33). Taxis are metered but often negotiate fixed fares — expect ₽300–500 (≈ $3.30–$5.50) for short trips.

Motorbike taxis are faster and cheaper at ₽100–200 (≈ $1.10–$2.20) per ride. Renting a motorbike costs ₽250–400 (≈ $2.70–$4.40) per day, but you'll need an International Driving Permit. For convenience, use ride-hailing apps like Grab or Bolt, which are widely available.

Where to Eat

Phuket is a food lover's paradise. For authentic southern Thai cuisine, head to Raya Restaurant in Phuket Town (mains ₽200–400/≈ $2.20–$4.40). Kan Eang@Pier in Chalong Bay serves fresh seafood with ocean views (mains ₽400–800/≈ $4.40–$8.70).

Street food is a must-try: Patong's Bangla Road has stalls selling pad thai for ₽100 (≈ $1.10) and mango sticky rice for ₽80 (≈ $0.87). For a splurge, Suay Restaurant in Cherngtalay offers creative Thai fusion (mains ₽600–1 200/≈ $6.50–$13). Budget travelers can eat well at local markets like Phuket Town's Sunday Walking Street, where dishes cost ₽50–150 (≈ $0.55–$1.63).

Tours & Excursions

Phuket is a launchpad for incredible day trips. The most popular is a Phi Phi Islands tour (₽2 500–4 000/≈ $27–$44), which includes snorkeling, lunch, and hotel pickup. James Bond Island tours (₽2 000–3 000/≈ $22–$33) take you through Phang Nga Bay's limestone caves.

For a cultural experience, a Phuket City & Temple tour costs ₽1 500–2 500 (≈ $16–$27). Adventure seekers love white-water rafting in Phang Nga (₽3 000–5 000/≈ $33–$55). I recommend booking tours through GetYourGuide for reliable operators and free cancellation.

Airport Transfer

Getting from Phuket International Airport to your hotel is straightforward. Taxis are available at the airport's official taxi stand, with fixed fares to Patong (₽800–1 000/≈ $8.70–$10.90), Karon (₽900–1 200/≈ $9.80–$13), and Rawai (₽1 200–1 500/≈ $13–$16.30). Shared minivans cost ₽200–300 (≈ $2.20–$3.30) per person, but may have multiple stops.

Public buses (airport bus to Phuket Town) cost ₽100 (≈ $1.10), then you'll need a taxi to your beach. For a hassle-free experience, pre-book a private transfer on Kiwitaxi — prices start at ₽1 500 (≈ $16) and include a meet-and-greet service.

Connectivity

Staying connected in Phuket is easy. Most hotels, cafes, and restaurants offer free Wi-Fi, though speeds vary. For reliable mobile data, buy a local SIM at the airport or 7-Eleven: AIS, TrueMove, and dtac offer tourist SIMs with 10–15 GB data for ₽300–600 (≈ $3.30–$6.50). Alternatively, get an eSIM before you travel — it's instant and lets you keep your number. I use Airalo, which offers affordable data plans starting from ₽400 (≈ $4.40) for 7 days.

Best Time to Visit

Phuket has a tropical monsoon climate. The high season (November to April) offers sunny skies, calm seas, and temperatures around 28–32°C (82–90°F). Prices for hotels and flights are at their peak, with a double room costing ₽4 000–10 000 (≈ $44–$109) per night. The shoulder season (May to October) sees occasional rain but fewer crowds and lower prices — rooms drop to ₽2 000–5 000 (≈ $22–$55).

The green season (June to September) has the most rain, but it often comes in short bursts, and you'll find great deals. For diving, the Similan Islands are only open from November to April. I recommend visiting in January or February for the best weather, or September for budget-friendly travel.

Visa & Documents

For citizens of Russia, Thailand offers a visa-free entry for stays up to 60 days (extendable by 30 days at an immigration office). You need a passport valid for at least 6 months from your entry date, a return ticket, and proof of accommodation. No visa is required for tourism.

For other nationalities, check with the Thai embassy. Important for Maldives travelers: Перед вылетом заполните декларацию IMUGA на imuga.immigration.gov.mv и получите QR-код — без него не посадят на рейс. For Phuket, no such form is needed, but it's good practice to have your hotel booking handy.

Currency & Money

The local currency is the Thai baht (THB), symbolized as ฿. Exchange rates fluctuate, but as of 2026, ₽1 = approximately 0.40 THB (so ₽100 ≈ 40 THB). You can exchange money at banks, exchange booths (SuperRich offers good rates), or ATMs (withdrawal fee of $7 (220 ฿)/≈ ₽550).

Credit cards are accepted at hotels, larger restaurants, and shops, but street vendors and local markets prefer cash. Bring some cash in USD or EUR for better rates at exchange counters. I recommend carrying at least $297 (10000 ฿) (≈ ₽25 000) in cash for a week, plus your card for emergencies.

Time Zone

Phuket is in Indochina Time (ICT), which is UTC+7. There is no daylight saving. Time difference from Moscow: +4 hours (when Moscow is 12:00 PM, Phuket is 4:00 PM). From Bangkok: same time. From London: +7 hours. From New York: +12 hours. From Sydney: -3 hours (when Sydney is 12:00 PM, Phuket is 9:00 AM).
