Phuket, Thailand’s largest island, is a tropical paradise that offers far more than just stunning beaches. From ancient temples and vibrant night markets to lush national parks and world-class diving spots, Phuket is a destination that caters to every type of traveler.

Whether you’re a first-time visitor or a seasoned explorer, the island’s rich culture, warm hospitality, and endless activities make it a must-visit in 2026. I’ve spent countless hours exploring Phuket’s hidden gems and popular attractions, and I’m excited to share my personal favorites with you. Let’s dive into the best things to do on this incredible island!

15 Best Attractions

1. Big Buddha (Wat Phra Sing)

The Big Buddha is one of Phuket’s most iconic landmarks, sitting atop Nakkerd Hill with panoramic views of the island. This 45-meter-tall marble statue is a serene spot for reflection and photography. The site is free to enter, but donations for maintenance are welcome.

Ticket price: Free (₽0), but donations of ₽100–₽200 (≈ $1–$2) are appreciated. Hours: Daily 6:00 AM – 6:30 PM. How to get there: Take a taxi or scooter from Patong (₽400–₽600, ≈ $4–$6) or join a guided tour. Tip: Visit early morning to avoid crowds and catch the sunrise over the Andaman Sea.

2. Wat Chalong Temple

This is Phuket’s most important Buddhist temple, known for its intricate architecture and the revered Luang Pho Chaem statue. The temple complex includes a grand pagoda with relics of the Buddha. It’s a peaceful place to learn about local culture and spirituality.

Ticket price: Free (₽0). Hours: Daily 7:00 AM – 5:00 PM. How to get there: Located in Chalong, a 20-minute drive from Patong (₽300–₽500, ≈ $3–$5 by taxi). Tip: Dress modestly (cover shoulders and knees) and remove shoes before entering prayer halls.

5. Old Phuket Town

This historic area is a treasure trove of Sino-Portuguese architecture, colorful shophouses, and street art. Wander through Soi Romanee and Thalang Road for boutique shops, cafés, and the famous Phuket Sunday Walking Street Market. Ticket price: Free (₽0). Hours: Open 24/7, but shops open 10:00 AM – 9:00 PM. How to get there: Take a taxi from Patong (₽500–₽700, ≈ $5–$7) or a local bus (₽50, ≈ $0.50). Tip: Visit on Sunday evening for the market—try local snacks like roti and mango sticky rice.

6. Patong Beach

Patong is Phuket’s most famous beach, known for its lively atmosphere, water sports, and nightlife. During the day, you can jet-ski, parasail, or simply sunbathe. At night, Bangla Road comes alive with bars and clubs. Ticket price: Free (₽0) for beach access; water sports from ₽1,000 (≈ $11) per activity.

Hours: Beach open 24/7; best visited 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM. How to get there: Walk from most Patong hotels or take a tuk-tuk (₽100–₽200, ≈ $1–$2). Tip: Avoid the midday heat (12 PM–2 PM) and bring cash for beachside massages (₽300–₽500, ≈ $3–$5).

7. Simon Cabaret Show

This world-famous cabaret features dazzling performances by transgender artists (kathoey) in elaborate costumes. It’s a fun, family-friendly show that celebrates Thai culture and artistry. Ticket price: ₽1,200–₽1,800 (≈ $13–$19) for standard seats. Hours: Shows at 6:00 PM, 7:30 PM, and 9:00 PM daily. How to get there: Located on Patong’s Rat-U-Thit Road, a short walk from the beach. You can feed, bathe, and learn about these gentle giants. Ticket price: ₽4,000–₽6,000 (≈ $43–$65) for a half-day visit, including lunch. Hours: Tours run 7:00 AM – 1:00 PM or 12:00 PM – 5:00 PM. 9. Promthep Cape Sunset Viewpoint

This is Phuket’s most famous sunset spot, offering breathtaking views of the Andaman Sea and nearby islands. The cape is also home to a lighthouse and a small shrine. Ticket price: Free (₽0). Hours: Open 24/7; best visited 5:00 PM – 6:30 PM. How to get there: Located near Rawai Beach, a 30-minute drive from Patong (₽500–₽700, ≈ $5–$7 by taxi). Tip: Arrive early (by 4:30 PM) to secure a good spot, and bring insect repellent for the evening.

10. Kata Noi Beach

Kata Noi is a quieter, more picturesque beach compared to Patong, with soft white sand and clear waters perfect for swimming and snorkeling. It’s ideal for families and couples seeking relaxation. Ticket price: Free (₽0). Hours: Open 24/7. How to get there: Take a taxi from Patong (₽400–₽600, ≈ $4–$6) or a local bus (₽50, ≈ $0.50). Tip: Rent a sunbed for ₽200–₽300 (≈ $2–$3) and enjoy fresh coconut water from beach vendors.

13. Coral Island (Koh Hae)

Just a short speedboat ride from Phuket, Coral Island offers pristine beaches, clear waters, and excellent snorkeling. It’s less crowded than Phi Phi and perfect for a half-day escape. Ticket price: ₽1,500–₽2,500 (≈ $16–$27) for a speedboat tour. Hours: Tours depart 9:00 AM – 2:00 PM. How to get there: Book via

14. Wat Phra Thong (Golden Buddha)

This temple houses a half-buried golden Buddha statue, believed to be ancient and sacred. The site is less touristy, offering a serene atmosphere. Ticket price: Free (₽0). Hours: Daily 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM. How to get there: Located in Thalang district, a 30-minute drive from Patong (₽500–₽700, ≈ $5–$7 by taxi). Tip: Combine with a visit to nearby Phuket Elephant Sanctuary for a full day.

15. Surin Beach

Surin Beach is a trendy, upscale beach known for its soft sand, clear water, and chic beach clubs like Catch Club. It’s a great spot for sunbathing and people-watching. Ticket price: Free (₽0). Hours: Open 24/7; best visited 9:00 AM – 5:00 PM. How to get there: Taxi from Patong (₽400–₽600, ≈ $4–$6). Tip: Visit during weekdays to avoid crowds, and try the seafood at the beachfront restaurants.

1-Day Itinerary

Morning (8:00 AM – 12:00 PM): Start at the Big Buddha for sunrise views, then visit Wat Chalong Temple. Lunch (12:00 PM – 1:00 PM): Enjoy local Thai food at a restaurant near Wat Chalong (₽200–₽400, ≈ $2–$4). Afternoon (1:00 PM – 4:00 PM): Explore Old Phuket Town, including Thalang Road and the Sunday market (if it’s a Sunday). Evening (5:00 PM – 7:00 PM): Head to Promthep Cape for sunset, then dinner at a seaside restaurant in Rawai (₽500–₽800, ≈ $5–$9).

  • Phang Nga Bay Tour: ₽2,500–₽4,000 (≈ $27–$43) – includes canoeing and James Bond Island visit.
  • Elephant Sanctuary Visit: ₽4,000–₽6,000 (≈ $43–$65) – half-day with feeding and bathing.

Book Tickets Online

Booking tickets online is the best way to save money and avoid long queues. For attractions like the Big Buddha or Wat Chalong, entry is free, but for shows and tours, online booking offers discounts. For example:

  • Simon Cabaret Show: ₽1,200–₽1,800 (≈ $13–$19) online vs. ₽2,000 (≈ $22) at the door.
  • Phuket FantaSea: ₽2,500–₽3,500 (≈ $27–$38) online, often with free shuttle.
  • Phi Phi Islands Tour: ₽3,500 (≈ $38) online vs. ₽4,500 (≈ $49) on the pier.

Free Attractions

Phuket has plenty of free attractions that are just as rewarding as paid ones:

  • Big Buddha: Free entry (donations optional).
  • Wat Chalong Temple: Free entry, but dress modestly.
  • Old Phuket Town: Free to explore, with street art and architecture.
  • Patong Beach & Kata Noi Beach: Free beach access.
  • Promthep Cape: Free sunset viewpoint.
  • Phuket Sunday Walking Street Market: Free to browse (food costs extra).
  • Khao Rang Hill Viewpoint: Free panoramic views of Phuket City.

For a full free day, start at the Big Buddha, then walk to Wat Chalong (30 minutes downhill), lunch in Phuket Town, and end at Promthep Cape for sunset.

Transportation

How much do attractions cost in Phuket?

Do I need a guide in Phuket?

A guide is recommended for historical sites and complex routes. Self-guided travel is possible with audio guides and maps.
